      Shop Smart

      • We swap pork loin in for ribs, and that one substitution is the whole budget story: loin runs about a third the per-pound price of ribs with far more meat and no bones to pay for.
      • Mustard, Worcestershire, vinegar, and honey are all pantry bottles that last several batches, so the recurring cost of this recipe is basically pork and rice.
      • Watch for whole pork loins on sale under $2 a pound. Buy the big one, cut it into batch-size pieces, and freeze; it holds quality for months.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the marinade: minced garlic, grated onion, Dijon mustard, Worcestershire sauce, apple cider vinegar, honey, salt, pepper, oregano, cumin, and cayenne.
      3. Cut the pork loin into thick slabs (about 1.5 inches), coat them in the marinade, and refrigerate at least 2 hours, overnight if you can plan that far ahead.
      4. Preheat the oven to 375F. Arrange the pork in a roasting pan, pour any extra marinade over, and cover tightly with foil.
      5. Roast covered for 35 to 40 minutes, then uncover, spoon the pan juices back over the meat, and roast 15 to 20 minutes more until the edges brown and the pork reaches at least 145F.
      6. Rest the pork 10 minutes, then slice or cube it and toss it back through the pan juices.
      7. While the pork roasts, cook 6.25 cups dry rice with water per package directions.
      8. Combine the rice, pork, and every drop of pan sauce in your weighed pot.

      Portion It

      Put the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot number, and divide by 14. Scoop each portion into a container, making sure the pan juices get shared out evenly. Every bowl carries 565 calories and 44 grams of protein.
