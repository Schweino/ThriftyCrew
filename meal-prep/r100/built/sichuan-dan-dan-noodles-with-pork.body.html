      • Rice noodles are the surprise big spender here at about $20.99. If your store has an international aisle or you have an Asian market nearby, bulk rice noodles can run half the price of the name-brand boxes.
      • One jar of Lao Gan Ma chili crisp looks pricey at the register, but it seasons this batch and several more after it. Cost per batch is a fraction of the sticker.
      • Frozen broccoli florets beat fresh on price here, about $5.82 for five bags, no trimming waste, and nothing dies in your crisper drawer while you procrastinate.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the sauce: peanut butter, soy sauce, hoisin, sugar, chicken broth, and about half the chili crisp. It will look thin now, it thickens when it hits the hot noodles.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot and brown the ground pork, breaking it into small crumbles. Season with the five-spice powder, black pepper, and salt, and let it get crispy at the edges.
      4. Add the garlic to the pork and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
      5. Cook the rice noodles per the package, drain, and rinse briefly so they do not glue themselves together.
      6. Steam or microwave the broccoli florets until just tender.
      7. Toss the noodles, sauce, and broccoli together in the big pot until coated, then top with the pork, green onions, chopped peanuts, and the rest of the chili crisp.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Portion each container to that number and every bowl lands at about 880 calories with 30 grams of protein. This is one of the heartier bowls in the library, built for big appetites.
