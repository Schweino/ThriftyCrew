      Shop Smart

      • This recipe stretches 1.25 lb of ground beef across 14 servings by leaning on the cheese filling for protein. That is the cheapest way to eat beef, using it as a flavor, not a slab.
      • Ricotta is the big-ticket item, so compare tub sizes; the 15 oz store-brand tubs almost always beat the small ones on price per ounce.
      • Store-brand marinara around $1.82 a jar bakes up the same as the premium jars once it is under cheese. Three jars here, so the savings are real.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Preheat the oven to 375F. Boil the pasta shells in salted water until just flexible, about 2 minutes shy of the package time, then drain and lay them out so they do not stick together.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a skillet over medium-high and brown the ground beef with the diced onion until no pink remains (160F for ground beef). Add the garlic for the last minute, then stir in the marinara and tomato paste and simmer 5 minutes.
      4. Make the filling: mix the ricotta, eggs, Parmesan, about a third of the mozzarella, Italian seasoning, garlic powder, salt, and pepper.
      5. Spread a layer of meat sauce across the bottom of two 9x13 pans. Spoon the filling into the shells and nestle them in, open side up.
      6. Pour the remaining meat sauce over the shells and top with the rest of the mozzarella.
      7. Cover with foil and bake 25 minutes, then uncover and bake 10 more until the cheese is bubbly and starting to brown. Rest 10 minutes before portioning.

      Portion It

      Transfer everything to your weighed pot, set it on the scale, subtract the empty-pot number, and divide by 14. That works out to a few stuffed shells plus sauce per container. Each bowl brings 560 calories and 38 grams of protein.
