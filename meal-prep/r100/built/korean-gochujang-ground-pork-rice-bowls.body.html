      Makes 14 servings · ~560 cal · 26g protein · 48g carbs · 28g fat · ~$1.65 per serving.

      Sweet, spicy, garlicky ground pork glazed in gochujang and mirin, piled over rice with green onions and toasted sesame.       Shop Smart

      • Ground pork at around $3.41 a pound is the whole reason this bowl is so cheap. It is routinely a dollar or more per pound under ground beef with the same job description.
      • Gochujang, mirin, sesame oil, and soy sauce all live in the pantry for months. The first trip stings a little, then every batch after this one costs mostly just the pork.
      • Fresh ginger freezes whole. Buy the two pieces, use what you need, and grate the rest straight from the freezer next time instead of buying again.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice: 2 cups dry with water per your usual method.
      3. Stir the sauce together: gochujang, mirin, sugar, soy sauce, and half the sesame oil.
      4. Brown the ground pork in your big pot over medium-high heat, breaking it up, until no pink remains. Drain if needed.
      5. Add the diced onion, garlic, and ginger and cook 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant.
      6. Pour in the sauce and simmer 5 minutes until it glazes the pork and thickens slightly.
      7. Finish with the rest of the sesame oil, most of the green onions, and the toasted sesame seeds. Serve over the rice with the last of the green onions on top.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and divide by 14. Portion that weight of glazed pork over even scoops of rice in 14 containers. Every bowl checks in at 560 calories and 26 grams of protein.
