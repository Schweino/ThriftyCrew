      Makes 14 servings · ~730 cal · 31g protein · 78g carbs · 34g fat · ~$2.97 per serving.

      Pad kee mao, drunken noodles, is the spicy, basil-loaded stir fry that ruins mild takeout forever. Each bowl packs 31 grams of protein from ground pork for about $2.97, with jalapeno heat and wide rice noodles that actually catch the sauce.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground pork covers the protein at about $13.16 for 3.75 pounds. It is consistently cheaper than the sliced pork most stir fry recipes call for, and it browns faster too.
      • Rice noodles at about $11.91 are the other big line. Buy them from the international aisle or an Asian market and that number can drop by a third.
      • Fish sauce, soy sauce, and rice vinegar are all buy-once bottles that live in the pantry. This batch uses about $1.60 of soy and vinegar combined off bottles that season months of cooking.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak or boil the rice noodles per the package until just shy of tender, then drain and rinse. They finish in the pan.
      3. Stir together the fish sauce, soy sauce, and rice vinegar for the sauce.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet or pot over high heat. Cook the onion 2 minutes, then add the garlic and jalapeno for 1 minute more.
      5. Add the ground pork and brown it hard, breaking it into crumbles, until cooked through with crispy edges.
      6. Add the noodles and the sauce and toss until the noodles soak it up and everything is coated.
      7. Kill the heat and fold in the fresh basil, sliced bell pepper, and lime juice. The basil should wilt in the residual heat, not cook.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Portion to that number and each bowl lands at about 730 calories with 31 grams of protein. A proper full-size dinner bowl.
