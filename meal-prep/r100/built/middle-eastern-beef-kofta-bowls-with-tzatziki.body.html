      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 47g protein · 63g carbs · 13g fat · ~$3.86 per serving.

      Kofta is spiced beef shaped into little logs and roasted until browned, then served over rice with cool garlic tzatziki, tomatoes, and herbs. It is a serious flavor-per-effort bargain.       Shop Smart

      • Cherry tomatoes were the second biggest line on this receipt at over $10. If romas or regular tomatoes are cheaper the week you shop, dice those instead and save a few dollars.
      • Making tzatziki from a big tub of plain Greek yogurt costs a fraction of the little store-made tubs, and the 32 ounce tub covered all 14 servings for under $3.
      • Beef is the anchor at nearly $29. Watch for the 5 pound value pack price and cook this recipe the week it drops.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the tzatziki: grate about a third of the cucumbers, salt them, and squeeze out the water hard. Stir into the Greek yogurt with half the garlic, a splash of lemon juice, the mint, and a pinch of salt. Refrigerate.
      3. Cook the rice: 4.5 cups dry with water per your usual method.
      4. Grate one of the red onions and squeeze it dry. Mix it into the ground beef with the remaining garlic, cumin, cinnamon, allspice, red pepper flakes, salt, and pepper.
      5. Shape the beef into small logs or ovals, about 3 per serving, and set them on foil-lined sheet pans.
      6. Broil or bake at 450 F for 12 to 15 minutes, flipping once, until browned outside and cooked through with no pink in the center.
      7. Slice the remaining cucumber and red onion and halve the cherry tomatoes.
      8. Build each bowl: rice, kofta, tomato and cucumber salad, cilantro on top, tzatziki in its own corner or cup.

      Portion It

      Weigh your finished kofta in the pot, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and divide by 14, then do the same for the rice. Each assembled bowl lands at 562 calories and 47 grams of protein.
