      Shop Smart

      • Feta is the most expensive ingredient per ounce here. Watch for tub sales, or swap in more parmesan if feta is priced badly the week you shop.
      • Making bechamel from butter, flour, and milk costs about $2.76 for a huge batch. A jarred white sauce would cost triple and taste worse. Whisking is free.
      • The beef is over half the receipt. The 4 pound pack at about $6.17 a pound was the play on our board, and every sale dollar off per pound saves you nearly $4 a batch.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the spaghetti two minutes short of package time, drain, and toss with a little of the melted butter and about a third of the feta.
      3. Make the meat sauce: brown the beef with the red onion and garlic in olive oil, then add diced tomatoes, tomato paste, beef broth, sugar, cinnamon, cloves, bay leaves, salt, and pepper. Simmer 20 minutes until thick, then pull the bay leaves.
      4. Make the bechamel: melt the butter in your big pot, whisk in the flour for 1 minute, then whisk in the milk gradually. Simmer, whisking, until it coats a spoon. Off heat, whisk in the nutmeg and most of the parmesan.
      5. Temper the beaten eggs into the warm bechamel a ladle at a time, whisking constantly so they enrich instead of scramble.
      6. Layer in a deep greased 9x13: pasta, all the meat sauce, then the bechamel poured over the top. Sprinkle with the remaining parmesan and feta.
      7. Bake at 350 F for 40 to 45 minutes until deep golden, then rest at least 20 minutes so it slices clean.

      Portion It

      Weigh your pot of finished meat sauce before layering, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and spread it evenly. After baking and resting, cut the pan into 14 squares and box them. Each square comes in at 773 calories and 56 grams of protein.
