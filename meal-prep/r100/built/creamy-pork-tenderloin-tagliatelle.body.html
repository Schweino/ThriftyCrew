      Shop Smart

      • One pound of pork tenderloin plus three quarters of a pound of bacon covers all 14 servings. Small amounts of two flavorful meats beat a big pile of one bland one, and the math agrees.
      • Store-brand fettuccine stands in for tagliatelle at $2.56 for 42 ounces. Same egg-pasta idea, same ribbon shape, half the specialty price.
      • Mushrooms and onion at under $4 combined bulk up the sauce so the meat can stay small. That is how the whole bowl stays under a buck and a half.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the fettuccine 1 minute short of package time, drain, and toss with a little of the butter so it does not stick.
      3. Chop the bacon and cook it in your big pot until the fat renders and it starts to crisp.
      4. Slice the pork tenderloin into thin strips, season with salt and pepper, and add it to the bacon. Cook 4 to 5 minutes until the pork is cooked through with no pink; thin strips pass 145 F almost immediately.
      5. Add the rest of the butter, the sliced mushrooms, and the diced onion. Cook 8 minutes or so until the mushrooms brown, then stir in the garlic for 1 minute.
      6. Pour in the splash of chicken broth to deglaze, then the heavy cream. Simmer 3 to 4 minutes until the sauce thickens slightly.
      7. Fold in the fettuccine and toss until every ribbon is coated. Season generously with black pepper.
      8. Portion into 14 containers while the sauce is still loose.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Weigh that into each container. Each bowl comes out to about 788 calories with 28 grams of protein.
