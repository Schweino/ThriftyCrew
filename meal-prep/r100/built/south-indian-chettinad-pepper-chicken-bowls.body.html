      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 46g protein · 67g carbs · 9g fat · ~$2.15 per serving.

      Chettinad pepper chicken is South India's answer to bland weeknights: chicken thighs in a dark, pepper-heavy masala that builds heat as you eat. Each bowl delivers 46 grams of protein for about $2.15, with only 9 grams of fat. Big flavor, lean numbers.

      Ingredients

      • Boneless Skinless Chicken Thigh (Member's Mark): 6.25 lb (2800 g)
      • Yellow Onion: 10.6 (1162 g)
      • Garlic: 12.25 tbsp (105 g)
      • Ginger (generic): 8.75 oz (245 g)
      • Dijon Mustard (generic): 7.5 oz (210 g)
      • Sugar (Great Value): 4.75 tbsp (59 g)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 4.25 cups dry (771 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Boneless skinless chicken thighs at about $2.45 a pound are the value play here, $15.31 covers the whole batch. Thighs stay juicy through a simmer where breast meat would dry out and cost more doing it.
      • The spice rack does the heavy lifting in this recipe. Black pepper, coriander, cumin, and turmeric look like $6 on paper, but those jars season a dozen batches, so the true cost per bowl is pennies.
      • Fresh ginger freezes beautifully. Buy the big piece, use what you need, and grate the rest straight from the freezer next time, no peeling required.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice per the package and set it aside.
      3. Finely chop the onions and mince the garlic and ginger. There is a lot of onion here on purpose, it cooks down into the body of the sauce.
      4. Cook the onions in a large pot over medium heat until deeply golden, 12 to 15 minutes. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 2 more minutes.
      5. Add the black pepper, ground coriander, cumin, turmeric, chili powder, and Italian seasoning and toast in the onions for 1 minute until fragrant.
      6. Cut the chicken thighs into bite-size pieces and add them with the salt. Stir to coat in the masala, then cover and simmer, stirring now and then, until the chicken hits 165F.
      7. Stir in the Dijon mustard and sugar, then simmer uncovered until the sauce thickens and clings to the chicken.
      8. Serve the pepper chicken over the rice.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Portion rice and chicken to that number and every bowl lands at about 561 calories with 46 grams of protein.
