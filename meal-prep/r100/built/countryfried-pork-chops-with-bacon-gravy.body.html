      Shop Smart

      • Pork chops at about $4.17 a pound are the whole budget here at $21.88. Watch the weekly ad; chops on sale can knock $5 or more off this batch.
      • A half pound of bacon flavors the entire gravy for $2.44. You are buying it as a seasoning, not a side, which is how bacon earns its keep on a budget.
      • Whole potatoes for mashing cost $1.36 for the batch. A box of instant mash or a tub of prepared potatoes covering 14 servings would run 3 to 4 times that.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Start the potatoes: peel, chunk, and boil them in salted water 15 to 20 minutes until fork tender. Drain, mash with the milk and a pinch of salt, and cover.
      3. Set up the dredge: whisk the eggs in one dish and mix the flour with garlic powder, onion powder, salt, and black pepper in another.
      4. Dip each pork chop in egg, then press into the seasoned flour on both sides. Save the leftover flour for the gravy.
      5. Chop the bacon and cook it in your big skillet until crisp. Lift the bacon out and leave the fat.
      6. Add olive oil to the bacon fat and fry the chops in batches, 4 to 5 minutes per side, until golden and the centers read 145 F. Rest them on a rack.
      7. Make the gravy: whisk about a quarter cup of the reserved seasoned flour into the drippings and cook 1 minute. Slowly whisk in the chicken broth and simmer until thickened, then stir the bacon back in.
      8. Portion mashed potatoes into 14 containers, top each with a chop, and ladle the bacon gravy over.

      Portion It

      Weigh the pot of mashed potatoes, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14 for the base, then split the chops and gravy evenly. Each plate lands at about 685 calories with 49 grams of protein.
