      Makes 14 servings · ~569 cal · 45g protein · 66g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.64 per serving.

      Bun thit nuong is the noodle bowl that eats like a salad and satisfies like a barbecue plate: charred pork over cool rice noodles with herbs, cucumber, and a sweet-tangy dressing. Each bowl delivers 45 grams of protein for about $2.64.

      Ingredients

      • Pork Loin: 5.25 lb (2380 g)
      • Rice Noodles (Thai Kitchen): 24.75 oz dry (700 g)
      • Cucumber: 7 (2100 g)
      • Fresh Mint: 17.5 tbsp (28 g)
      • Fresh Cilantro: 35 tbsp (35 g)
      • Peanuts (Great Value): 1 cups (128 g)
      • Carrots (generic): 7 (427 g)
      • Fish Sauce (Thai Kitchen): 1 cups (284 g)
      • Sugar (Great Value): 1 cups (197 g)
      • Lime Juice: 0.75 cups (158 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Pork loin is the value cut here at about $9.48 for 5.25 pounds, one of the cheapest per-pound proteins in the case. Sliced thin and marinated, it eats like the expensive stuff.
      • Fish sauce does double duty as marinade and dressing base. One bottle covers this batch and many more, the per-batch cost is around a dollar.
      • Buy whole cucumbers and carrots and cut them yourself. The pre-cut versions cost two to three times more per pound for someone else's ten minutes of knife work.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the pork loin into thin strips. Marinate it in about a third of the fish sauce and a third of the sugar for at least 20 minutes.
      3. Make the nuoc cham dressing: stir the remaining fish sauce and sugar with the lime juice and about 2 cups of warm water until the sugar dissolves. Taste it, it should hit sweet, salty, and sour in that order.
      4. Cook the rice noodles per the package, drain, and rinse under cold water. These bowls are meant to be eaten cool or room temperature.
      5. Sear the pork in a very hot pan in batches, letting it char at the edges, until cooked through to 145F.
      6. Slice the cucumbers, shred the carrots, and rough-chop the mint and cilantro.
      7. Build bowls: noodles down, pork on top, vegetables and herbs around it, peanuts over everything. Pack the nuoc cham separately and pour it on when you eat.

      Portion It

      Weigh your noodles and pork together, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14, then split the vegetables, herbs, and peanuts evenly. Each bowl lands at about 569 calories with 45 grams of protein.
