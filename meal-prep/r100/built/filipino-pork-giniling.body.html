      Giniling is the Filipino cousin of picadillo: savory ground pork simmered in a soy and tomato sauce with potatoes, carrots, sweet golden raisins, and a boiled egg on top.       Shop Smart

      • Ground pork usually runs cheaper per pound than ground beef. The 4 lb pack drops the unit price further, and this recipe uses 3.75 lb of it, so almost nothing goes to waste.
      • Potatoes are the stretcher here. About 91 cents of spuds adds two pounds of food to the pot without watering down the flavor.
      • Eggs at $1.50 a dozen are the cheapest topping upgrade in the store. One bottle of fish sauce and one bag of golden raisins season several batches, so their real cost per bowl is pennies.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Hard boil the 12 eggs: cover with cold water, bring to a boil, kill the heat, lid on for 12 minutes, then cool, peel, and slice.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in your biggest pot and cook the diced onion until soft, about 5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute more.
      4. Add the ground pork and break it up as it browns. Cook until no pink remains, then stir in the soy sauce and fish sauce.
      5. Stir in the crushed tomatoes and tomato paste. Add the diced potatoes, carrots, and red bell pepper, cover, and simmer 15 to 20 minutes until the potatoes are fork tender.
      6. Stir in the frozen peas and golden raisins and simmer 5 more minutes. Taste and season with salt and black pepper.
      7. Portion into 14 containers and divide the sliced eggs across the tops.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot number you wrote down, and divide the difference by 14. That is one serving in grams. Scoop it out on the scale and each bowl lands at about 571 calories and 32 grams of protein.
