      Shop Smart

      • Ground pork is quietly one of the best protein deals in the meat case, usually cheaper per pound than ground beef. The 5 lb value packs drop the price further.
      • Fresh ginger costs pennies when you buy just the knob you need, and the rest freezes whole. Grate it frozen; it is actually easier.
      • One can of store-brand coconut milk is often a dollar less than the name brand. This recipe uses two cans, so that is two dollars back in your pocket for reading a shelf tag.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Preheat the oven to 400F and line two sheet pans. In a big bowl, mix the ground pork with the grated onion, most of the garlic, the ginger, soy sauce, bread crumbs, half the cilantro, red pepper flakes, salt, and pepper. Do not overwork it.
      3. Roll the mixture into golf-ball-size meatballs (you should get 40 to 45) and space them out on the sheet pans.
      4. Bake 15 to 18 minutes until browned and cooked through; ground pork needs to hit 160F in the center.
      5. While they bake, make the coconut rice: combine the dry rice, both cans of coconut milk, and enough water to hit your usual rice ratio. Bring to a boil, cover, and simmer low 18 to 20 minutes, then rest 5 minutes and fluff.
      6. Whisk the drizzle: lime juice, sriracha, rice vinegar, and the remaining garlic.
      7. Combine the rice and meatballs in your big pot, pour the drizzle over, and toss gently so the meatballs stay whole. Finish with green onions and the rest of the cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the loaded pot, subtract the empty-pot number, and divide by 14. Scoop each portion into a container, aiming for about 3 meatballs per bowl. Each one lands at 841 calories and 36 grams of protein.
