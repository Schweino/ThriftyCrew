      Shop Smart

      • Lo mein noodles are the biggest line at about $22.51. An Asian grocery sells comparable noodles in bulk for roughly half the name-brand price, worth the trip if you make noodle recipes often.
      • Ground pork at about $3.51 a pound is one of the cheapest proteins in the case that is not chicken. Watch for 2 lb rolls on markdown and freeze them flat.
      • Chili crisp, hoisin, and soy are buy-once bottles. This batch uses maybe $5.66 worth combined, and the jars keep seasoning batch after batch from the fridge door.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the lo mein noodles until just al dente, drain, and toss with a small splash of oil so they do not clump.
      3. Brown the ground pork in a large pot over medium-high heat, breaking it up and letting it sit undisturbed a few minutes at a time so it gets crispy edges. Season with salt and black pepper.
      4. Add the garlic and ginger to the pork and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
      5. Stir in the chili crisp, hoisin sauce, soy sauce, and lime juice and let it bubble for 2 minutes so the sauce coats the meat.
      6. Add the noodles and most of the green onions and toss until every strand is glossy.
      7. Finish with the toasted sesame seeds and the rest of the green onions.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Portion to that number and each bowl lands at about 874 calories with 39 grams of protein. A big bowl, built to actually fill you up.
