      Shop Smart

      • Potatoes are the ultimate budget filler: 7 pounds for $3.08 stretches 4.5 pounds of chorizo across 14 full bowls.
      • Chorizo tubes are the cheap cut of the pork case at about $1.50 per 9 ounce tube, and the seasoning is already inside the meat. You are buying spice and fat in one package.
      • Tomatillos, cilantro, and lime make a quick fresh salsa for about $3. A jarred salsa verde of the same volume runs $6 or more.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the potatoes into half-inch cubes, toss with olive oil, salt, chili powder, and garlic powder, and roast at 425 F for 30 to 35 minutes until crispy, flipping once.
      3. While the potatoes roast, brown the chorizo in your big pot over medium heat, breaking it up, 8 to 10 minutes until fully cooked and starting to crisp. Pork chorizo is done when it reads 160 F or higher and no pink remains.
      4. Add the diced red bell pepper to the chorizo and cook 4 to 5 minutes until softened in the rendered fat.
      5. Make the quick salsa: finely chop the tomatillos and stir with the cilantro, lime juice, and a pinch of salt and black pepper. For a smoother salsa, pulse it in a blender.
      6. Fold the roasted potatoes into the chorizo and peppers so everything gets coated.
      7. Portion into 14 containers and spoon the tomatillo salsa over each, or pack it on the side to keep it bright.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished pot of chorizo, peppers, and potatoes, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Each bowl comes out to about 656 calories with 25 grams of protein.
