      Shop Smart

      • Pork chops are the headline at about $29.17 for 7 pounds. That is a serious protein haul, and chops routinely hit sale prices under $3.50 a pound, time the buy and this bowl gets even cheaper.
      • The marinade is built from bottles you buy once: fish sauce, soy, oyster sauce, and honey. Their combined cost against this batch is a few dollars off jars that last months.
      • Eggs and canned diced tomatoes round out the bowl for under $5 combined. The canned tomatoes skip the produce-aisle price roulette and taste fine chilled next to rich pork.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the marinade: lemongrass paste, garlic, fish sauce, soy sauce, oyster sauce, honey, brown sugar, half the vegetable oil, and the grated onion. Coat the pork chops and marinate at least 30 minutes, overnight is better.
      3. Cook the rice per the package and set it aside.
      4. Sear the chops in a ripping hot pan or grill them, a few at a time, letting the sugars char at the edges. Cook to 145F and rest 5 minutes before slicing.
      5. Simmer the leftover marinade in the pan drippings for a few minutes at a full boil to make a safe, glossy drizzle sauce.
      6. Fry the eggs in the remaining oil, one per serving, leaving the yolks as soft or firm as your meal prep nerves allow.
      7. Slice the cucumbers and drain the diced tomatoes for the fresh side.
      8. Build bowls: rice, sliced pork, egg on top, cucumber and tomato beside, sauce and green onions over everything.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice and sliced pork in the pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14, then add an egg and an even share of the vegetables to each container. Every bowl lands at about 950 calories with 65 grams of protein. This is the biggest bowl on the site, plan your day around it.
