      Makes 14 servings · ~644 cal · 51g protein · 53g carbs · 22g fat · ~$1.91 per serving.

      Etouffee means smothered, and that is exactly what happens here: chicken thighs buried in a roux-thickened Cajun gravy with the holy trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper.       Shop Smart

      • Seven pounds of boneless skinless chicken thighs is the backbone here, and the club pack price per pound is why this stays near two bucks a bowl.
      • The holy trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper costs a couple dollars total. Buy whole heads and bunches, never the pre-chopped tubs at triple the price per ounce.
      • Flour and oil make the roux for pennies. Restaurants charge you for the 20 minutes of stirring; at home, that labor is free and you can listen to a podcast.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the roux: heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium, whisk in the flour, and stir near-constantly for 10 to 15 minutes until it turns the color of peanut butter. Do not walk away; a burnt roux starts over.
      3. Add the butter, diced onion, celery, and green bell pepper and cook 5 to 6 minutes until softened. Add the garlic for 1 minute more.
      4. Gradually whisk in the chicken broth so the roux stays smooth, then add the Worcestershire, Cajun seasoning, thyme, bay leaves, and hot sauce.
      5. Add the chicken thighs, cut into large bite-size pieces, and bring to a gentle simmer.
      6. Simmer uncovered 25 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the gravy thickens and the chicken reaches 165F. Pull the bay leaves and stir in most of the green onions.
      7. Meanwhile, cook 3.75 cups dry rice with water per package directions.
      8. Combine the rice and etouffee in your weighed pot, or portion rice first and ladle the etouffee over, your call. Finish with the remaining green onions.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight into each of 14 containers, gravy and all. Every bowl lands at 644 calories and 51 grams of protein.
