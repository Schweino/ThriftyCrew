      Shop Smart

      • Pork loin at around $2 a pound is the whole reason this batch is so cheap. Whole loins go on deep sale often; buy one, cube half for this, freeze the rest.
      • Carrots, celery, and onion are the cheapest flavor base in cooking. Buy whole carrots and a full celery bunch, not the pre-cut cups, and you pay a third the price.
      • We use fettuccine instead of pappardelle because it is the same egg-noodle idea at a fraction of the cost per ounce. Nobody can tell under a ladle of ragu.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork loin into 2-inch chunks and season with salt and pepper. Heat the vegetable oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high and sear the pork in batches until browned on a couple of sides. Set aside.
      3. In the same pot, cook the diced onion, carrots, and celery for 5 to 6 minutes until softened, then add the garlic and tomato paste and stir 1 minute.
      4. Pour in the beef broth and diced tomatoes, scraping up the browned bits. Add the thyme and Italian seasoning, then return the pork and its juices.
      5. Bring to a simmer, cover, and braise on low heat (or in a 300F oven) for 2 to 2.5 hours, until the pork shreds easily with a fork.
      6. Shred the pork right in the pot with two forks and simmer uncovered 10 to 15 minutes to thicken the sauce. Taste for salt.
      7. Boil the fettuccine in salted water to al dente, drain, and fold it into the ragu until every ribbon is coated.

      Portion It

      Set the loaded pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Twirl even portions into 14 containers; the sauce keeps the pasta from drying out all week. Each bowl lands at 578 calories and 37 grams of protein.
