      Shop Smart

      • Ground turkey usually undercuts ground beef by a couple dollars a pound for the same lean ratio. The 3 lb pack is the better per-pound deal, and the extra portion freezes fine.
      • Store-brand ziti at under a dollar a box is one of the best calorie-per-dollar buys in the store. Stock up during pasta sales; it keeps for a year plus.
      • Buy the Parmesan wedge or tub, not the shaker can. Better flavor per ounce, and this recipe only needs 1.75 oz, so one tub covers many batches.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Preheat the oven to 375F. Boil the ziti in salted water until just shy of al dente, then drain.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high and brown the ground turkey with the minced garlic, breaking it up as it cooks, until no pink remains; ground turkey should reach 165F. Season well with salt and pepper.
      4. Pour in the marinara sauce and simmer 5 minutes so the turkey soaks up the tomato flavor.
      5. Fold in the drained ziti, most of the chopped fresh basil, and half the mozzarella until everything is coated in sauce.
      6. Spread into two 9x13 pans, dollop the ricotta over the top in big spoonfuls, then finish with the remaining mozzarella and the Parmesan. Bake 20 to 25 minutes until melted and golden at the edges.
      7. Rest 10 minutes, scatter the last of the basil over the top, then combine into your weighed pot for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Scoop even portions into 14 containers and they will reheat evenly all week. Each bowl holds 644 calories and 41 grams of protein.
