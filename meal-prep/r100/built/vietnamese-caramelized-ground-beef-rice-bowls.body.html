      Shop Smart

      • The 93/7 ground beef is the anchor at about $14.61 for 3.75 pounds. Lean beef matters here, the glaze needs to stick to meat, not swim in grease.
      • Cucumbers are the sneaky volume play, 14 of them keeping every bowl fresh and crunchy. Buy them loose, not shrink-wrapped, the loose ones run cheaper per pound.
      • Lemongrass paste in the tube costs more per ounce than fresh stalks, but there is zero waste and it keeps in the fridge for months. For a few teaspoons per batch, the tube wins on real cost.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice per the package and set it aside.
      3. Prep the fresh toppings: slice the cucumbers, halve the cherry tomatoes, shred the carrots, and thinly slice the red onion and green onions.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet over high heat and brown the ground beef hard, breaking it up, until cooked through with crispy edges.
      5. Add the garlic, jalapeno, and lemongrass paste and stir fry 1 minute.
      6. Add the brown sugar, fish sauce, soy sauce, and the splash of chicken broth. Cook, stirring, until the sugar melts into a dark, glossy caramel that coats the beef, 3 to 5 minutes.
      7. Fold in most of the green onions and pull it off the heat.
      8. Build bowls: rice, caramelized beef, then the fresh vegetables on top with a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice and beef in the pot, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14, then split the fresh vegetables evenly across the containers. Each bowl lands at about 700 calories with 38 grams of protein.
