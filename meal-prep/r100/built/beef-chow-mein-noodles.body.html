      Shop Smart

      • The lo mein noodles are the biggest cost in this batch. Check the Asian aisle price per ounce against spaghetti; in a pinch, spaghetti swaps in and cuts this recipe's cost nearly in half.
      • Green cabbage is one of the cheapest vegetables per pound in the entire produce section, and a whole head shreds down into a mountain of stir-fry filler.
      • Oyster sauce looks expensive per bottle, but it lasts several batches in the fridge. Amortize it out and it is pennies a bowl.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the lo mein noodles 1 minute short of package directions, drain, rinse briefly with cool water, and toss with a splash of the oil so they do not clump.
      3. Whisk the sauce: soy sauce, oyster sauce, chicken broth, sugar, and flour. Set it by the stove.
      4. Heat a little oil in your largest skillet or wok over high heat. Scramble the beaten eggs into ribbons, then set them aside.
      5. Add more oil and brown the ground beef with the garlic, breaking it up, until no pink remains; ground beef should hit 160F. Season with salt and pepper.
      6. Pile in the shredded cabbage and carrots and stir-fry 3 to 4 minutes until they wilt but keep a little crunch.
      7. Pour in the sauce and let it bubble 1 to 2 minutes until it thickens, then add the noodles, eggs, and green onions and toss until everything is glossy and coated.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot of noodles, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Tong each portion into a container by weight; noodles love to sneak extra into early boxes. Every bowl comes out to 706 calories and 38 grams of protein.
