      Shop Smart

      • Walnuts are the big line at over $11. Buy them in the baking aisle bags, not the snack aisle, and check the store brand first. Price per ounce swings wildly between the two.
      • One bottle of pomegranate molasses looks pricey but covers this batch and keeps for a year. Amortized per bowl, it is under a dollar of tang and shine.
      • Chicken thighs at about $2.48 a pound are the right cut and the cheap cut. Breast would cost more and dry out in a long simmer. Skip the upgrade that is a downgrade.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice: 3.75 cups dry with water per your usual method.
      3. Toast the walnuts in your dry pot over medium heat, stirring, 5 minutes until fragrant. Cool slightly, then grind them fine in a food processor.
      4. Cook the diced onion in the olive oil until golden, then stir in the turmeric and cinnamon for 30 seconds.
      5. Add the chicken thighs cut into chunks and stir 2 to 3 minutes to coat.
      6. Add the ground walnuts and enough water to make a loose sauce, bring to a simmer, cover, and cook low for 30 minutes, stirring so the walnuts do not stick.
      7. Stir in the pomegranate molasses, sugar, salt, and pepper and simmer uncovered 15 to 20 more minutes until the stew is thick and dark and the chicken reads 165 F.
      8. Taste and balance: more molasses for tart, a pinch more sugar for sweet. Serve over the rice.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot of stew, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and divide by 14, then ladle that weight over even scoops of rice in 14 containers. Every bowl comes in at 638 calories and 29 grams of protein.
