      Makes 14 servings · ~616 cal · 53g protein · 43g carbs · 25g fat · ~$3.39 per serving.

      Koobideh is the Persian kabob house classic: ground beef whipped with grated onion until it clings, then grilled into juicy, savory kabobs served over buttered rice. Ours skips the skewers, keeps the flavor, and stacks 53 grams of protein into every bowl for about $3.39.

      Ingredients

      • 93/7 Ground Beef (Member's Mark): 7 lb (3175 g)
      • Yellow Onion: 3.5 (385 g)
      • Garlic: 4 tbsp (35 g)
      • Butter (generic): 0.75 cups (147 g)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 3.75 cups dry (700 g)

      • This recipe is 7 pounds of beef, full stop. At about $6.17 a pound it is over 90 percent of the receipt, so shop it the week ground beef goes on sale and save real money.
      • Everything else in the bowl costs about $4 combined. Onion, garlic, butter, rice, spices. That is the whole supporting cast.
      • There is no marinade, no breadcrumbs, no filler to buy. The onion juice is the tenderizer, and it costs 97 cents.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice: 3.75 cups dry with water per your usual method. Melt the butter into the hot rice and fluff.
      3. Grate the onions on a box grater and squeeze the pulp firmly to remove most of the juice. Wet meat falls apart, so squeeze like you mean it.
      4. Knead the beef with the squeezed onion, garlic, paprika, turmeric, salt, and pepper for a good 3 to 4 minutes until it turns sticky and pasty. That texture is what holds koobideh together.
      5. Shape into long flat logs, about one wide log per serving, and press a light zigzag pattern in with your fingers, kabob-house style.
      6. Broil on high on a foil-lined pan 4 to 5 minutes per side, or grill over hot coals, until browned outside with no pink in the middle.
      7. Rest 5 minutes, then serve over the buttered rice with any pan juices poured back on top.

      Portion It

      Weigh your finished kabobs in the pot, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and divide by 14, then do the same with the buttered rice. Each bowl lands at 616 calories and a heavyweight 53 grams of protein.
