      Makes 14 servings · ~687 cal · 50g protein · 65g carbs · 23g fat · ~$2.55 per serving.

      Al pastor is that sweet-smoky taco truck flavor, pork marinated in achiote and chipotle with charred pineapple on top.       Shop Smart

      • Pork loin is one of the cheapest cuts of meat in the store per pound. Buy the whole loin, not the pre-cut chops, and slice it yourself to save even more.
      • The achiote paste looks pricey up front, but one block covers several batches. Amortized out, it costs you pennies per bowl. That is the whole trick with specialty ingredients.
      • Canned pineapple beats fresh here twice: it costs less per ounce, and the juice in the can goes straight into the marinade instead of down the drain.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Blend the marinade: orange juice, achiote paste, chipotles in adobo, apple cider vinegar, garlic, half the onion, brown sugar, cumin, oregano, salt, pepper, and about half the olive oil. Blend until smooth.
      3. Slice the pork loin into thin strips, toss with the marinade, and let it sit at least 30 minutes in the fridge (overnight is better).
      4. Start the rice: cook 3.5 cups dry rice with water per package directions, then stir in a squeeze of the pineapple juice at the end.
      5. Heat the remaining olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Sear the pork in batches so it browns instead of steams, about 4 to 5 minutes per batch, until cooked through to 145F with no pink.
      6. Push the pork aside, add the drained pineapple chunks and remaining onion, and let them char for 3 to 4 minutes. Toss everything together with any leftover marinade and simmer 2 minutes until it coats the meat.
      7. Warm the seasoned black beans in a small pot.
      8. Combine the rice, pork, pineapple, and beans in your big pot, then top each portion with a sprinkle of the Mexican cheese blend when you pack the containers.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-bowl gram target. Scoop it into 14 containers, add the cheese on top, and you are done. Each bowl lands at 687 calories and 50 grams of protein.
