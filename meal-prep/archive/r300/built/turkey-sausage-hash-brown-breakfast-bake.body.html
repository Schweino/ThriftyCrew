Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 28g protein · 56g carbs · 27g fat · ~$3.93 per serving (at everyday cost).

Breakfast for dinner is not a compromise, it is a strategy. Eggs are the cheapest complete protein in the store, hash browns are the cheapest potato you can buy already shredded, and smoked turkey sausage brings the flavor without the price of bacon. This bakes into one pan and reheats beautifully, which most egg dishes cannot claim. Each serving runs 566 calories with 28 grams of protein for about $3.93 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Smoked Turkey Sausage (Butterball): 2.75 lb (1270 g)
  • Olive Oil: 3.75 tbsp (50 g)
  • Onion Powder: 0.25 tbsp (2 g)
  • Frozen Hash Browns (Great Value): 34 cups (2851 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 2 (205 g)
  • Red Bell Pepper (generic): 2 (224 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 1 tbsp (7 g)
  • Cheddar Cheese, Shredded (Great Value): 15 oz (422 g)
  • Eggs (Great Value): 11 large eggs (560 g)
  • Milk (Fairlife): 2.75 cups (686 g)
  • Garlic Powder: 0.25 tbsp (3 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.75 tbsp (32 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Frozen shredded hash browns cost a fraction of what refrigerated ones do and thaw in the pan anyway. Buy the largest bags and check the per-ounce tag before you grab four small ones.
    • Smoked turkey sausage ropes go on sale in cycles and freeze for months in the original package. Buy several when the price drops.
    • Eggs are the cheapest protein per gram in this entire recipe. Buy them by the dozen at whatever store is running the loss leader that week, since they keep for weeks past the carton date.
    • Shredded cheddar in 8 oz bags is the value play here. You need enough to bind the top layer, and nobody will taste the difference between store brand and name brand once it browns.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet and brown the sliced smoked turkey sausage until the edges char in spots, then set it aside. Those browned edges are the whole flavor of the dish.
    3. In the same skillet, cook the diced onion and red bell pepper for 5 minutes until soft, then combine them with the sausage.
    4. Spread the frozen hash browns across the bottom of a greased 9x13 pan and season them with the garlic powder, onion powder, salt, and black pepper.
    5. Scatter the sausage and vegetable mixture over the hash browns, then cover the whole thing with about two thirds of the shredded cheddar.
    6. Whisk the eggs with the milk, a good pinch of salt, and black pepper until completely uniform, then pour the custard evenly over the pan so it soaks down through the layers.
    7. Bake at 375F for 45 to 55 minutes, until the center is set and does not jiggle when you nudge the pan. Add the rest of the cheddar in the last 10 minutes.
    8. Rest 15 minutes before portioning. Egg bakes finish setting as they cool and cut far more cleanly once they do.

    Portion It

    Weigh the whole pan, subtract the empty pan weight, and divide by 14. Cut to the scale, since egg bakes puff unevenly and the middle squares are always heavier than they look. Every serving comes to 566 calories and 28 grams of protein.
