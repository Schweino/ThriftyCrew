Makes 14 servings · ~580 cal · 32g protein · 75g carbs · 15g fat · ~$2.52 per serving (at everyday cost).

Fried rice was invented to use up yesterday's rice, which makes it the original meal prep dish. Restaurants cook it in enormous batches all day long, so scaling this to fourteen containers is not some clever adaptation. It is just how the dish has always been made. Pork tenderloin, egg, peas, carrots, and a hit of sesame oil at the end. Use cold rice from the fridge and you get separate, chewy grains. Use warm rice and you get porridge, so plan ahead by a day.

Ingredients

  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 5 tbsp (69 g)
  • Pork Tenderloin: 3 lb (1310 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 2 (221 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 7.7 (470 g)
  • Frozen Green Peas (generic): 9 oz (258 g)
  • Garlic: 2.5 tbsp (22 g)
  • Ginger (generic): 1.25 tbsp (8 g)
  • Eggs (Great Value): 8 large eggs (385 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 6.5 cups dry (1182 g)
  • Soy Sauce (generic): 0.75 cups (185 g)
  • Sesame Oil (generic): 3.75 tbsp (52 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 2.25 tbsp (15 g)
  • Green Onions: 8 tbsp (48 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (22 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Pork tenderloin goes on sale constantly and freezes perfectly. It is lean, it slices thin without a fight, and buying two when the price drops sets up two batches.
    • Cook your rice the day before. This is a free upgrade. Cold rice fries properly, hot rice steams, and the difference is the whole texture of the dish.
    • Frozen peas beat canned and fresh on every count here. Cheaper per ounce, better color, and they go in straight from the bag with no thawing.
    • Sesame oil goes in at the end, not in the pan. It is expensive per bottle and its flavor cooks right off, so a small drizzle at the finish gets you far more out of it.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cook the rice a day ahead if you can, spread it on a sheet pan to cool, and refrigerate it uncovered.
    3. Slice the pork tenderloin thin against the grain and season it with salt and black pepper.
    4. Heat some of the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok over high heat and cook the pork in batches until just done, then set it aside.
    5. Beat the eggs, scramble them in the same pan until barely set, and set them aside with the pork.
    6. Add the rest of the oil and cook the diced onion, carrots, garlic, and ginger until the carrots soften.
    7. Add the cold rice and the frozen peas and stir-fry hard, pressing the rice against the hot pan so it toasts rather than steams.
    8. Return the pork and egg, pour in the soy sauce, toss everything together, then finish off the heat with the sesame oil and the sliced green onions.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Weigh that number into each container instead of eyeballing scoops, because fried rice packs unevenly. Every bowl lands near 580 calories with 32 grams of protein.
