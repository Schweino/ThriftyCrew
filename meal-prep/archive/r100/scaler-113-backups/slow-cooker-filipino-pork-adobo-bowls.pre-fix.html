      Adapted from Skinnytaste.

      Makes 14 servings · ~576 cal · 54g protein · 55g carbs · 12g fat · ~$1.45 per serving.

      Filipino adobo is one of the great cheap dinners of the world, built on soy sauce, vinegar, and garlic. Lean pork loin slow cooks in that tangy sauce until tender, then goes over rice with green beans. At around a dollar sixty a serving with 54 grams of protein, this makes 14 bowls that punch way above their price.

      Ingredients

      • Pork Loin: 3,200 g (7.1 lb)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 900 g (4.5 cups)
      • Green Beans: 560 g (1.2 lb)
      • Soy Sauce: 180 g (180 g)
      • White Vinegar: 120 g (120 g)
      • Garlic: 40 g (13 clove)
      • Yellow Onion: 120 g (0.8 cups)

      Shop Smart

      • Adobo runs on pantry basics you likely already own. Lean on soy sauce and plain white vinegar, two of the cheapest flavor makers in the store.
      • Pork loin keeps this lean and affordable. Buy the loin over pre-cut pork and slice it yourself.
      • Frozen green beans mean no waste and no chopping. Keep a bag of frozen green beans ready for busy nights.

      Make It

      1. Add the pork loin, sliced onion, and garlic to the slow cooker.
      2. Pour in the soy sauce and white vinegar and give it a stir.
      3. Cover and cook on low for 7 to 8 hours until the pork is tender.
      4. Shred or chunk the pork and stir it back into the tangy sauce.
      5. Steam the green beans until just tender and cook the rice.
      6. Spoon the pork over rice and serve the green beans alongside.
