      Makes 14 servings · ~822 cal · 46g protein · 69g carbs · 42g fat · ~$2.74 per serving.

      Rolling enchiladas one at a time is a fine way to spend an evening if you have one to spare. Layering them like a lasagna gets you the same dinner in a quarter of the time. Tortillas, pulled pork in a barbecue and enchilada sauce mix, black beans, corn and a great deal of cheddar, stacked in a pan and baked until the top browns. The barbecue sauce is the twist, and it is the reason people go back for a second square.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the block of cheddar and shred it. This bake uses a lot of cheese, and block cheese runs cheaper per ounce than bagged shreds while melting noticeably better.
      • Corn tortillas over flour for a layered bake. They cost less, they hold their structure under sauce, and they will not go gummy in the middle of the pan.
      • Cook the pork shoulder in the slow cooker the day before. Splitting the job across two days costs nothing and turns this into a 20 minute assembly on the night you actually want to eat it.
      • Canned black beans and corn round out the pan for pennies. They add bulk, fiber and texture so you need less meat and less cheese per square.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slow cook the pork shoulder with salt, black pepper, ground cumin and chili powder until it shreds, then pull it and drain off the excess liquid.
      3. Cook the diced onion and chopped green bell peppers in the olive oil until soft, then add the minced garlic for the last minute.
      4. Toss the pulled pork with the cooked vegetables, the barbecue sauce and about half the enchilada sauce until everything is coated.
      5. Spread a thin layer of the remaining enchilada sauce in a large baking pan, then layer tortillas, pork mixture, drained black beans, drained corn and shredded cheddar. Repeat the layers until the pan is full.
      6. Finish with tortillas, the last of the enchilada sauce and a heavy layer of cheddar on top.
      7. Bake at 375F for 30 to 35 minutes until it bubbles at the edges, then let it rest 15 minutes before cutting so the layers set.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished bake in its pan, subtract the pan weight, divide by 14, and cut squares to that exact weight instead of eyeballing them. Every serving lands near 822 calories with 46 grams of protein, which makes this one of the biggest plates in the section.
