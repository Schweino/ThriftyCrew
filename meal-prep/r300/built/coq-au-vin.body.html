      Makes 14 servings · ~659 cal · 51g protein · 59g carbs · 22g fat · ~$2.19 per serving.

      Coq au vin is the French farmhouse braise that Julia Child taught America to make: chicken cooked slowly with bacon, mushrooms, onions, and carrots until the sauce turns dark and glossy and the meat gives up without a fight. This build braises in beef and chicken broth so it stays cheap and stays on the grocery board. If you happen to have red wine open, swap a cup of it in for some of the broth and you are back to the original. Each bowl runs 659 calories and 51 grams of protein for about $2.19 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Mushrooms are the second biggest line after the chicken, and they earn it. They give the braise its meaty depth. Buy whole white mushrooms and slice them yourself, because pre-sliced costs more and goes slimy faster.
      • A quarter pound of bacon flavors the entire pot. Buy a full pound, use what you need, and freeze the rest in strips so it does not go to waste.
      • Boneless thighs are cheaper than breast and they are the only cut that survives a long braise with the texture intact.
      • Beurre manie, which is just butter kneaded with flour, is how the French thicken a sauce without a packet. Flour and butter cost pennies and the result beats any store bought gravy mix.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the chopped bacon in a heavy pot until crisp, then lift it out and leave the fat behind.
      3. Season the chicken thighs with salt and pepper and brown them hard in the bacon fat in batches. This is the color of the finished dish, so take it further than feels comfortable.
      4. Add the quartered onions and carrots and cook until they take on color, then add the garlic and tomato paste and cook another minute.
      5. Pour in the beef broth and chicken broth, scraping every brown bit off the bottom, and add the thyme and bay leaves. Return the chicken and the bacon.
      6. Cover and simmer low for about 45 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the vegetables are soft.
      7. Brown the mushrooms separately in butter over high heat and add them at the end. Mushrooms thrown in at the start turn gray and rubbery, and browning them apart is what keeps them worth eating.
      8. Knead the rest of the butter with flour into a paste and stir it into the simmering pot a little at a time until the sauce coats a spoon. Cook the rice and serve the braise over it.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container first, then set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Make sure each container gets mushrooms and carrots and not just chicken swimming in sauce. Every portion lands at 659 calories, and like every good braise this one improves overnight.
