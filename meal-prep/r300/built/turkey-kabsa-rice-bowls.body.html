      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 42g protein · 72g carbs · 11g fat · ~$2.42 per serving.

      Kabsa is the national rice dish of Saudi Arabia, and it is built the smart way: the meat cooks first, then the rice cooks in that same spiced broth so nothing gets wasted. Cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, and coriander do the work, and toasted almonds and golden raisins go on top at the end. Our turkey version runs 564 calories and 42 grams of protein for about $2.42 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • The turkey breast is roughly two thirds of this batch cost. Frozen turkey roasts are the cheapest form of it, and they go on deep markdown right after the holidays. Buy then, cook later.
      • Almonds look expensive on the bag, but this recipe uses only a quarter cup of them. That is the whole lesson on specialty ingredients: judge them by what the recipe uses, not by the sticker.
      • Skip the pre-made kabsa mix if your store carries one at a premium. Cinnamon, cumin, coriander, allspice, cloves, and paprika are jars you already own, and mixing them yourself costs nothing extra.
      • Rice and broth are the base and both are pantry buys. A big bag of rice and a carton of broth stretch across this batch and several more after it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Toast the almonds dry in a skillet until they smell nutty, about 4 minutes, then set them aside with the golden raisins. This is your topping and it should not go in the pot.
      3. Cut the turkey breast into large chunks. Heat the olive oil in a wide heavy pot and brown the turkey on all sides, then set it aside.
      4. Cook the diced onion in the same pot for 6 minutes, then add the garlic, ginger, and the whole spice mix of cinnamon, cumin, coriander, allspice, paprika, cloves, and bay leaves, stirring one minute.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste, then the diced tomatoes, grated carrot, and chopped jalapeno. Add the chicken broth, return the turkey, and simmer covered for 30 minutes until the turkey reaches 165F and the broth tastes like the dish.
      6. Pull the turkey out and shred or chop it. Rinse the rice, then stir it into the simmering broth, cover, and cook on low 18 to 20 minutes until the liquid is gone and the grains are tender.
      7. Fold the turkey back in with the butter and the lime juice, then let the pot rest covered for 10 minutes so the rice finishes steaming.
      8. Move everything into your weighed pot and fluff it with a fork before you portion.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Scoop it out, then scatter the toasted almonds and golden raisins across all 14 containers so the topping stays crisp instead of going soft in the rice. Each bowl lands at 564 calories and 42 grams of protein.
