      Makes 14 servings · ~638 cal · 42g protein · 62g carbs · 23g fat · ~$1.65 per serving.

      Cordon bleu means blue ribbon, and the original dish involves pounding meat flat, rolling it around ham and cheese, breading it, and praying it does not leak in the pan. Nobody is doing that fourteen times. This takes all the same parts, turkey, ham, Swiss, mustard, a buttery crumb top, and lays them into a casserole. Each serving carries 42 grams of protein and 638 calories for about $1.65. Two proteins in one pan for that price is arithmetic worth paying attention to.

      About $1.65 a serving, and it has both turkey and ham in it. Diced ham is one of the most underrated cheap proteins in the store, and a little of it flavors an entire pan.

      Shop Smart

      • Diced ham in the 16 oz packs costs far less per pound than deli ham sliced at the counter, and it is already cut for exactly this job.
      • Swiss cheese is the priciest cheese in this recipe, but you only need enough for the flavor to register. Buy it in whatever pack size gives you the lowest price per ounce and shred it yourself.
      • Bread crumbs for the topping are cheap by the canister and last for many batches. Stale bread run through a processor with a little melted butter is even cheaper.
      • A frozen turkey breast roast is the cheapest cooked turkey available most of the year. Roast, dice, and you are set.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice and spread it across the bottom of a greased 9x13 pan as the base layer.
      3. Melt most of the butter in a large pot over medium heat, add the garlic for one minute, then whisk in the flour and cook two minutes until it stops smelling raw.
      4. Pour in the milk slowly while whisking and cook until the sauce thickens, then season with the Dijon mustard, garlic powder, salt, black pepper, and a small pinch of nutmeg.
      5. Take the pot off the heat and melt in the shredded Swiss cheese, stirring until the sauce is smooth. Swiss will turn stringy over direct heat, so patience here pays.
      6. Fold the diced turkey breast and the diced ham into the sauce, then pour it over the rice and spread it level.
      7. Toss the bread crumbs with the remaining melted butter until they look like wet sand, then scatter them across the top in an even layer.
      8. Bake at 350F for 30 to 35 minutes until the crumbs are golden and the edges bubble, then rest 10 minutes before portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole pan, subtract the empty pan weight, and divide by 14. Cut to the scale so the rice layer and the crumb top ride together into every container. Each serving comes to 638 calories and 42 grams of protein.
