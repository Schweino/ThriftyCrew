      Makes 14 servings · ~573 cal · 49g protein · 44g carbs · 22g fat · ~$1.98 per serving.

      Scalloped potatoes with a pork chop on top is a dish that has been feeding families in this part of the country for about a hundred years, and the reason is simple. Thin sliced potatoes, onion, and a creamy sauce that comes together from a can of soup and some milk, all baked slowly until the potatoes are tender and the top edges go brown and crusty. Six ingredients. The pork drips down into the potatoes the whole time it bakes, and that is the entire secret.

      Shop Smart

      • Slice the potatoes fresh instead of buying frozen scalloped kits. The boxed and frozen versions cost several times more per serving and the sliced-by-hand version bakes up better.
      • Bone-in chops cost less per pound and stay juicier through the bake. That is a rare case of the cheaper option also being the better one.
      • Store brand cream of mushroom soup, bought by the multipack. It is the same soup and it is baked into a cream sauce anyway.
      • Slice the potatoes thin and even. A mandoline or a steady hand and a sharp knife. Even slices cook at the same rate, which means no crunchy surprises and no wasted oven time.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 350F and butter a large deep baking dish.
      3. Peel the potatoes and slice them thin and even, and slice the onion thin as well.
      4. Whisk the cream of mushroom soup with the milk until it is smooth, and season it with salt and black pepper.
      5. Layer half the potatoes and half the onion in the dish, pour over half the sauce, then repeat with the rest and dot the top with the butter.
      6. Season the pork chops with salt and black pepper, brown them quickly in a hot skillet for color, and lay them on top of the potatoes.
      7. Cover with foil and bake for about 75 minutes, then uncover for 20 minutes more until the potatoes are tender all the way through and the top has browned.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished casserole in its dish, subtract the dish weight, divide by 14, and cut portions to that exact weight so every container gets the same ratio of chop to potato. Every serving lands near 573 calories with 49 grams of protein, which is a lot of protein for a dollar and change.
