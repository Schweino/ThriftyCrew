      Makes 14 servings · ~621 cal · 40g protein · 56g carbs · 28g fat · ~$4.79 per serving.

      Japchae is the dish that shows up at every Korean celebration, and the noodle is the whole reason. Dangmyeon, the Korean glass noodles made from sweet potato starch, turn chewy and translucent instead of soft, and they refuse to go mushy in the fridge. That is a rare quality in a noodle and it makes this one of the better things you can pack into a container. Everything gets cooked separately, then tossed together in one enormous bowl with sesame oil and soy. It eats well warm, and it eats just as well cold straight out of the fridge.

      Shop Smart

      • This is the priciest bowl in the batch and the beef is why. Cutting the beef back and letting the vegetables carry more of the volume brings the number down fast, which is closer to how japchae is traditionally proportioned anyway.
      • Dangmyeon comes in big packs and keeps forever. Korean glass noodles are shelf stable for a very long time, so buying the large bag once covers several batches with no waste risk.
      • Mushrooms are the second big line. Whole white mushrooms cost less per pound than pre-sliced, and slicing a few packs takes about five minutes.
      • Frozen spinach saves real money over fresh. Fresh spinach cooks down to almost nothing, so you would be buying several bags to get the same weight the frozen box gives you.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the beef thin against the grain and marinate it with some of the soy sauce, sugar, garlic, sesame oil, and black pepper for at least twenty minutes.
      3. Boil the Korean glass noodles until chewy and translucent, drain, rinse briefly, then cut through the pile a few times with scissors so they are manageable.
      4. Toss the drained glass noodles with the remaining soy sauce, sugar, and sesame oil while still warm so they absorb the dressing.
      5. Thaw and squeeze the frozen chopped spinach dry, then season it lightly with salt and sesame oil.
      6. Cook the vegetables separately in the vegetable oil, one type at a time, so each keeps its own texture and color: onions, then carrots, then red bell peppers, then mushrooms.
      7. Sear the marinated beef in the hot pan last, in batches, until browned and just cooked through.
      8. Combine the dressed glass noodles, all the vegetables, the spinach, and the beef in your biggest bowl, toss thoroughly with your hands or tongs, and finish with the green onions and toasted sesame seeds.

      Portion It

      Set the full bowl on the scale, subtract the empty weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Toss hard right before you weigh, because the noodles sink and the vegetables float, and a scoop off the top is not the same as a scoop from the bottom.
