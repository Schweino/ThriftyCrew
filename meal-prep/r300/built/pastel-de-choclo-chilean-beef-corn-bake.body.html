      Makes 14 servings · ~580 cal · 44g protein · 36g carbs · 28g fat · ~$3.69 per serving.

      Pastel de choclo is Chile's answer to shepherd's pie, except the topping is sweet corn instead of mashed potato and it is scented with fresh basil. Underneath sits pino, the seasoned beef and onion filling studded with olives, raisins, and hard boiled egg. Chileans argue about the raisins the way Americans argue about beans in chili, so put them in and take a side. The corn layer bakes into something between a custard and a cornbread, and the whole tray cuts into squares.

      Shop Smart

      • The beef is where the money goes and where the savings are. This bake is otherwise corn, onions, and eggs. Buy ground beef on markdown, freeze it in batch portions, and this number comes down.
      • Canned corn is cheaper than fresh and it blends smoother. The traditional version grinds fresh kernels, but the corn layer gets pureed and baked either way, so the can wins on both price and effort.
      • Fresh basil in the corn is not a garnish, it is the signature. If you have a windowsill, one basil plant costs about what two clamshells do and keeps giving all summer.
      • Olives and raisins together sound wrong and are exactly right. Both come from small packages that cover this batch and then some, so the cost per bowl is tiny for how much they change the dish.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the olive oil in a large pot and cook the diced yellow onions slowly until soft and sweet, which is the base of the pino.
      3. Add the garlic, ground cumin, paprika, salt, and black pepper and cook for a minute.
      4. Add the ground beef, break it up, and cook until browned, then sprinkle in the all-purpose flour and stir it through.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth and simmer until the filling is thick and saucy rather than wet, then fold in the olives and golden raisins.
      6. For the corn layer, blend the sweet corn with the milk and fresh basil until mostly smooth, then cook it in a pot with the butter and grits, stirring, until it thickens to a soft porridge.
      7. Spread the beef filling in a large baking dish, arrange the hard boiled eggs over it, and spread the corn mixture across the top.
      8. Sprinkle the sugar over the corn layer and bake at 400 degrees until the top is set and browned in patches.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole baking dish, subtract the empty dish weight, and divide by 14. Cut and lift to that weight so nobody ends up with all corn and no pino. Every serving comes in at 580 calories with 44 grams of protein. Rest it a good twenty minutes before cutting or the corn layer will not hold its edge.
