      Makes 14 servings · ~690 cal · 52g protein · 52g carbs · 28g fat · ~$5.05 per serving.

      Shoko is Ghanaian home cooking in one pot: beef braised in a fried tomato and pepper base, thickened with ground peanuts, and turned deep green with spinach at the end. It is rich without being heavy, and it reheats better on day three than it does on day one. Each bowl carries 52 grams of protein and 690 calories for about $5.05.

      Shop Smart

      • Frozen chopped spinach beats fresh here by a mile. It is already cooked down, it costs a fraction per pound of usable greens, and this stew wants it wilted anyway.
      • Canned diced tomatoes are the base. Store brand and name brand are the same tomatoes in most cases, so check the price per ounce tag and let the math pick.
      • Peanuts are the traditional thickener. Buy plain roasted peanuts and grind them yourself in whatever you have, since ground peanut costs more per ounce for the same food.
      • Chuck roast is the cut that belongs in a long braise. Do not upgrade it. A tender cut would cost more and fall apart worse.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck into bite sized cubes, season with salt and pepper, and brown them hard in oil in batches. Set the beef aside.
      3. Blend the diced tomatoes, a large share of the onion, the bell peppers, jalapeno, garlic, and ginger into a rough puree.
      4. Cook the remaining chopped onion in the same pot until soft, then pour in the puree and fry it, stirring often, until it darkens and the raw tomato smell is gone.
      5. Grind the peanuts to a coarse meal and stir them in along with the coriander, cumin, and other pantry spices.
      6. Return the beef with the beef broth, cover, and simmer 60 to 90 minutes until the beef is fork tender and the sauce has tightened.
      7. Stir in the chopped spinach and cook 5 more minutes, just until it is fully wilted and folded through.
      8. Cook the rice separately, then taste the stew and correct the salt.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop that weight of stew into each container and give every one the same weight of rice. Each bowl lands at 690 calories with 52 grams of protein.
