      Makes 14 servings · ~732 cal · 50g protein · 90g carbs · 23g fat · ~$3.49 per serving.

      Turkish pasta throws out the rulebook that says pasta gets a red sauce or a white one. Here you get three layers at once. Spiced ground beef in tomato on the bottom, cold garlic yogurt spooned over the middle, and paprika butter poured sizzling over the top so it streaks red through the white. The hot and cold contrast is the entire idea, and it is the same logic behind manti, the Turkish dumpling dish. The yogurt is also cheap protein, which is a nice thing to be true about the best-tasting part.

      Shop Smart

      • Greek yogurt in the big tub is the value buy. Single-serve cups cost several times as much per ounce, and this dish uses a real amount of it.
      • Canned crushed tomatoes in the large cans are the cheapest tomato per ounce. This sauce uses a lot, so buying the big cans instead of the small ones matters here.
      • Paprika butter is two ingredients and it makes the dish. Butter and paprika, warmed together until the color bleeds out. That is the entire fancy finishing sauce.
      • Fresh mint is the priciest small item on this list. Dried mint is actually traditional in Turkish cooking and costs a fraction, so it is a fair swap if the fresh price is ugly that week.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the penne in well salted water to al dente, drain, and toss it with a little of the olive oil so it does not stick.
      3. Heat the rest of the olive oil in a large pot and cook the diced yellow onions until softened.
      4. Add most of the garlic, then the ground beef, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste, ground cumin, paprika, dried oregano, red pepper flakes, salt, and black pepper and cook until the paste darkens.
      6. Add the crushed tomatoes and simmer uncovered until the sauce is thick and no longer watery.
      7. Stir the remaining garlic and the lemon juice into the greek yogurt with a pinch of salt, and keep it cold.
      8. Melt the butter with a spoonful of paprika until it foams and turns red, then chop the fresh mint for the finish.

      Portion It

      Weigh the pasta, the beef sauce, and the yogurt separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1 where it applies, and divide each by 14. Pasta down first, sauce over it, and store the garlic yogurt and paprika butter in small separate containers so the yogurt stays cold and does not break. Every serving comes in at 732 calories with 50 grams of protein. Assemble each one right before you eat it.
