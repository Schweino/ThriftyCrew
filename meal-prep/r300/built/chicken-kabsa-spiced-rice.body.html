      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 32g protein · 73g carbs · 14g fat · ~$1.45 per serving.

      Kabsa is the national dish of Saudi Arabia and the centerpiece of every family gathering there. Chicken and rice cook in the same pot with a warm spice blend, tomato, and dried lime, then get finished with toasted almonds and golden raisins. Everything happens in one vessel, which is why it feeds a crowd without a sink full of pans. Each bowl carries 561 calories and 32 grams of protein for about $1.45 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned diced tomatoes are the biggest line here, and they should be, because the chicken and rice barely register. When tomatoes go on sale, that is the week to make kabsa.
      • The kabsa spice blend is just cinnamon, cumin, coriander, cardamom, nutmeg, cloves, and paprika from your own rack. Buying a branded kabsa mix costs several times more for the same jars in a different container.
      • Toast the almonds in a dry pan before they go in. It costs nothing, takes three minutes, and roughly doubles how much they taste like almonds.
      • Golden raisins and almonds are both bought once and used across many recipes, so treat them as pantry investments rather than one-dish expenses.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Rinse the rice until the water runs mostly clear and let it drain. This is what keeps kabsa fluffy instead of sticky.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a wide heavy pot and brown the chicken thighs on both sides, then set them aside.
      4. Cook the chopped onion until soft, then add the garlic and ginger for a minute.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and all the spices, cinnamon, cumin, coriander, garam masala, nutmeg, cloves, paprika, salt, and pepper, and toast them in the oil for 30 seconds until the pot smells like a spice market.
      6. Add the diced tomatoes and grated carrots, return the chicken, add water to cover, and simmer covered for 25 minutes until the chicken is cooked through. Pull the chicken out and set it aside.
      7. Add the drained rice to the pot, make sure the liquid sits about an inch above it, bring it to a boil, then cover and cook on the lowest heat for 18 minutes. Do not lift the lid, because every peek costs you steam.
      8. Rest it covered off the heat for 10 minutes, then rest the chicken back on top, add the lime juice, and fold in the toasted almonds and golden raisins.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Shred or chop the chicken into the rice before scooping, or portion it separately by weight, so no container ends up as plain rice. Every serving lands at 561 calories.
