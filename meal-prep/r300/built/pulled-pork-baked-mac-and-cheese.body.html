      Makes 14 servings · ~862 cal · 33g protein · 51g carbs · 58g fat · ~$1.91 per serving.

      Barbecue joints figured this out a long time ago. A scoop of pulled pork on top of mac and cheese is better than either one by itself. This version braises pork shoulder with orange juice, jalapeno and garlic until it shreds, builds a real three cheese sauce from a butter and flour roux, and bakes the whole thing until the top browns. It is rich, it is filling, and it costs less per serving than the side order at the barbecue place.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy blocks of cheese and shred them yourself. Bagged shredded cheese costs more per ounce and is coated in starch that keeps it from melting smooth. This sauce deserves better.
      • Braise the pork the day before. The pork can sit in the fridge overnight and the assembly the next day takes 20 minutes. That is a free schedule upgrade.
      • A butter and flour roux is a fraction of the cost of a cheese sauce jar. Pennies of flour and a few tablespoons of butter do the same job better, and you control the salt.
      • Jalapeno and orange juice are the cheap flavor moves. One pepper and a splash of juice cut through all that dairy for almost nothing, which is why the dish does not feel heavy.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the pork shoulder with salt, black pepper, ground cumin, dried oregano and dried thyme, sear it in the olive oil, then slow cook it with the diced onion, minced jalapeno, garlic and orange juice until it shreds.
      3. Boil the pasta shells in salted water until just under al dente. They will finish in the oven, so pulling them early is the difference between good and mushy.
      4. Melt the butter in a large pot, whisk in the flour, and cook it two minutes until it smells nutty but has not colored.
      5. Pour in the heavy cream slowly while whisking, bring it to a bare simmer, then add the Dijon mustard and take it off the heat.
      6. Stir in the shredded cheddar, mozzarella and Mexican cheese blend a handful at a time until the sauce is smooth, then fold in the drained pasta.
      7. Spread half the mac in a large baking dish, layer the pulled pork across it, top with the rest of the mac, and bake at 375F for 25 minutes, then broil briefly for a browned top.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished dish, subtract the pan weight, divide by 14, and cut to that exact weight so every container gets the same ratio of pork to pasta. Every serving lands near 862 calories with 33 grams of protein. Reheat covered with a splash of milk so the sauce loosens back up.
