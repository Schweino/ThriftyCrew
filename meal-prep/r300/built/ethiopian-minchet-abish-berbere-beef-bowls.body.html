      Makes 14 servings · ~574 cal · 32g protein · 76g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.14 per serving.

      Minchet abish is the Ethiopian ground beef stew, and the secret is patience with the onions. You cook a mountain of them down slowly in spiced butter until they collapse into a jammy base, and that base is the sauce. No thickener, no flour, just onions that gave up. Then berbere goes in, which is the Ethiopian spice blend built on chilies, fenugreek, and warm spices, and the whole pot turns brick red. It is traditionally scooped up with injera. Over rice it works just as well and it portions a lot cleaner.

      Ingredients

      • Butter (generic): 7 tbsp (98 g)
      • Yellow Onion: 3.5 (385 g)
      • Garlic: 4 tbsp (35 g)
      • Berbere Seasoning (Frontier Co-op): 7 tbsp (48 g)
      • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (21 g)
      • 93/7 Ground Beef (Member's Mark): 3.5 lb (1588 g)
      • Diced Tomatoes (Great Value): 1 can (420 g)
      • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 6.75 cups dry (1258 g)

      Shop Smart

      • This recipe has six things in it. Beef, butter, onions, garlic, tomatoes, and rice. When the list is that short, the beef price is the whole game, so buy it marked down and freeze.
      • Berbere is worth buying real. One jar seasons this pot many times and there is no substitute that gets you close. It is the reason this tastes Ethiopian.
      • Do not rush the onions. The slow cook is free flavor. Twenty extra minutes on low costs nothing and it is the difference between a great stew and a thin one.
      • Buy the onion bag, not the loose bulbs. This dish uses a lot of them and the bag price per pound is meaningfully lower.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the yellow onions fine and cook them in a dry pot over medium-low heat until they release their water and start to collapse.
      3. Add the butter and keep cooking the onions slowly until they are soft, golden, and jammy. This is the step that makes the dish, so do not hurry it.
      4. Stir in the garlic and the berbere seasoning and cook for a minute so the spices bloom in the butter.
      5. Add the ground beef, break it up thoroughly, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      6. Stir in the diced tomatoes and enough water to loosen it, then simmer uncovered until the sauce is thick and the oil starts to separate at the edges.
      7. Season with salt and black pepper to taste and let it sit off the heat for a few minutes to settle.
      8. Cook the rice according to the package to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the stew pot separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Even rice base, even ladle of stew. Every bowl lands near 574 calories with 32 grams of protein. Berbere gets stronger overnight, so day three will have more heat than day one.
