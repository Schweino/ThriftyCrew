      Makes 14 servings · ~700 cal · 39g protein · 62g carbs · 32g fat · ~$3.17 per serving.

      Giouvetsi is named after the clay pot it was traditionally baked in, and the method is beautifully simple. Beef braises in a tomato sauce spiced with cinnamon until it is tender, then dry orzo goes straight into the same pot with more liquid and finishes in the oven, drinking up all that braising sauce as it swells. No separate pot of pasta, no draining, nothing lost down the sink. Cinnamon in a savory tomato sauce is a Greek habit worth adopting. It does not read as dessert, it just makes the beef taste deeper.

      Shop Smart

      • Chuck roast is the cut this dish wants. It needs a long braise to get tender, so the cheaper cut is also the correct one. Watch for the family pack price.
      • Orzo is one of the cheapest pastas per pound. It is also the only shape that works here, because it needs to cook in a shallow layer of liquid without clumping.
      • Cook the pasta in the sauce and save a pot. That is not a shortcut, it is the actual traditional method, and it means all the flavor stays in the dish.
      • Buy parmesan as a wedge or tub. This dish gets a serious amount of it stirred in and scattered on top, and the shaker can costs more per ounce for less cheese.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the beef chuck roast into large cubes and season them with salt and black pepper.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large oven-safe pot and brown the beef in batches, giving each piece room, then set it aside.
      4. Cook the diced red onions and carrots in the same pot until softened and picking up the browned bits.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it until it darkens, then add the diced tomatoes, ground cinnamon, sugar, and beef broth.
      6. Return the beef, cover, and braise in a moderate oven until the meat is fork tender, which is the point of no return for this dish. Do not rush it.
      7. Stir the dry orzo into the pot with enough hot water or broth to cover it, since the pasta will drink a surprising amount.
      8. Return it to the oven uncovered until the orzo is tender and the liquid is mostly absorbed, then stir in most of the parmesan cheese and scatter the rest over the top.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole pot, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Stir it well before you weigh, because the orzo settles and the beef floats. Every serving comes in at 700 calories with 39 grams of protein. Orzo keeps absorbing overnight, so a splash of water on reheat brings it right back.
