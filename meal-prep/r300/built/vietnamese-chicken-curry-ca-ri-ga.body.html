      Makes 14 servings · ~708 cal · 27g protein · 70g carbs · 34g fat · ~$3.02 per serving.

      Ca ri ga is Vietnam's curry, and it sits in its own lane. Lemongrass and coconut milk give it perfume, potato and sweet potato give it body, and it is seasoned with fish sauce rather than the spice cabinet, which is why it tastes lighter and more soup-like than the Indian and Thai curries on this site. It is traditionally eaten with a baguette for dipping, which tells you everything about Vietnam's history in one detail. Each bowl runs 708 calories and 27 grams of protein for about $3.02.

      Shop Smart

      • The curry roux blocks are the biggest line in this recipe. Curry powder plus a spoonful of flour cooked in oil does the same thickening job for a small fraction of the cost, and it moves the dish closer to the traditional Vietnamese version anyway.
      • Coconut milk is the second biggest line. Store brand, full fat, bought on sale and stacked in the pantry, because the cans keep for a year.
      • This pot uses a lot of broth. Homemade stock from a roasted chicken carcass costs you nothing but freezer space, and boxed broth on sale is the next best thing.
      • Potatoes and sweet potatoes are the cheap volume here. Buy both by the bag and cut them in large chunks so they do not disintegrate into the sauce during the simmer.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken thighs into large chunks and toss them with the ground turmeric, lemongrass paste, minced garlic, salt and black pepper. Let them sit at least 30 minutes.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pot over medium-high and brown the marinated chicken in batches until the turmeric has gone from yellow to gold, then set it aside.
      4. Cook the sliced onion in the same pot for 5 minutes, scraping the bottom, then return the chicken and pour in the chicken broth.
      5. Bring it up to a simmer and cook covered for 20 minutes so the chicken starts to give.
      6. Add the potato and sweet potato in large chunks and simmer another 15 to 20 minutes, uncovered, until they are just fork tender. Do not stir hard or they will fall apart on you.
      7. Break the curry roux into pieces and stir it in until it dissolves and the broth thickens, then add the coconut milk and the sugar and bring it back to a bare simmer for 5 minutes.
      8. Taste it and adjust the salt. It should be lightly sweet, coconut-forward and thinner than an Indian curry, because that is what ca ri ga is.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen equal containers, potatoes and all. Each one carries 708 calories, and reheat this gently, since a hard boil will split the coconut milk and turn the potatoes to mush.
