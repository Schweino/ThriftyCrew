      Makes 14 servings · ~879 cal · 43g protein · 63g carbs · 49g fat · ~$1.90 per serving.

      Chuckwagon food, more or less. Ground pork and bacon browned together, stirred into sweet baked beans, then topped with biscuit dough and baked until the biscuits are golden and the beans are bubbling up around the edges. It is six ingredients, it is loud, and it is the kind of pan that empties fast at a family table. There is nothing sophisticated about it and it does not need to be.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned baked beans by the big can. The large cans run well under the small ones per ounce, and this bake goes through several of them.
      • Buy ground pork when it is marked down and freeze it flat. It is usually cheaper per pound than ground beef and it brings more flavor to a dish like this one.
      • Bacon is doing seasoning work. Render it first and cook the ground pork in that fat, and the whole pan tastes smoky without needing an expensive smoked meat.
      • Refrigerated biscuit dough is the shortcut, but flour and butter are the cheap route. If the cans are not on sale, a quick drop biscuit mixed from pantry staples costs less and bakes the same.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Chop the bacon and render it in a large oven-safe skillet or a wide pot until crisp, then lift it out and leave the fat behind.
      3. Brown the ground pork in that bacon fat over medium-high heat, breaking it up as it goes, until it is cooked through and starting to catch color.
      4. Add the diced onion and cook until soft, then stir in the minced garlic for the last minute.
      5. Pour in the canned baked beans with their sauce, return the bacon, and season with salt and black pepper. Simmer five minutes so everything comes together.
      6. Spread the mixture in a large baking dish and separate the biscuit dough into pieces, laying them across the top with a little space between so they can spread.
      7. Bake at 375F for 25 to 30 minutes until the biscuits are golden through the middle and the beans bubble at the edges.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished bake in its dish, subtract the dish weight, divide by 14, and portion to that exact weight so every container gets both beans and biscuit. Every serving lands near 879 calories with 43 grams of protein, which makes this a very big plate for the money.
