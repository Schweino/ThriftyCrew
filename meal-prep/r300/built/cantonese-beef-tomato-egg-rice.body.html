      Makes 14 servings · ~768 cal · 50g protein · 63g carbs · 35g fat · ~$3.29 per serving.

      Tomato and egg is the first thing a lot of Chinese cooks learn to make, and adding beef turns a quick lunch into an actual dinner. The tomatoes break down into a sweet-sour sauce, the eggs go in soft and silky, and a cornstarch slurry pulls it all together into something glossy that clings to rice. A little ketchup and sugar sounds like cheating and is genuinely how Cantonese restaurants balance the acid. That is a genuinely high protein number for a beef and egg bowl, and eggs are the reason.

      Shop Smart

      • Eggs are carrying this dish and they are cheap. Buy them two dozen at a time. Between the eggs and the tomatoes, most of the volume here costs very little.
      • Canned diced tomatoes beat fresh in winter, every time. They are picked ripe, they cost less, and they break down into sauce faster than a pale grocery store tomato ever will.
      • The beef is the one big line, so shop it hard. Everything else on this list is pantry staples and eggs.
      • Cornstarch is the cheapest thickener there is. A single box lasts a year of stir fries and it is what gives this sauce its restaurant gloss.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt, then scramble them in some of the vegetable oil over medium heat until just barely set and still soft. Pull them out early, since they will finish in the sauce.
      3. Stir the cornstarch into a little cold water to make a smooth slurry and set it aside.
      4. Add the rest of the oil to the pan and cook the ground beef with the garlic powder until browned with no pink remaining.
      5. Add the diced tomatoes and cook them down until they collapse and the sauce turns thick and glossy.
      6. Stir in the ketchup, sugar, soy sauce, and chicken broth and simmer for a few minutes so the flavors settle.
      7. Pour in the cornstarch slurry while stirring and cook until the sauce thickens and turns shiny.
      8. Fold the scrambled eggs back in gently so they stay in soft curds, and season with salt and black pepper to taste.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Scoop that weight into each container, spooning carefully so the egg curds get shared out instead of ending up in whichever container you filled first.
