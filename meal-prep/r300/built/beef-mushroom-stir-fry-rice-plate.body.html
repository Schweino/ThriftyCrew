      Makes 14 servings · ~572 cal · 30g protein · 85g carbs · 11g fat · ~$2.92 per serving.

      Mushrooms are the reason this dish works. They soak up the oyster sauce gravy, they bring a savory depth that makes a modest amount of beef taste like more, and they cost a fraction of what another pound of steak would. The Cantonese technique here is velveting: the sliced beef gets tossed with cornstarch and a little baking soda before it hits the pan, which is why restaurant beef is tender and yours usually is not. It takes four extra minutes and it is the single biggest upgrade you can make to any stir fry.

      Shop Smart

      • Mushrooms stretch the beef and that is the whole strategy. Buy whole white mushrooms rather than pre-sliced, since the sliced packs cost noticeably more per pound for thirty seconds of knife work.
      • Velveting is free tenderness. Cornstarch and a pinch of baking soda cost essentially nothing and they let you use a cheaper cut without it turning chewy.
      • Oyster sauce is one bottle that keeps for months. It is the backbone of the gravy here and of a lot of other stir fries, so the cost per batch is small once you own it.
      • Cook the rice ahead so it is not the bottleneck. This stir fry cooks in about eight minutes once the pan is hot, and you do not want to be standing there waiting on a rice cooker.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the beef thin against the grain and toss it with most of the cornstarch, a pinch of baking soda, some soy sauce, and a splash of water, then let it sit for at least fifteen minutes.
      3. Cook the rice according to the package while the beef rests.
      4. Stir the remaining cornstarch into a little cold water and mix it with the oyster sauce, the rest of the soy sauce, the beef broth, the chicken broth, sugar, and black pepper to make the sauce.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok until it smokes and sear the beef in batches in a single layer, just until browned, then set it aside. Do not cook it through, since it will finish in the sauce.
      6. Add the butter to the pan and cook the mushrooms hard, without stirring much, until they release their water, it cooks off, and they brown.
      7. Add the ginger, garlic, and the white parts of the green onions and cook until fragrant.
      8. Pour in the sauce mixture, bring it to a boil until it thickens and turns glossy, return the beef, add the sesame oil and the green onion tops, and toss to coat.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the stir fry pan separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Even rice base, even weight of beef, mushrooms, and gravy. Every serving lands near 572 calories with 30 grams of protein, so be generous with the gravy when you portion rather than leaving it in the pan.
