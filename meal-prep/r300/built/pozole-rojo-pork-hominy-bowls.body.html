      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 28g protein · 69g carbs · 19g fat · ~$1.81 per serving.

      This is the classic. Pork and hominy in a red broth built from dried chiles you toast yourself, the pot that has been cooked in Mexico since before anyone was writing recipes down. Guajillo brings the color and a mild fruity heat, ancho brings the raisin sweetness, and a couple of arbol chiles bring the fire. The hominy is what makes it pozole and nothing else. Nothing chewy quite like it.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy dried chiles in the big bag from the Hispanic aisle. The branded packets on the spice rack cost several times more per ounce for the exact same dried chile.
      • Canned hominy saves you a day. The dried version needs soaking and hours of cooking. Canned is rinse and go, and the per serving price is about the same.
      • Pork shoulder is the correct cut and the cheap one. This is one of those happy cases where the traditional choice is also the value choice. Bone-in makes better broth.
      • Toasting the chiles is a free upgrade. Thirty seconds a side in a dry pan and the sauce goes from flat to deep. It costs nothing but attention.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Stem and seed the dried guajillo and ancho chiles, toast them in a dry skillet about 30 seconds a side until they smell nutty, then soak them in hot water for 20 minutes.
      3. Blend the softened chiles with some of their soaking water, several cloves of garlic, the chili powder, ground cumin, dried oregano and a spoon of cocoa powder into a smooth sauce, then strain it.
      4. Cube the pork shoulder, season it with salt and black pepper, and brown it hard in the vegetable oil in your big pot, working in batches.
      5. Fry the strained chile sauce in the pot for five minutes so it darkens and loses its raw edge, then return all the pork.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, add the dried arbol chiles whole if you want heat, and simmer covered for about 2 hours until the pork is fully tender.
      7. Add the drained hominy and simmer 20 minutes more so it takes on the red broth, then cook the rice per the package to serve alongside.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice across your 14 containers, then set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and ladle that exact weight over the rice. Every bowl lands near 564 calories with 28 grams of protein. It gets better every day it sits.
