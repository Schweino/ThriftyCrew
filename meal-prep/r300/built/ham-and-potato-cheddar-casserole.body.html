      Makes 14 servings · ~579 cal · 33g protein · 71g carbs · 20g fat · ~$1.70 per serving.

      Ham is the protein everybody forgets about. It is already cooked, already seasoned, and it goes on deep discount around every holiday. Here it goes into a scratch cheddar sauce with potatoes and peas and bakes into the kind of casserole that shows up at church potlucks and disappears first. No condensed soup can involved, which matters more than you would think. A real roux with milk, broth, and shredded cheddar costs less than the canned shortcut and does not taste like a can.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy ham right after a holiday. Grocery stores mark down whole and half hams hard the week after Easter and Christmas, and ham freezes for months. That single habit is where most of the savings on this dish lives.
      • Shred your own cheddar from a block. Pre-shredded is coated in starch to keep it from clumping, which is exactly what makes it grainy in a cheese sauce. The block is also cheaper per ounce.
      • Make the sauce, skip the can. Butter, flour, milk, and broth is a roux, it takes five minutes, and it costs a fraction of two cans of condensed soup.
      • Frozen peas go in straight from the bag. They thaw in the hot sauce, hold their color through baking, and cost less than canned per ounce.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Peel and slice the potatoes thin and even, about the thickness of a coin, so they cook through at the same rate.
      3. Melt the butter in a large pot, stir in the flour, and cook the paste for a minute so it loses its raw taste.
      4. Whisk in the milk and chicken broth a little at a time until the sauce is smooth and thickened, then stir in the diced yellow onion.
      5. Take the pot off the heat, add most of the shredded cheddar, and stir until it melts. Adding cheese off the heat keeps the sauce from breaking.
      6. Fold in the diced ham, the sliced potatoes, and the frozen peas, and season with salt and black pepper.
      7. Spread everything into a large baking dish, cover with foil, and bake at 375 degrees until a knife slides through the potatoes with no resistance.
      8. Uncover, scatter the remaining cheddar over the top, and bake until browned and bubbling, then let it rest fifteen minutes before portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole baking dish, subtract the empty dish weight, and divide by 14. Cut and scoop to that weight rather than guessing at squares, because a casserole is never as even as it looks. Every serving comes in at 579 calories with 33 grams of protein. Let it cool fully before you snap the lids on or the potatoes go watery.
