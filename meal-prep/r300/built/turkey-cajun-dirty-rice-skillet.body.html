      Makes 14 servings · ~657 cal · 43g protein · 75g carbs · 16g fat · ~$2.25 per serving.

      Dirty rice earns its name honestly. The rice turns brown because chicken livers and browned meat get cooked into it, and that is where all the depth comes from. Onion, celery, and green bell pepper form the Cajun holy trinity, bacon starts the pan, and turkey carries the protein. Each serving lands at 657 calories with 43 grams of protein for about $2.25.       Shop Smart

      • Chicken livers are one of the last genuine bargains in the meat case. A tub costs a fraction of what an equal weight of any muscle meat runs, and this dish is built to use them.
      • You only need a quarter pound of bacon to start the pan. Buy a full package, freeze it flat in portions, and it will start several skillets like this one.
      • Buy the whole celery bunch and both onions and peppers loose. The holy trinity is the cheapest flavor base in cooking and there is no reason to pay for pre-diced.
      • Rice from the big bag is the cheapest starch per serving in the store. This skillet uses six and a half cups dry, so buy accordingly.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Chop the bacon and render it in your largest heavy pot over medium heat until crisp, then leave the fat in the pot. That fat is the foundation.
      3. Rinse and trim the chicken livers, pat them dry, and cook them hard in the bacon fat for 4 to 5 minutes until browned outside and barely pink inside. Pull them out and chop them fine.
      4. Add the ground turkey to the pot and brown it well, letting it stick and scrape up off the bottom. The browned bits on the pan are what make the rice dirty.
      5. Stir in the diced onion, green bell pepper, and celery and cook 6 to 8 minutes until soft, then add the garlic, Cajun seasoning, dried thyme, salt, and black pepper.
      6. Return the chopped livers and bacon to the pot, add the dry rice, and stir for two minutes so every grain gets coated in the fat and seasoning.
      7. Pour in the chicken broth, drop in the bay leaves, bring it to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for 20 minutes without lifting the lid.
      8. Kill the heat and let it sit covered another 10 minutes, then pull the bay leaves, fluff with a fork, and fold in the sliced green onions.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop that weight into each container while the rice is still warm and loose. Every serving delivers 657 calories and 43 grams of protein.
