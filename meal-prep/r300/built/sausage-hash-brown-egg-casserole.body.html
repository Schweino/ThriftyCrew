      Shop Smart

      • Ground pork seasoned at home beats bought breakfast sausage. The salt, pepper, garlic powder and onion powder are already in your cabinet, and you skip the markup on the pre seasoned tube.
      • Frozen hash browns are the second biggest line. The big bags are the better per ounce buy. Shredded and diced both work, so grab whichever is cheaper that week.
      • Buy a block of cheddar and shred it yourself. Pre shredded is coated in starch to keep it from clumping, which also keeps it from melting well. A block is usually cheaper and melts smoother.
      • Milk and eggs are the whole binder. Two of the cheapest items in the store are what hold this entire casserole together.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 350F and grease two large baking dishes.
      3. Brown the ground pork in a skillet, breaking it into crumbles, seasoning as it cooks with the salt, black pepper, garlic powder and onion powder. Drain off the excess fat or the casserole will come out greasy.
      4. Spread the frozen hash browns across the bottoms of the baking dishes. There is no need to thaw them first.
      5. Scatter the cooked sausage over the potatoes, then most of the shredded cheddar over that.
      6. Whisk the eggs and milk together with a little more salt and pepper until it is completely uniform, then pour it evenly over both dishes.
      7. Top with the rest of the cheddar and bake until the center is set and no longer jiggles when you nudge the pan.
      8. Let it rest at least ten minutes before you cut it. Cutting a hot casserole gets you a puddle instead of a square.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14, then cut the casserole into squares and adjust each container to hit that weight. Every bowl lands near 576 calories and 37 grams of protein.
