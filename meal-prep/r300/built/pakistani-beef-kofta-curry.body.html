      Makes 14 servings · ~631 cal · 40g protein · 56g carbs · 27g fat · ~$2.58 per serving.

      Kofta curry is Pakistani comfort food built on patience with an onion. You fry the onions until they are deep golden, whisk in yogurt instead of tomato, and drop hand rolled beef meatballs straight into the gravy to finish. The result is creamy without cream and rich without much money. Each bowl carries 40 grams of protein and 631 calories for about $2.58.

      Shop Smart

      • This recipe eats onions by the pound. Buy the big bag, not loose singles, and store them somewhere dark and dry so they last until the next batch.
      • Plain Greek yogurt is the gravy, so do not buy the flavored kind by mistake. The large tub is always cheaper per ounce than the little cups.
      • Ground beef is the one real expense. Buy it in the largest pack you will actually use and portion it into freezer bags the same day.
      • Whole spices cost less per use than blends, but if you are starting out, the jars of cumin, coriander, and turmeric you already own will carry this dish fine.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the ground beef with a small amount of finely grated onion, minced jalapeno, chopped cilantro, the flour, and the ground cumin, coriander, chili powder, and salt. Knead it until it is smooth and slightly sticky.
      3. Roll the mixture into even meatballs and set them on a tray in the fridge while you build the gravy.
      4. Slice the rest of the onions thin and fry them in the vegetable oil over medium heat, patiently, until they are deep golden brown. This step is the entire dish, so do not rush it.
      5. Add the minced garlic and the turmeric and cook 1 minute, then take the pot off the heat for a moment.
      6. Whisk the Greek yogurt smooth and stir it in off the heat so it does not split, then return the pot to low and cook until the oil starts to separate at the edges.
      7. Lower the koftas into the gravy in a single layer, add a cup or so of water, cover, and simmer 25 to 30 minutes. Shake the pot instead of stirring so the meatballs hold together.
      8. Finish with garam masala and the remaining cilantro, and cook the rice separately.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Split the koftas evenly by count first, then pour the same weight of gravy over each, and add the same weight of rice to every container. Each bowl lands at 631 calories with 40 grams of protein.
