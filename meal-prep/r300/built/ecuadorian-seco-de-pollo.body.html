      Makes 14 servings · ~590 cal · 56g protein · 50g carbs · 15g fat · ~$1.94 per serving.

      Seco de pollo is the Sunday chicken stew of Ecuador, and the name is a little joke, since seco means dry and this is anything but. Achiote turns the whole pot a deep orange, citrus and a long slow braise break the chicken down until it falls off the fork, and a genuinely enormous amount of cilantro goes in at the end. Over rice, each bowl runs 590 calories and 56 grams of protein for about $1.94. That is a genuinely high protein number for a stew over rice.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned tomatoes are the volume in this pot and this recipe uses a lot of them. This is a case-buy item: they go on sale constantly, they sit on a shelf for a year, and buying ten cans at the sale price instead of full price saves real money.
      • Achiote paste is the cheapest big-impact ingredient in this recipe. A tablespoon colors and flavors the whole pot, one jar lasts many batches, and the Latin aisle price beats the specialty shelf every time.
      • Cilantro goes in by the bunch, not the sprig. Buy two bunches from the produce case rather than clamshells of leaves, and stand the stems in a glass of water in the fridge so they last the week.
      • Boneless skinless thighs are the correct cut for a two-hour braise and the eight pound club pack is the best per-pound price in the meat case.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the chicken thighs with the ground cumin, dried oregano, salt and black pepper. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium-high and brown them in batches until deeply colored, then set them aside.
      3. Stir the achiote paste into the hot oil and let it bloom for 30 seconds. The oil will turn bright orange, and that color is what ends up in every bite.
      4. Add the diced onion, diced green bell pepper and garlic and cook 6 to 8 minutes until soft and the onions are translucent.
      5. Blend about half the cilantro with the orange juice into a green purÃ©e and pour it in, along with the diced tomatoes and the beef broth. Scrape the bottom of the pot clean.
      6. Return the chicken and all its juices, bring to a simmer, then cover and cook at a low bubble for 60 to 75 minutes until the chicken is falling apart and the sauce has reduced and thickened.
      7. While it braises, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes.
      8. Break the chicken into big shreds in the sauce, stir in the rest of the chopped cilantro off the heat, and fold the cooked rice through.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, same amount in each. Each one carries 590 calories, and like most long braises, this one is better on day three than it was on the day you made it.
