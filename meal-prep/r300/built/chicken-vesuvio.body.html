      Makes 14 servings · ~607 cal · 52g protein · 34g carbs · 27g fat · ~$1.71 per serving.

      Chicken Vesuvio is Chicago Italian, born in the old neighborhood restaurants and never quite famous outside of them. Chicken and potato wedges roast in the same pan with garlic, olive oil, oregano, and lemon until the potatoes go crisp at the corners, and a handful of peas goes in at the end for color. It is a whole dinner in one pan with no side dish required. Each serving lands at 607 calories and 52 grams of protein for about $1.71 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Potatoes in the five pound bag are the best deal in the produce section, and here they are half the meal rather than a side you have to make separately. One pan, no extra starch to buy.
      • Boneless thighs are the cheap cut and they stay juicy through a hot roast. Breast would need babysitting and would cost more for the privilege.
      • Frozen peas cost almost nothing and beat fresh in every way that matters here. They go in at the very end and just need to warm through.
      • Lemon juice from the bottle is fine for this. The lemon is a background note under the garlic and oregano, not the star, so there is no reason to pay for fresh.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F. Cut the potatoes into thick wedges and toss them with some of the olive oil, oregano, salt, and pepper.
      3. Season the chicken thighs with salt, pepper, oregano, and italian seasoning, then brown them hard in the rest of the olive oil in a large oven safe pan. Set them aside.
      4. Add the potato wedges to the same pan cut side down and let them take on color for 5 minutes without moving them. That crust is the whole point of vesuvio potatoes.
      5. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds, then pour in the chicken broth and lemon juice and scrape the bottom of the pan.
      6. Nestle the chicken back in among the potatoes, put the pan in the oven, and roast 30 to 35 minutes until the potatoes are tender and the chicken is cooked through.
      7. Stir the butter into the pan juices off the heat so the sauce goes glossy.
      8. Scatter the frozen peas over the top and let the residual heat warm them through, about 3 minutes. Do not roast them or they turn gray and sad.

      Portion It

      Set the full pan on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Get chicken, potatoes, and peas into every container, and spoon the garlicky pan juices over each one before you close the lid. Every portion comes in at 607 calories.
