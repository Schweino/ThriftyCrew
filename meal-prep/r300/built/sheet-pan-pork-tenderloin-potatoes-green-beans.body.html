      Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 46g protein · 65g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.35 per serving.

      Four ingredients, one pan, and a spice rub you already own. Pork tenderloin is the leanest cut on the pig and it cooks fast, so the potatoes get a head start in the oven and the tenderloin and green beans join them for the last stretch. Everything finishes at the same moment. This is the recipe to keep in your back pocket for the night you have no time and no patience.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork tenderloin usually comes two to a package. The two pack is nearly always cheaper per pound than a single, and the second one freezes for weeks with no loss.
      • Potatoes in the ten pound bag. This pan takes a serious pile of them, and the bag price per pound is a fraction of what loose potatoes run.
      • Frozen green beans go straight on the pan. No trimming, no washing, no spoiling in the crisper, and they cost less than fresh year round.
      • The rub comes out of jars you already have. Chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, paprika, salt and pepper. There is no marinade to buy and no packet to open.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F and mix the salt, black pepper, chili powder, ground cumin, garlic powder and paprika into a rub.
      3. Cut the potatoes into even chunks, toss them with olive oil and part of the rub, and spread them on sheet pans in a single layer. Crowding is what turns roasted potatoes into steamed ones.
      4. Roast the potatoes alone for 20 minutes so they get a head start.
      5. Pat the pork tenderloins dry, coat them in olive oil and the rest of the rub, and push the potatoes aside to make room on the pans.
      6. Roast 15 minutes, then add the green beans tossed in the remaining olive oil and roast about 10 minutes more.
      7. Pull the pans when the tenderloin reaches 145F inside, then rest the meat 10 minutes before slicing so the juices stay in it.

      Portion It

      Divide the potatoes and green beans across your 14 containers, then set your mixing pot of sliced tenderloin on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and add that exact weight to each. Every plate lands near 566 calories with 46 grams of protein for very little work.
