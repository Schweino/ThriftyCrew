      Makes 14 servings · ~591 cal · 52g protein · 44g carbs · 21g fat · ~$1.18 per serving.

      Suya is Nigerian street food, sold off charcoal grills at night, and the magic is in the rub. Ground peanuts, smoked paprika, cayenne, onion powder and garlic powder make a dry spice mix called yaji that crusts onto the meat as it cooks. We use peanut butter to carry it, roast the chicken on sheet pans instead of skewers, and build the whole thing into a rice bowl. Each container lands 591 calories and 52 grams of protein for about $1.18, which is the second cheapest bowl in this whole batch.

      Shop Smart

      • This is a six line grocery list. When a recipe is this short, buy the best version of the one thing that matters, which is the chicken, and let everything else be store brand.
      • Peanut butter is standing in for the ground peanuts in a traditional yaji rub. Buy the big plain jar, not the natural style, since the added oil helps the rub stick to the meat.
      • The seven pound club pack of boneless skinless thighs is the whole meat purchase and it is the best price per pound in the case. Break it down and freeze half if you are not cooking a double batch.
      • Cayenne and smoked paprika carry the heat and the color. Buy both in bags from an international grocer, where the same amount costs a fraction of what the branded jars charge.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the suya rub: whisk the peanut butter with the garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, cayenne pepper, black pepper, salt and just enough chicken broth to loosen it into a thick paste that will spread.
      3. Cut the chicken thighs into large strips and work the rub into every piece with your hands. Let them sit at least 30 minutes, or refrigerate overnight for a deeper crust.
      4. Heat the oven to 450F and put a rack in the upper third. Brush two sheet pans with the vegetable oil so nothing welds itself to the metal.
      5. Spread the chicken in a single layer with space between the pieces. Crowded suya steams and goes gray instead of crusting.
      6. Roast 18 to 22 minutes, then run the broiler for 2 to 3 minutes at the end until the edges char in spots and the peanut rub sets into a crust. Watch it, because peanut burns fast.
      7. While it roasts, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes.
      8. Chop the chicken into bite-size pieces, fold it into your mixing pot with the rice and the pan drippings, and stir the hot sauce through the whole batch.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same amount in every container, every time. Each bowl carries 591 calories, and the peanut crust holds up on reheat better than any breading would.
