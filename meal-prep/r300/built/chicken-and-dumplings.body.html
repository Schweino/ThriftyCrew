      Shop Smart

      • Flour, butter, and milk are the cheapest way to add real substance to a pot of soup. Buying a bag of flour and a box of butter covers this recipe many times over.
      • Boneless thighs give you dark meat that stays tender through a long simmer for less than breast costs. In a broth dish especially, thighs are the better ingredient as well as the cheaper one.
      • Celery and carrots are cheap and they build the broth. Buy the whole bunch of celery and use the leaves too, since they carry more flavor than the stalks do.
      • Fair warning worth planning around: dumplings soften as they sit. They are still good on day four, but they will not be pillowy the way they are the first night. If that bothers you, keep some of the dough back and cook it fresh midweek.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken thighs into large pieces and simmer them in the chicken broth with the onion, carrots, celery, bay leaves, salt, and pepper for about 30 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the broth tastes like something.
      3. Pull the chicken out, shred it, and return it to the pot. Fish out the bay leaves.
      4. Whisk the cornstarch with a little cold water and stir it into the simmering pot to thicken the broth slightly. Cold water first, always, or you get lumps you will never break up.
      5. Make the dumpling dough by cutting the cold butter into the flour with baking powder and salt until it looks like coarse crumbs, then stirring in the milk just until it comes together. Overworking the dough makes tough dumplings, so stop mixing while it still looks shaggy.
      6. Drop spoonfuls of dough onto the surface of the simmering pot, spacing them apart since they will swell.
      7. Cover tightly and steam 15 minutes without lifting the lid. Every peek releases the steam that is actually cooking them.
      8. Cook the rice separately and put a scoop in the bottom of each container to stretch the batch further.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Count the dumplings and split them evenly by hand, since weighing alone will not stop one container from getting three and another getting one. Each portion lands at 564 calories.
