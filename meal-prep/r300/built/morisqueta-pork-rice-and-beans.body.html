      Makes 14 servings · ~765 cal · 48g protein · 71g carbs · 30g fat · ~$2.51 per serving.

      Morisqueta is a Michoacan plate and it is beautifully simple in its construction. A bed of plain white rice, a ladle of soupy beans, and pork simmered in a roasted tomato salsa on top, with cheese and cilantro to finish. Three components, layered instead of stirred, and every one of them cheap. It is home cooking from a state that does not export many of its recipes, and it batches almost perfectly.

      Shop Smart

      • Tomatillos are cheaper than they look. Buy them by the pound with the husks on, peel and rinse the sticky coating off, and you have the tang that makes this salsa work.
      • Arbol chiles are sold by the bag for pocket change. A few of them bring real heat to a big pot of salsa, and the bag will outlast several batches.
      • Buy the shredded Mexican cheese blend on sale and freeze it. Shredded cheese freezes fine and thaws ready to use, so there is no reason to pay the regular price.
      • Keep the three components separate until you plate. Rice, beans and pork stored apart reheat much better than one stirred-together mess, and it costs nothing to do it right.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cube the pork shoulder, season with salt and black pepper, and brown it in a heavy pot with a little olive oil, then add the bay leaves and enough water to nearly cover and simmer 90 minutes until tender.
      3. While the pork cooks, blend the diced tomatoes, the husked tomatillos, the jalapeno, the arbol chiles, half the onion and the garlic into a rough salsa.
      4. Cook the remaining diced onion in the pot, pour in the blended salsa, and let it fry and reduce for 15 minutes until it darkens and thickens.
      5. Return the tender pork to the salsa and simmer another 20 minutes so the meat takes it on.
      6. Cook the rice per the package, and warm the kidney beans with a little of their liquid so they stay soupy rather than dry.
      7. Layer each portion rice first, then beans, then the saucy pork, and finish with the shredded Mexican cheese blend and chopped cilantro.

      Portion It

      Divide the rice across your 14 containers, then the beans, then set your pot of saucy pork on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and lay that exact weight on top of each. Every plate lands near 765 calories with 48 grams of protein, so this is one of the biggest servings in the section.
