      Harissa and coconut milk should not work together, and then you taste it. The harissa brings smoke and heat, the coconut milk rounds it off, and beef meatballs simmer right in the sauce so they soak up both.       Shop Smart

      • Harissa paste looks expensive on the shelf and cheap on the plate. One jar seasons multiple batches and it keeps for months in the fridge after opening.
      • Ground beef is the biggest line. Watch for the club size packs, break them down into batch sized portions the day you get home, and freeze what you are not cooking.
      • Canned coconut milk is a pantry staple worth buying several of when it is on promotion. It does not spoil and it turns up in a lot of recipes.
      • One bunch of cilantro covers both the meatball mix and the garnish. Stand it stems down in a glass of water in the fridge and it will hold for a week.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Grate or very finely chop about a third of the onion and mix it into the ground beef with half the garlic, a good handful of chopped cilantro, the garam masala, salt, and pepper.
      3. Roll the mixture into meatballs of a consistent size. Uniform meatballs cook evenly and portion evenly, which matters more here than looks.
      4. Sear the meatballs in the vegetable oil in batches, turning to brown them on several sides. They do not need to cook through yet.
      5. Chop the remaining onion and cook it in the same pot until soft, then add the rest of the garlic for a minute.
      6. Stir in the harissa paste and let it fry for a minute, then pour in the coconut milk and bring it to a gentle simmer.
      7. Return the meatballs, cover, and simmer 20 to 25 minutes until they are cooked through and the sauce has thickened around them.
      8. Cook the rice separately and scatter the remaining cilantro over the top before portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Count the meatballs out evenly first, then spoon the same weight of sauce over each, and give every container the same weight of rice. Each bowl lands at 652 calories with 30 grams of protein.
