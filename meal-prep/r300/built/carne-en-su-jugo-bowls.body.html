      Makes 14 servings · ~770 cal · 69g protein · 25g carbs · 40g fat · ~$5.00 per serving.

      Carne en su jugo translates to meat in its own juice, and that is exactly the technique. Thin sliced steak simmers in a tomatillo and jalapeno salsa with crisped bacon and beans until the whole thing turns into a brothy bowl that eats like a stew. It is a Jalisco classic and the highest protein dish in this batch by a wide margin. Each bowl carries 69 grams of protein and 770 calories for about $5.00.

      Shop Smart

      • Tomatillos are cheapest in season and at Latin groceries, where they are often sold loose by the pound rather than in a shrink wrapped tray.
      • Canned beans are the right call for a batch this size. Dried beans are cheaper still if you plan ahead, but canned keeps the cook to one afternoon.
      • Bacon ends and pieces work perfectly since everything gets chopped anyway. Where a store sells them, they cost far less than sliced bacon.
      • Cilantro goes in by the bunch, twice, in the salsa and on top. Buy two bunches and store them stems down in water in the fridge.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Chop the bacon and cook it in a large pot until crisp. Lift it out with a slotted spoon and leave the fat behind.
      3. Slice the steak thin against the grain, season it with salt, cumin, and black pepper, and sear it in the bacon fat in batches until just browned.
      4. Husk and rinse the tomatillos, then blend them with the jalapenos, most of the cilantro, and the garlic into a rough green salsa.
      5. Pour the salsa into the pot, scraping up everything stuck to the bottom, and simmer 10 minutes until it darkens slightly.
      6. Return the beef with enough water to make it brothy, and simmer 20 to 25 minutes until the meat is tender.
      7. Drain and rinse the beans and stir them in, then cook 5 more minutes so they warm through and pick up the salsa.
      8. Finish each bowl with the crisped bacon, chopped raw onion, and the remaining cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight into each of 14 containers, stirring between scoops so the beans and beef split evenly, and keep the bacon and raw onion in a separate bag so they stay crisp. Each bowl lands at 770 calories with 69 grams of protein.
