      Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 32g protein · 83g carbs · 9g fat · ~$1.78 per serving.

      Calabacitas means little squashes, and in New Mexico and northern Mexico it means zucchini cooked down with corn, tomato, and chile until everything melts together. It is late-summer garden food, built for the weeks when zucchini is practically free. Add ground turkey and black beans and serve it over rice and you have a full dinner. Each serving runs 570 calories with 32 grams of protein for about $1.78.

      Shop Smart

      • Zucchini hits rock bottom prices in late summer and climbs hard in winter. Buy it in season, and if it is expensive, yellow squash usually costs less that same week.
      • Canned black beans and canned corn are among the cheapest protein and produce in the store, and both keep for years. Stock up when they go on multi-buy.
      • Buy jalapenos loose, not in a package. You need a couple, they cost pennies, and the rest freeze whole for the next skillet.
      • A small can of tomato paste is all this needs, and it deepens the sauce more than an extra can of tomatoes would. Freeze the leftover paste in tablespoon blobs.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Brown the ground turkey in your largest skillet over medium-high heat, breaking it into crumbles and letting it take on real color before you stir it around.
      3. Add the diced onion and the minced jalapeno and cook 5 minutes, then stir in the garlic and the ground cumin for one more minute.
      4. Push everything to the side, add the tomato paste to the bare part of the pan, and let it cook against the metal for two minutes until it darkens and loses its tinny edge.
      5. Add the diced zucchini and cook 6 to 8 minutes over high heat. Keep the pan hot so the squash browns rather than releasing water and boiling itself.
      6. Stir in the canned diced tomatoes, the drained corn, and the drained black beans, then season with salt and black pepper and simmer 10 minutes to bring it together.
      7. Cook the rice separately while the skillet simmers, so the grains stay distinct instead of soaking up all the sauce.
      8. Taste for salt, then fold in the chopped fresh cilantro off the heat and serve the calabacitas over the rice.

      Portion It

      Weigh the skillet and the rice together, subtract your empty pot weight, and divide by 14. Zucchini keeps releasing water as it sits, so portion soon after cooking and let the scale even it out. Each serving comes to 570 calories and 32 grams of protein.
