      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 32g protein · 70g carbs · 15g fat · ~$1.94 per serving.

      San choy bow is the Cantonese mince that usually gets scooped into lettuce cups at the start of a meal. Turned onto rice it becomes an actual dinner, and it keeps every good thing about the original: oyster sauce depth, crunchy celery and carrot, peanuts on top. Each bowl runs 564 calories and 32 grams of protein for about $1.94.

      Shop Smart

      • Oyster sauce is the flavor engine here and one bottle covers this batch four times over. It is the single best value bottle in the Asian aisle, so buy it once and stop buying stir-fry sauce packets forever.
      • Celery and carrot together cost very little and they add the crunch that keeps this from being just a bowl of meat. Cheap vegetables doing structural work is the whole budget cooking playbook.
      • Buy peanuts by the 16 ounce jar. This batch uses a fraction of it, so your share is a fraction of the jar while it keeps feeding recipes and snacks for months.
      • Mushrooms add savory depth for very little. If they are not on sale, skip them and add a little extra celery, since the texture matters more here than the specific vegetable.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the sauce together: oyster sauce, soy sauce, chicken broth, sesame oil, and honey. Having it premixed matters, because a stir-fry does not wait for you to measure.
      3. Dice the celery, carrot, and mushrooms small, about the size of the meat crumbles. Even dice is what makes this dish eat right instead of feeling like meat with vegetables thrown at it.
      4. Heat a wide skillet or wok over high, add the ground turkey, and brown it hard, breaking it into fine crumbles, until it is cooked through to 165F.
      5. Push the meat aside, add the minced garlic, grated ginger, and chopped jalapeno, and cook 30 seconds until they smell sharp and sweet.
      6. Add the celery, carrot, and mushrooms and stir-fry 4 minutes over high heat. You want them cooked but still snapping.
      7. Pour in the sauce and toss for 2 minutes until it reduces and coats everything, then stir in most of the sliced green onions.
      8. Cook the rice, move it into your weighed pot, and roughly chop the peanuts for the top.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Divide the mince evenly across all 14, then scatter the chopped peanuts and remaining green onion on top so they stay crunchy instead of going soft in the sauce. Each bowl lands at 564 calories and 32 grams of protein.
