Makes 14 servings · ~592 cal · 41g protein · 47g carbs · 27g fat · ~$3.80 per serving (at everyday cost).

Filipino menudo has nothing to do with the Mexican soup of the same name. This one is a tomato braise of pork, potatoes, carrots, and bell peppers, sweetened with raisins and rounded out with chickpeas. It shows up at every family party in the Philippines because it stretches a modest amount of meat across a lot of people and it holds beautifully on a buffet table for hours. That last part is precisely what we ask of a meal prep, so this dish arrived pre-qualified for the job.

    Shop Smart

    • Pork shoulder is the right cut and the cheap one at the same time. It needs the long braise to break down, which means the budget choice is also the correct choice.
    • Potatoes, carrots, and canned tomatoes are doing the volume work. That is the whole trick of this dish. The meat sets the flavor and the cheap stuff sets the portion size.
    • Canned chickpeas are the quiet protein add. They cost almost nothing per can and they soak up the tomato sauce better than anything else in the pot.
    • Raisins sound strange and are not optional. They melt into the sauce and give it the faint sweetness that makes menudo taste like menudo rather than like generic pork stew.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the pork shoulder into cubes and season with salt and black pepper.
    3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot, stir in the achiote paste until it colors the oil, then brown the pork in batches.
    4. Add the diced yellow onion and garlic and cook until softened.
    5. Stir in the diced tomatoes and fish sauce, bring to a simmer, cover, and cook until the pork is starting to turn tender.
    6. Add the cubed potatoes, carrots, and chicken livers and simmer uncovered until the vegetables are cooked through and the sauce reduces.
    7. Fold in the chickpeas, diced green and red bell peppers, and golden raisins, and cook just until the peppers soften but keep some bite.
    8. Taste and adjust the salt, then cook rice separately to serve underneath.

    Portion It

    Weigh the rice and the menudo in separate pots, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1 each time, and divide each by 14. Even rice base first, even ladle of stew second. Every bowl comes in at 592 calories with 41 grams of protein. This one reheats particularly well because the potatoes keep drinking up the sauce.
