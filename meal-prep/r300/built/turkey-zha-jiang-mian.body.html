      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 25g protein · 70g carbs · 21g fat · ~$1.72 per serving.

      Zha jiang mian is the Beijing noodle bowl that built the whole category: a dark, salty, fried sauce spooned over noodles with raw cucumber and carrot piled on top for crunch. The traditional version leans on pork belly and fermented bean paste. Ours uses ground turkey with hoisin carrying the sweet-savory bean note, and it comes to 567 calories and 25 grams of protein for about $1.72 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • Hoisin sauce is the backbone here. One jar covers several batches and keeps in the fridge for months, so the real per-bowl cost keeps dropping every time you use it.
      • Lo mein noodles are the second biggest line. Buy the multipack, or check the international aisle where the same dried noodles often run noticeably less per pack than the Americanized brand.
      • Cucumber and carrot go in raw as the garnish and together they cost almost nothing. Do not skip them. The cold crunch against the hot sauce is the entire point of the dish.
      • Green onions are a real line here because this recipe uses a lot of them. Regrow the root ends in water on your windowsill and the next batch costs you nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Prep the toppings first and refrigerate them: cut the cucumber and carrot into thin matchsticks. They go on cold, so getting them out of the way early is the smart order.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a wok or heavy skillet, add the grated ginger, minced garlic, and the white parts of the green onions, and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
      4. Add the ground turkey and brown it hard, breaking it into fine crumbles, until it is cooked through to 165F and starting to catch on the pan.
      5. Add the hoisin sauce and soy sauce and fry them with the meat for 3 to 4 minutes. This frying step is what the dish is named for, and it is where the sauce loses its raw edge and turns glossy.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth with a pinch of sugar, cinnamon, black pepper, and the bay leaves, then simmer 20 minutes until the sauce is thick enough to coat a spoon.
      7. Boil the lo mein noodles just to the firm side of done, drain them, and rinse them under cold water so they do not keep cooking and clump in the containers.
      8. Move the noodles into your weighed pot and toss them with a small spoon of oil so they stay loose.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of noodles on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the noodle bed for each container. Spoon the sauce evenly over all 14, then top each with the cold cucumber and carrot matchsticks and the green onion tops. Every bowl lands at 567 calories and 25 grams of protein.
