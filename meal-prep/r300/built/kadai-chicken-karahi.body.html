      Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 32g protein · 63g carbs · 19g fat · ~$1.50 per serving.

      Kadai chicken is named after the pan it is cooked in, a deep steel wok that gets ripping hot. The technique is bhuna: cook the tomato and spices down hard until the oil separates and the masala clings to the meat instead of swimming around it. No cream sauce to hide behind, no coconut milk. The spice blend is coarsely crushed, not powdered, so you get little bursts of coriander and chile as you eat. Each bowl comes in at 566 calories and 32 grams of protein for about $1.50.

      Shop Smart

      • Dried chiles are the second biggest line and the whole flavor of a kadai masala. Buy them in a bag from the Latin or international aisle rather than the spice rack, where the same chiles cost several times more per ounce, and store them in a sealed jar.
      • Crush your kadai masala coarsely with the flat of a knife or a rolling pin instead of buying it ground. Coarse is the point, ground is a different dish, and you already own the tools.
      • Tomato sauce in cans is cheaper and more consistent than fresh tomatoes for this, and it gets you to the oil-separating stage faster because there is less water to cook off.
      • A half pint of heavy cream is all this recipe uses and it goes in at the very end. Buy the small carton, since the rest of a quart will go bad long before your next batch.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the kadai masala: toast the dried chiles and the ground coriander in a dry pan for a minute, then crush them coarsely with the ground cumin, garam masala, italian seasoning and ground cinnamon. Coarse, not powdered.
      3. Melt the butter in your largest heavy pan over medium-high and cook the sliced onion for 8 to 10 minutes until browned at the edges.
      4. Add the garlic and cook a minute, then add the tomato sauce, chili powder, ground turmeric and salt. Cook this down hard for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring, until the sauce darkens and you can see butter pooling at the edges. That separation is the whole technique.
      5. Add the chicken thighs cut into chunks and turn them in the masala. Cook uncovered 15 to 20 minutes, stirring often, until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce is clinging rather than pooling.
      6. Stir in most of the crushed kadai masala and the sliced green bell pepper and cook 4 more minutes, so the pepper stays crisp instead of collapsing.
      7. Turn off the heat and stir in the heavy cream and the chopped cilantro. Save a pinch of the crushed masala to scatter over the top.
      8. Cook the rice separately, let it steam covered for 10 minutes, and fold it into the pot so every serving gets curry and rice together.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same number in every container. Each one carries 566 calories, and because this sauce is thick rather than soupy, it does not turn the rice to mush by day four.
