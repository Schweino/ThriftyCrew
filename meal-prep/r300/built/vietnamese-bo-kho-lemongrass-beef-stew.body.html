      Makes 14 servings · ~593 cal · 33g protein · 58g carbs · 25g fat · ~$4.12 per serving.

      Bo kho is Vietnamese beef stew, and the two things that make it Vietnamese rather than generic are lemongrass and annatto. The lemongrass gives it that bright citrus edge underneath the beef, and the annatto in the achiote paste turns the broth a deep orange that no amount of tomato could manage. Served over rice noodles, each bowl carries 593 calories and 33 grams of protein for about $4.12 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Lemongrass paste in a tube is the smart buy. Fresh stalks cost more, take work, and you throw most of each one away. The tube lives in the fridge for months and covers many batches.
      • Carrots are doing a lot of the work here. They add sweetness, bulk, and color for very little money, and a three pound bag is dramatically cheaper per pound than the little cello packs.
      • Rice noodles are the second biggest expense, so buy the boxes rather than the fancy fresh noodles. They cook in a couple minutes and hold in the fridge better than wheat noodles do.
      • Chili crisp and fish sauce are both one-time buys that last for ages. If you are stocking a pantry for Asian cooking, these two plus soy sauce cover an enormous amount of ground.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck into large cubes and marinate it with the garlic, ginger, lemongrass paste, fish sauce, five-spice powder, brown sugar, and black pepper for at least 30 minutes. Overnight is better if you can plan that far ahead.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot and brown the beef in batches. Set it aside and keep the marinade.
      4. Cook the sliced onion in the same pot until soft, then stir in the achiote paste, tomato paste, chili powder, and paprika and cook for a minute until the oil goes orange.
      5. Return the beef with the remaining marinade, the chicken broth, soy sauce, and bay leaves. Add water until the meat is just covered and bring it to a simmer.
      6. Cover and cook low for about 2 hours, then add the carrots in thick chunks and simmer uncovered another 30 minutes until they are tender and the broth has body.
      7. Stir in the chili crisp, taste for salt and fish sauce, and pull it off the heat.
      8. Cook the rice noodles just to tender, rinse them cold, and portion them into the containers before ladling the stew over. Top with cilantro and torn basil.

      Portion It

      Weigh the noodles into each container first, then put the stew pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Ladle to that number and every bowl lands at 593 calories. Keep the herbs in a small bag on the side and add them after reheating, because basil that has been microwaved four days in a row is not doing anybody any favors.
