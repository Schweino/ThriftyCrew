      Makes 14 servings · ~668 cal · 58g protein · 76g carbs · 12g fat · ~$2.75 per serving.

      This is the bowl every Vietnamese lunch spot builds its reputation on. Chicken thighs marinated in lemongrass, fish sauce, garlic, and honey, roasted until the edges caramelize, then piled over rice with cucumber, carrot, and cilantro and drenched in nuoc cham. It has one of the best protein-per-calorie ratios in this batch: 668 calories and 58 grams of protein for about $2.75 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Lemongrass paste in a tube is the single best shortcut in Vietnamese cooking. Fresh stalks are tough, wasteful, and more expensive, and the paste keeps in the fridge for months.
      • Fish sauce smells alarming in the bottle and tastes like nothing but savory depth in the finished dish. One bottle covers many batches, so do not let the price of the good stuff scare you off.
      • Boneless thighs are correct here for the same reason they are correct everywhere else in this batch. They roast without drying out and they cost a fraction of breast.
      • Cucumber, carrot, and cilantro are the cheapest way to make a bowl look and taste fresh. Keep them raw and separate and they hold their crunch all week.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk most of the fish sauce with the lemongrass paste, garlic, honey, soy sauce, black pepper, and a little sugar into a marinade. Hold back some fish sauce, sugar, lime juice, and the jalapeno for the sauce.
      3. Toss the chicken thighs in the marinade and refrigerate at least 2 hours. Overnight is better and this is one of the few recipes where that really shows.
      4. Make the nuoc cham by stirring the reserved fish sauce, lime juice, sugar, and warm water together until the sugar dissolves, then add the minced jalapeno. Taste it. It should be sharp, salty, and sweet all at once.
      5. Heat the oven to 425F, lay the chicken on a sheet pan in a single layer, and roast 25 to 30 minutes until the edges char. Crowding the pan steams the chicken and you lose the caramelization entirely, so use two pans if you have to.
      6. Cook the rice while the chicken roasts.
      7. Rest the chicken 5 minutes, then slice it across the grain and toss it with any juices left on the pan.
      8. Shred the carrots, slice the cucumber thin, and chop the cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container, then weigh the sliced chicken on top so nobody gets shorted on the protein. Add the cucumber and carrot to one side and keep the nuoc cham in a small side container so the vegetables stay crisp. Every bowl lands at 668 calories.
