      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 39g protein · 44g carbs · 23g fat · ~$3.34 per serving.

      Beef tips and gravy is diner food made honestly: cubes of chuck cooked until tender in a from scratch gravy, no packet required. Cornstarch thickens it at the end, which means you control how thick it gets instead of hoping a packet guessed right. Each bowl carries 39 grams of protein and 561 calories for about $3.34.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy a chuck roast and cube it yourself instead of buying packaged stew meat or beef tips. You will pay less per pound and you will know exactly what cut you got.
      • Cornstarch is the cheapest thickener there is and a box lasts years. Mix it with cold water before it goes anywhere near the hot pot or you will be picking out lumps.
      • Beef broth is the backbone of the gravy. Bouillon base in a jar makes a lot of broth for very little money and keeps in the fridge for months.
      • Only one onion goes in, so the seasoning matters more than usual. Do not skip the paprika, Dijon, and Worcestershire from the pantry list.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck roast into large cubes, pat them dry, and season with salt, black pepper, and paprika.
      3. Sear the cubes in the olive oil in batches over high heat until they have real color, then move them to the slow cooker.
      4. Cook the chopped onion and minced garlic in the same pan until soft, scraping up every browned bit, then add them to the cooker.
      5. Pour in the beef broth along with the Worcestershire sauce and Dijon mustard from the pantry list.
      6. Cover and cook on low for 7 to 8 hours, until the beef cubes give with no resistance.
      7. Whisk the cornstarch with a little cold water, stir it into the hot liquid, and cook on high 15 to 20 minutes until the gravy thickens and turns glossy.
      8. Cook the rice separately and spoon the beef tips and gravy over it. In a diner it would be mashed potatoes, and if you keep potatoes on hand that works too.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of beef tips and gravy into each container, then give every one the same weight of rice. Each bowl lands at 561 calories with 39 grams of protein.
