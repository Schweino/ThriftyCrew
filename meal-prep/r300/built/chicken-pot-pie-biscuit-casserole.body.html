      Makes 14 servings · ~582 cal · 25g protein · 61g carbs · 26g fat · ~$1.27 per serving.

      Pot pie has one real problem as a meal prep dish, which is that a bottom crust turns to wet cardboard in the fridge. This version fixes it by skipping the bottom entirely and topping a creamy chicken and vegetable filling with biscuits, which hold up far better and taste better anyway. It bakes in a 9x13 and cuts by the square. Each serving lands at 582 calories and 25 grams of protein for about $1.27 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned biscuits are the single largest expense here. If your store runs them on a buy one get one, that is the week to make this, because everything else on the list is pantry cheap.
      • The filling is butter, flour, milk, and broth, which is the cheapest cream sauce in cooking. There is no canned soup required and it tastes noticeably fresher without one.
      • Frozen peas cost a fraction of fresh and are picked at peak, so there is no quality argument against them. Same with buying potatoes in the bigger bag.
      • Chicken breast is used in a modest amount on purpose. This is a vegetable and gravy dish with chicken in it, and stretching the meat is exactly how the per-serving number stays where it is.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 375F. Dice the potatoes small and boil them until just tender, then drain. Small dice matters, because big chunks will still be hard when the biscuits are done.
      3. Cook the diced chicken breast in a little of the butter until browned and cooked through, then set it aside.
      4. Melt the rest of the butter in a large pot, cook the diced onion until soft, add the garlic, then whisk in the flour and cook it for 2 minutes.
      5. Add the chicken broth and milk slowly while whisking, then simmer until it thickens into a proper gravy. Season with the poultry seasoning, salt, and pepper.
      6. Fold in the chicken, potatoes, and frozen peas and pour everything into a large baking dish.
      7. Separate the biscuits and arrange them across the top with a little space between each one so they can spread and brown.
      8. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until the biscuits are deep golden on top and cooked through underneath. Pale biscuits mean raw dough in the middle, so trust the color and not the timer.

      Portion It

      Score the top into 14 even squares before you cut so each portion gets its own biscuit share. Weigh two or three portions on the scale to confirm you are close and adjust from there. Every serving comes in at 582 calories. Reheat uncovered so the biscuit tops crisp back up instead of steaming soft.
