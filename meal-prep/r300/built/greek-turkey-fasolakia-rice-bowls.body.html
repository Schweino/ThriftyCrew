      Makes 14 servings · ~657 cal · 30g protein · 72g carbs · 26g fat · ~$1.99 per serving.

      Fasolakia is the Greek braise of green beans and potatoes cooked slow in olive oil until everything goes tender and sweet. Traditionally it is a meatless summer dish. We brown turkey into the pot and serve it over rice, which turns a light lunch into a container that actually holds you until dinner. It runs 657 calories and 30 grams of protein for about $1.99.

      Shop Smart

      • Frozen green beans cost less per cup than fresh and come trimmed. In a braise this long, fresh beans end up in exactly the same place, so save the money and the knife work.
      • Potatoes by the 3 pound bag run barely more than a couple loose ones. Keep them somewhere cool and dark and they will outlast your interest in cooking.
      • This dish drinks a cup and a quarter of olive oil, and that is not a mistake. It is what makes a Greek braise taste Greek. Buy the big bottle so the per-ounce price stays sane.
      • Oregano, cumin, and bay leaves are the whole spice list, and they cost almost nothing from jars already in your cabinet. Cheap seasoning is where budget cooking wins or loses.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the olive oil in a wide heavy pot and cook the sliced onion over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes until it is soft and sweet, then add the garlic for one minute.
      3. Add the ground turkey and brown it, breaking it up, until it is cooked through to 165F.
      4. Stir in the diced tomatoes, the oregano, cumin, bay leaves, salt, and black pepper, and let it bubble for 5 minutes.
      5. Cut the potatoes into chunks and add them along with the green beans and enough water to come about halfway up the vegetables.
      6. Cover and simmer on low for 35 to 40 minutes, until the potatoes are fork tender and the beans have gone soft and olive-drab. Do not chase bright green beans here, that is a different dish.
      7. Pull the bay leaves, stir in the lemon juice, and taste for salt. Let it sit off the heat for 10 minutes so it tightens up.
      8. Cook the rice, move it into your weighed pot, and keep the braise in its own pot for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then spoon the braise evenly across all 14, scooping from the bottom every time so the potatoes and beans get shared out fairly. Each bowl lands at 657 calories and 30 grams of protein.
