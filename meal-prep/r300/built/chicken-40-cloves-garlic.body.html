      Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 39g protein · 75g carbs · 11g fat · ~$1.31 per serving.

      This is a five ingredient French classic and one of them is garlic in an amount that makes people laugh when they read it. Do not be alarmed. Whole cloves braised slowly in broth turn sweet, mild, and creamy, nothing like the sharp bite of raw or minced garlic. The name is a promise rather than a limit, and scaled up to feed fourteen you are going to be peeling for a while. Each bowl carries 570 calories and 39 grams of protein for about $1.31 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy whole heads of garlic, not the peeled tubs or the jarred minced. Whole heads are cheaper by a wide margin, they keep for weeks on the counter, and peeled garlic that has been sitting in liquid will not give you the sweet, creamy result this dish depends on.
      • Smash each clove lightly with the flat of a knife and the skin slips right off. That trick turns a tedious job into a ten minute one, which matters a lot at this quantity.
      • Boneless thighs are the cheap cut and the correct one. There is very little else in this pot, so the chicken has to stay juicy on its own.
      • With only five real ingredients, quality actually shows. Use a decent olive oil here, since there is nothing else in the pan to hide behind.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Peel all the garlic. Smash each clove with the flat of a knife first and the skins come off easily. Keep the cloves whole.
      3. Season the chicken thighs with salt, pepper, cumin, thyme, and dill and brown them in the olive oil in batches until deeply colored. Set them aside.
      4. Turn the heat down to medium-low, add all the garlic to the pan, and cook it slowly for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until the cloves turn golden and soft. Burnt garlic will ruin the entire pot, so low and slow is the only setting here.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth and scrape the bottom of the pan clean, then return the chicken with any juices.
      6. Cover and simmer 30 minutes until the chicken is tender and the garlic cloves have collapsed into the sauce.
      7. Uncover and simmer 10 more minutes to reduce, mashing a few cloves against the side of the pan to thicken the sauce naturally.
      8. Cook the rice and taste for salt before you portion.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container, then set the pan on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Make sure the soft garlic cloves get spread around, since they are the best part and it would be a shame for one container to get all of them. Every portion lands at 570 calories.
