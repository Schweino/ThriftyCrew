      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 46g protein · 54g carbs · 17g fat · ~$2.23 per serving.

      Gumbo is a roux dish first and everything else second. You stir flour and fat over heat until it turns the color of an old penny, and that dark roux is where the flavor, the color, and the whole identity of the pot come from. Skip it, rush it, or scorch it and you do not have gumbo, you have soup. Take your time and you get 562 calories and 46 grams of protein per bowl for about $2.23 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • The holy trinity of onion, bell pepper, and celery is the cheapest flavor base in American cooking. Buy the whole bunch of celery and a couple of green peppers and you have the foundation of a dozen Cajun dishes.
      • Flour and butter cost pennies and they are the single most important ingredient in the pot. Nothing you can buy in a jar replaces a real dark roux.
      • Chicken breast is the biggest line here. Buy the family pack and cut it down yourself rather than paying for the small tray.
      • Cajun seasoning, smoked paprika, thyme, and bay leaves are pantry jars that turn up in every Louisiana recipe. Buy the larger containers, since the price per ounce is far better than the little bottles and none of it goes to waste.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken breast into large pieces, season it with the cajun seasoning, and brown it in the vegetable oil in a heavy pot. Set it aside.
      3. Melt the butter in the same pot, whisk in the flour, and now settle in. Stir constantly over medium-low heat for 25 to 30 minutes until the roux turns the color of milk chocolate. Do not answer the phone. If black flecks appear, throw it out and start over, because a burnt roux poisons the entire pot.
      4. Add the diced onion, bell pepper, and celery straight into the hot roux. It will hiss and stop the cooking, which is exactly what you want. Cook 8 minutes until soft.
      5. Add the garlic, thyme, smoked paprika, italian seasoning, bay leaves, salt, and pepper and stir for a minute.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth slowly while whisking so the roux incorporates smoothly instead of clumping, then return the chicken.
      7. Simmer uncovered for 45 minutes, skimming any fat that rises. The gumbo should thicken to the point where it coats a spoon but still pours.
      8. Cook the rice, then stir the sliced green onions in off the heat.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container first, then set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Stir the pot thoroughly before each scoop, because the chicken settles and the last bowl otherwise ends up as broth. Every portion lands at 562 calories, and gumbo genuinely improves for the first two or three days.
