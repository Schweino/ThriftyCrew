      Makes 14 servings · ~725 cal · 57g protein · 91g carbs · 12g fat · ~$3.05 per serving.

      There are two kinds of dumplings in this world. Rolled and cut, which is a project, and dropped, which is a spoon and a bowl of batter. This one is dropped. The turkey and vegetables go into the slow cooker in the morning and do their thing all day. An hour before dinner you stir together flour, milk, butter and baking powder, drop spoonfuls onto the surface, put the lid back on, and let the steam do the rest. It is the most comfortable food in this entire section.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy a frozen turkey breast roast in season. Around the holidays it hits its low for the year. Cook it, pull it, freeze it in bags, and dishes like this one get much cheaper for months.
      • Flour, milk, butter and baking powder is the whole dumpling. There is no mix to buy and no biscuit tube needed. That is the cheapest carb you can put on top of a stew.
      • Carrots and celery in bags and bunches, not singles. This pot goes through a lot of both, and loose produce is priced for a household of two.
      • Lift the lid as little as you can. Every peek dumps the steam the dumplings need to cook through, and that is a free mistake to avoid.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside. It goes under the stew in the containers.
      3. Put the diced onion, sliced carrots, sliced celery and minced garlic in the slow cooker with the chicken broth, dried thyme, Italian seasoning, salt and black pepper.
      4. Add the cooked turkey breast, put the lid on, and cook on low for 5 to 6 hours until the vegetables are tender and the broth tastes like a real stock.
      5. Whisk together the flour, baking powder and salt, then cut in the softened butter and stir in the milk until you have a thick, sticky batter.
      6. Turn the slow cooker to high, drop spoonfuls of the batter across the surface of the stew, and put the lid back on.
      7. Cook 45 to 60 minutes without lifting the lid, until a dumpling comes out clean on a toothpick and the stew has thickened underneath.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice into your 14 containers, then set the crock on your scale, subtract the empty weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and portion that exact weight into each one, making sure everybody gets dumpling. Every serving lands near 725 calories with 57 grams of protein, which is the biggest protein number in this whole batch.
