      Makes 14 servings · ~872 cal · 52g protein · 65g carbs · 44g fat · ~$4.94 per serving.

      Beef pares is Manila street food at its most honest: chunks of beef braised until they surrender, a sweet and salty soy braise thickened into sauce, and garlic fried rice underneath catching all of it. This is a long, lazy cook, which is exactly what you want on a Sunday. Each bowl carries 52 grams of protein and 872 calories for about $4.94.

      Shop Smart

      • The source calls for brisket. Chuck roast braises the same way, shreds the same way, and costs meaningfully less per pound, so that is what we build on.
      • Chuck goes on sale in cycles. When it drops, buy two roasts and freeze the second whole. It thaws fine and this recipe does not care about a freezer trip.
      • Garlic is the whole personality of the fried rice. Buy heads, not the jarred stuff, and slice it thin so it crisps instead of burning.
      • Day old rice makes better garlic fried rice than fresh. Cook the rice the night before, spread it on a sheet pan, and let it dry out in the fridge.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck roast into large cubes. Sear them hard in a little oil, in batches, until they have real color on at least two sides.
      3. Add chopped onion, half the garlic, and the ginger to the pot and cook until the onion goes soft and translucent.
      4. Pour in the chicken broth, soy sauce, and brown sugar. Add bay leaves and black pepper, scraping the browned bits off the bottom.
      5. Cover and simmer low for 2 to 2.5 hours, until the beef pulls apart with a spoon. Add water if the pot gets too dry.
      6. Uncover for the last 20 minutes and let the sauce reduce until it coats the beef instead of pooling around it.
      7. For the garlic rice, fry the remaining sliced garlic in the rest of the oil until golden, then toss in the cooked cold rice and stir until every grain is coated and hot.
      8. Finish with sliced green onions over the beef.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your beef portion for each container. Add the same weight of garlic rice to every one and you have fourteen identical bowls. Each comes in at 872 calories with 52 grams of protein.
