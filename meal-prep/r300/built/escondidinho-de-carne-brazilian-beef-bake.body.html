      Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 38g protein · 51g carbs · 24g fat · ~$2.37 per serving.

      Escondidinho means little hidden thing, and the hidden thing is the meat. Seasoned beef goes on the bottom, creamy mashed potato goes over it like a blanket, cheese goes on top, and it bakes until the whole surface blisters. Brazil makes this with catupiry, a soft tangy cheese that does not exist here, so cream cheese is the standard stand-in and it works. Cilantro through the beef is what keeps it from tasting like a British shepherd's pie with a tan.

      Shop Smart

      • Potatoes are the cheapest thing in this dish and they are half of it. Buy the big bag and store them cool and dark, and the mash layer costs next to nothing.
      • Cream cheese replaces catupiry and is a fraction of the price of imported cheese. Buy the store brand bricks, which behave identically once melted into a mash.
      • Shred the mozzarella from a block. Bagged shreds are coated in anti-caking starch that keeps them from melting into the smooth sheet this topping wants.
      • Cilantro is what makes it Brazilian. One bunch costs pocket change and it is the difference between this and a generic beef and potato bake.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Peel and boil the potatoes until they fall apart on a fork, then drain them well and let the steam escape for a minute.
      3. Mash the potatoes with the milk, butter, parmesan cheese, salt, and black pepper until smooth, then set the mash aside.
      4. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet and cook the diced yellow onion and garlic until softened.
      5. Add the ground beef, break it up, and cook until browned with no pink remaining, then drain off the excess fat.
      6. Stir in the chili powder, ground cumin, salt, and black pepper, then take the pan off the heat and fold in the cream cheese and the chopped cilantro until melted through.
      7. Spread the beef in an even layer in a large baking dish and blanket it with the mashed potato.
      8. Scatter the mozzarella over the top and bake at 400 degrees until melted, bubbling, and browned in spots.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole baking dish, subtract the empty dish weight, and divide by 14. Cut to that weight so the beef layer gets divided as evenly as the potato does. Every serving comes in at 570 calories with 38 grams of protein. Let it rest fifteen minutes so the layers hold together when you lift them out.
