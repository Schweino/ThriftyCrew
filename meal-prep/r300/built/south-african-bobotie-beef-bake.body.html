      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 44g protein · 45g carbs · 24g fat · ~$3.58 per serving.

      Bobotie is South Africa's national dish and it is stranger and better than it sounds. Curried ground beef gets sweetened with raisins and honey, sharpened with vinegar, bound with milk-soaked bread, and then a savory egg custard is poured over the top and baked until it sets like a golden lid. Sweet, sour, spiced, and creamy all at once. It has been made in the Cape for centuries, and it happens to be perfectly suited to a big dish and fourteen containers.

      Shop Smart

      • The ground beef is the line that matters. Everything else in this bake is bread, milk, eggs, and pantry spices. Buy beef on markdown and freeze it, because that is where the savings are.
      • Stale bread is the correct bread. Bobotie traditionally uses bread that has gone dry, so the heels and end slices you were about to throw out are exactly right.
      • Curry powder and turmeric are cheap and they are the dish. A couple of jars season this bake many times over, so the spice cost per bowl rounds to nothing.
      • Raisins and honey are what make it bobotie. Skip them and you have a curried meatloaf. They are the sweet counterweight the whole thing balances on.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Tear the sandwich bread into pieces and soak it in most of the milk until it goes soft, then squeeze it out and set the milk aside.
      3. Melt the butter in a large pot and cook the diced yellow onions until deeply softened and golden.
      4. Add the garlic, ginger, curry powder, ground turmeric, and black pepper and cook for a minute so the spices bloom in the fat.
      5. Add the ground beef, break it up, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      6. Stir in the honey, golden raisins, white vinegar, worcestershire sauce, beef broth, the soaked bread, and salt, then simmer until the mixture is thick and no longer soupy.
      7. Spread the beef into a large greased baking dish, press it flat, and tuck the bay leaves into the surface.
      8. Whisk the eggs with the reserved milk and the heavy cream, pour that custard over the beef, and bake at 350 degrees until the top is set and golden.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole dish, subtract the empty dish weight, and divide by 14. Cut to that weight so every container gets its share of the custard top and not just the meat underneath. Every serving comes in at 564 calories with 44 grams of protein. Let it rest at least twenty minutes so the custard finishes setting before you cut it.
