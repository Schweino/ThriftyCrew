      Shop Smart

      • Smoked sausage plus Cajun seasoning replaces andouille. Real andouille carries a specialty price. Kielbasa style smoked sausage with a good Cajun blend gets you the same smoky heat for much less.
      • Canned diced tomatoes are the bulk of the sauce. Buy store brand by the case when they go on sale. They keep for years and every third recipe on this site uses them.
      • Cornstarch is the cheapest thickener there is. A few tablespoons whisked into cold liquid does what a whole extra cup of cream would do.
      • Green peppers instead of red will trim this cost. Red bell peppers are the priciest produce on the list here and the Cajun seasoning is doing the flavor work anyway.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the smoked sausage into coins, dice the onion and red bell pepper, and mince the garlic.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large deep pot and brown the sausage coins until the edges take on color, then set them aside.
      4. Cook the onion and pepper in the sausage fat until they soften, then add the garlic and the Cajun seasoning and stir for a minute so the spices bloom.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth and the diced tomatoes and bring it to a boil, scraping the bottom clean.
      6. Stir in the dry pasta, drop it to a simmer, and cook uncovered while stirring often so nothing sticks. The pasta will drink most of the liquid.
      7. Whisk the cornstarch into a little cold water to make a slurry, then stir it into the pot and let it thicken for a minute.
      8. Turn the heat low, return the sausage, and stir in the heavy cream and parmesan until the sauce goes smooth.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, stirring between scoops so the sausage stays even. Every bowl lands near 660 calories and 25 grams of protein.
