      Makes 14 servings · ~568 cal · 31g protein · 89g carbs · 7g fat · ~$1.70 per serving.

      In New Orleans, red beans and rice is Monday food. Monday was wash day, the pot could sit on the stove unattended all morning, and the ham bone from Sunday went in with the beans. This version seasons ground turkey like Cajun sausage and simmers it with kidney beans, tomatoes, and the holy trinity over rice. Each serving carries 31 grams of protein and 568 calories for about $1.70. Beans and rice have been the best protein-per-dollar math in America for two centuries and nothing has come along to beat it.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned kidney beans are the cheapest protein in this recipe by a wide margin. Dried beans are cheaper still if you are willing to soak them overnight, and one bag replaces three cans.
      • The holy trinity of onion, green bell pepper, and celery is the cheapest flavor base you can build. Buy the whole celery bunch and use the leafy tops in the pot too.
      • Canned diced tomatoes and tomato paste are pantry items with a year of shelf life. Buy the store brand and keep extras, because they end up in half the recipes on this site.
      • Season the ground turkey yourself with thyme, cayenne, and paprika instead of buying packaged sausage. You get the same flavor for the price of plain ground meat.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Brown the ground turkey in your largest pot over medium-high heat, breaking it into coarse crumbles and letting it get real color before you stir it.
      3. Season the turkey heavily with dried thyme, salt, and black pepper while it browns, so it reads like sausage rather than plain ground meat.
      4. Add the diced onion, green bell pepper, and celery and cook 8 minutes until soft, then stir in the garlic for one more minute.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and let it cook against the bottom of the pot for two minutes until it darkens, which takes the raw edge off it.
      6. Add the canned diced tomatoes, the drained kidney beans, and the hot sauce, then simmer uncovered for 25 to 30 minutes, mashing some of the beans against the side of the pot to thicken the gravy.
      7. Cook the rice separately while the beans simmer, so the grains stay distinct instead of dissolving into the sauce.
      8. Taste for salt and heat, then serve the beans over the rice or fold them together for portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the beans and the rice together, subtract your empty pot weight, and divide by 14. Bean gravy settles heavy at the bottom of the pot, so stir before every scoop and let the scale keep everyone honest. Each serving comes to 568 calories and 31 grams of protein.
