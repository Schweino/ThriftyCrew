      Makes 14 servings · ~668 cal · 30g protein · 37g carbs · 45g fat · ~$2.34 per serving.

      Massaman is the gentlest of the Thai curries and the one with the longest passport. It came into southern Thailand through Persian and Indian traders, which is why it carries potatoes and peanuts and a sweetness that the other curries do not have. It is thick, mild enough that kids will eat it, and it is basically a stew wearing a curry's clothes. Each serving lands at 668 calories and 30 grams of protein for about $2.34.

      Shop Smart

      • Coconut milk is the dominant line here by a distance. Buy full fat store brand cans and buy them when they are on sale, since a stack of cans in the pantry keeps for a year and this recipe wants several.
      • Potatoes and carrots are what make this the cheapest-eating of the Thai curries, because they add real volume for pennies. Buy both by the bag rather than loose.
      • Peanut butter and chopped peanuts both go in, and one jar of each covers many batches. Buy the plain sixteen ounce jar rather than anything specialty.
      • Chicken breast is the largest single line. Thighs would cut that noticeably and would survive the long simmer better, so make the swap if the price matters more than the label.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Spoon the thick cream off the top of the unshaken coconut milk cans into your largest pot, keeping the thin liquid aside. Cook it with the olive oil over medium-high for 3 to 5 minutes until the oil separates.
      3. Add the curry paste and the diced onion and fry them in that oil for 3 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens and smells toasted.
      4. Add the chicken cut into chunks along with the garlic and grated ginger, and turn everything until coated.
      5. Pour in the reserved coconut liquid, then add the cubed potatoes and sliced carrots. Bring it to a simmer.
      6. Stir in the peanut butter, brown sugar and fish sauce, then simmer uncovered 30 to 35 minutes until the potatoes are fork tender and the sauce has reduced and thickened around them.
      7. Turn off the heat and stir in the lime juice, tasting as you go. Massaman should read sweet first, salty second and sour last, in that order.
      8. Fold in most of the chopped peanuts and the cilantro, holding some of each back to scatter on top.

      Portion It

      Set the loaded pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same amount in every container. Each one carries 668 calories, and the potatoes mean this holds its texture on reheat better than a rice-heavy bowl does.
