      Makes 14 servings · ~582 cal · 35g protein · 47g carbs · 27g fat · ~$3.06 per serving.

      Kerala beef fry, or ularthiyathu, is what happens when you refuse to stop cooking. The beef gets pressure cooked with spices until it is fall-apart tender, and then, instead of serving it, you dump it into a pan of caramelized shallots and curry leaves and dry-roast it until every drop of sauce is gone and the meat is dark, crusted, and coated in spice. That second stage is the entire dish. It is nothing like the two keema recipes in the library. This is dry, intense, and almost pure protein and spice.

      Shop Smart

      • Chuck roast is the right cut and the whole cost of the dish. It needs the pressure cooking to break down, so buy the cheap tough cut on purpose and shop the markdowns.
      • Ground fennel is the signature and it is a small jar. It is the warm licorice note that says Kerala, and one jar covers many batches.
      • Onions are the second component, not a garnish. A serious amount of sliced onion cooked down dark is what the beef gets coated in, and onions are cheap by the bag.
      • A pressure cooker or an instant pot pays for itself on dishes like this. Without one, this becomes a two-hour braise, which works, it just costs you the afternoon.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the beef chuck roast into bite sized cubes.
      3. Toss the beef with the chili powder, ground coriander, ground turmeric, black pepper, garam masala, ground fennel, salt, most of the garlic, most of the ginger, and the diced yellow onion.
      4. Pressure cook the seasoned beef with a small splash of water until it is completely tender, then open it up and boil off any remaining liquid.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide heavy pan and cook the sliced red onions and sliced jalapenos slowly until deeply browned and sweet.
      6. Add the rest of the garlic and ginger and cook until fragrant.
      7. Add the cooked beef and dry-roast it over medium heat, stirring and scraping, until the moisture is gone and the meat is dark and crusted at the edges. This takes patience and it is where the dish is made.
      8. Taste for salt, then cook the rice according to the package to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the beef pan separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Even rice base, even weight of beef. Every bowl comes in at 582 calories with 35 grams of protein. Because the sauce is cooked off, this reheats better than almost any curry and will not go soggy on you.
