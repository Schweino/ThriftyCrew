      Makes 14 servings · ~581 cal · 34g protein · 73g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.03 per serving.

      Stuffed peppers taste great and take forever. You hollow, you par-cook, you stuff, you bake, and then you do it 14 more times. This soup skips every one of those steps and keeps the flavor. Ground turkey, bell peppers, tomato, rice and a savory beef broth base, finished with parmesan. Same dinner, a third of the work.

      Shop Smart

      • Green bell peppers are the cheap ones. Red, yellow and orange peppers are the same vegetable further along and they cost two or three times as much. Green is the right call here.
      • Canned diced tomatoes and tomato sauce are the base. Buy them by the case when they hit a sale price. They keep for years and they show up in half the recipes on this site.
      • Rice from the warehouse bag costs almost nothing per serving. That is why it is the carb in so many of these builds. One bag covers this batch and a dozen more.
      • Worcestershire is the secret in this pot. One bottle lasts a very long time and it is what makes a tomato and turkey soup taste like it simmered all day.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside. Cooking it in the soup turns the whole pot to mush by day three.
      3. Brown the ground turkey in the olive oil in your big pot over medium-high heat, breaking it up as it goes, until no pink is left.
      4. Add the diced onion and the chopped green bell peppers and cook until they soften, then stir in the garlic for the last minute.
      5. Pour in the beef broth, the diced tomatoes with their juice, the tomato sauce and the Worcestershire sauce.
      6. Add the bay leaves, dried oregano and dried thyme, bring it to a simmer, and let it go for 25 to 30 minutes so the peppers go tender and the flavors settle.
      7. Fish out the bay leaves, season with salt and black pepper, and stir the grated parmesan in off the heat.

      Portion It

      Scoop the cooked rice into your 14 containers first, then set the full soup pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and ladle that exact weight over the rice. Every serving lands near 581 calories with 34 grams of protein.
