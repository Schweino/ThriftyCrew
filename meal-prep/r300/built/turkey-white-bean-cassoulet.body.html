      Makes 14 servings · ~565 cal · 30g protein · 76g carbs · 15g fat · ~$1.84 per serving.

      Cassoulet is the great slow bean bake of southwest France, and the traditional version takes three days and a duck. This is the version a working person can actually make. smoked turkey sausage, bacon, turkey breast and white beans go into one pot with tomato and herbs, then bake low and slow until the top forms a crust and the beans underneath go completely creamy. French technique, American grocery budget.

      Shop Smart

      • Beans are the soul of a cassoulet, and beans are cheap. Canned cannellini save you the overnight soak for very little extra money, and they hold their shape through a long bake.
      • Sausage and bacon are seasonings here. Between them they flavor the entire pot, so you do not need much of either. Buy the larger packs and freeze the balance in portions.
      • Canned diced tomatoes on case sale. This bake goes through a real stack of them, so buy when the shelf tag drops and store them. They last for years.
      • Crack the crust and stir it back in, twice. That is the classic move and it costs nothing. Every time you break the top and push it under, the whole dish gets creamier.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside. It goes under the cassoulet in the containers.
      3. Brown the sliced smoked turkey sausage and the chopped bacon in the olive oil in a heavy oven-safe pot, then set the meat aside and leave the fat.
      4. Cook the diced onion and sliced carrots in that fat until soft, then stir in the minced garlic, Italian seasoning, dried thyme and red pepper flakes.
      5. Add the diced tomatoes with their juice, the drained cannellini beans, the browned sausage and bacon, and the shredded turkey breast. Season with salt and black pepper.
      6. Bring it to a simmer on the stove, cover, and bake at 325F for about 90 minutes.
      7. Uncover for the last 30 minutes so a crust forms on top, breaking that crust and stirring it under once or twice as it bakes.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice into your 14 containers, then set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight over the rice. Every serving lands near 565 calories with 30 grams of protein. This is one of the few dishes that genuinely tastes better on day three.
