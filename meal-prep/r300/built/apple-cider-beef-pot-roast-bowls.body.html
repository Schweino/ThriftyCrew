      Shop Smart

      • Plain apple juice does the job of cider for less money and it is on the shelf all year. Skip the seasonal spiced cider, which is sweeter and priced like a treat.
      • Carrots are the biggest produce line here. The three pound bag almost always beats loose carrots on price per pound, and they keep for weeks in the crisper.
      • Dijon mustard and tomato paste are both small amounts from containers that last. Freeze leftover tomato paste in spoonfuls so the rest of the can does not rot in the fridge.
      • Chuck roast, again, on the sale cycle. Every pot roast on this site lives or dies on what you paid for the beef.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Pat the chuck roast dry, season it with salt, black pepper, thyme, and Italian seasoning, and sear it in the vegetable oil until browned on all sides.
      3. Move the roast to the slow cooker. Whisk the apple juice, beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, tomato paste, Dijon mustard, and minced garlic together.
      4. Pour that liquid over the roast, scraping any browned bits from the searing pan in with it.
      5. Cut the carrots and potatoes into large chunks so they survive the full cook, and tuck them around the roast.
      6. Cover and cook on low for 8 hours, until the beef gives with no resistance and the vegetables are soft through.
      7. Lift the roast out, shred it, and return it to the pot with the vegetables.
      8. Taste the liquid. If it reads too sweet, a splash more Dijon or a pinch of salt will square it up.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop that weight into each of 14 containers, mixing as you go so each one gets beef, carrot, and potato in the same proportion. Each bowl lands at 598 calories with 48 grams of protein.
