      Makes 14 servings · ~581 cal · 55g protein · 53g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.02 per serving.

      Dakdoritang is the Korean braise where chicken and potatoes cook down in a gochujang sauce until the potatoes are falling apart at the edges and thickening everything around them. It is a one pot dish that gets better the second day, which is exactly what you want from a meal prep recipe. Each bowl carries 581 calories and 55 grams of protein for about $2.02 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Boneless thighs are the whole reason this number works. They cost a fraction of breast per pound, they do not dry out during a braise, and they carry more flavor. For any stew, thighs beat breast on every measure including price.
      • A tub of gochujang costs a few dollars and lasts through many batches. It is the single highest return item you can add to a pantry if you like Korean food, because it turns cheap ingredients into something that tastes engineered.
      • Potatoes and carrots in the big bags are the cheapest bulk in the produce aisle, and here they are not filler. The potato starch is what thickens the braising liquid into a sauce.
      • Sesame oil, mirin, and soy sauce are one-time buys that carry across every Korean and Japanese recipe on the site, so the real cost of this sauce after the first batch is close to nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the gochujang, soy sauce, honey, mirin, garlic, ginger, sesame oil, red pepper flakes, and black pepper into a sauce. Taste it and decide how hot you want the week to be, because this is your only chance to adjust.
      3. Cut the chicken thighs into large pieces and toss them with about half the sauce.
      4. Cut the potatoes and carrots into big chunks. Small pieces will disappear into the sauce over a long braise, and you want them to stay recognizable.
      5. Put the chicken, potatoes, carrots, and onion into a wide heavy pot, pour the rest of the sauce over, and add the chicken broth. It should come about halfway up, not cover everything.
      6. Bring it to a boil, then drop to a low simmer, cover, and cook 25 minutes.
      7. Uncover, add the jalapeno, and simmer another 15 to 20 minutes while the liquid reduces into a thick glossy sauce. Stir gently so the potatoes stay whole.
      8. Scatter the green onions over the top off the heat.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop to that gram target and make sure each container gets both chicken and potato rather than one or the other. Every portion lands at 581 calories, and this reheats beautifully because the sauce keeps everything moist.
