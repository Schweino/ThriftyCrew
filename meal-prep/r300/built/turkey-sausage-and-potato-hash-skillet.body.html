      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 28g protein · 64g carbs · 24g fat · ~$2.59 per serving.

      A hash is what you make when you want dinner to have crisp edges. Potatoes and Brussels sprouts roasted hard until they caramelize, smoked turkey sausage browned in the same pan, red pepper and onion softened into the whole thing. Each serving runs 567 calories with 28 grams of protein for about $2.59. It reheats better than most skillet dinners because the potatoes were cooked past the point of being fragile in the first place.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy potatoes in the big sack. This skillet works through most of a 7 lb bag and loose potatoes cost noticeably more per pound.
      • Brussels sprouts drop to their lowest price in fall and winter, and frozen sprouts work here too since they are getting browned hard anyway. Compare the fresh pound price against the frozen bag.
      • Smoked turkey sausage ropes freeze indefinitely in the original casing. Buy several when they cycle onto sale, because they are the cost driver in this recipe.
      • Skip pre-cut potatoes and sprouts entirely. The convenience markup on trimmed vegetables is one of the steepest in the store and the knife work here is ten minutes.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the potatoes into half inch cubes and halve the Brussels sprouts, then toss everything with the olive oil, salt, and black pepper.
      3. Spread the potatoes and sprouts across two sheet pans in a single layer. Crowding is the enemy of browning, and two pans is the difference between roasted and steamed.
      4. Roast at 425F for 30 to 35 minutes, flipping once, until the potatoes are crisp at the corners and the sprout leaves are dark and papery.
      5. While they roast, slice the smoked turkey sausage into coins and brown them in a large skillet over medium-high until both cut sides char.
      6. Add the diced onion and red bell pepper to the sausage and cook 6 to 8 minutes until they soften and pick up color.
      7. Pour the chicken broth into the skillet and scrape the bottom clean, letting it reduce down to almost nothing so the flavor stays in the pan rather than pooling.
      8. Fold the roasted potatoes and sprouts into the skillet, taste for salt, and finish with the sliced green onions off the heat.

      Portion It

      Set the pan on the scale, subtract the empty pan weight, and divide by 14. Stir the hash before each scoop so the sausage does not all end up in the first four containers. Every serving lands at 567 calories and 28 grams of protein.
