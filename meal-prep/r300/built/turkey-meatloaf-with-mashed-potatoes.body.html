      Makes 14 servings · ~599 cal · 32g protein · 62g carbs · 23g fat · ~$1.54 per serving.

      Meatloaf gets mocked, and meatloaf does not care. It is one of the few dinners that actually improves after a night in the fridge, and it slices to weight better than almost anything else you can cook. This one is turkey, bound with egg and bread crumbs, glazed with ketchup and barbecue sauce, served over rice.       Shop Smart

      • Ground turkey in the largest pack you can freeze is the single biggest lever on this recipe's price. Everything else in the pan costs pennies by comparison.
      • Bread crumbs are the item people overpay for the most. A canister lasts many batches, and stale sandwich bread run through a food processor is free.
      • Ketchup, barbecue sauce, Worcestershire, and Dijon are all shelf-stable for a year or more. Buy the store-brand bottles once and they will carry you through a dozen recipes.
      • Rice bought in the big bag is the cheapest starch in the store per serving. There is no such thing as rice going bad on you before you use it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Melt the butter in a skillet and cook the diced onion and green bell pepper for 5 minutes until soft, then add the garlic for one minute and let the mixture cool. Hot vegetables will start cooking the egg.
      3. In a large bowl, whisk the eggs with the heavy cream, Dijon mustard, Worcestershire sauce, salt, and black pepper, then stir in the bread crumbs and let them soak for 5 minutes.
      4. Add the ground turkey, the cooled vegetables, and the shredded cheddar. Mix with your hands only until it comes together. Overworking meatloaf turns it dense and rubbery.
      5. Divide the mixture between three loaf pans or shape it into three free-form loaves on a sheet pan. Smaller loaves cook faster and slice more evenly than one giant brick.
      6. Stir the ketchup and barbecue sauce together and brush half of it over the loaves, then bake at 350F for 45 to 55 minutes until the centers read 165F.
      7. Brush on the rest of the glaze and return the loaves for 10 minutes so it sets into a lacquer instead of running off.
      8. Cook the rice while the loaves rest at least 15 minutes, then slice the meatloaf and portion it over the rice.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice and the sliced meatloaf together, subtract your empty pot weight, and divide by 14. Meatloaf slices look identical and weigh nothing alike, so let the scale settle it. Every serving comes to 599 calories and 32 grams of protein.
