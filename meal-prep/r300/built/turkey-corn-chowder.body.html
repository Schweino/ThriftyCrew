      Shop Smart

      • Buy potatoes in the big bag. This chowder leans on them hard, and the per pound price on an eight or ten pound bag is a fraction of what loose potatoes cost.
      • Canned corn beats frozen for a chowder. It is cheaper, it is already soft, and the liquid in the can can go right into the pot instead of down the drain.
      • Bacon is the flavor engine, so a little goes far. Render it first, cook everything else in that fat, and you get smoke through the entire pot from a quarter pound.
      • Turkey breast is the one line worth shopping. Buy a frozen roast when the holiday price hits, cook it, and freeze it pulled and portioned for chowders and soups all winter.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Chop the bacon and render it in your big pot with the olive oil until crisp. Lift the bacon out and leave the fat.
      3. Add the butter, then the diced onion and celery, and cook until soft. Stir in the garlic for the last minute.
      4. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir for two minutes so it loses the raw taste and coats everything.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth while stirring, add the peeled and cubed potatoes, and simmer until the potatoes are fork tender and starting to break down.
      6. Add the drained corn and the cooked turkey breast and bring it back to a simmer.
      7. Stir in the heavy cream and the hot sauce, warm it through without boiling, then season with salt and black pepper and scatter the reserved bacon back on top.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide by 14 and ladle that exact weight into each container. Every bowl lands near 581 calories with 36 grams of protein. Potato chowders thicken hard in the fridge, so a splash of broth on reheat brings it right back.
