      Makes 14 servings · ~671 cal · 53g protein · 46g carbs · 28g fat · ~$4.59 per serving.

      Take everything good about French onion soup and put a chuck roast in it. Six pounds of onions cook down into something dark and sweet, the beef broth and Worcestershire go deep and savory, and eight hours later you have a pot roast that tastes nothing like the usual one.       Ingredients

      • Beef Chuck Roast: 7.5 lb (3360 g)

      • Worcestershire Sauce (Lea & Perrins): 7 tbsp (119 g)
      • Yellow Onion: 6.4 (700 g)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 3.75 cups dry (700 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Onions are the second star, so buy the big mesh bag. Sweet onions are lovely and unnecessary here, since a long cook makes plain yellow onions sweet on their own.
      • Chuck roast again, and again the advice is the same. Buy it on the sale cycle and freeze what you do not cook this week.
      • A bottle of Worcestershire sauce lasts a year or more and turns up in half the beef recipes on this site. It is one of the best value bottles in the pantry.
      • Boxed beef broth is fine, but bouillon base in a jar costs less per cup and stores in the fridge for months. Either works in a pot this heavily seasoned.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice all the onions thin. This is the long part of the prep, so put on something to listen to and get through it.
      3. Season the chuck roast with salt, black pepper, garlic powder, and thyme, and set it in the slow cooker.
      4. Pile the sliced onions over and around the roast. They will look like far too much and they will cook down to a fraction of that.
      5. Pour in the beef broth and the Worcestershire sauce.
      6. Cover and cook on low for 8 hours, until the roast shreds under a fork and the onions have collapsed into the liquid.
      7. Shred the beef in the cooker and stir it through the onions and broth so every strand picks up the gravy.
      8. Cook the rice separately while the beef finishes.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of beef and onions into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 671 calories with 53 grams of protein.
