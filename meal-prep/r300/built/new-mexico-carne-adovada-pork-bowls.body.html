      Makes 14 servings · ~645 cal · 38g protein · 59g carbs · 28g fat · ~$2.53 per serving.

      New Mexico takes chile seriously enough to have made it the state question. Red or green? This is the red answer. Carne adovada is pork shoulder marinated in a thick red chile puree, then braised in that same puree until the meat falls apart and the sauce turns almost black. There is no tomato in it, no cream, nothing hiding behind the chile. It is one of the great American regional dishes and almost nobody outside the state has had it.

      Shop Smart

      • Dried chiles from the Hispanic aisle, in the bag. Ancho and guajillo in the big bags cost a fraction of the branded spice-rack packets, and they keep a year sealed in the pantry.
      • Pork shoulder is the right cut and the cheap cut. A long braise in acidic chile sauce is exactly what a tough shoulder wants. A lean loin would dry out here.
      • Marinate overnight. It is free. The vinegar in the puree tenderizes the pork while you sleep, which means less cooking time and better texture for zero extra dollars.
      • Vinegar and a spoon of sugar do the balancing. Cheap pantry items keep the chile from turning bitter, which is the mistake that ruins most homemade red chile.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Stem and seed the dried ancho and guajillo chiles, toast them briefly in a dry pan, then soak in hot water for 20 minutes until fully soft.
      3. Blend the chiles with some soaking water, the garlic, white vinegar, soy sauce, sugar, ground cumin, dried oregano, dried thyme, salt and black pepper into a thick smooth puree, then strain out the skins.
      4. Cut the pork shoulder into large cubes, coat every piece in the red puree, and refrigerate overnight.
      5. Brown the diced onion in the vegetable oil in a heavy oven-safe pot, then add the pork with all of its marinade and the bay leaves.
      6. Cover and braise at 325F for about 3 hours, stirring once an hour, until the pork shreds under a fork and the sauce has gone dark and thick.
      7. Cook the rice per the package, then taste the chile for salt and pull out the bay leaves before you portion.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice across your 14 containers, then set the braising pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight of pork and red chile over the rice. Every bowl lands near 645 calories with 38 grams of protein.
