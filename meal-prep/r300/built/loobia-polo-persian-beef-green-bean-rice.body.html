      Makes 14 servings · ~590 cal · 26g protein · 87g carbs · 14g fat · ~$1.54 per serving.

      Loobia polo is Persian layered rice, and the layering is not decoration. Rice gets parboiled, drained, and then steamed in a pot over a base of oil until the bottom forms tahdig, the crackly golden crust that Iranian families fight over at the table. Beef, green beans, tomato, turmeric, and cinnamon go through the middle. The cinnamon is the part that surprises people. It is not sweet here, it just makes everything taste rounder. Fourteen containers of this is one pot and one steam.

      Shop Smart

      • Rice and green beans are the volume, and both are cheap. That is why this comes in near the bottom of the price list despite having real beef in it.
      • Frozen green beans beat fresh here. They get simmered into the beef anyway, so the snap does not matter, and the bag price per pound is far lower.
      • Turmeric and cinnamon are pennies per batch. Persian cooking gets its depth from spices you already own rather than from expensive ingredients.
      • Do not skip the tahdig step even though it is optional. The oil in the bottom of the pot costs nothing and it turns a good rice dish into the one people ask you to make again.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then parboil it in heavily salted water until the grains are soft outside and still firm in the center, and drain it.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a pan and cook the diced yellow onion until golden, then add the garlic and cook until fragrant.
      4. Add the ground beef, break it up, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste, ground turmeric, ground cinnamon, salt, and black pepper and cook for a minute so the paste darkens.
      6. Add the green beans and a splash of water, cover, and simmer until the beans are tender and the mixture is thick rather than wet.
      7. Pour the vegetable oil into the bottom of a large heavy pot, then layer the parboiled rice and the beef mixture in alternating layers, mounding the rice toward the center.
      8. Cover the lid with a clean towel, steam over the lowest heat until the rice is fluffy and a crust has formed on the bottom, then finish with the lemon juice.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Stir the layers together gently before weighing so every container gets rice and beef instead of one or the other, and break the crust into pieces and share it out. Every serving comes in at 590 calories with 26 grams of protein.
