      Makes 14 servings · ~624 cal · 26g protein · 89g carbs · 19g fat · ~$1.77 per serving.

      Smoked sausage, cabbage and apple is one of those old pairings that works because the sweet and the salty need each other. A splash of cider vinegar and a spoon of dijon keep it from tipping into dessert territory. Served over rice it lands at 26 grams of protein for about $1.77, and apples are one of the cheapest fruits on the board most of the year.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy apples loose, not in the bag. Bagged apples are usually smaller and priced by convenience. Buying loose lets you pick firm ones that hold up to heat, and it is often cheaper per apple.
      • A firm tart apple is the right apple. Softer sweet varieties turn to sauce in the pan. You want something that keeps its shape when it cooks.
      • Cabbage is doing most of the volume work. A couple of heads fill the pan for very little. That is why this dish feeds fourteen the way it does.
      • Apple juice and dijon cost almost nothing per batch. A bottle of juice and a jar of mustard both last for months, and together they are what makes this taste finished rather than plain.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the smoked sausage into coins, slice the onion, core and shred the cabbage, and core and slice the apples thick.
      3. Heat the olive oil in your largest skillet and brown the sausage coins hard on both sides, then set them aside.
      4. Cook the sliced onion in the sausage fat until it softens and picks up the color from the pan.
      5. Add the cabbage in batches, letting each handful wilt before the next goes in, and cook until it turns tender and sweet.
      6. Add the apple slices and cook just a few minutes. You want them softened but still holding their shape, not collapsed.
      7. Whisk the chicken broth, apple juice, dijon mustard, apple cider vinegar and dried thyme together, pour it into the skillet, and simmer until it reduces and glazes everything.
      8. Return the sausage, season with salt and black pepper, and cook the rice separately to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount over rice in each container. Every bowl lands near 624 calories and 26 grams of protein.
