      Shop Smart

      • Rice and boneless thighs are the two cheapest things you can build a dinner on, and this recipe is essentially both of them plus seasoning. That is the whole reason the per-bowl number is what it is.
      • Claypot rice traditionally uses Chinese sausage, or lap cheong. This build prices bratwurst because that is what an ordinary Omaha store actually stocks. Slice it thin and render it in the pan and it plays the same part for a lot less hunting.
      • Oyster sauce is a jar that lasts through many batches and it is doing most of the flavor work here. If your pantry has one Chinese ingredient, make it this one.
      • White mushrooms are cheaper than any specialty variety and they still release the savory liquid that flavors the rice. Buy the whole pack and slice them yourself, since pre-sliced costs more and dries out faster.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken thighs into bite sized pieces and marinate them with the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sesame oil, and a little salt and pepper for at least 30 minutes.
      3. Slice the mushrooms and the sausage thin. Thin matters, because everything on top needs to cook through in the same time the rice does.
      4. Rinse the rice until the water runs mostly clear, then put it in a heavy pot or rice cooker with the chicken broth.
      5. Lay the marinated chicken, mushrooms, and sausage across the top of the rice. Do not stir. The rice steams underneath while the toppings drip down into it, and stirring destroys the whole method.
      6. Cook covered on low for about 25 minutes, or run a standard rice cooker cycle, then let it sit covered off the heat for 10 more minutes.
      7. Lift the lid, pour any remaining marinade over the top, and now fold everything together gently.
      8. Scatter the sliced green onions across the pot before portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fold well before you scoop so the chicken and mushrooms are distributed evenly through the rice instead of sitting in a layer on top. Every container lands at 569 calories.
