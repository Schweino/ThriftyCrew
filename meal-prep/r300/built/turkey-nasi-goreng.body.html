      Shop Smart

      • Kecap manis is the one thing you cannot fake here, and it is worth the bottle. It keeps for a year in the pantry and this batch uses only a portion of it, so the real cost per bowl keeps falling.
      • Turkey breast is two thirds of the batch. Frozen roasts are the cheap form, and the clearance shelf after the holidays is where you should be buying turkey for the whole year.
      • Cook the rice the day before and refrigerate it. Cold day-old rice fries into separate grains instead of a sticky clump, and it costs you nothing but planning.
      • Fourteen eggs cost very little and they add a fried egg to every single container. That is the cheapest way to make a bowl feel like a restaurant plate.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice a day ahead if you can and chill it uncovered. Cold rice is the entire secret to fried rice that is not mush.
      3. Cut the turkey breast into small cubes and season them with salt and black pepper.
      4. Heat some of the vegetable oil in your largest skillet or wok over high heat and sear the turkey in batches until browned and cooked through to 165F. Set it aside.
      5. Add the rest of the oil, then the sliced onion, minced garlic, and chopped jalapeno, and stir-fry 3 minutes until fragrant and just softening.
      6. Add the cold rice and press it into the pan, letting it sit undisturbed for a minute at a time so it toasts, then tossing. Do this for about 5 minutes.
      7. Pour in the sweet soy sauce, return the turkey, and toss for 2 minutes until every grain is dark and coated. Taste for salt.
      8. Fry the eggs separately, one per container, and slice the cucumber into rounds while the rice cools enough to portion.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of fried rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Top each with a fried egg, a few cucumber rounds, and the sliced green onions. Every bowl lands at 560 calories and 39 grams of protein.
