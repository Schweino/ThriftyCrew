      Makes 14 servings · ~571 cal · 33g protein · 49g carbs · 27g fat · ~$2.69 per serving.

      Minestrone was never a recipe so much as a strategy: whatever vegetables you have, plus beans, plus a little pasta, in a good broth. This version leans on hot Italian sausage for depth, chickpeas and artichoke hearts for body, and green beans and tomatoes for the rest. It carries 33 grams of protein for about $2.69 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • Artichoke hearts are the surprise expense. Canned in water is cheaper than marinated in oil and works better in a soup, since you are not paying for oil you drain away.
      • Canned diced tomatoes go on sale constantly. This recipe uses a lot of them. Buy a case when the price drops and you will use every can within a month.
      • Fresh basil is the smallest quantity for the biggest price. Dried oregano is already carrying the herb weight. Treat the basil as optional if the board shows it high.
      • Chickpeas and green beans are the cheapest volume in the pot. That is exactly how minestrone was designed to work, and it is why this feeds fourteen.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the celery and onion, mince the garlic, drain and quarter the artichoke hearts, and drain and rinse the chickpeas.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large pot and brown the hot Italian sausage in crumbles until it takes on color.
      4. Add the celery and onion and cook until they soften, then add the garlic and the dried oregano for a minute.
      5. Pour in the diced tomatoes and the beef broth, season with salt and black pepper, and bring it up to a simmer.
      6. Add the green beans, the artichoke hearts and the chickpeas and simmer until the vegetables are tender.
      7. Stir in the pasta shells and cook only until they are just tender. Overcooked pasta turns to paste after a night in the fridge, and this soup is meant to be reheated.
      8. Fold in the chopped fresh basil off the heat and stir the parmesan through right before you portion.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount into each container, stirring the pot so the sausage and beans stay evenly spread. Every bowl lands near 571 calories and 33 grams of protein.
