      Makes 14 servings · ~563 cal · 33g protein · 81g carbs · 11g fat · ~$2.56 per serving.

      Do not skip past this one because of the word pumpkin. Canned pumpkin does not make chili taste like pie. It makes it thick, a little sweet in the background, and velvety in a way that usually takes an hour of reducing. Add a spoon of cocoa powder and a pinch of cinnamon, the same move behind a good mole, and you get a chili with more depth than the standard chili powder and beans version. Nobody guesses the pumpkin. Everybody asks what is in it.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy plain canned pumpkin, not pie filling. The pie filling is already sugared and spiced and it costs more. You want the one with a single ingredient on the label.
      • Cocoa powder is a savory ingredient too. A container lasts years and a spoonful of it is what separates a good chili from a flat one. Same idea as a mole.
      • Stock up on canned pumpkin in November. It is the one month of the year the price bottoms out, and the cans are good for a very long time.
      • Frozen chopped spinach disappears into chili. It costs less than fresh, it will not go bad on you, and nobody eating it will notice it is in there.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside covered.
      3. Brown the ground turkey in your big pot over medium-high heat, breaking it up until it is cooked through.
      4. Add the diced onion and chopped green bell peppers and cook until soft, then stir in the garlic for the last minute.
      5. Stir in the chili powder, cocoa powder, ground cinnamon, ground cumin, cayenne, salt and black pepper and toast them against the hot pan for a minute.
      6. Add the canned pumpkin puree, the tomato paste, the diced tomatoes with their juice and the chicken broth, and stir until the pumpkin is fully blended into the base.
      7. Add the drained cannellini beans, simmer 30 minutes, then stir in the thawed chopped spinach at the end. Keep the sour cream and cilantro for topping at reheat.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice into your 14 containers, then set the chili pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight over the rice. Every serving lands near 563 calories with 33 grams of protein once the sour cream and cilantro go on top.
