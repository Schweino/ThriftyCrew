      Makes 14 servings · ~683 cal · 47g protein · 52g carbs · 30g fat · ~$1.89 per serving.

      Thit kho is the dish Vietnamese families make for Tet and then eat happily for the next week, because it holds beautifully and the flavor deepens every day. Pork braised in a dark caramel with fish sauce, with hard boiled eggs sitting in the pot soaking it all up. Going all pork shoulder instead of belly pushes it to 47 grams of protein for about $1.89 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork shoulder instead of belly is the whole trick. The traditional build is half belly, which is mostly fat at a premium price. Shoulder braises just as tender and the protein number jumps.
      • Eggs are how you get protein for almost nothing. They braise right in the pot, take on the color and the flavor, and cost a fraction of what more meat would.
      • Soda stands in for coconut soda. The traditional recipe uses a coconut soda that is hard to find outside an Asian market. Plain soda with the sugar in this recipe does the same job of building the caramel.
      • Fish sauce is not optional and one bottle lasts a year. There is no substitute for it here. Buy a decent bottle once and it will carry many batches of this and a dozen other recipes.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork shoulder into large chunks and slice the onion. Hard boil the eggs, cool them and peel them.
      3. Melt the sugar in a heavy pot over medium heat without stirring and let it turn a deep amber caramel. Watch it closely, because caramel goes from amber to burnt in about ten seconds.
      4. Add the pork chunks to the caramel and stir to coat every piece, then add the sliced onion.
      5. Pour in the fish sauce and the soda, which will hiss and seize the caramel at first. Keep stirring and it will loosen back out.
      6. Add enough water to nearly cover the pork, bring it to a simmer, and skim off whatever foam rises in the first few minutes.
      7. Cover and braise very low until the pork is tender and the liquid has reduced into a glossy sauce, stirring occasionally.
      8. Add the peeled eggs for the last stretch so they take on color and flavor, season with salt and black pepper, and cook the rice separately.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount over rice in each container, splitting the eggs evenly between them. Every bowl lands near 683 calories with a serious 47 grams of protein.
