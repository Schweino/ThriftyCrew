      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 31g protein · 76g carbs · 13g fat · ~$1.66 per serving.

      This is the humble Cantonese rice plate: seasoned minced meat in a glossy brown gravy, spooned over rice with peas stirred through. The gravy is the whole reason it belongs in a meal prep rotation, because sauce is what keeps lean turkey from turning into cardboard by Thursday.       Shop Smart

      • Oyster sauce and soy sauce carry all the flavor here for very little, and both bottles keep for months. Once your pantry has them, dinners like this stop requiring a shopping trip.
      • Frozen peas cost almost nothing for this batch and add color, sweetness, and a little fiber. They are the most useful bag in your freezer and they never spoil on you.
      • Cornstarch is what turns broth into gravy, and it costs almost nothing for the whole batch. It is the cheapest ingredient in the store that changes a dish this much.
      • The turkey is well over half the cost, so buy the 4 pound pack. Everything else on this list is measured in cents.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide skillet over medium-high, add the ground turkey, and brown it, breaking it into fine crumbles, until it is cooked through to 165F.
      3. Add the diced onion and minced garlic and cook 4 minutes until the onion softens and goes translucent.
      4. Pour in the chicken broth, oyster sauce, soy sauce, and black pepper, and bring it to a simmer for 5 minutes so the flavors settle in together.
      5. Whisk the cornstarch with a half cup of cold water until smooth. Cold water, always, since cornstarch in hot liquid turns into lumps you cannot whisk out.
      6. Stir the slurry into the simmering pan and cook 2 minutes until the sauce turns glossy and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
      7. Add the frozen peas and the sesame oil and cook 2 minutes more, just until the peas are hot and still bright green.
      8. Cook the rice, move it into your weighed pot, and keep the gravy in its own pan for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then ladle the gravy evenly over all 14, stirring between scoops so the meat does not sink and leave the last few containers swimming in plain sauce. Each bowl lands at 567 calories and 31 grams of protein.
