      Shop Smart

      • Buying sausage in bulk chubs beats links. You are removing the casings anyway to roll meatballs, so pay for the loose ground version and skip the casing premium.
      • Kale is the biggest produce line here. Bunches on sale are the cheapest form. If the board shows kale high, the bagged chopped kale or frozen kale both roast fine.
      • Cherry tomatoes cost more than regular tomatoes for a reason. They blister and pop, which is the point. If you need to trim the budget, halved regular tomatoes get you most of the way.
      • Bread crumbs, milk and eggs bind the whole batch for almost nothing. That is the cheapest structural work in any recipe on this site, and it stretches the sausage further.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 400F and line two or three large sheet pans.
      3. Soak the bread crumbs in the milk for a few minutes, then mix them with the hot Italian sausage, eggs, smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, black pepper and red pepper flakes. Mix gently, because packed meatballs turn out dense.
      4. Roll even meatballs and set them on the pans along with the potato chunks tossed in some of the olive oil.
      5. Roast until the meatballs brown and the potatoes go crisp at the corners.
      6. Add the cherry tomatoes to the pans and roast a few more minutes until they blister and split.
      7. Whisk the red wine vinegar, dijon mustard, brown sugar and remaining olive oil into a dressing while the pans are in the oven.
      8. Pull the pans, scatter the torn kale over the hot food so it wilts from the residual heat, pour the dressing over everything, and toss it all together in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, counting the meatballs so nobody gets shorted. Every bowl lands near 692 calories and 32 grams of protein.
