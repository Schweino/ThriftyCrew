      Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 37g protein · 76g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.15 per serving.

      Fideo is thin noodle, and sopa de fideo is the tomato noodle dish that Mexican kids come home to. The move that makes it work is toasting the dry noodles in oil until they turn the color of a brown paper bag before any liquid goes near them. That toasting is where all the flavor comes from. Cooked thin it is a starter soup. Cooked thick, with ground beef in it, it becomes dinner, and that is the version we built. Five ingredients plus your spice rack.

      Shop Smart

      • Regular spaghetti broken into short pieces is fideo. The little bags of pre-cut fideo cost more per ounce for a noodle you can make with your hands in ten seconds.
      • The beef is the entire receipt. Five ingredients means nowhere to hide, so buy ground beef when it is marked down and freeze it in batch portions.
      • Canned tomatoes and tomato sauce cost almost nothing and do everything. Stock up when they hit the low price, because they keep for years.
      • Toast the noodles. It is free. That one step is the whole difference between this and boiled pasta with tomato sauce on it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Break the spaghetti into pieces roughly an inch long.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large wide pot and toast the broken pasta over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it turns golden brown. Watch it closely, because it goes from perfect to burnt in about thirty seconds.
      4. Scoop the toasted pasta out and set it aside.
      5. Add the ground beef to the same pot and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      6. Stir in the onion powder, garlic powder, dried oregano, ground cumin, salt, and black pepper and cook for a minute.
      7. Add the diced tomatoes, tomato sauce, and chicken broth and bring it to a boil.
      8. Return the toasted pasta, lower the heat, and simmer uncovered until the noodles are tender and the liquid has cooked down to a thick sauce rather than a soup.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Weigh it into the containers, because toasted fideo settles unevenly and scoops lie to you. Every serving comes in at 570 calories with 37 grams of protein. It thickens overnight, so add a splash of water when you reheat.
