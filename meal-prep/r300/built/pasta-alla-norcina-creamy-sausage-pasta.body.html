      Makes 14 servings · ~879 cal · 33g protein · 66g carbs · 54g fat · ~$1.56 per serving.

      Norcia is a small town in Umbria that Italy trusts so completely with pork that the word for a butcher shop there became the name of this pasta. The real version is sausage crumbled out of its casing, cooked in cream until the sauce clings, and finished with parmesan. No tomato, nothing fancy, and it is one of the most satisfying things you can put in a container. Restaurants serve it in a portion that could sink a boat. We cut the cream back to something a human can eat five nights in a row.

      Shop Smart

      • Hot Italian sausage in the rope or the tray is the same meat. Squeeze it out of the casing and it becomes crumbles. You are paying for seasoning work someone else already did, and it is still cheaper than buying the spices for one batch.
      • Heavy cream is the second biggest line on this receipt. It is also the one thing you cannot fake here. Half and half breaks under heat and gives you a thin, sad sauce.
      • Buy parmesan as a wedge or a tub, not a shaker. The shelf-stable green can is mostly filler, and this sauce leans on real cheese to thicken.
      • Store-brand penne is identical to the name brand. Pasta is flour and water shaped by a machine, and the machines are the same ones.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the penne in well salted water to just under al dente, since it will finish in the sauce. Save a couple cups of the pasta water before draining.
      3. Squeeze the sausage out of its casings and break it into rough crumbles.
      4. Heat the olive oil in a wide pot and cook the sausage over medium-high heat until browned in spots, not just gray.
      5. Add the finely diced yellow onion and cook until soft and translucent.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, scrape the bottom of the pot clean, and let it reduce by about half.
      7. Lower the heat, stir in the heavy cream, and simmer gently until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. Do not let it boil hard or it will separate.
      8. Add the drained penne and the parmesan, toss until every piece is coated, loosen with pasta water if needed, and season with salt and plenty of black pepper.

      Portion It

      Put the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Scoop that exact weight into each container so the sauce gets shared evenly instead of pooling in the last three. Every bowl comes in at 879 calories with 33 grams of protein. Reheat with a splash of milk or water and stir, and the cream comes right back together.
