      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 42g protein · 62g carbs · 17g fat · ~$3.29 per serving.

      Sanieh means the tray, and that tells you everything about how this Lebanese dish is built. Spiced ground beef gets pressed flat across the bottom, sliced potatoes go over it, tomato and pepper go on top, and a thin tomato sauce is poured over the whole thing before it goes in the oven. Allspice and cinnamon in the meat are what make it read as Lebanese rather than as meatloaf. It bakes in one tray, cuts into squares, and portions by weight like it was designed for this.

      Shop Smart

      • The ground beef is the whole receipt here. Everything else on this list is potatoes, onions, and canned tomatoes. Buy beef when it is marked down and freeze it in batch-sized portions, because that is the only lever that moves this number.
      • Allspice and cinnamon are the flavor and they cost pennies per batch. Two jars you probably already own are what separate this from a generic beef and potato bake.
      • Slice the potatoes thin and even. Uneven slices mean some are raw while others are mush, and you end up baking the tray far longer than it needs, which dries out the beef.
      • Buy the big potato bag and the onion bag. Loose produce carries a convenience markup on the two cheapest vegetables in the store.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the ground beef with finely diced onion, garlic, eggs, bread crumbs, ground allspice, ground cinnamon, salt, and black pepper until it just comes together.
      3. Press the beef mixture in an even layer across the bottom of a large greased baking tray and bake it alone at 400 degrees until it firms up and releases its fat.
      4. Pour off the rendered fat while the beef par-bakes, then peel and slice the potatoes into even rounds.
      5. Toss the potato rounds with the vegetable oil, salt, and black pepper and layer them over the beef.
      6. Scatter sliced green bell peppers over the potatoes.
      7. Whisk the diced tomatoes and tomato paste with a little water into a loose sauce and pour it evenly across the tray.
      8. Cover with foil and bake until the potatoes are completely tender, then uncover and bake until the top browns and the sauce tightens.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole tray, subtract the empty tray weight, and divide by 14. Cut to that weight rather than eyeballing squares, since the potato layer is never as even as it looks from above. Every serving comes in at 562 calories with 42 grams of protein. Let it rest twenty minutes before cutting or the layers slide apart.
