      Makes 14 servings · ~645 cal · 43g protein · 50g carbs · 29g fat · ~$1.78 per serving.

      Real chicken cordon bleu means pounding cutlets flat, rolling them around ham and cheese, breading each one, and praying nothing leaks in the oven. Nobody is doing that fourteen times on a Sunday. This casserole takes the same four flavors, chicken, ham, swiss, and dijon, and layers them in one dish with a cracker topping. Each serving carries 645 calories and 43 grams of protein for about $1.78 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Diced ham is the second biggest line and it earns its place. It brings protein and a cured, salty depth that chicken alone cannot, and it costs less per pound than most cuts of meat in the case.
      • Swiss cheese is what makes this cordon bleu rather than generic cheesy chicken. Buy the block and slice or shred it yourself, since pre-shredded swiss is usually a poor value and does not melt as well.
      • Cream of chicken soup is the binder, and it goes on sale constantly. Grab several cans when it does, because it keeps for years.
      • Butter crackers crushed with melted butter make the topping. A sleeve costs very little and gives you crunch that bread crumbs cannot match, which matters more than it sounds like it should in a soft casserole.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 350F and cook the rice, since it goes into the dish rather than under it.
      3. Cook the chicken breast and cut it into bite sized pieces.
      4. In a large bowl, stir together the cream of chicken soup, sour cream, dijon mustard, onion powder, garlic powder, salt, and pepper into a smooth sauce. The dijon is not optional. It is what keeps a rich casserole from tasting flat.
      5. Fold in the cooked rice, chicken, and diced ham until everything is coated.
      6. Spread half the mixture into a large baking dish, layer the swiss cheese across it, then add the rest of the mixture on top.
      7. Crush the butter crackers, toss them with the melted butter, and scatter them over the surface in an even layer.
      8. Bake 30 to 35 minutes until it bubbles around the edges and the topping is golden, then rest 10 minutes before cutting.

      Portion It

      Score the bake into 14 even squares and lift one into each container. Weigh a couple of them to confirm your squares are honest, since the corners always look bigger than they are. Every portion lands at 645 calories. Reheat uncovered so the cracker topping crisps instead of going soft.
