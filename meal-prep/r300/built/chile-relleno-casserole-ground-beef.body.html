      Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 36g protein · 62g carbs · 18g fat · ~$1.91 per serving.

      A real chile relleno is a roasted poblano, stuffed by hand, dipped in whipped egg batter, and fried one at a time. It is delicious and it is a terrible idea for fourteen servings. The casserole version keeps everything that matters and throws out the part that does not scale. Roasted poblanos and seasoned beef layer into a dish, a cheddar and egg custard gets poured over the whole thing, and it bakes into something that puffs up golden and sets like a savory pudding. Same flavors, one dish, no standing at a fryer.

      Shop Smart

      • Poblanos are the second biggest line and there is no substitute. Buy them when they are cheap and roast a big batch at once. Roasted peppers freeze beautifully in bags.
      • Eggs and cheese push the protein up cheaply. That custard is doing real nutritional work here, which is why the beef amount can stay modest.
      • Shred cheddar off a block. Bagged shreds carry a starch coating that makes an egg custard grainy, and the block is cheaper per ounce anyway.
      • Roast the peppers under the broiler, not on a grill. Same char, no charcoal, and you can do a whole sheet pan at once while the beef browns.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Broil the poblano peppers on a sheet pan, turning once, until the skins blister and blacken, then steam them in a covered bowl for ten minutes and slip the skins off.
      3. Slit the peppers open, pull out the seeds, and lay them flat.
      4. Brown the ground beef with the diced yellow onion, salt, and black pepper until no pink remains, then drain the fat.
      5. Layer half the poblanos in a large greased baking dish, spread the beef over them, scatter on most of the cheddar cheese, and top with the remaining poblanos.
      6. Whisk the eggs with the milk, all-purpose flour, a dash of hot sauce, and salt until smooth with no lumps.
      7. Pour the custard evenly over the layers and let it settle for a few minutes so it works its way down.
      8. Bake at 350 degrees until the center is set and no longer jiggles, scatter the last of the cheddar on top, and bake a few minutes more until melted. Cook the rice separately to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the baking dish separately, subtracting the empty weight each time, and divide by 14. Cut the casserole to weight rather than into squares, because the pepper layers make it uneven. Every serving comes in at 566 calories with 36 grams of protein. Let it rest twenty minutes before cutting so the custard finishes firming up.
