      Makes 14 servings · ~560 cal · 32g protein · 74g carbs · 15g fat · ~$2.83 per serving.

      This is Iraqi home cooking at its most economical. Eight ingredients, one pot, and a stew that gets its body from spinach and chickpeas rather than from a pile of meat. The beef is there for flavor and richness, but the chickpeas are quietly doing a lot of the protein work, which is why this bowl lands where it does on both cost and nutrition. It is the kind of dish that exists in one form or another across the whole region, because it is what you make when you want dinner to be filling and you do not want to spend much.

      Shop Smart

      • Frozen spinach is the single biggest thing in this pot and one of the cheapest. Fresh spinach cooks down to almost nothing, so buying frozen is not a compromise here, it is the correct choice by a wide margin.
      • Canned chickpeas add real protein for very little money. If you want to push the price lower, dried chickpeas soaked overnight cost a fraction of the canned per serving.
      • Beef is a flavor ingredient in this recipe, not the bulk. That is the whole design, and it is why the price per bowl stays reasonable.
      • Tomato paste beats canned sauce for thickening. A small can of paste gives you more concentrated tomato per dollar than a larger can of sauce and it does not water down the stew.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the olive oil in a large pot and cook the diced yellow onion until softened and lightly golden.
      3. Add the ground beef, break it up, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it for a couple of minutes until it darkens and coats everything, which takes the tinny edge off.
      5. Thaw the frozen chopped spinach, squeeze out most of the water, and add it to the pot.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, bring to a simmer, and cook uncovered until the spinach has melted into the sauce and the stew thickens.
      7. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, add them, and simmer another ten minutes so they take on the sauce.
      8. Season with salt and black pepper to taste, then cook the rice according to the package to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the stew pot separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Even rice base, even ladle of stew, and make sure the chickpeas get spread around rather than settling in the last few containers. Every bowl comes in at 560 calories with 32 grams of protein.
