      Makes 14 servings · ~780 cal · 58g protein · 57g carbs · 33g fat · ~$5.63 per serving.

      Birria is the Jalisco braise that took over every taco menu in America, and the thing most people miss is that the chiles do the work, not the meat. Guajillo, ancho, and arbol get toasted and soaked, then blended into a sauce that chuck roast braises in for hours until it shreds into its own deep red consomme. Each bowl carries 58 grams of protein and 780 calories for about $5.63.

      Shop Smart

      • Dried guajillo and ancho chiles are far cheaper in the Latin foods aisle or at a Mexican grocery than in the little cellophane spice packets. Buy the bag, not the packet.
      • Stored in a sealed bag away from light, dried chiles last a year. One purchase covers this recipe several times over, which is why the per batch cost is lower than the shelf price suggests.
      • Chuck roast is the correct cut. Birria wants collagen, and that is precisely what makes the cheap roast cheap.
      • Toasting the chiles before soaking is free and it is the difference between a flat sauce and a deep one. Thirty seconds a side in a dry pan is all it takes.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Stem and seed the guajillo, ancho, and arbol chiles, then toast them in a dry pan about 30 seconds a side until they smell fragrant. Do not let them blacken or the sauce turns bitter.
      3. Cover the toasted chiles with hot water and soak 20 minutes until they are soft and pliable.
      4. Blend the drained chiles with the diced tomatoes, onion, garlic, white vinegar, cumin, oregano, cinnamon, and cloves until completely smooth. Strain it if you want a silkier consomme.
      5. Cut the chuck roast into large chunks, season with salt and black pepper, and sear them in the olive oil until browned.
      6. Pour the chile sauce and the beef broth over the beef, add the bay leaves, and bring it to a bare simmer.
      7. Cover and cook 3.5 to 4 hours on the lowest heat that keeps it moving, until the beef falls apart when pushed.
      8. Shred the beef and return it to the consomme, then taste and correct the salt and the vinegar.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of shredded beef and consomme into each of 14 containers, making sure every one gets its share of the liquid and not just the meat. Each bowl lands at 780 calories with 58 grams of protein.
