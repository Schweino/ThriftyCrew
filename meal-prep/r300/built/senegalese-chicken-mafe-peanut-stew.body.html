      Mafe is Senegal's groundnut stew, and it has been feeding large tables on small budgets for a very long time. Peanut butter melts into tomato and broth until the whole pot turns the color of terra cotta and thickens on its own, no flour and no cream involved. Chicken goes in early and comes out falling apart. Over rice, each bowl runs 586 calories and 36 grams of protein for about $1.42. Peanut butter as a dinner ingredient is one of the best deals in the store and almost nobody uses it that way.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the big jar of plain peanut butter and skip anything labeled natural or no-stir if you are counting pennies. This recipe uses less than a cup, so one jar covers several batches and the rest goes on toast.
      • Canned diced tomatoes are the volume in this pot and they are one of the few groceries that barely moved in price. Buy them by the case when they hit a sale, since they sit on a shelf for a year without complaining.
      • Boneless skinless thighs are the braising cut. They cost less per pound than breast and they will not turn stringy after an hour in a hot pot.
      • One jalapeno is the entire heat budget. Traditional mafe uses a scotch bonnet, so if your store carries habanero and you like the fire, one of those swaps in without changing the price much.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the chicken thighs with salt, black pepper and smoked paprika. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pot over medium-high and brown the chicken in batches, working in a single layer so it colors instead of steams. Set it aside.
      3. Drop the diced onion into the same pot and cook 5 to 6 minutes until soft and starting to catch, then add the garlic and the minced jalapeno and cook one more minute.
      4. Stir the peanut butter directly into the hot onions and let it toast for a minute. It will look wrong and seize up. That is exactly what it should do.
      5. Add the diced tomatoes and the chicken broth a little at a time, whisking as you go so the peanut butter loosens into a smooth sauce instead of clumping.
      6. Return the chicken and any juices to the pot, bring it to a simmer, then cover and hold it at a low bubble for 45 minutes to an hour, stirring now and then so the bottom does not scorch. Peanut butter sauces catch fast.
      7. While it simmers, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes.
      8. Pull the chicken, shred or chop it, and stir it back into the sauce with the cooked rice folded through so every bowl gets both.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Scoop the same number into all fourteen containers. Each one delivers 586 calories, and this one genuinely tastes better on day three than it did on day one.
