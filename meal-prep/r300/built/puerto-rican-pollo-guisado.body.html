      Pollo guisado is the stew every Puerto Rican grandmother makes a little differently and every one of them insists hers is correct. Chicken braised down with sofrito, sazon, olives, potatoes and carrots until the broth turns into gravy and the potatoes start to fall apart on purpose. This is a big, generous pot. Each bowl runs 577 calories and 51 grams of protein for about $2.50, and there is one line item driving that number that I want to talk about.

      Shop Smart

      • Jarred salsa is the most expensive thing in this recipe by far, well past what the potatoes, carrots and onions cost combined. Real sofrito is onion, bell pepper, garlic and cilantro blended together, it freezes in ice cube trays for months, and it costs a fraction of what the jar does.
      • Chicken thighs are the second big line and the right cut for a long braise. The eight pound club pack is the best per-pound buy in the case, and this recipe uses nearly all of it.
      • Potatoes and carrots are the cheapest volume you can put in a stew. Buy both in bags rather than loose and you will pay roughly half the per-pound price.
      • Sazon is coriander, cumin, garlic powder and paprika with annatto for color. Mixing your own from jars you already own beats buying the little packets, especially if you make this often.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the chicken thighs with the sazon seasoning, dried oregano, salt and black pepper. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pot over medium-high and brown them in batches until they have real color, then set them aside.
      3. Cook the diced onion in the same pot for 5 minutes, then add the garlic and cook one more minute, scraping the fond up off the bottom.
      4. Stir in the salsa and let it cook down for 3 to 4 minutes until it darkens and most of the water has left it. This is your sofrito step, and it needs the time.
      5. Add the tomato sauce, bay leaves and chicken broth, then return the chicken with all its juices and bring the pot up to a simmer.
      6. Cover and simmer 25 minutes, then add the cubed potato and sliced carrots and cook another 20 to 25 minutes uncovered, until the potatoes are tender and the broth has reduced into a proper gravy.
      7. Stir in the olives during the last 5 minutes so they warm through without going rubbery.
      8. Pull the bay leaves, break the chicken into large chunks with a spoon, and stir the whole pot so the potato starch thickens the gravy.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same scoop in every container. Each one carries 577 calories, and stews like this genuinely improve by day two once the gravy has had time to settle in.
