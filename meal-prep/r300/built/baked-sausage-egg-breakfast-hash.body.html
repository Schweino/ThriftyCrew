      Makes 14 servings · ~788 cal · 42g protein · 49g carbs · 46g fat · ~$1.84 per serving.

      Breakfast for dinner is an underrated financial strategy, because eggs and potatoes are two of the cheapest things in any store. Roasted potatoes, crumbled pork sausage, bacon, peppers and onion, with eggs cracked into wells and baked until the whites just set. It hits 42 grams of protein for about $1.84.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy eggs by the three dozen flat. This recipe uses a lot of them and the large pack is always cheaper per egg than three separate cartons.
      • Ground pork is cheaper than packaged breakfast sausage. Season it yourself with the smoked paprika, thyme, onion powder and garlic powder you already own and you get the same thing for less.
      • Half a pound of bacon is a seasoning, not a serving. You are after the rendered fat and the smoke. Buy the one pound package and use the rest for another recipe this week.
      • Red onions and red peppers can both go yellow and green. Nothing about this dish requires the pricier colors, and the savings show up immediately.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F. Dice the potatoes small, chop the red bell pepper and red onion, and chop the bacon.
      3. Toss the potatoes with the olive oil, smoked paprika, dried thyme, onion powder, garlic powder, salt and black pepper, then spread them across two large sheet pans and roast until they start to crisp.
      4. While they roast, cook the chopped bacon and the ground pork in a skillet, breaking the pork into crumbles, until both are browned and the fat has rendered.
      5. Add the pepper and onion to that skillet and cook until they soften in the sausage fat.
      6. Scatter the sausage mixture over the roasted potatoes and toss it together on the pans.
      7. Make wells in the hash with the back of a spoon and crack the eggs into them. A well is not optional here, because eggs cracked onto a flat surface run everywhere.
      8. Return the pans to the oven and bake just until the whites set and the yolks are still soft, then combine everything gently in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, distributing the eggs by count so every one is equal. Every bowl lands near 788 calories and 42 grams of protein.
