      Pork and beans out of a can is a side dish. Pork and beans made like this is dinner. Pork shoulder braised for three hours in orange juice, chipotles in adobo and tomato paste until it shreds, then white beans stirred in to soak up all that sauce. The orange juice is doing two jobs, tenderizing the meat and balancing the smoke. Every ingredient is something you can find in any grocery store in America.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the whole pork shoulder, not the pre-cubed stew meat. Cubing it yourself takes ten minutes with a sharp knife and saves real money per pound.
      • Chipotles in adobo are the cheapest big flavor in the store. A couple of small cans season this whole batch. Freeze what you do not use in a flat bag and break off pieces later.
      • Orange juice from a carton is fine here. You are cooking it down, not drinking it, so buy the store brand and use the rest for breakfast.
      • Canned cannellini beans go in at the end. Added late they hold their shape and absorb the sauce. Added early they turn to paste, and paste is not what you want.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cube the pork shoulder, pat it dry, season generously with salt and black pepper, and brown it hard in the olive oil in a heavy pot, in batches so it sears rather than steams.
      3. Cook the diced onion in the rendered fat until soft, then add the minced garlic, the ground cumin, ground coriander and ground allspice and stir for a minute.
      4. Stir in the tomato paste and let it darken against the bottom of the pot for two minutes. That step is what separates a deep sauce from a flat one.
      5. Add the chopped chipotles in adobo, the orange juice and the chicken broth, scrape the pot clean, and return the pork along with the bay leaves and dried oregano.
      6. Cover and braise gently for about 3 hours, until the pork shreds under a fork and the liquid has reduced into a thick sauce.
      7. Stir in the drained cannellini beans, simmer 15 more minutes so they take on the sauce, and pull out the bay leaves before portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight into each container. Every serving lands near 621 calories with 47 grams of protein. It thickens in the fridge, so a splash of broth on reheat brings the sauce right back.
