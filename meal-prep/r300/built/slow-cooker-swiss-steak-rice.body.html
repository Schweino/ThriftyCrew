      Makes 14 servings · ~575 cal · 34g protein · 52g carbs · 24g fat · ~$3.05 per serving.

      Swiss steak has nothing to do with Switzerland. The name comes from swissing, an old term for pounding tough meat until it gives up, and that is the whole idea here. Flour dredged beef braises in tomatoes, peppers, and onion until it cuts with a fork. No cream, no cheese, and almost no money. Each bowl carries 34 grams of protein and 575 calories for about $3.05.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned diced tomatoes are the sauce. Compare the store brand against the name brand by the price per ounce tag, because in a long braise the difference on the plate is nothing.
      • Green bell peppers cost less than red, yellow, or orange, and in a braise the color cooks out anyway. Buy green here without a second thought.
      • Flour is the thickener and you already own it. Dredging the beef before browning does double duty, giving you crust and gravy from one step.
      • Chuck roast is right for this, but so is any tough, cheap cut your store marks down. That is the point of the technique.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck roast into thick slabs or large cubes, season them with salt and black pepper, and dredge each piece in the flour.
      3. Brown the dredged beef in the vegetable oil in batches over medium high heat. Do not crowd the pan or the flour will go gummy instead of golden.
      4. Move the browned beef to the slow cooker and scatter the sliced onion, sliced bell peppers, and minced garlic over it.
      5. Pour in the diced tomatoes with their juice, the beef broth, and the Worcestershire sauce.
      6. Cover and cook on low for 7 hours, until the beef is fork tender and the sauce has thickened from the flour on the meat.
      7. Break the beef into large pieces in the sauce, then taste and correct the salt and pepper.
      8. Cook the rice separately and serve the beef and sauce over it.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of beef and sauce into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 575 calories with 34 grams of protein.
