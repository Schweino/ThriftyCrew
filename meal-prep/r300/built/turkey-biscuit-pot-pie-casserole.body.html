      Makes 14 servings · ~575 cal · 33g protein · 68g carbs · 18g fat · ~$1.72 per serving.

      A real pot pie means rolling pastry, chilling it, blind baking it, and then watching it collapse the moment you try to portion it. This version says no thank you and drops canned biscuits on top instead. Creamy turkey and peas underneath, golden biscuit lids above, and it slices into containers like it was designed to. Each serving runs 575 calories with 33 grams of protein for about $1.72.

      Ingredients

      • Turkey Breast (generic (USDA)): 2.75 lb (1225 g)

      • Cream of Mushroom Soup (Campbell's (USDA SR)): 1.8 cans (522 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Refrigerated biscuit cans go on multi-buy sales constantly and this recipe needs four of them. Watch for the deal, and remember the cans keep in the fridge for weeks past the date.
      • One frozen turkey breast roast covers this whole batch. Roasting it yourself costs a fraction of what deli turkey runs per pound, and the pan drippings make the filling better.
      • Cream of mushroom soup thickens the filling for pennies. Store brand is fine, and two cans is all you need since flour and milk do the rest of the work.
      • Frozen peas are the cheapest way to get vegetables into a pot pie, and the store-brand 12 oz bags beat canned peas on both price and texture.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the flour into about a cup of the cold milk in a jar until there are no lumps. Doing this cold is the whole trick to a smooth filling.
      3. Warm the rest of the milk with the condensed cream of mushroom soup in a large pot over medium heat, stirring until it is smooth.
      4. Pour in the flour slurry and cook 4 to 5 minutes, stirring, until the sauce thickens to gravy. Season with salt and plenty of black pepper.
      5. Fold in the diced turkey breast and the frozen peas, then pour the filling into a greased 9x13 pan. It should be hot going into the oven so the biscuits cook through from below.
      6. Open the biscuit cans and cut each biscuit in half, then arrange the halves cut side down across the top of the filling in even rows.
      7. Bake at 375F for 30 to 35 minutes, until the biscuits are deep golden on top and cooked through underneath. If they brown too fast, tent the pan with foil.
      8. Rest 10 minutes before portioning so the filling sets under the biscuits instead of running.

      Portion It

      Weigh the whole pan, subtract the empty pan weight, and divide by 14. Portion filling and biscuit together into each container so every serving gets its lid. Each one comes to 575 calories and 33 grams of protein.
