      Shop Smart

      • Buy the chuck roast whole and cube it yourself. Packaged stew meat costs more per pound and is often a mix of trim from cuts that cook at different speeds.
      • Carrots, celery, and onion are the cheapest flavor in any kitchen. Buy them as bunches and bags, chop what you need, and use the rest in next week's pot.
      • Beef broth is the liquid volume of this recipe, so it is worth buying base in a jar rather than boxes. It makes far more broth per dollar.
      • This is the freezer recipe of the whole beef library. Make the full batch, freeze half, and you have bought yourself a free week later.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck roast into large cubes, pat them completely dry, and season with salt and black pepper.
      3. Brown the beef in the olive oil in batches over high heat, giving each piece room. This is the step that makes the stew, so take your time.
      4. Cook the chopped onion, garlic, carrots, and celery in the same pot until they soften and pick up the browned bits.
      5. Stir in the flour and the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes so the flour loses its raw taste.
      6. Return the beef and pour in the beef broth and Worcestershire sauce. Add the bay leaves and thyme.
      7. Cover and simmer very low for 2 to 2.5 hours, until the beef is tender enough to break with a spoon.
      8. Add the cubed potatoes for the last 30 minutes, then pull the bay leaves and correct the salt and pepper.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight into each of 14 containers, stirring between scoops so the meat and vegetables split evenly. Each bowl lands at 566 calories with 44 grams of protein.
