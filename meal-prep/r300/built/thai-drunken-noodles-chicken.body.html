      Makes 14 servings · ~587 cal · 26g protein · 89g carbs · 14g fat · ~$3.03 per serving.

      Pad kee mao means drunken noodles, and depending on who you ask it is named for what you eat after a night out or what you want to drink while eating it. Either way it is wide rice noodles seared in a hot pan with chile, garlic, basil and a dark salty sauce, and it is the noodle dish nobody else on this site is making. Each bowl runs 587 calories and 26 grams of protein for about $3.03, which makes it the most expensive plate in this batch. There is a reason and I am going to name it.

      Shop Smart

      • Rice noodles are the single biggest line and the international aisle is where you win. The same wide rice noodles in the Asian grocery run well under half of what the branded boxes in a regular supermarket charge.
      • Fresh basil is the second biggest line. A living plant from produce costs about what a couple of clamshells do and keeps giving, which cuts this bowl down noticeably.
      • Oyster sauce and soy sauce are the whole sauce, and this recipe uses a cup of each, so buy the large bottles. The per-ounce price on the big bottle is usually half of the small one.
      • Do not soak the noodles the night before. Buy them dry, soak them the day you cook, and they will not turn into a single fused brick in the pan.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the dry rice noodles in warm water until they are pliable but still firm in the center, about 20 minutes. They finish cooking in the pan, so pulling them early is correct.
      3. Whisk the oyster sauce, soy sauce, fish sauce and sugar together with a splash of water. Have it ready at the stove; there is no time to mix once the pan is hot.
      4. Slice the chicken thighs thin. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet or wok as hot as it goes and sear the chicken in batches for 3 to 4 minutes until browned, then set it aside.
      5. Add the minced garlic, sliced jalapeno and sliced onion to the same pan and cook 1 minute until fragrant and just starting to soften.
      6. Add the drained noodles and the sauce and toss constantly for 3 to 4 minutes. Let the noodles sit still for a few seconds at a time so they char in spots, then move them again. Those charred edges are the whole point of the dish.
      7. Return the chicken and toss everything together until the sauce has coated every strand and there is no liquid pooling in the pan.
      8. Kill the heat and fold in the fresh basil and green onions so they wilt from the residual heat instead of cooking down to nothing.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, same number in each. Each one carries 587 calories, and rice noodles reheat best with a tablespoon of water and a lid, which steams them loose instead of drying them out.
