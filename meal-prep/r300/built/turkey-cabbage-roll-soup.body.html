      Makes 14 servings · ~572 cal · 26g protein · 93g carbs · 8g fat · ~$1.69 per serving.

      Golabki, holubtsi, sarma. Every corner of Eastern Europe has a name for meat and rice rolled up in a cabbage leaf and baked in tomato. Every one of them takes an afternoon. This soup takes the same ingredients, skips the rolling entirely, and lets the cabbage go tender right in the pot. Grandma would have been fine with it. She had things to do too.

      Shop Smart

      • Cabbage is the cheapest filling vegetable you can buy. One head is a couple dollars, keeps for weeks in the crisper, and cooks down into something that eats like a lot more than it cost.
      • Canned diced tomatoes carry this pot. Watch for the case sale, buy a stack, and you have the base for this and half a dozen other recipes on the site.
      • Ground turkey freezes flat. Buy the larger pack, press it into one pound sheets in freezer bags, and it thaws in an hour instead of overnight.
      • Cook the rice separately, always. Rice simmered in the soup keeps drinking broth in the fridge and turns day four into paste. Cooked apart, it stays rice.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside covered.
      3. Brown the ground turkey in olive oil in your big pot over medium-high heat, breaking it up, until it is cooked through.
      4. Add the diced onion, celery and carrots and cook until they start to soften, then stir in the garlic for the last minute.
      5. Core and chop the cabbage into ribbons and pile it into the pot. It will look like too much. Give it five minutes and it will not be.
      6. Pour in the diced tomatoes with their juice and the chicken broth, add the dried oregano, Italian seasoning and dried thyme, and bring it to a simmer.
      7. Simmer 25 to 30 minutes until the cabbage is fully tender, then season with salt and black pepper and stir in the chopped fresh basil off the heat.

      Portion It

      Scoop the cooked rice into your 14 containers first, then set the soup pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and ladle that exact weight over the rice in each one. Every serving lands near 572 calories with 26 grams of protein.
