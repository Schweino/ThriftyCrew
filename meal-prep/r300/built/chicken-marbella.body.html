      Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 41g protein · 66g carbs · 15g fat · ~$2.10 per serving.

      Chicken Marbella made its name in the early eighties as the dinner party dish that could be made entirely ahead, and forty years later that is exactly why it belongs in a meal prep rotation. Chicken marinates overnight in red wine vinegar, oregano, garlic, olives, and dried fruit, then roasts in the same pan with brown sugar on top. It is salty, sweet, and sour at once, and it gets better every day it sits. Each serving lands at 566 calories and 41 grams of protein for about $2.10 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Chicken breast is the biggest line here, so buy it in the family pack and portion it down yourself. The per pound difference between the family pack and the small tray is one of the most reliable savings in the store.
      • Capers and olives feel like luxury items but they are shelf stable, cheap by the jar, and used in modest amounts. One jar of each covers multiple batches and they do not go bad in the meantime.
      • Golden raisins are a fraction of the price of prunes and do the same job of sweet chewy contrast against the vinegar.
      • Red wine vinegar and dried oregano are the flavor of this dish and both are pantry items you already have. That is the whole reason a fancy sounding recipe lands where it does on price.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the marinade by combining the red wine vinegar, olive oil, garlic, oregano, bay leaves, olives, capers with a little of their brine, golden raisins, salt, and pepper.
      3. Cut the chicken breast into large pieces, put them in the marinade, and refrigerate overnight. Marbella genuinely does not work with a thirty minute marinade, so plan this one a day ahead.
      4. Heat the oven to 350F. Spread the chicken and all the marinade into a large baking dish in a single layer so everything roasts rather than steams.
      5. Sprinkle the brown sugar over the top and pour the chicken broth around the edges, not over the sugar.
      6. Roast 45 to 50 minutes, basting with the pan juices two or three times along the way. The basting is what builds the glaze, so it is worth opening the oven for.
      7. Cook the rice while the chicken roasts.
      8. Let the pan rest 10 minutes, then pull the bay leaves and slice the chicken. The juices in the bottom of the pan are the sauce, so do not leave any of them behind.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container, then weigh the chicken on top and spoon the pan juices, olives, and raisins over everything. Set the pan on the scale, subtract its empty weight, and divide by 14 if you want to be exact about it. Every portion lands at 566 calories, and this is one of the few dishes that genuinely peaks on day three.
