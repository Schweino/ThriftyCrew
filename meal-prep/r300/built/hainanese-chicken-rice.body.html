      Makes 14 servings · ~681 cal · 40g protein · 84g carbs · 19g fat · ~$1.74 per serving.

      Hainanese chicken rice is Singapore's national dish and it is a masterclass in wasting nothing. You poach the chicken gently, then cook the rice in the poaching broth with garlic and ginger, so the grains carry every bit of flavor the meat gave up. The ginger garlic sauce on the side is what people actually dream about. Each bowl lands at 681 calories and 40 grams of protein for about $1.74 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • The poaching liquid is the point. Never pour it down the drain, because it becomes the rice, and rice cooked in chicken broth instead of water is the entire difference between this dish and plain chicken and rice.
      • Buy rice in the largest bag your store carries. It is the cheapest calorie in the building and it keeps essentially forever in a sealed container.
      • Ginger and garlic are used in real volume here for the sauce, so buy a full hand of ginger rather than the shriveled little knob. It freezes well and grates fine straight from frozen.
      • This dish leans lighter on protein per calorie than most in the lineup, so weigh a slightly generous share of chicken into each container and go a little lighter on the rice if you are watching that ratio.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Bring a big pot of chicken broth to a bare simmer with a few smashed ginger slices and green onion tops. It should barely move, with a bubble breaking every second or two. A rolling boil makes chicken rubbery.
      3. Lower the chicken breasts in and poach gently until just cooked through, about 15 minutes depending on thickness. Pull them out and drop them straight into ice water for 5 minutes, which stops the cooking and firms up the texture.
      4. Skim the fat off the top of the broth and reserve every drop of the liquid.
      5. Melt the butter with some of the vegetable oil in a pot, fry the minced garlic and ginger until fragrant, add the rinsed rice, and toast it for two minutes.
      6. Pour in the reserved poaching broth, bring it to a boil, then cover and cook on low until the rice is done, about 18 minutes. Rest it covered for 10 minutes more.
      7. Make the sauces while the rice cooks. Blend the rest of the ginger and garlic with the remaining vegetable oil, salt, and lime juice for the ginger garlic sauce, and stir the soy sauce with the sugar for the dark sauce. Blend the jalapeno with a little of each for the chili sauce.
      8. Slice the chilled chicken thick across the grain.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container first, then weigh the sliced chicken on top so every portion gets the same amount of meat. Put the sauces in small side containers rather than pouring them over, since they are meant to be added at eating time and they will make the rice soggy if they sit on it all week.
