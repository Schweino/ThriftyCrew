      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 27g protein · 54g carbs · 26g fat · ~$1.28 per serving.

      Somebody figured out that the good part of an egg roll is the filling, and the wrapper is just an expensive way to deliver it. So we skipped the wrapper. Ground pork, a whole head of cabbage, carrots, and ginger hit one skillet, get a good soak of soy sauce, and go over rice. That is the entire operation. Cabbage and ground pork happen to be two of the cheapest ways to fill a container on our whole grocery board, which is why this one lands near the bottom of the price list without tasting like it.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy a whole head of cabbage, not the bagged slaw. The bag is convenience packaging on the cheapest vegetable in the store, and shredding a head takes about four minutes with a knife.
      • Ground pork beats ground beef here on both price and flavor. It has the fat to carry the soy and ginger, and it is consistently cheaper per pound.
      • Rice is why this bowl is filling and still cheap. A big bag runs through many batches, so it barely moves the per-serving number at all.
      • Carrots and green onions are pocket change. Buy the pound bag of carrots instead of the pre-shredded cup and you get several batches out of it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice according to the package and set it aside to steam.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wide pot over medium-high heat.
      4. Add the ground pork, break it apart, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      5. Stir in the garlic, ginger, and diced yellow onion and cook until the onion softens.
      6. Add the shredded cabbage and carrots and cook, stirring often, until the cabbage wilts down but still has some bite.
      7. Pour in the soy sauce, toss everything together, and season with salt and black pepper to taste.
      8. Combine the rice and the pork and cabbage mixture in your big pot, stir, and finish with the sliced green onions.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide the remainder by 14. Scoop that weight into each container and you are done. Every bowl lands near 564 calories with 27 grams of protein. Cabbage keeps releasing water as it sits, so leave the lids cracked until everything cools.
