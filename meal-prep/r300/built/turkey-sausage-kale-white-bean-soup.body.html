      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 28g protein · 72g carbs · 18g fat · ~$1.84 per serving.

      This is the brothy Italian country soup, the one with no cream and no potato hiding the truth. Smoked turkey sausage, garlic, kale and creamy cannellini beans in chicken broth, spooned over rice so it eats like a meal instead of a starter. The beans do the thickening for free, which is the kind of trick that keeps a pot honest and cheap at the same time.

      Ingredients

      • Smoked Turkey Sausage (Butterball): 4.25 lb (1984 g)

      • Cannellini Beans (generic): 2.4 cans (602 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Smoked turkey sausage is the whole line item. It comes in ropes, it freezes beautifully, and it is already cooked, so buy several when the price is good and pull one rope at a time.
      • Canned cannellini beans are the cheapest thickener in the store. Mash a scoop of them against the side of the pot and the broth goes creamy without a drop of dairy.
      • Buy kale by the bunch, not the clamshell. The bagged and boxed stuff costs more per usable leaf and wilts faster in the fridge.
      • The rice is a rounding error. A bag from the warehouse aisle covers this batch and many more, which is exactly why it is the carb of choice here.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice separately per the package and set it aside covered. It goes under the soup, not in the pot.
      3. Slice the smoked turkey sausage into coins and brown them in olive oil in your big pot over medium-high heat until the edges catch color.
      4. Drop the heat, add the minced garlic and the red pepper flakes, and stir for about a minute until it smells like an Italian kitchen.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth and scrape the browned bits off the bottom, then bring it to a simmer.
      6. Add the drained cannellini beans and mash a scoop of them against the side of the pot to thicken the broth.
      7. Stir in the kale and simmer until it goes tender and dark, about 10 minutes, then season with salt and black pepper.

      Portion It

      Scoop the cooked rice into your 14 containers first and spread it flat. Then set the full soup pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and ladle that exact weight over the rice in each one. Every serving lands near 564 calories with 28 grams of protein.
