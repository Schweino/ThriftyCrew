      Makes 14 servings · ~620 cal · 38g protein · 54g carbs · 25g fat · ~$1.59 per serving.

      Soboro don is the Japanese lunchbox classic: sweet-savory ground meat on one side of the rice, fluffy scrambled egg on the other, green onion down the middle. It is built for exactly what we do here, because both toppings cook in minutes, portion by weight, and reheat without turning sad. Each bowl comes in at 620 calories and 38 grams of protein for about $1.59.

      Shop Smart

      • Eggs are the second biggest line here and they are still the cheapest protein on it. Buy them by the 18 count or the flat, since the per-egg price drops every time the carton gets bigger.
      • Mirin looks like a specialty purchase and it is, but one bottle covers this batch several times over. Once it is in your pantry, an entire category of Japanese cooking opens up for very little per recipe.
      • Soy sauce is the cheapest flavor in Asian cooking. A cup of it costs almost nothing here. Buy the big bottle, it never goes bad, and it makes weeknight food taste finished.
      • One bunch of green onions covers the batch. Save the white root ends, stick them in a glass of water on the windowsill, and they grow back. Free groceries are the best kind.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the sauce first: soy sauce, mirin, sugar, chicken broth, and grated ginger, whisked together. Split it into two bowls, a larger share for the turkey and a smaller one for the eggs.
      3. Heat a little of the olive oil in a wide skillet, add the ground turkey, and break it apart into fine crumbles as it cooks. Fine is the goal, so keep chopping at it with your spoon.
      4. Pour the larger share of sauce over the turkey and simmer, stirring often, until the liquid cooks down and glazes the meat and it has reached 165F. Move it to a bowl.
      5. Wipe the skillet, add the rest of the oil, and beat the eggs with the smaller share of sauce.
      6. Cook the eggs over medium-low, stirring constantly with chopsticks or a fork, until they set into small soft curds. Pull them the moment they stop looking wet, since they will keep cooking in the pan.
      7. Cook the rice and move it into your weighed pot.
      8. Slice the green onions thin and keep them separate for topping.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then spoon the turkey down one side and the egg down the other, split evenly across all 14, and scatter the green onions over the top. Each bowl lands at 620 calories and 38 grams of protein.
