      Makes 14 servings · ~706 cal · 42g protein · 72g carbs · 28g fat · ~$3.02 per serving.

      Moussaka is the Greek answer to lasagna, except the layers are roasted eggplant instead of pasta and the top is a thick bechamel that puffs and browns in the oven. It bakes in one pan and cuts into clean squares, which is exactly what you want when you are portioning out a week. Each square lands at 706 calories and 42 grams of protein for about $3.02 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground beef is the biggest line on the list, and it is the one to shop on sale. The eggplant is second, and eggplant prices swing hard by season, so a summer batch costs noticeably less than a January one.
      • Crushed tomatoes and tomato paste do more work per dollar here than any fresh tomato could. The sauce simmers long enough that canned is the better ingredient, not just the cheaper one.
      • The bechamel is flour, butter, and milk, which is the cheapest sauce in cooking. Parmesan and eggs give it the body and the color, and both are used in small amounts, so buy the packages and use the rest during the week.
      • Bread crumbs on the bottom of the pan are not decoration. They soak up the liquid the eggplant gives off and keep the bottom layer from turning to soup, and a canister of them lasts through many batches.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the eggplant into thick rounds, salt them, and let them sweat in a colander for 30 minutes. Pat them dry, brush with olive oil, and roast at 425F until soft and golden, about 25 minutes. This step is what keeps moussaka from turning watery.
      3. While the eggplant roasts, cook the onion in olive oil until soft, add the garlic, then brown the ground beef and break it up as it cooks.
      4. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, beef broth, sugar, cinnamon, oregano, bay leaves, salt, and pepper. Simmer until it thickens and most of the liquid is gone, about 30 minutes. A wet meat sauce makes a sloppy bake.
      5. Make the bechamel. Melt the butter, whisk in the flour and cook it for two minutes, then add the milk slowly while whisking until it thickens. Off the heat, stir in the parmesan and nutmeg, then temper in the beaten eggs so they do not scramble.
      6. Scatter bread crumbs across the bottom of a large baking dish, then layer roasted eggplant, meat sauce, more eggplant, and pour the bechamel over the top so it reaches every corner.
      7. Bake at 350F for 45 minutes, until the top is set and browned in patches. Let it rest at least 30 minutes before cutting. Cut it hot and the layers slide apart.
      8. Cook the rice while the bake rests and put a scoop in the bottom of each container to round the meal out.

      Portion It

      Weigh the finished bake in its dish, subtract the empty dish weight, and divide by 14 to get your target. Easier still, score the top into 14 even squares before you cut, then check a couple of them on the scale to confirm you are close. Either way each portion comes in at 706 calories and holds its shape in the container all week.
