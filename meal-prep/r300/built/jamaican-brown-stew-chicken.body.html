      Makes 14 servings · ~579 cal · 35g protein · 84g carbs · 9g fat · ~$1.08 per serving.

      Brown stew chicken is the other side of Jamaican cooking, the one that does not involve fire and allspice. You burn a little sugar in the pot until it goes dark, brown the chicken in it, then build a gravy around thyme, scallion, ketchup and bell pepper. Sweet, savory and deeply brown, which is exactly where the name comes from. Over rice, each bowl runs 579 calories and 35 grams of protein for about $1.08.

      Shop Smart

      • Chicken breast is by far the biggest line in this recipe, so this is the one place to be picky. Watch for the sale cycle on the big club packs, and know that boneless thighs would do this braise better and cheaper if you are willing to make the swap.
      • You do not need bottled browning sauce. It is burnt sugar and water, and this recipe already has brown sugar in it, so caramelizing a spoonful in the pot gets you the same color for free.
      • Ketchup and worcestershire are the backbone of the gravy and both are pantry items that cost pennies per batch. Store brand ketchup performs identically once it is cooked into a sauce.
      • Green bell peppers run cheaper than red or yellow almost every week of the year, and green is what this dish traditionally uses, so there is no tradeoff to make.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken into large pieces and season it with the black pepper, dried thyme, paprika, salt, grated ginger, minced garlic and half the sliced green onions. Let it sit at least 30 minutes.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium-high, sprinkle in the brown sugar, and let it melt and darken to the color of strong coffee. Do not walk away; the window between browned and burnt is about twenty seconds.
      4. Add the chicken in batches and turn it in the dark sugar until every piece is coated and seared. Set it aside as it browns.
      5. Cook the diced onion and sliced green bell pepper in the same pot for 5 minutes, scraping the bottom to lift the caramel.
      6. Stir in the ketchup, worcestershire sauce and hot sauce, pour in the chicken broth, and bring it to a simmer.
      7. Return the chicken with all its juices, cover, and simmer 30 to 35 minutes until the gravy thickens and the chicken reads 165F. Do not boil it hard; breast meat will tighten up on you.
      8. While it simmers, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes, then fold the rice through the stew with the rest of the green onions.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Same number in every container. Each one lands at 579 calories, and the gravy is what keeps the meat from drying out through a week of microwaving.
