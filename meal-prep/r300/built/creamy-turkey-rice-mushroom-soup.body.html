      Makes 14 servings · ~698 cal · 36g protein · 50g carbs · 37g fat · ~$2.56 per serving.

      Bacon, butter and mushrooms build the base. Wild rice gives it that nutty chew, turkey breast gives it the muscle, and a splash of cream at the end pulls it all together. This is a cold weather soup that eats like a full plate of food, and a squeeze of lemon at the finish keeps the whole rich thing from sitting too heavy.

      Shop Smart

      • Wild rice is the expensive ingredient, not the turkey. Check the bulk bins and the warehouse club before the boxed brands. If it is out of reach that week, a long grain brown rice will carry the soup at a fraction of the price.
      • Buy whole white mushrooms and slice them yourself. Pre-sliced costs more per ounce and goes slimy days sooner.
      • Bacon is a seasoning here, not a main. Buy a pound, use what the recipe calls for, and freeze the rest in flat portions for the next few batches.
      • The flour and butter roux replaces store-bought thickener. Pennies of flour do the job that a can of condensed soup charges you a couple dollars for.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the wild rice separately per the package until tender and set it aside. It takes longer than white rice, so start it first.
      3. Chop the bacon and render it in your big pot with the olive oil until crisp, then lift the bacon out and leave the fat behind.
      4. Add the butter, then the diced onion, celery and carrots, and cook until soft. Add the sliced mushrooms and cook until they give up their water and start to brown.
      5. Stir in the garlic, the dried thyme and the Italian seasoning, then sprinkle the flour over everything and stir for two minutes to cook out the raw taste.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth slowly while stirring so the roux does not lump, then simmer until it thickens.
      7. Add the cooked wild rice, the turkey breast and the heavy cream, warm it through without boiling, then finish with the lemon juice, the reserved bacon, salt and black pepper.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide by 14 and ladle that exact weight into each container. Every bowl lands near 698 calories with 36 grams of protein, so this one is a serious dinner. It thickens overnight, so thin it with a splash of broth when you reheat.
