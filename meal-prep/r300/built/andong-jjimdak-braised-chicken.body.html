      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 50g protein · 67g carbs · 10g fat · ~$1.91 per serving.

      Andong jjimdak comes from a market town in Korea and it is the quiet cousin to the spicy chicken stews. No chili paste here. The flavor is dark soy, garlic, and a fair amount of sweetness, with Korean glass noodles (dangmyeon) dropped in at the end to soak up everything left in the pot. Those noodles are the best part and everyone at the table knows it. Each bowl runs 562 calories and 50 grams of protein for about $1.91 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Korean glass noodles (dangmyeon) are the biggest single line on the list and there is no substitute that behaves the same way. They are sweet potato starch, they stay springy instead of going soft, and one box does the whole batch.
      • Boneless thighs cost far less than breast and are the only cut that makes sense for a braise this long. They stay juicy where breast would go stringy.
      • Honey and brown sugar together give jjimdak its glaze. Buy honey in the large jug rather than the little bear, since the price per ounce is dramatically better and honey keeps forever.
      • Potatoes and carrots in bulk bags cost almost nothing and add real volume. The potato starch also helps thicken the braising liquid so it coats the noodles instead of pooling.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the Korean glass noodles (dangmyeon) in cold water for 30 minutes while everything else cooks. Do not boil them yet.
      3. Cut the chicken thighs into large pieces and blanch them in boiling water for two minutes, then drain and rinse. This gives you a clean braising liquid rather than a murky one.
      4. Whisk the soy sauce, oyster sauce, brown sugar, honey, garlic, ginger, black pepper, and a spoonful of cocoa powder together. The cocoa is what gives jjimdak its dark color without turning it bitter.
      5. Put the chicken in a wide pot with the sauce and enough water to come halfway up, add the dried arbol chiles, and simmer covered for 20 minutes.
      6. Add the potatoes, carrots, and onion in big chunks and cook another 20 minutes until they are tender and the liquid has reduced by about a third.
      7. Drain the soaked noodles, add them to the pot with the sliced jalapeno, and cook 5 more minutes until they turn clear and springy. They will drink up a lot of the sauce, which is the point.
      8. Taste for salt and sweetness, then pull it off the heat before the noodles go soft.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Use tongs to get an even share of noodles into each container, because they tangle and one scoop can easily take double. Every portion lands at 562 calories.
