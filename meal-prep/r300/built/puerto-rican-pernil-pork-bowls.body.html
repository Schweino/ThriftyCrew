      Makes 14 servings · ~640 cal · 44g protein · 43g carbs · 30g fat · ~$2.03 per serving.

      Pernil is the pork shoulder that shows up at every Puerto Rican Christmas, every graduation, every family gathering worth the name. It gets stabbed all over, packed with a garlic and oregano wet rub, left overnight, then roasted low and slow until the meat pulls apart with a fork. Serve it over rice and you have the cheapest cut of pork on the board turned into something people fight over the leftovers of.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork shoulder is the cheapest good protein in the store. Look for it labeled Boston butt or picnic shoulder. Bone-in usually costs less per pound and the bone adds flavor while it roasts.
      • Buy garlic by the head. This rub uses real garlic, mashed with oregano and salt, and jarred minced garlic will not give you the same punch for the money.
      • Marinate overnight and the seasoning cost drops. Given 12 to 24 hours, a small amount of garlic, vinegar, lime and dried oregano flavors the entire roast. Rushed, you would need three times as much.
      • One bag of rice covers this batch and many more. It is the reason a dish this satisfying can land where it does on the per serving math.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mash the garlic with salt, dried oregano, ground cumin, onion powder, chili powder, black pepper and a little sugar into a paste, then loosen it with the olive oil, apple cider vinegar and lime juice.
      3. Cut deep slits all over the pork shoulder and force the paste into every one of them, then rub whatever is left across the whole surface. Cover and refrigerate at least overnight.
      4. Lay a bed of thick sliced onion in a roasting pan, set the pork on top fat side up, and roast covered at 300F for about 4 hours.
      5. Uncover, raise the oven to 425F, and roast 20 to 30 minutes more until the outside is dark and crisp.
      6. Rest the pork 20 minutes, then pull it apart with two forks and toss the meat through the pan juices and the softened onions.
      7. Cook the rice per the package while the pork rests, so it is hot when you assemble.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice across your 14 containers, then set your mixing pot of pulled pork on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and lay that exact weight over the rice in each one. Every bowl lands near 640 calories with 44 grams of protein.
