      Makes 14 servings · ~606 cal · 34g protein · 60g carbs · 27g fat · ~$2.13 per serving.

      Gai pad med mamuang is Thai cashew chicken, and the thing that makes it different from every Chinese cashew dish is the seasoning: fish sauce instead of just soy, and a lot more of it. Peppers and onion stay crunchy, the cashews get toasted first so they stay crisp instead of going soft, and the sauce is thin and salty rather than thick and gloppy. Each bowl comes in at 606 calories and 34 grams of protein for about $2.13.

      Shop Smart

      • Cashews are the expensive part. Buy them from the bulk bin or in a large bag rather than in the small cans on the snack aisle, where you pay a premium for the packaging and nothing else.
      • Toast the cashews in a dry pan before they go in. It costs nothing, takes three minutes, and doubles how much they taste like cashews, which matters when they are the priciest thing in the pan.
      • Green bell peppers cost less than red almost every week. This recipe uses both, so if the red ones are priced like jewelry that week, buy two green and nobody will notice.
      • Fish sauce is the ingredient that makes this Thai instead of generic, and one bottle costs a few dollars and lasts a year in the pantry. Do not substitute more soy sauce for it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Toast the cashews in a dry skillet over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes, shaking the pan, until they smell nutty and go a shade darker. Tip them onto a plate immediately so they stop cooking.
      3. Whisk the soy sauce, fish sauce and sugar together with a splash of water and set the sauce next to the stove.
      4. Cook the rice first and let it steam covered off the heat while you stir fry.
      5. Slice the chicken breast thin. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet or wok over high heat and sear the chicken in batches for 3 to 4 minutes until browned and cooked through, then set it aside.
      6. Add the minced garlic to the hot pan for 15 seconds, then the sliced onion, red bell pepper and green bell pepper. Stir fry 2 to 3 minutes so they soften at the edges but keep their bite.
      7. Return the chicken, pour in the sauce, and toss for a minute until everything is coated and the liquid has reduced to a glaze.
      8. Kill the heat and fold in the toasted cashews and the green onions, then combine with the rice in your mixing pot.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen equal containers. Each one carries 606 calories, and if you want the cashews crunchy all week, hold half of them out and drop them on top after reheating.
