Makes 14 servings · ~700 cal · 30g protein · 90g carbs · 24g fat · ~$3.89 per serving (at everyday cost).

Pelau is Trinidad's Sunday pot, and it starts with an act that looks like a mistake: sugar melted in hot oil until it turns almost black. Chicken hits that caramel and takes the color, then rice, pigeon peas, coconut milk and sweet potato all cook down in the same pot until it is one thick, brown, sweet-and-savory thing. Each bowl lands 700 calories and 30 grams of protein for about $3.89 (at everyday cost). Big flavor, one pot, fourteen dinners.

    Shop Smart

    • Pigeon peas are the single most expensive line in this recipe, more than the chicken. Check the international aisle rather than the canned vegetable aisle, where they are usually cheaper, and buy dried if you are willing to soak them overnight.
    • Coconut milk is the next biggest line. Store brand cans, full fat, bought on sale, and stacked in the pantry since they keep for a year.
    • Sweet potatoes go in whole chunks and hold their shape through the whole cook. Buy them by the bag rather than the each and pick medium ones, since the giant ones cook unevenly.
    • Brown sugar is what makes the caramel, and one bag lasts through many batches. Keep it sealed tight or it turns into a brick you have to chisel.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Season the chicken thighs with the cajun seasoning, minced garlic, grated ginger, dried thyme, black pepper, salt and most of the chopped cilantro. Let it marinate at least an hour, or overnight.
    3. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear and set it aside to drain.
    4. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium-high, add the brown sugar, and let it melt and bubble until it goes dark amber and starts to smoke slightly. This is the burnt sugar step and it is what makes pelau pelau, so stay at the stove.
    5. Add the marinated chicken all at once and stir hard so every piece takes the caramel. Cook 5 to 7 minutes until browned, then add the diced onion, minced jalapeno, ketchup and bay leaves.
    6. Stir in the drained rice, pigeon peas and cubed sweet potato and coat everything in the caramel.
    7. Pour in the coconut milk and chicken broth, season with the paprika and more salt, and bring to a boil. Drop to the lowest heat, cover tightly, and cook 25 to 30 minutes without lifting the lid.
    8. Rest it covered off the heat 10 minutes, pull the bay leaves, and fold from the bottom with the remaining cilantro so the browned rice at the base gets spread through.

    Portion It

    Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen equal containers, no favorites. Each one carries 700 calories, and a splash of water before you reheat loosens the rice back up.
