      Vodka sauce is really just tomato paste cooked until it goes brick red, then softened with cream. We build it exactly that way and skip the vodka entirely, because it does not change the sauce and it does not belong on a grocery list this tight. Hot Italian sausage, red pepper flakes, kale and short tube pasta, at 31 grams of protein for about $1.66.

      Shop Smart

      • Skip the vodka. It is the single most expensive optional ingredient in Italian American cooking and the sauce does not miss it. Cooking the tomato paste properly is what actually builds that flavor.
      • Store brand ziti or rigatoni, whichever is cheaper. Any short tube with ridges holds this sauce. Brand loyalty on dry pasta is money thrown away.
      • Heavy cream is the line to watch. It is the biggest non meat cost here. Evaporated milk will get you a lighter but still creamy sauce for considerably less.
      • Red pepper flakes replace the Calabrian chilis. The original calls for a specialty jarred chili. Flakes from your spice rack bring the same heat and cost nothing extra.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the onion, mince the garlic, and strip the kale leaves from their stems and tear them.
      3. Boil the pasta in well salted water until just shy of tender, then reserve a mug of the starchy water before you drain it. That water is what makes the sauce cling later.
      4. Heat the olive oil in a wide pot and brown the hot Italian sausage in crumbles until it has color, not just gray.
      5. Add the onion and cook until soft, then the garlic and the red pepper flakes for a minute.
      6. Stir in the tomato paste and keep stirring for three or four minutes until it darkens from bright red to brick. This step is the whole sauce, so do not rush it.
      7. Pour in the heavy cream, stir until it turns a uniform blush color, then fold in the torn kale and let it wilt.
      8. Add the drained pasta and the parmesan, splash in the reserved pasta water a little at a time, and toss until the sauce coats every piece.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, tossing between scoops so the sausage does not settle to the bottom. Every bowl lands near 792 calories and 31 grams of protein.
