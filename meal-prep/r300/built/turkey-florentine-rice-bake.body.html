      Shop Smart

      • Frozen chopped spinach in 10 oz boxes costs a fraction of fresh spinach for the same cooked volume. Fresh would need several pounds to equal what five boxes deliver here.
      • Squeeze the thawed spinach dry in a clean towel before it goes in. Skipping this is how a cream sauce turns into a puddle, and wasted sauce is wasted money.
      • White mushrooms in 8 oz packs beat pre-sliced on price every time. Slicing three packs takes about five minutes.
      • Rice from the big bag is the cheapest base you can build a casserole on, and it never spoils on the shelf.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice and spread it across the bottom of a greased 9x13 pan as the base layer.
      3. Thaw the frozen chopped spinach and squeeze it dry in a clean kitchen towel, wringing hard. You want a compact ball, not a wet clump.
      4. Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat, add the diced onion and sliced mushrooms, and cook 8 to 10 minutes until the mushroom liquid cooks away.
      5. Whisk in the flour and cook two minutes, then add the chicken broth and the milk gradually, whisking, and cook until the sauce thickens.
      6. Season with salt, black pepper, and the curry powder. It is a small amount and it is what separates this from every other cream sauce.
      7. Off the heat, melt in most of the shredded cheese, then fold in the squeezed spinach and the diced turkey breast.
      8. Pour the sauce over the rice, top with the remaining cheese, and bake at 375F for 30 minutes until bubbling and browned, then rest 10 minutes before portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pan on the scale, subtract the empty pan weight, and divide by 14. Cut through the rice base and the creamed spinach layer together so every container gets both. Each serving comes to 572 calories and 35 grams of protein.
