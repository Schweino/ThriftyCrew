      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 37g protein · 47g carbs · 24g fat · ~$2.56 per serving.

      The name has nothing to do with weddings. It comes from the Italian minestra maritata, the married soup, and the marriage in question is greens and meat. Little turkey meatballs, tender pasta shells, kale and spinach, all floating in a clean chicken broth with parmesan stirred in at the end. It is a soup that looks fancy in the container and costs less than a drive-through combo. That is the whole point of this operation.

      Shop Smart

      • The ground turkey is the entire budget. Everything else here is broth, vegetables and pasta. Buy the turkey when the price drops, then freeze it flat in one pound sheets so it thaws in an hour instead of a day.
      • Frozen chopped spinach beats fresh for this pot. It costs less per usable ounce, it keeps for months, and it goes into hot broth straight from the box. Nobody is washing sand out of anything.
      • Buy parmesan in a tub, not a shaker. The shaker stuff costs more per ounce and it will not melt into the broth the same way. Grate it yourself and you get a better soup for less money.
      • Fresh basil is the one splurge here. If the packs at the store look tired, leave it out. The soup holds up fine without it and you keep the money.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the ground turkey with the bread crumbs, eggs, milk, some of the grated parmesan and the chopped fresh basil. Season with salt and pepper, then roll the mix into small meatballs about the size of a grape.
      3. Line a sheet pan, spread the meatballs out, and bake at 400F until browned, roughly 15 minutes. They finish cooking in the broth later, so do not fuss over the centers.
      4. Chop the onion, carrots and celery small and sweat them in olive oil in your big pot over medium heat until soft, about 8 minutes.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth, bring it to a simmer, then add the pasta shells and cook until they are just shy of tender.
      6. Stir in the kale and the thawed chopped spinach and simmer until the greens go dark and silky.
      7. Slide the meatballs into the pot, simmer a few minutes more so the flavors marry, then kill the heat and stir in the rest of the parmesan.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down in Step 1. Divide what is left by 14 and ladle that exact weight into each container. Every bowl lands near 562 calories with 37 grams of protein, which makes this a dinner and not a cup of soup you eat before dinner.
