      Makes 14 servings · ~563 cal · 28g protein · 37g carbs · 34g fat · ~$1.18 per serving.

      This is the whole plate on one pan. Potatoes on the bottom soaking up drippings, green beans tucked around the edges, and chicken thighs under a garlic parmesan coat that browns into something close to a crust. No side dish to plan, no second pot to wash. Each container comes in at 563 calories and 28 grams of protein for about $1.18, which is roughly what a drive-through charges for a soda.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy potatoes in the big bag, not loose. The per-pound price on a five or ten pound bag is usually half of what the loose bin charges, and potatoes keep for weeks somewhere cool and dark.
      • Frozen green beans beat fresh here on both price and behavior. They go straight from the freezer to the pan, they do not go slimy in the crisper drawer waiting on you, and the store brand bags are the cheapest vegetable per serving in this recipe.
      • Buy the tub of grated parmesan rather than a wedge. This recipe uses a few ounces and the tub keeps in the fridge for months, so you get several batches out of one purchase.
      • Mayonnaise is the secret to the coating, not a garnish. It keeps the parmesan glued to the chicken and the meat from drying out, and a jar you already have on the door costs you almost nothing per batch.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F. Cut the potatoes into bite-size chunks, toss them with most of the olive oil, salt, black pepper and paprika, and spread them across two large sheet pans in a single layer.
      3. Roast the potatoes alone for 20 minutes, until the edges are starting to color. They need the head start; the chicken does not.
      4. While they roast, stir together the melted butter, mayonnaise, minced garlic, grated parmesan, lemon juice and italian seasoning into a thick paste.
      5. Pat the chicken thighs dry and coat every piece in the parmesan garlic mixture, front and back.
      6. Pull the pans, push the potatoes to the edges, and nestle the coated thighs into the middle with the green beans scattered around them.
      7. Roast another 20 to 25 minutes, until the chicken reads 165F, the coating is browned and blistered in spots, and the potatoes are crisp underneath.
      8. Chop the chicken into pieces, scrape everything into your mixing pot with the pan drippings, and toss so the garlic butter coats the potatoes and beans too.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. That number goes into every container, no more and no less. Each one lands at 563 calories, and the potatoes hold their texture on reheat far better than rice does.
