Makes 14 servings · ~569 cal · 43g protein · 58g carbs · 16g fat · ~$2.70 per serving (at everyday cost).

The name is the instruction. You dump five things into a pan, cover it with foil, and you do not lift that foil until the timer goes off. The rice cooks in the beef broth and the cream of mushroom soup, taking on everything that drips off the chops above it, and the whole thing steams into a casserole with no stirring and no watching. Five ingredients. About ten minutes of actual work. Some recipes earn their keep by being clever and some earn it by being easy.

Ingredients

  • Cream of Mushroom Soup (Campbell's (USDA SR)): 3.5 cans (1043 g)
  • Beef Broth (Great Value): 7 cups (1680 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 4.75 cups dry (867 g)
  • Garlic Powder: 0.25 tbsp (3 g)
  • Onion Powder: 0.25 tbsp (2 g)
  • Pork Chops: 5.25 lb (2380 g)
  • Onion Soup Mix (Great Value): 3.5 oz (99 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Bone-in chops cost less and cook better here. The bone keeps the meat from drying out during a long covered bake, and it comes off the price per pound too.
    • Store brand condensed soup is the same soup. Buy the generic cream of mushroom by the four pack. Once it is baked into rice, nobody could pick out the label.
    • Regular long grain white rice, not instant. Instant rice costs several times more per serving and turns to paste under an hour of covered heat. Plain white rice is what this method was built for.
    • Onion soup mix is the entire seasoning. One envelope replaces a whole rack of jars. If you buy the box of packets rather than singles, it costs very little per batch.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Heat the oven to 350F and grease a large deep baking pan.
    3. Stir the cream of mushroom soup together with the beef broth until smooth, then whisk in most of the onion soup mix.
    4. Spread the uncooked rice evenly across the bottom of the pan and pour the soup mixture over it. Shake the pan flat, do not stir.
    5. Season the pork chops with garlic powder, onion powder, salt and black pepper, and lay them across the top of the rice in a single layer.
    6. Sprinkle the last of the onion soup mix over the chops, cover the pan tightly with foil, and get a good seal. Steam is what cooks the rice.
    7. Bake for about 90 minutes without lifting the foil once, then rest 10 minutes before you uncover it.

    Portion It

    Weigh the finished casserole in its pan, subtract the pan weight, divide by 14, and portion to that exact weight so everybody gets chop and rice in the same ratio. Every serving lands near 569 calories with 43 grams of protein.
