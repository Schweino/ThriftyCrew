      Makes 14 servings · ~614 cal · 54g protein · 43g carbs · 24g fat · ~$2.11 per serving.

      Sunday dinner, in one pot, on any day of the week. A pork loin gets seared hard, then roasts on a bed of carrots, celery, onion and potatoes with apple juice and broth underneath. When it comes out, you build a real gravy right in the same pot from the drippings and a spoonful of flour. Roast, vegetables and gravy, and exactly one thing to wash. That is a good trade.

      Shop Smart

      • Watch for whole pork loin on sale and buy the biggest one. Whole loin is one of the most reliably discounted cuts in the meat case, and it runs far under the price of precut roasts.
      • Apple juice instead of wine. It deglazes the pot just as well, adds a touch of sweetness to the gravy, and costs a small fraction of even a cheap bottle.
      • Buy carrots, celery and potatoes in bags and bunches. This pot takes a lot of all three, and the bag price per pound is where the savings live.
      • The gravy costs about a nickel. Flour, butter and the drippings you already have. There is no reason to buy a jar or a packet for this.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Pat the pork loin dry, season it hard with salt, black pepper, dried thyme and Italian seasoning, and sear it on every side in olive oil in a heavy Dutch oven until deeply browned.
      3. Lift the roast out. Cook the chopped onion, carrots and celery in the same pot until they start to color, then stir in the minced garlic and the bay leaves.
      4. Pour in the apple juice, scrape every browned bit off the bottom, then add the chicken broth.
      5. Set the roast back on the vegetables, tuck the quartered potatoes around it, cover, and roast at 325F for about 90 minutes until the pork is tender.
      6. Move the roast and the vegetables to a board and let the meat rest 15 minutes before you slice or shred it.
      7. Set the pot over medium heat, whisk the flour into the butter to make a paste, stir it into the drippings, and simmer until the gravy thickens. Season and pull the bay leaves.

      Portion It

      Divide the potatoes and vegetables across your 14 containers, then set your mixing pot of pork on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and add that exact weight of meat to each. Ladle gravy over the top. Every plate lands near 614 calories with 54 grams of protein.
