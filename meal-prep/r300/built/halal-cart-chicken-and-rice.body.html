      Shop Smart

      • Greek yogurt is the base of the white sauce and the big tub costs far less per ounce than the little cups. Buy plain, buy the tub, and use the rest for breakfast.
      • Olive oil is a surprisingly large line here because the marinade uses a real amount of it. Buy the big jug rather than the small bottle, and there is no need for extra virgin in a marinade that is going into a hot oven.
      • Cucumbers and tomatoes make the salad component. Buy cucumbers loose and use canned diced tomatoes for the batch, since fresh tomatoes turn to water in a container by day three.
      • The turmeric rice is what makes this taste like the cart. Turmeric, cumin, cinnamon and garam masala from an international grocer cost a fraction of the branded jars and this recipe uses all four.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Marinate the chicken: whisk the olive oil, white vinegar, minced garlic, ground coriander, paprika, ground cumin, onion powder, salt and black pepper, then turn the thighs in it and refrigerate at least 4 hours or overnight.
      3. Heat the oven to 450F. Spread the chicken in a single layer on sheet pans and roast 20 to 25 minutes, running the broiler at the end until the edges char in spots. Let it rest, then chop it into rough bite-size pieces on a cutting board, cart style.
      4. Make the turmeric rice: toast the rice in a little oil with the ground turmeric, ground cinnamon and garam masala for two minutes, then add the chicken broth, bring to a boil, cover, and cook on low 18 to 20 minutes. Rest it covered another 10 minutes and fluff.
      5. Make the white sauce: whisk the greek yogurt, mayonnaise, lemon juice, a little of the white vinegar, salt and black pepper until smooth. Refrigerate it, because it is better cold and it tightens as it sits.
      6. Dice the cucumber and drain the canned tomatoes well, then toss them with the diced red onion into a quick salad.
      7. Layer the chopped chicken and rice together in your mixing pot so the chicken juices get into the grains.
      8. Keep the white sauce, the hot sauce and the cucumber salad in separate containers so nothing goes soggy in the fridge.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of chicken and rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. That is the base of every container, and the salad and sauces go beside it. Each bowl carries 710 calories, which is the biggest plate in this batch and the right one for a long day.
