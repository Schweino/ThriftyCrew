      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 30g protein · 72g carbs · 16g fat · ~$2.46 per serving.

      Bulgogi is defined by its marinade, not its cut. Soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic, and grated fruit, which is the part most people skip and the part that actually matters, because the enzymes in the apple tenderize the beef while the sugars help it caramelize in the pan. This build uses ground beef to keep the price honest, so you get all of that flavor without paying steak money. Each bowl lands at 564 calories and 30 grams of protein for about $2.46 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground beef is the whole budget line here, and it is also why this dish costs a third of what a sliced bulgogi would. If you want to splurge, thin sliced sirloin works in the same marinade, but the ground version is the reason the number is what it is.
      • One apple in the marinade is doing real chemical work. Grate it, do not chop it, so the juice gets into the meat instead of sitting in chunks on top.
      • Sesame oil, mirin, rice vinegar, and soy sauce are all one-bottle purchases that carry across dozens of Asian recipes. Buy them once and the marginal cost of every Korean dinner after that drops sharply.
      • Frozen spinach and a couple of carrots are the cheapest vegetables you can put in a rice bowl. Buy the box of frozen spinach, use what you need, and keep the rest in the freezer indefinitely.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Grate the apple and the onion into a bowl and whisk in the soy sauce, sesame oil, mirin, brown sugar, rice vinegar, garlic, ginger, and black pepper. That is your bulgogi marinade and it should smell sweet, salty, and toasty all at once.
      3. Mix about two thirds of the marinade into the ground beef and let it sit at least 30 minutes in the fridge. Hold the rest back for finishing.
      4. Start the rice so it is ready when the beef is.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium-high and cook the beef in two batches, breaking it up as it goes. Let each batch sit undisturbed for a minute at a time so it browns instead of steaming. Browning is where the caramel notes come from.
      6. Pour the reserved marinade into the skillet with all the beef and let it bubble down for 2 to 3 minutes until it glazes the meat.
      7. Warm the frozen spinach and the shredded carrots briefly in a separate pan so they keep their color, then season lightly with salt.
      8. Sprinkle the toasted sesame seeds and sliced green onions over everything just before packing.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into the containers first, then set the beef skillet on the scale, subtract the empty weight, and divide by 14. Add the spinach and carrots to the side of each container so they do not soak up the sauce all week. Every bowl comes out at 564 calories with an even share of the beef.
