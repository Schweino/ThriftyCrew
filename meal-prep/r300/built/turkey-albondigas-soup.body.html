      Makes 14 servings · ~575 cal · 33g protein · 71g carbs · 18g fat · ~$2.62 per serving.

      Sopa de albondigas is the Mexican meatball soup, and the trick that makes it different from every other meatball soup is the rice mixed right into the meat. It swells as the meatballs cook and gives them a soft, almost tender texture instead of a dense one. Around them go potatoes, carrots, celery, zucchini and a tomato broth thick with cilantro. It is a whole dinner in a bowl and it reheats better than most.

      Shop Smart

      • Zucchini is a summer buy. In season it is one of the cheapest vegetables on the shelf. Out of season it is one of the most expensive. Make this pot when the price is right and it drops a good bit.
      • Bread crumbs are worth making, not buying. Heels and stale slices, dried in a low oven and pulsed, cost you nothing and bind meatballs exactly the same way.
      • Buy potatoes and carrots in bags. Loose produce is priced for people buying two. You are buying for 14 servings, so the bag price wins every time.
      • Canned diced tomatoes carry the broth. Buy them by the case on sale. They keep for years and this pot goes through a stack of them.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the ground turkey with the eggs, bread crumbs, uncooked rice, minced garlic, dried oregano, ground cumin, salt and black pepper, then roll it into meatballs about the size of a golf ball.
      3. Brown the meatballs on a sheet pan in a hot oven, around 400F for 15 minutes, so they hold together when they hit the broth.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in your big pot and cook the diced onion until soft, then add the carrots and celery and give them a few minutes.
      5. Pour in the diced tomatoes with their juice and the chicken broth, bring to a simmer, and add the cubed potatoes.
      6. Slide the meatballs in gently and simmer 20 minutes, until the rice inside them has swelled and the potatoes are tender.
      7. Add the chopped zucchini and simmer just 5 more minutes so it stays firm, then stir in the chopped cilantro off the heat.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide by 14 and ladle that exact weight into each container, spreading the meatballs evenly so nobody gets a bowl of broth. Every serving lands near 575 calories with 33 grams of protein.
