      Makes 14 servings · ~568 cal · 25g protein · 50g carbs · 29g fat · ~$2.45 per serving.

      The name is the best part. Little bits of seasoned pork cling to the noodles as you lift them, and somebody in Sichuan decided that looked like ants climbing a tree. What it actually is: a one-pan noodle dinner where the noodles finish cooking right in the broth, so they drink up every drop of the doubanjiang and soy. That is also why it reheats without turning into a dry brick. Traditionally this is made with glass noodles, and rice noodles are the version your regular grocery store actually stocks.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground pork is the quiet bargain of the meat case. It runs well under ground beef per pound and it carries a spicy sauce better, which is why this bowl lands where it does on cost.
      • Doubanjiang is a one-jar purchase that outlives the batch. That fermented chili bean paste is the whole flavor of the dish, and a single jar seasons this pot many times over.
      • Buy the rice noodles in the plain box, not the meal kit. Same noodle, and the kit is mostly a sauce packet you are about to make from scratch anyway.
      • A knob of fresh ginger keeps for weeks. Store it unpeeled in the freezer and grate off what you need, which beats letting a whole piece go soft in the crisper drawer.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the rice noodles in warm water until pliable but not soft, then drain and set aside.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat and cook the ginger for about thirty seconds, until it smells sharp.
      4. Stir in the doubanjiang and cook it in the oil for another minute so the paste turns deep red and loses its raw edge.
      5. Add the ground pork, break it up, and cook until no pink remains.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, soy sauce, and sugar, and bring it up to a simmer.
      7. Add the drained noodles, turn the heat to low, and simmer uncovered until the noodles are tender and most of the liquid has been absorbed.
      8. Kill the heat, fold in the green onions, and season with salt and black pepper to taste.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot back on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide what is left by 14. Scoop that exact number into each container so nobody gets the sad noodle bowl. Every serving lands near 568 calories with 25 grams of protein. Let them cool with the lids off before you stack them, or the steam turns the noodles to mush.
