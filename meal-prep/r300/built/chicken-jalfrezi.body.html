      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 38g protein · 74g carbs · 10g fat · ~$1.65 per serving.

      Jalfrezi started as a way to use up leftover roast meat, stir fried with onions and peppers so it came back to life instead of getting thrown out. That origin story is why it is the driest of the Indian curries, more stir fry than stew, with peppers that still have snap in them. Dry curries hold their texture in a container far better than saucy ones do. Each bowl runs 562 calories and 38 grams of protein for about $1.65 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned diced tomatoes are the base of the sauce and the biggest line on the list. Buy them by the case when they hit a sale, since they keep for years and turn up in half the recipes on this site.
      • Green bell peppers are cheaper than red nearly every week and they hold their bite better through cooking, which is exactly what jalfrezi wants.
      • Boneless thighs cost less than breast and stay tender in a fast, hot pan. This is a stir fry, so the chicken sees high heat and thighs forgive that much better.
      • Garam masala, cumin, turmeric, and chili powder are jars you buy once and use across every Indian recipe. Buying whole containers instead of the tiny bottles cuts the per-batch cost of the seasoning to nearly nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the dried chiles in hot water for 15 minutes and blend them smooth with a little of the soaking liquid.
      3. Cut the chicken thighs into bite sized pieces and toss with turmeric, salt, and a spoonful of the white vinegar.
      4. Start the rice.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet and sear the chicken hard until browned at the edges, then pull it out. It does not need to be cooked through yet.
      6. Cook the sliced onions until golden, add the garlic and the blended chile paste, then the cumin, coriander, turmeric, paprika, garam masala, and chili powder and toast them for a minute.
      7. Add the tomato paste and the canned tomatoes and simmer hard, uncovered, until the sauce thickens and darkens and the oil starts to separate, about 20 minutes. This reduction is the whole difference between jalfrezi and a watery tomato curry.
      8. Return the chicken with the bell peppers and cook 6 to 8 minutes, just until the chicken is done and the peppers are still crisp. Finish with the rest of the vinegar and a big handful of cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container, then set the skillet on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Because this curry is thick rather than soupy, each portion holds its shape and the peppers stay recognizable all week. Every bowl lands at 562 calories.
