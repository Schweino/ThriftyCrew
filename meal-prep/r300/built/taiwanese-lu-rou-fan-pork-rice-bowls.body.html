      Makes 14 servings · ~689 cal · 38g protein · 76g carbs · 25g fat · ~$2.07 per serving.

      Lu rou fan is the dish Taiwan runs on. Diced pork braised slowly in soy sauce, caramelized sugar and five spice until it turns glossy and falls apart, ladled over rice with a braised egg on top. Street vendors sell it for pocket change and it deserves the reputation. Our version uses pork shoulder instead of belly, which cuts the fat and pushes it to 38 grams of protein for about $2.07.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork shoulder instead of pork belly is the budget and health move. Belly is traditional and it is also mostly fat you are paying for. Shoulder braises just as tender and carries far more protein per dollar.
      • Fried onions replace the fried shallots. Traditional lu rou fan starts with crispy fried shallots, which are pricey. Yellow onions fried golden in the same oil get you most of the way there.
      • Five spice powder is the whole soul of the dish. One small jar lasts a year of batches. This is not the ingredient to substitute or skip.
      • Eggs braised in the sauce are cheap protein. Hard boil them, peel them, and let them sit in the braise. They soak up the flavor and add real protein for very little money.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Hand dice the pork shoulder into small pieces rather than grinding it. The texture of lu rou fan comes from distinct little cubes, not a paste.
      3. Hard boil the eggs, cool them, and peel them so they are ready to go into the braise.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot and fry the sliced onions until they turn deep golden and crisp at the edges, then lift most of them out and set them aside.
      5. Brown the diced pork in that same oil until the pieces have color and the fat starts to render.
      6. Add the garlic and the sugar and stir until the sugar melts and coats everything in a dark glaze, which takes only a minute or two and is the step that makes this dish look right.
      7. Pour in the soy sauce and the chicken broth, add the sliced mushrooms, the five spice powder and the black pepper, then return the fried onions.
      8. Add the peeled eggs, cover, and simmer very low until the pork is falling apart. Cook the rice and blanch the broccoli near the end so both are fresh.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount over rice in each container, with an even share of egg and broccoli in each one. Every bowl lands near 689 calories and 38 grams of protein.
