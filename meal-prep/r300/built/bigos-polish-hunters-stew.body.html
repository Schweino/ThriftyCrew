      Makes 14 servings · ~578 cal · 25g protein · 79g carbs · 17g fat · ~$1.41 per serving.

      Bigos is the national dish of Poland and it was built for exactly what we do here: a big pot of sauerkraut, cabbage and mixed meat that gets better every single time you reheat it. Poles will tell you the third day is the best day, and they are right. Sauerkraut and cabbage keep the cost down to about $1.41 a bowl while it still delivers 25 grams of protein.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned sauerkraut is the budget move. The refrigerated jars cost more and this stew simmers for hours anyway, which erases any difference in texture. Buy the cans.
      • Chicken thighs stretch the sausage. Mixing a cheaper meat with the smoked sausage is the traditional build, and it is also why the cost per bowl lands so low.
      • Caraway seeds are worth the one time buy. They barely register on the cost list and they are the flavor most people cannot name but immediately recognize as Polish.
      • The mushrooms here are a whisper, not a shout. A quarter cup flavors the whole pot. Buy one package and use the rest somewhere else this week.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Drain the sauerkraut but do not rinse all the tang out of it. Core and shred the fresh cabbage, dice the onions, and slice the mushrooms.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large heavy pot and brown the chicken thighs and the sliced smoked sausage until both have real color on them. Take them out and set them aside.
      4. Cook the onions in the same pot until soft, then add the mushrooms and let them give up their water.
      5. Stir in the sauerkraut, the shredded cabbage, the tomato sauce, the caraway seeds and the bay leaves.
      6. Return the meat, add enough water to keep everything just submerged, and bring it to a bare simmer.
      7. Cover and cook low and slow for a couple of hours, stirring now and then. This is a stew that rewards patience, not heat.
      8. Stir in the golden raisins near the end, season with salt and black pepper, and cook the rice separately to serve it over.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount into each container over a scoop of rice. Every bowl lands near 578 calories and 25 grams of protein.
