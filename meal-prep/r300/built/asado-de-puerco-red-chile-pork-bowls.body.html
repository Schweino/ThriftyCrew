      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 32g protein · 82g carbs · 9g fat · ~$1.19 per serving.

      In Chihuahua and across the border in El Paso, asado de puerco is the dish that comes out for weddings, baptisms and Christmas Eve. Pork simmered slowly in red chile sauce until the sauce clings to every piece and the meat surrenders. Traditionally you toast and blend the dried chiles yourself. Canned red enchilada sauce gets you most of the way there on a weeknight, which is why this one lands where it does on the receipt.

      Ingredients

      • Olive Oil: 3 tbsp (40 g)
      • Pork Loin: 3.75 lb (1667 g)
      • Garlic: 2.5 tbsp (22 g)
      • Enchilada Sauce (Old El Paso): 5 cans (1441 g)
      • Tomato Sauce (generic): 1 can (450 g)
      • Rice (Member's Mark): 7.25 cups dry (1320 g)

      Shop Smart

      • Canned enchilada sauce is the shortcut worth taking. Toasting and blending dried chiles is better, but it is also an hour and a blender. The canned sauce costs about a dollar and gets you close.
      • Pork loin goes on sale hard. Watch for the whole loin price and cut it down yourself. You will pay a fraction of what precut cubes or chops cost per pound.
      • Plain tomato sauce rounds the chile. One cheap can takes the edge off the heat and gives the sauce body. It is the least expensive ingredient in the pot and it matters.
      • Rice from the big bag is why this costs what it does. The carb here is close to free per serving, which leaves the budget for the meat.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork loin into bite-sized cubes and pat them dry, then season with salt, ground cumin and black pepper.
      3. Sear the pork in the olive oil in a heavy pot over high heat, in batches, until the pieces are browned on at least two sides. Crowding the pan is the one mistake to avoid here.
      4. Drop the heat, add the minced garlic, and stir for a minute until it smells sweet.
      5. Pour in the enchilada sauce and the tomato sauce, scrape the bottom of the pot clean, and bring it to a bare simmer.
      6. Cover and simmer gently for about 90 minutes, stirring now and then, until the pork is tender and the sauce has thickened and darkened.
      7. Cook the rice per the package while the pork simmers, and taste the sauce for salt at the end.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice across your 14 containers, then set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight of pork and red chile sauce over the rice. Every bowl lands near 567 calories with 32 grams of protein.
