      Makes 14 servings · ~572 cal · 39g protein · 74g carbs · 12g fat · ~$1.69 per serving.

      Nikujaga translates, more or less, to meat and potatoes. In Japan it is the dish people name when you ask what home tastes like. Thin sliced meat, potatoes, onions, and carrots simmer in a sweet soy broth of mirin, sugar, and soy sauce until the potatoes go soft at the edges and take on the color of the broth. Eastern Japan traditionally makes it with pork, and that is the version here. It is gentle food, and it is one of the least expensive stews you can put together.

      Shop Smart

      • Potatoes carry this dish and cost almost nothing. Buy the ten pound bag. You will use the rest, and the per-pound price is a fraction of the loose bin.
      • Pork loin slices thin if you freeze it for thirty minutes first. That firms it up enough to cut paper thin with a regular knife, which saves you from buying pre-sliced hot pot meat at a premium.
      • Mirin is a one-bottle investment that lasts a very long time. It is the sweetness and the shine in this broth, and it turns up in a lot of Japanese cooking after this.
      • Snow peas are the priciest thing on the list, and they go in last. They are there for color and snap, so a small handful per batch does the job.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Freeze the pork loin briefly, then slice it as thin as you can manage against the grain.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide pot and cook the pork over medium-high heat just until it loses its pink, then push it to the side.
      4. Add the sliced yellow onions and cook until they turn translucent.
      5. Cut the potatoes into large chunks and the carrots on a diagonal, and add both to the pot.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, mirin, soy sauce, and sugar, and bring it up to a simmer.
      7. Lay a lid or a round of parchment directly on the surface, drop the heat to low, and simmer until the potatoes are tender all the way through but not falling apart.
      8. Add the snow peas in the last two minutes so they stay bright, then season with salt and black pepper to taste.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Weigh that amount into each container and make sure the potato chunks get spread evenly rather than piled into the first four. Every serving lands near 572 calories with 39 grams of protein. Nikujaga is famously better the next day, so this one is doing you a favor.
