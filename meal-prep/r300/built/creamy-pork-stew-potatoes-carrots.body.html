      Shop Smart

      • Pork shoulder is the cheapest braising meat we track. It is nearly the entire cost of this recipe, so buy it when the board shows it low and freeze what you do not use in two pound blocks.
      • Heavy cream is the one splurge line. A pint carries the batch. If you want to shave the cost, half the cream still gives you a silky broth, just a lighter one.
      • Buy carrots and celery whole, not pre cut. A bunch of celery and a two pound bag of carrots run a fraction of the bagged sticks and matchsticks, and ten minutes of knife work is the only difference.
      • Flour, thyme, bay leaves, mustard and hot sauce are already in your kitchen. That entire flavor backbone shows up as a pantry line, not a shopping line.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork shoulder into bite sized cubes, pat them dry, and toss them with the flour, salt and black pepper until every piece is dusted.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the pork in batches. Crowding the pan steams the meat instead of searing it, so give it room and take your time.
      4. Pull the pork out and drop the butter in, then cook the diced onion, carrots and celery until they soften and pick up the browned bits.
      5. Add the garlic, dried thyme and italian seasoning and stir until you can smell them, about one minute.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth along with the Worcestershire, dijon mustard, hot sauce and bay leaves, scraping the bottom of the pot clean.
      7. Return the pork, add the cubed potatoes, and simmer covered until the meat is tender and the potatoes give way to a fork.
      8. Fish out the bay leaves, turn the heat low, and stir in the heavy cream. Let it warm through without boiling so it stays smooth.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount into each container, stirring the pot between scoops so the meat does not all settle in the last few. Every bowl lands near 665 calories and 38 grams of protein.
