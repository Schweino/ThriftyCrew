      Makes 14 servings · ~605 cal · 57g protein · 54g carbs · 15g fat · ~$1.88 per serving.

      Every Haitian kitchen keeps a jar of epis in the fridge. It is a green marinade of cilantro, garlic, bell pepper, citrus and vinegar, and it goes into almost everything, starting with this. Chicken gets washed in citrus, marinated in epis overnight, browned, then braised down in a tomato sauce until the meat gives up. Over rice, each bowl runs 605 calories and 57 grams of protein for about $1.88. That protein number is one of the highest in this whole batch.

      Shop Smart

      • Make a double batch of epis while the blender is already dirty. Cilantro, garlic, bell pepper and citrus blended together freeze beautifully in an ice cube tray, and having it on hand turns a lot of plain weeknight chicken into something worth eating.
      • Bottled lemon juice is the practical choice here. This recipe uses a real volume of it for both the citrus wash and the marinade, and buying that many fresh lemons would cost several times more.
      • Green bell peppers are cheaper than red almost every week and they are the traditional pepper in epis, so there is nothing to give up by buying the cheap ones.
      • Tomato paste in small cans beats the tube on price per ounce. This recipe uses two cans worth, so buy exactly what you need and nothing dries out in the fridge.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Wash the chicken thighs in a bowl of water with the lemon juice and apple cider vinegar, rubbing the pieces and then draining them well. This citrus wash is a real step in Haitian cooking, not a superstition, and it sets up the flavor.
      3. Make the epis: blend the fresh cilantro, garlic, green bell pepper, dried thyme, black pepper and salt with a splash of the lemon juice until it is a coarse green paste. Rub it into the chicken and refrigerate at least four hours or overnight.
      4. Scrape the marinade off the chicken and reserve it. Heat a large heavy pot over medium-high and brown the thighs in batches until they take color, then set them aside.
      5. Add the diced onion to the pot and cook 5 minutes, then stir in the tomato paste and the reserved epis and cook 3 minutes until the paste turns brick red.
      6. Add water or broth to loosen the sauce, scrape the bottom clean, and return the chicken with its juices. Cover and simmer 35 to 40 minutes until the chicken is tender and reads 165F.
      7. While it braises, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes. Toast the peanuts in a dry skillet until they smell nutty, then chop them coarsely.
      8. Chop the chicken, fold the rice through the sauce, and scatter the toasted chopped peanuts over the top for crunch.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Same amount in all fourteen containers. Each one carries 605 calories, and I would hold the peanuts back and add them at reheat time so they stay crunchy instead of going soft in the sauce.
