      Makes 14 servings · ~679 cal · 46g protein · 24g carbs · 44g fat · ~$2.60 per serving.

      Walk through a market in Ecuador and you will find whole roasted pigs sitting on counters with a cook carving pieces off to order. That is hornado. The marinade is unapologetic about garlic, an amount that looks like a mistake until you taste it, plus achiote for color and lime for brightness. Roasted low for hours, the meat goes soft and the outside turns crackling. Served with potatoes, it is a plate worth the wait.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the biggest bone-in pork shoulder you can fit in the pan. Price per pound drops as the roast gets bigger, and the bone is free flavor for the drippings.
      • Buy garlic in the mesh bag, not by the single head. This marinade uses a serious amount of it, and the bag price per head is a fraction of the loose price.
      • Achiote paste is a small block that lasts. One brick colors and seasons this roast and several others. It lives in the Hispanic aisle for a few dollars.
      • Save the drippings. The pan juices under a roast like this are the best sauce you will make all week, and you already paid for them. Skim the fat and spoon them over the potatoes.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mash the peeled garlic with salt into a paste, then blend it with the achiote paste, the lime juice, ground cumin and black pepper into a thick marinade.
      3. Score the fat cap on the pork shoulder in a crosshatch and rub the marinade into every cut and across the whole roast. Cover and refrigerate overnight if you can.
      4. Set the pork in a roasting pan, pour the beef broth into the bottom, dot the top with the butter, and cover tightly with foil.
      5. Roast at 300F for about 4 hours, basting with the pan juices once an hour, until the meat pulls away from the bone.
      6. Uncover, raise the oven to 425F, and roast 25 to 30 minutes more until the outside is deeply browned and crisp.
      7. Boil the potatoes until tender while the roast rests, then pull the pork apart and spoon the skimmed pan juices over everything.

      Portion It

      Divide the potatoes across your 14 containers, then set your mixing pot of pulled pork on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and lay that exact weight alongside. Spoon the pan juices over the top. Every plate lands near 679 calories with 46 grams of protein.
