      Makes 14 servings · ~592 cal · 26g protein · 60g carbs · 27g fat · ~$1.83 per serving.

      Panang is the thick one. Where green curry runs loose and soupy, panang reduces down until it coats the back of a spoon, and it gets its roundness from ground peanuts worked right into the paste. We build the paste from scratch here with dried chiles, peanuts, lemongrass, ginger and garlic, which sounds ambitious until you realize it is one blender and four minutes. Each bowl comes in at 592 calories and 26 grams of protein for about $1.83.

      Shop Smart

      • Making your own paste is the actual savings here. Dried chiles, peanuts, lemongrass paste and aromatics cost less than premade panang paste and taste considerably louder.
      • Fresh basil is the most expensive fresh ingredient in this recipe. A living basil plant from the produce section runs about the price of two clamshells and keeps growing on a sunny sill.
      • Two cans of coconut milk, store brand and full fat, cover this whole batch. Do not shake them; you want the thick cream separated out for frying the paste.
      • Frozen green beans are the vegetable of choice here on price and behavior. They cost less than fresh, need no trimming, and go into the pot straight from the bag.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the dried chiles in hot water for 20 minutes. Drain them and blend with the peanuts, lemongrass paste, ginger, garlic, ground coriander, salt and a splash of the soaking water into a thick, coarse paste.
      3. Spoon the thick cream off the top of the unshaken coconut milk cans into your largest pot, reserving the thin liquid. Cook it with the vegetable oil over medium-high for 3 to 5 minutes until the oil splits out.
      4. Add your panang paste and the diced onion and fry it in that oil for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring hard, until it darkens a shade and smells nutty and sharp at the same time.
      5. Add the chicken thighs cut into strips and turn them in the paste for 3 minutes, then pour in the reserved coconut liquid and the chicken broth.
      6. Simmer uncovered 20 to 25 minutes, letting the sauce reduce until it is genuinely thick. Panang is supposed to cling, not pool, so give it the time.
      7. Season with the fish sauce and sugar, add the green beans, and cook 4 minutes until they are tender but still have snap.
      8. Turn off the heat and stir in the fresh basil. Cook the rice separately, steam it covered 10 minutes, and fold it in or bed the curry on top of it.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, one number, no eyeballing. Each one carries 592 calories, and reheat at half power so the coconut sauce stays smooth.
