      Makes 14 servings · ~593 cal · 41g protein · 46g carbs · 26g fat · ~$2.13 per serving.

      Souvlaki means little skewers, and pork is what Greeks actually put on them. Not chicken, not lamb, pork. The magic is in the marinade: olive oil, vinegar, lemon, garlic, oregano, and mustard, given enough time to work its way into the meat. Here is the part that matters for meal prep. Skewers are the only piece of this dish that refuses to scale, so we skip them entirely and roast the marinated cubes on sheet pans. Same char, same flavor, and you are not threading meat onto sticks for forty minutes.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork tenderloin is the tender cut that goes on sale constantly. Watch for it, buy two, and freeze one. It thaws overnight and marinates while it does.
      • The marinade is nearly free. Vinegar, lemon juice, garlic, mustard, and dried oregano are all pantry items, and the olive oil is the only meaningful cost in it.
      • Give it time, not more ingredients. Marinating overnight costs nothing and does more for the flavor than anything you could add to the bowl.
      • Red onion roasts right alongside the pork. One sheet pan, two components, and the onion picks up the marinade drippings for free.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the olive oil, white vinegar, lemon juice, garlic, dijon mustard, dried oregano, italian seasoning, paprika, salt, and black pepper into a marinade.
      3. Cut the pork tenderloin into large cubes, toss them in the marinade, and refrigerate for at least four hours and ideally overnight.
      4. Heat the oven hot, around 425 degrees, and cut the red onions into thick wedges.
      5. Spread the marinated pork and the onion wedges on sheet pans in a single layer, drizzled with the vegetable oil. Crowding the pan steams the meat instead of browning it, so use two pans.
      6. Roast until the pork is cooked through and the edges are browned and caught in spots.
      7. Cook the rice according to the package while the pork roasts.
      8. Squeeze any remaining lemon juice over the hot pork straight off the pan and let it rest a few minutes before portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the roasted pork separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Equal rice base, equal weight of pork and onion on top. Every bowl comes in at 593 calories with 41 grams of protein.
