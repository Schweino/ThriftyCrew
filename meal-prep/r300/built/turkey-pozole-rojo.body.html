      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 47g protein · 85g carbs · 14g fat · ~$3.40 per serving.

      Pozole is a celebration soup in Mexico, the pot that comes out for birthdays, Christmas and the days after. The red version gets its color and its depth from dried ancho and guajillo chiles, toasted and blended into the broth. Hominy gives it that chewy, nutty bite you cannot get from anything else, and the shredded turkey makes it a serious protein dinner. Crisp cabbage, cilantro and lime go on at the table, not in the pot.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy dried chiles from the Hispanic aisle, not the spice rack. The big bags of ancho and guajillo cost a fraction of what the little branded packets do, and they keep for a year sealed in the pantry.
      • Canned hominy is the cheap route. Dried hominy needs an overnight soak and hours of cooking. Canned is drained, rinsed and ready, and it costs about the same per serving.
      • Turkey breast is a holiday buy. Grab a frozen roast when the seasonal price hits, cook and shred it, and freeze it in portions. Pozole is exactly what that freezer stash is for.
      • Keep the garnishes separate. The shredded cabbage, cilantro and lime go into their own small containers. Add them at the table so they stay crisp instead of going limp in the broth.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Stem and seed the dried ancho and guajillo chiles, toast them in a dry skillet for about 30 seconds a side until they smell nutty, then cover them in hot water and soak 20 minutes.
      3. Blend the softened chiles with some of their soaking water, a chunk of the onion and several cloves of garlic into a smooth red sauce, then strain it.
      4. Heat the olive oil in your big pot, cook the rest of the diced onion and garlic until soft, and fry the strained chile sauce in the hot oil for five minutes so it darkens and loses its raw edge.
      5. Pour in the chicken broth along with the dried oregano, ground cumin, bay leaves, salt and black pepper, then simmer 30 minutes.
      6. Add the drained hominy and the shredded turkey breast and simmer another 20 minutes until everything has taken on the red broth. Stir in the splash of white vinegar to brighten it.
      7. Shred the cabbage and chop the cilantro into separate containers, cut the lime, and toast or crisp the tortillas to serve alongside.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide by 14 and ladle that exact weight into each container, keeping the cabbage, cilantro, lime and tortillas packed separately. Every bowl lands near 567 calories with 47 grams of protein, which is one of the strongest protein numbers in the whole soup section.
