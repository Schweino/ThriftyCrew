      Makes 14 servings · ~582 cal · 26g protein · 99g carbs · 9g fat · ~$2.67 per serving.

      Singapore noodles are the dish that proves curry does not have to be a stew. Thin rice noodles get tossed with curry powder and turmeric until every strand goes gold, then it is peppers, onion, egg, and turkey folded through and cilantro on top. It cooks fast and eats bright, and each bowl lands at 582 calories and 26 grams of protein for about $2.67.

      Shop Smart

      • Rice noodles are half this batch cost, so this is the one place shopping around really pays. An Asian market sells the same thin rice vermicelli for a fraction of what the grocery store charges for the boxed brand.
      • Red bell peppers are one of the bigger produce lines here. Green peppers cost about half as much and work fine, or buy red when they hit the seasonal low in late summer.
      • Curry powder and turmeric are the entire flavor identity of this dish and they cost almost nothing for the batch. Buy them in bags at an international market rather than the tiny grocery store jars.
      • Eggs stretch this dish for pocket change. Any time you can add protein to a noodle bowl for that price, do it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Soak the rice noodles in hot water until they are just pliable, about 8 minutes, then drain them well. They should still be firm, because they finish cooking in the pan.
      3. Beat the eggs, scramble them in a hot oiled pan until just set, and slide them out onto a plate to add back at the end.
      4. Brown the ground turkey in the same pan over high heat, breaking it into crumbles, until it is cooked through to 165F, then set it aside with the eggs.
      5. Add the sliced onion, sliced red bell pepper, minced garlic, grated ginger, and chopped jalapeno, and stir-fry 4 minutes until the peppers soften but still snap.
      6. Sprinkle the curry powder and turmeric directly into the hot pan and stir for 30 seconds until it smells toasty. Dry spice in hot oil is what makes these noodles taste like the takeout version.
      7. Add the drained noodles and the soy sauce and toss with tongs for 3 minutes until every strand is coated gold and heated through.
      8. Fold the turkey and egg back in, kill the heat, and stir in the green onions and most of the cilantro before moving everything into your weighed pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Lift with tongs from the bottom of the pot each time so the turkey and peppers get spread out instead of settling, then finish each with the reserved cilantro. Every bowl lands at 582 calories and 26 grams of protein.
