      Shop Smart

      • Buy bratwurst in the five pack, and buy them on sale. Brats are the biggest cost line here by a wide margin, and they freeze in the package without any trouble at all.
      • Canned sauerkraut is the cheap way in. A few cans cover this batch cheaply, and the long bake softens them into the potatoes anyway.
      • The nine pound potato bag is the right buy. This recipe eats most of it and the per pound price is roughly half what loose potatoes cost.
      • Broth replaces the beer. The traditional version braises brats in beer. Beef broth does the same job for a fraction of the price and you do not have to buy a six pack to cook dinner.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 400F and grease two large baking dishes or deep sheet pans.
      3. Cut the potatoes into even chunks and spread them across the pans, then season them with salt and black pepper.
      4. Drain the sauerkraut, squeeze out some of the liquid, and scatter it over the potatoes.
      5. Lay the bratwurst on top so they sit above the kraut and can brown instead of steam.
      6. Pour the beef broth around the edges of the pans, then cover them tightly with foil.
      7. Bake covered until the potatoes are tender all the way through, then pull the foil off and bake again until the brats are browned and split.
      8. Slice the brats into thick coins and combine everything with the pan juices in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, keeping the sausage coins evenly counted. Every bowl lands near 581 calories and 25 grams of protein.
