      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 27g protein · 72g carbs · 16g fat · ~$1.23 per serving.

      Babi kecap is Indonesian Chinese home cooking at its most straightforward. Pork goes into a pot with kecap manis, that thick sweet soy syrup, plus garlic, ginger, and a few dried chilies, and then you leave it alone until the meat gives up and the sauce turns to lacquer. There is nothing to time and nothing to fuss over. The classic version uses pork belly, but shoulder gets you the same fall-apart texture with a lot more actual meat per dollar, so that is the swap we made.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork shoulder is the cheapest cut that braises well, full stop. Belly costs more per pound and most of what you pay for renders out into the pot as fat.
      • Kecap manis is one bottle that changes a lot of dinners. If your store does not carry it, soy sauce simmered with brown sugar gets you close, which is the standard home workaround.
      • Dried chilies cost almost nothing and keep for a year. A bag in the pantry replaces three different jarred chili products.
      • Braises reward the cheap cut and punish the expensive one. If you ever find yourself reaching for tenderloin here, put it back. It will just go dry.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork shoulder into large cubes, roughly bite and a half sized, since they shrink as they braise.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot and brown the pork in batches, giving each piece room so it sears instead of steams.
      4. Add the garlic, ginger, sliced red onion, and dried arbol chiles and cook until fragrant.
      5. Return all the pork, pour in the sweet soy sauce and soy sauce, and add enough water to come most of the way up the meat.
      6. Stir in the ground coriander and ground cloves, bring it to a bare simmer, cover, and cook low and slow until the pork is fork tender.
      7. Uncover and let the liquid reduce until it turns dark, thick, and glossy enough to coat the meat, then season with salt and black pepper.
      8. Cook the rice according to the package while the sauce reduces.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the pork pot separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1 each time, and divide each by 14. That way every container gets the same rice base and the same amount of pork and sauce rather than whatever the ladle happened to grab. Every bowl comes in at 561 calories with 27 grams of protein.
