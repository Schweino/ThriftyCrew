      Makes 14 servings · ~679 cal · 53g protein · 52g carbs · 28g fat · ~$2.93 per serving.

      Chuletas guisadas is what happens when a Puerto Rican kitchen decides that a pork chop should never, ever be dry again. You sear the chops, then let them stew in a tomato and sofrito gravy with olives and peppers until they surrender. It is a completely different animal from a Southern smothered chop, which leans on cream and onion. This one is bright, acidic, and salty from the olives. It also happens to carry a genuinely high protein number, which is not something you often say about comfort food.

      Shop Smart

      • Pork chops are the entire cost of this dish. Everything else on the list is pennies. Buy bone-in family packs when they hit a sale price and freeze them flat, and this number drops noticeably.
      • Sofrito is cheaper to build than to buy. Onion, bell pepper, garlic, and cilantro blended together is the base, and a jar of the pre-made stuff costs several times what the produce does.
      • Olives are worth the can. They are the salty note that makes the gravy taste Puerto Rican instead of generic, and one small can covers the batch.
      • Sazon and adobo blends stretch a long way. One packet or shaker seasons many dinners, so the cost per bowl of the spice work is essentially nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the pork chops all over with sazon, dried oregano, salt, and black pepper, and let them sit while you prep.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large heavy pot and sear the chops in batches until browned on both sides, then set them aside.
      4. Add the diced yellow onion and green bell peppers to the pot and cook until soft, scraping up the browned bits.
      5. Stir in the garlic, salsa, and white vinegar and cook for a minute.
      6. Add the tomato sauce, chicken broth, olives, and bay leaves, then return the chops and any juices to the pot.
      7. Cover and simmer gently until the pork is tender enough to pull apart with a fork, then uncover and let the gravy thicken.
      8. Cook the rice according to the package, fish out the bay leaves, and finish the stew with the chopped cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the stew pot separately, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Give every container the same rice base, then the same weight of pork and gravy. Every serving comes in at 679 calories with 53 grams of protein, a big protein number for a bowl.
