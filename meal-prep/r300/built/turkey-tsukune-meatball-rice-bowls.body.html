      Makes 14 servings · ~720 cal · 43g protein · 88g carbs · 17g fat · ~$2.50 per serving.

      Tsukune are the Japanese meatballs you find on skewers at a yakitori counter, glazed in a sweet soy tare until they shine. Ours bake on sheet pans and then get tossed in the same glaze, which is faster and does not require a charcoal grill in your kitchen. Over rice with sesame and green onion, each bowl runs 720 calories and 43 grams of protein for about $2.50.

      Shop Smart

      • Mirin is the priciest bottle on this list, and it is also the ingredient that makes the glaze taste right. Buy it once from an international market where it usually runs half the grocery store price.
      • Ginger and garlic together cost almost nothing here and they are the entire aromatic base. Buy a big knob of ginger, freeze it, and grate off what you need.
      • The turkey is the biggest line by far. The 6 pound buy drops the price per pound, and ground turkey is one of the safest things in the store to freeze and thaw.
      • Bread crumbs, cornstarch, sesame oil, and sesame seeds together cost very little for this batch and all four sit in the pantry for months. Cheap pantry depth is what makes recipes like this easy to repeat.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Make the tare glaze first: simmer the soy sauce, mirin, brown sugar, rice vinegar, chicken broth, and some of the garlic and ginger for 10 minutes until it thickens slightly, then stir in the cornstarch slurried with a splash of cold water to finish it glossy.
      3. Mix the ground turkey with the bread crumbs, eggs, sesame oil, the rest of the minced garlic and grated ginger, most of the sliced green onions, salt, and black pepper.
      4. Wet your hands and roll the mixture into meatballs. Wet hands are the trick here, since this mix is soft on purpose and that softness is what makes tsukune tender.
      5. Set them on lined sheet pans and bake at 400F for 18 to 20 minutes, until browned and cooked through to 165F.
      6. Toss the hot meatballs in about two thirds of the glaze while they are still warm enough to drink it in, and save the rest for the containers.
      7. Cook the rice and move it into your weighed pot.
      8. Slice the remaining green onions and set them out with the toasted sesame seeds for topping.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Divide the meatballs by count across all 14, spoon the reserved glaze over each, and finish with sesame seeds and green onion. Each bowl lands at 720 calories and 43 grams of protein.
