      Makes 14 servings · ~662 cal · 50g protein · 77g carbs · 14g fat · ~$1.95 per serving.

      Galinhada is Brazil's answer to arroz con pollo, and it comes from Minas Gerais, where they cook it in one big pot for a crowd and everyone eats standing up. Chicken browns first, then the rice cooks in the drippings and the broth until it turns golden and every grain has picked up flavor. Peas and scallions go on at the end for color. Each bowl runs 662 calories and 50 grams of protein for about $1.95. One pot, no fuss, fourteen dinners.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy rice in the biggest bag you can store. It is the cheapest calorie in this recipe by a mile, it never goes bad, and every one-pot dish on this site leans on it.
      • Tomato paste in small cans is cheaper per ounce than the tube, and this recipe uses about three cans worth, so you will not be throwing away a half-used tube.
      • Frozen peas beat canned on price, color and texture. A single twelve ounce bag covers this batch for pocket change and the rest of the bag keeps in the freezer indefinitely.
      • Green bell peppers cost less than red almost every week of the year, and this dish traditionally uses green, so there is nothing to trade away.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chicken thighs into large pieces and season them with salt, black pepper and the lemon juice. Let them sit 20 minutes while you chop everything else.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium-high and brown the chicken in batches until it has real color on both sides. Do not rush this, because the fond on the bottom of the pot is the entire flavor base. Set the chicken aside.
      4. Cook the diced onion and green bell pepper in the same pot for 5 minutes, then add the garlic and the tomato paste and cook 2 more minutes until the paste turns brick red.
      5. Stir in the dry rice and toast it for 2 minutes so every grain gets coated in the tomato and the drippings.
      6. Add the diced tomatoes, chicken broth and bay leaves, then nestle the chicken back in with all its juices and bring the pot to a boil.
      7. Drop to the lowest heat, cover tightly, and cook 20 to 25 minutes without lifting the lid, until the rice is tender and the chicken reads 165F.
      8. Scatter the frozen peas over the top, re-cover, and let it sit off the heat 10 minutes. Then pull the bay leaves, fluff from the bottom, and fold in the sliced green onions.

      Portion It

      Set the loaded pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same scoop in every container. Each one carries 662 calories, and a splash of water before reheating brings the rice back to where it started.
