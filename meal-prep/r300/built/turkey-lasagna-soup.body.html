      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 41g protein · 60g carbs · 17g fat · ~$2.41 per serving.

      Lasagna soup is what happens when somebody admits out loud that layering a lasagna is the least fun part of making one. Same flavors, same ricotta and parmesan, none of the assembly. It is a one pot dinner that hits 562 calories and 41 grams of protein a bowl for about $2.41, and it freezes better than an actual lasagna does.

      Shop Smart

      • Ziti is about the cheapest structure you can put in a soup. Broken lasagna noodles work too if you have a box sitting around, and honestly that is the more authentic move here.
      • Crushed tomatoes in the 28 ounce can are one of the best values in the aisle. Buy the big cans, not the small ones, because tomato in a soup this size is a volume ingredient.
      • One tub of ricotta covers this batch with a little left over. It goes in as dollops at serving, not stirred through, so a small amount goes a long way visually and texturally.
      • The turkey is nearly half the cost. Buy the 5 pound pack when it is on sale and portion it into freezer bags the day you get home, while you still care.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat a little olive oil in a big pot and brown the ground turkey hard, breaking it up, until it is cooked through to 165F. Let it get some color, because color is flavor in a soup that cooks this fast.
      3. Add the diced onion and minced garlic and cook 5 minutes until soft.
      4. Stir in the italian seasoning, dried basil, red pepper flakes, salt, and black pepper and toast them for one minute.
      5. Add the crushed tomatoes, the diced tomatoes with green chilies, and the beef broth, and simmer 20 minutes so it stops tasting like separate ingredients.
      6. Cook the ziti separately in salted water and drain it. Keep it separate. Pasta cooked in soup keeps swelling in the fridge and by Wednesday you have a casserole.
      7. Stir most of the parmesan into the hot soup and let it melt in, then tear the fresh basil over the pot off the heat.
      8. Move the drained pasta into your weighed pot and hold the ricotta out for the containers.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of drained pasta on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the pasta in each container. Ladle the soup evenly over all 14, then top each with a dollop of ricotta and the rest of the parmesan. Every bowl lands at 562 calories and 41 grams of protein.
