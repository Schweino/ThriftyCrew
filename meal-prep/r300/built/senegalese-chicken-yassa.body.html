      Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 47g protein · 57g carbs · 14g fat · ~$1.51 per serving.

      Yassa is proof that onions are the most underrated ingredient in the grocery store. Six pounds of them cook down into something jammy and almost sweet, sharpened with lemon and dijon mustard, with chicken braised right in the middle of it. Senegal has been making this dish for generations and the ingredient list has barely changed, because it did not need to.       Shop Smart

      • Onions are the star, not the filler, so buy the big mesh bag instead of picking them one at a time. The per-pound price on a two or five pound bag is a fraction of the loose bin, and they keep for a month in a dry cabinet.
      • Bottled lemon juice is the right call here. This recipe wants a real slug of it, and squeezing that much fresh citrus costs several times more for a braise where nobody will taste the difference.
      • Green olives are the second biggest line after the chicken. They are optional if you are pinching, but they are what makes the dish taste finished, so buy the small cans on sale and keep them on the shelf.
      • Dijon mustard is doing heavy lifting for pennies. One jar covers this batch many times over, and the store brand performs exactly like the imported one once it hits a hot pot.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Toss the chicken thighs with the lemon juice, half the sliced onion, the minced garlic, grated ginger, black pepper and salt. Let that sit in the fridge for at least an hour, or overnight if you plan ahead.
      3. Lift the chicken out and shake off the onion. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pot over medium-high and brown the thighs in batches until they have real color, then set them aside.
      4. Dump every bit of the onion, the marinade included, into the pot. Turn the heat to medium and let them cook down for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they collapse into a soft golden jam. This step is the recipe. Do not rush it.
      5. Stir in the dijon mustard, the minced jalapeno and the bay leaves, then pour in the chicken broth and scrape the bottom of the pot clean.
      6. Return the chicken and its juices, cover, and simmer 30 to 35 minutes until the thighs are tender and read 165F.
      7. While it simmers, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes.
      8. Pull the bay leaves, stir in the olives, chop the chicken into bite-size pieces, and fold the cooked rice through the pot so every serving gets sauce and grain together.

      Portion It

      Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Same scoop in every container. Each one carries 567 calories and the onion sauce keeps the chicken from drying out no matter how many times you reheat it.
