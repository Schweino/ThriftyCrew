      Makes 14 servings · ~564 cal · 29g protein · 70g carbs · 16g fat · ~$2.43 per serving.

      Sauerbraten is the German sweet and sour pot roast, and the gravy is thickened with crushed gingersnap cookies. That is not a gimmick, it is how it has been done for centuries, and it produces a sauce nothing else quite matches. The vinegar marinade does the tenderizing before the slow cooker even starts. Each bowl carries 29 grams of protein and 564 calories for about $2.43.

      Shop Smart

      • Gingersnap cookies are the thickener and the sweetness. The plain store brand snaps work better than a soft or frosted cookie, and they cost less.
      • Red wine vinegar is a long life pantry bottle and the marinade uses a real amount of it. Buy the larger bottle, since the per ounce price is usually much better.
      • Marinating overnight is free tenderizing. It means you can use the cheapest chuck in the case and still end up with beef that shreds.
      • Whole cloves and bay leaves cost very little and last a year in the jar. They are what makes this taste German rather than just tangy.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Combine the red wine vinegar with the sliced onion, cloves, bay leaves, salt, and pepper. Submerge the chuck roast in it and refrigerate overnight, turning it once if you think of it.
      3. Lift the roast out and pat it dry, keeping the marinade. Sear it in the vegetable oil until it is browned on all sides.
      4. Set the roast in the slow cooker, pour in the strained marinade along with the beef broth, and add the minced garlic.
      5. Cover and cook on low for 8 hours, until the beef shreds with no effort.
      6. Lift the roast out and shred it. Whisk the flour into a little cold water and stir it into the hot liquid.
      7. Crush the gingersnaps fine and whisk them in with the brown sugar. Cook on high 15 to 20 minutes until the gravy goes thick and glossy.
      8. Return the shredded beef to the gravy and cook the rice separately.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of beef and gingersnap gravy into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 564 calories with 29 grams of protein.
