      Makes 14 servings · ~600 cal · 45g protein · 33g carbs · 32g fat · ~$5.00 per serving.

      Real Hungarian gulyas is not the macaroni skillet the school cafeteria called goulash. It is a slow paprika braise, chuck roast and onions cooked down until the sauce turns deep red and the potatoes go soft at the edges. Each bowl lands at 600 calories and 45 grams of protein for about $5.00 a serving. The pot does the work. You mostly stir and wait.

      Shop Smart

      • Chuck roast is almost the entire cost of this batch. Every other line in the list is pocket change next to it, so time this recipe to a sale on chuck and the per-bowl number falls hard.
      • Green bell peppers are the second biggest produce hit, and green is exactly what the dish wants. Red and yellow cost more and would sweeten a stew that is supposed to be earthy.
      • Caraway seeds look like a single-recipe purchase, but one jar covers many batches. Same with the paprika, which needs to be real Hungarian sweet paprika and needs to be fresh. Old paprika is red dust with no flavor left in it.
      • Canned diced tomatoes beat fresh here on both cost and consistency. You are simmering them for hours, so nobody is going to notice the difference except your wallet.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck into large cubes, pat them dry, and season with salt and pepper. Brown them in the olive oil over medium-high heat in batches, then set the meat aside. A crowded pot steams instead of searing, and the searing is where the flavor comes from.
      3. Drop the butter into the same pot, add the sliced onions, and cook them low and slow until they collapse and turn golden, about 15 minutes. Stir in the garlic for the last minute.
      4. Pull the pot off the burner and stir in all the paprika. This is the one step people get wrong. Paprika scorches in seconds over direct heat and turns bitter, and there is no fixing it after that.
      5. Return the beef, then add the diced tomatoes, chopped green peppers, caraway seeds, bay leaves, and enough chicken broth to nearly cover everything. Bring it up to a bare simmer.
      6. Cover and simmer gently for about 2 hours, stirring every half hour, until a fork slides through the beef with no argument.
      7. Add the carrots and potatoes in big chunks and simmer uncovered another 30 to 40 minutes, until they are tender and the sauce has tightened up. Fish out the bay leaves.
      8. Taste for salt, then let the pot sit off the heat for 10 minutes before you portion it.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your gram target per container. Scoop to the number instead of eyeballing it and every bowl comes out at 600 calories with the same amount of beef in it. Goulash reheats better than almost anything you will make, and day three is honestly the best day.
