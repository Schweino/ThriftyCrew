      Makes 14 servings · ~675 cal · 39g protein · 59g carbs · 30g fat · ~$2.02 per serving.

      Soboro don is the bento box bowl every Japanese kid grows up with. Sweet soy ground beef on one half, fluffy scrambled egg crumbles on the other, rice underneath, green onion over the top. The two halves cook in separate pans and both of them cook in big flat batches, which is exactly the shape we want for fourteen containers. The eggs are not a garnish here either. They push the protein count up considerably for very little money.

      Shop Smart

      • Eggs are the cheapest protein in the store and this dish leans on that. Buying them by the multi-dozen flat instead of single cartons drops the per-egg cost noticeably.
      • Ground beef here is a topping, not the main event. The rice and eggs carry the volume, which is why this bowl lands well under most beef dinners.
      • Mirin and oyster sauce are pantry bottles that keep for ages. They are what give the soboro its glossy sweet-salty finish and they cost almost nothing per batch.
      • Green onions regrow on a windowsill. Stick the white root ends in a glass of water and you get another round of tops in about a week, free.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice according to the package and set it aside to steam.
      3. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt, melt the butter in a wide skillet over low heat, and cook the eggs slowly, stirring constantly with chopsticks or a fork so they break into fine soft crumbles rather than one sheet.
      4. Scrape the egg crumbles out and set them aside.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in the same pan and cook the garlic briefly until fragrant.
      6. Add the ground beef, break it up very fine, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      7. Pour in the soy sauce, mirin, oyster sauce, and sugar, and simmer until the liquid reduces and glazes the beef.
      8. Season with salt and black pepper to taste and slice the green onions for the top.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice, the beef, and the egg separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1 where it applies, and divide each by 14. Rice in first, then the beef down one side and the egg down the other, and green onion across the middle. Every bowl comes in at 675 calories with 39 grams of protein.
