      Makes 14 servings · ~583 cal · 26g protein · 51g carbs · 30g fat · ~$1.36 per serving.

      This is the kind of dish that gets made on a weeknight in a Chinese home and never makes it onto a restaurant menu, because there is nothing to it. Ground pork, a serious amount of grated ginger, and a pile of carrots go in one pan, get a splash of soy and sesame oil, and land on rice. Carrots and ground pork are two of the cheapest items on our grocery board, which is the entire reason this bowl comes in where it does. Do not go light on the ginger. It is the dish.

      Shop Smart

      • Carrots in the bag are one of the best values in produce. Buy the multi-pound bag and shred them yourself. The pre-shredded cups cost several times as much per pound and dry out faster.
      • Ground pork is the protein bargain of the meat case. It consistently undercuts ground beef and it takes ginger and soy far better.
      • Fresh ginger, not the powder. Ground ginger is a completely different flavor and it will not carry this dish. Keep a knob in the freezer and grate from frozen.
      • Sesame oil goes in off the heat. It is the priciest bottle involved and its aroma cooks away, so save it for the very end and you use far less.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice according to the package and set it aside to steam.
      3. Grate or julienne the carrots and grate the ginger, and slice the green onions, keeping the whites and greens separate.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and cook the ginger, garlic, and green onion whites for about thirty seconds.
      5. Add the ground pork, break it up, and cook until browned with no pink remaining.
      6. Add the carrots and cook, stirring often, until they soften and start to sweeten.
      7. Pour in the soy sauce and chicken broth and let it bubble down until the pan is glossy rather than wet.
      8. Take the pan off the heat, stir in the sesame oil and the green onion tops, and season with salt and black pepper.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the pork pot separately, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14 so every container gets an equal base and an equal topping. Every bowl lands near 583 calories with 26 grams of protein.
