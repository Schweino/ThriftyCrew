      Makes 14 servings · ~838 cal · 37g protein · 65g carbs · 48g fat · ~$2.56 per serving.

      Pad kra pao is the dish Thai people actually eat on a Tuesday. Ground pork hits a screaming hot pan with a pile of garlic and chilies, gets a splash of fish sauce, oyster sauce, and soy, and then a mountain of basil goes in at the very end and collapses into it. Five minutes of cooking, and it tastes like something that should have taken an hour. Holy basil is the traditional leaf and it is nearly impossible to find outside a Thai market, so this build uses regular fresh basil, which is the substitution every American cook makes. Note that the numbers below cover the pork itself. Rice is the standard partner and is not counted in this batch.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground pork is the whole point and the whole cost. It is the largest line on this receipt by a wide margin, so buy it when the meat case marks it down and freeze it in batch-sized bags.
      • Fresh basil is the second biggest line, and it is worth it. If you have a sunny windowsill, one basil plant costs about what a single clamshell does and keeps producing all summer.
      • Fish sauce, oyster sauce, and soy sauce are three bottles that never run out fast. They are the backbone of a dozen dinners and each one costs pennies per batch once you own it.
      • Jalapenos stand in for Thai chilies without complaint. They are cheaper, always stocked, and you control the heat by how many seeds you leave in.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mince the garlic and jalapenos together, coarsely, so you get bursts of heat rather than an even burn.
      3. Stir the fish sauce, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, and chicken broth together in a bowl so the sauce goes in all at once later.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in a very large skillet or wok over high heat until it shimmers.
      5. Add the garlic and jalapenos and cook for about thirty seconds, just until fragrant, without letting the garlic brown.
      6. Add the ground pork and cook hard, breaking it up, until it is browned and the liquid has cooked off.
      7. Pour in the sauce mixture and let it bubble and glaze the pork, then season with salt and black pepper to taste.
      8. Kill the heat, dump in all the basil at once, and fold it through until it just wilts.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Scoop that weight into each container. Every serving comes in at 838 calories with 37 grams of protein. The basil darkens overnight, which is normal and does not hurt a thing.
