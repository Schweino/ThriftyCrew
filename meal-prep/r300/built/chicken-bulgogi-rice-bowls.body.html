      Shop Smart

      • Nine ingredients and eight of them are pantry staples. This is the recipe to make when the fridge is empty and payday is Friday, because the only thing you actually have to go buy is the chicken.
      • Soy sauce, brown sugar and sesame oil are all one-time buys that last months. Sesame oil is the one to not cheap out on, because a little goes a long way and the toasted version is what makes this smell like Korean food.
      • The soda in the marinade is a real technique, not a gimmick. The acid and the carbonation tenderize the meat, and a two liter bottle is one of the cheapest things in the store and covers many batches.
      • Boneless thighs are cheaper than breast and they stay juicy under a broiler, which is exactly what this method needs. The four pound club pack is the best per-pound price in the case.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the soy sauce, brown sugar, zero-sugar soda, chicken broth, minced garlic, grated ginger, sesame oil, salt and black pepper into a marinade until the sugar dissolves.
      3. Slice the chicken thighs thin, about a quarter inch, and turn them in the marinade until every piece is coated. Refrigerate at least 4 hours and preferably overnight.
      4. Cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes while the oven heats to 450F.
      5. Lift the chicken out of the marinade, letting the excess drip off, and spread it in a single layer across two sheet pans. Crowding it will steam the meat and you will lose the caramelized edges you came for.
      6. Roast 15 to 18 minutes, then run the broiler for 2 to 3 minutes until the edges char and the sugar in the marinade turns dark and sticky. Watch it closely; sugar goes from beautiful to ruined fast.
      7. Pour the leftover marinade into a small pot, bring it to a hard boil for a full 2 minutes, and reduce it into a glaze. Boiling is not optional, since raw chicken sat in it.
      8. Chop the roasted chicken, toss it in the reduced glaze in your mixing pot, and fold the cooked rice through.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same scoop, all fourteen. Each one carries 580 calories, and this is a good candidate for adding a handful of raw vegetables to each container at reheat time if you want more on the plate.
