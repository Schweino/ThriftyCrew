      Shop Smart

      • Pesto is far and away the biggest line here by a wide margin. Buy the large jar rather than the small one, since the per-ounce price is usually a third lower, and check the refrigerated case against the shelf-stable aisle before you decide.
      • Rotini is the cheap workhorse of the pasta aisle. Any short shape with ridges works, since ridges hold pesto and smooth shapes let it slide off.
      • Feta is the second real cost. Buy the tub in brine rather than the pre-crumbled cup, and crumble it yourself for roughly half the price per ounce.
      • Save a cup of the pasta water before you drain. It costs nothing and it is what turns thick pesto into a sauce that actually coats the noodles instead of clumping.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the rotini in well salted water to just under done. Reserve a cup or two of the starchy water before you drain, because you will want it in a minute.
      3. While the pasta cooks, brown the ground turkey in a large pot, breaking it into crumbles, with the garlic powder, salt, and black pepper, until it is cooked through to 165F.
      4. Strip the kale off its stems and chop it, then stir it into the hot turkey and cook 3 minutes until it wilts and goes dark green.
      5. Add the drained pasta to the pot with the turkey and kale and kill the heat.
      6. Stir in the basil pesto off the heat, adding splashes of the reserved pasta water until the sauce loosens and coats every piece. Off the heat matters, since boiling pesto turns it dull and bitter.
      7. Taste and adjust with salt and pepper, remembering that the feta coming next brings its own salt.
      8. Move everything into your weighed pot and hold the feta back for the containers.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Scoop it out, then crumble the feta over the top of each so it stays in recognizable pieces instead of melting away into the sauce. Every bowl lands at 611 calories and 37 grams of protein.
