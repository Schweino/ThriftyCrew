      Shop Smart

      • Green bell peppers cost noticeably less than red, yellow, or orange. Fajitas char the peppers anyway, so the color difference disappears on the plate.
      • Poblanos are usually sold loose and cheap. They are the difference between fajitas that taste homemade and fajitas that taste like a restaurant, so do not skip them.
      • Bottled lime juice is fine in a marinade where it is doing chemistry rather than garnish work, and it costs far less than a bag of limes you will not finish.
      • Buy sirloin whole and slice it yourself. Pre sliced fajita meat carries a markup and is usually cut with the grain, which is the wrong direction.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the olive oil, orange juice, lime juice, minced garlic, chopped cilantro, cumin, chili powder, oregano, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper into a marinade.
      3. Slice the sirloin thin against the grain, toss it in the marinade, and let it sit at least 30 minutes while you cut the vegetables.
      4. Slice the onions, poblanos, and bell peppers into strips of roughly the same thickness so they cook at the same rate.
      5. Get a wide pan very hot with the vegetable oil and sear the beef in batches, about 90 seconds a side. Pull it out and set it aside.
      6. In the same pan, cook the peppers and onions over high heat until they are charred at the edges but still have some bite.
      7. Return the beef and any juices to the pan, toss everything together for 30 seconds, and kill the heat.
      8. Cook the rice separately and keep the sour cream cold to spoon on at serving.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of steak and peppers over the same weight of rice in each of 14 containers, and keep the sour cream in a separate small container so it does not go watery. Each bowl lands at 568 calories with 32 grams of protein.
