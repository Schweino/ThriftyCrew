      Shop Smart

      • Ketchup, brown sugar, cider vinegar, and mustard are the sauce. All four are long life pantry items, so buy the larger containers and stop paying the small size premium.
      • Chuck roast is the cut that shreds. Buy it on the sale cycle and freeze a second one, because this recipe works just as well with beef that has been frozen.
      • The dry rub is all spices you likely already own. Paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, and cumin cost pennies per batch out of jars you keep anyway.
      • Green onions are a finisher, not a structural ingredient. One bunch covers the whole batch with room to spare.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, cumin, salt, and black pepper into a rub and press it into the chuck roast on all sides.
      3. Sear the roast in the olive oil over high heat until the rub darkens and forms a crust, then move it to the slow cooker.
      4. Pour the beef broth into the bottom of the cooker, cover, and cook on low for 8 hours until the beef shreds under a fork.
      5. While it cooks, whisk the ketchup, brown sugar, apple cider vinegar, Dijon mustard, Worcestershire sauce, and a shot of hot sauce together for the barbecue sauce.
      6. Shred the beef and drain off most of the cooking liquid, leaving just enough to keep it moist.
      7. Pour the barbecue sauce over the shredded beef, stir it through, and cook on high 30 more minutes so the sauce tightens onto the meat.
      8. Cook the rice separately and scatter the sliced green onions over the top.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of saucy beef into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 686 calories with 35 grams of protein.
