      Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 38g protein · 23g carbs · 34g fat · ~$3.70 per serving.

      Kharcho is the soup Georgia built on walnuts. Ground walnuts thicken the broth into something closer to a stew, tomatoes and vinegar keep it sharp, and rice cooked right in the pot makes it a meal instead of a starter. Coriander, cloves, and dill do the rest. Each bowl carries 38 grams of protein and 562 calories for about $3.70.

      Shop Smart

      • Walnuts are cheapest in the baking aisle rather than the snack aisle, and cheapest of all around the holidays. Buy then and keep them in the freezer, where they will not go rancid.
      • Rice cooks directly in the soup and thickens it as it goes. That means no separate pot and no separate cost line for a base.
      • Dried dill stands in for fresh without complaint in a long simmer. A jar lasts a year and costs less than two bunches of the fresh stuff.
      • Chuck roast again. A long simmer wants connective tissue, and connective tissue is exactly what the cheap cuts have.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck into small cubes and simmer them in salted water for about 75 minutes, skimming the foam, until tender. Keep the broth in the pot.
      3. In a separate pan, cook the chopped onion in the vegetable oil until soft and just golden.
      4. Grind the walnuts to a coarse paste. A food processor is easiest, but a bag and a rolling pin works.
      5. Stir the onions, ground walnuts, and diced tomatoes into the beef pot along with the coriander, cloves, cayenne, and garam masala.
      6. Add the rice and simmer gently 20 to 25 minutes, stirring often, until the rice is tender and the soup has thickened.
      7. Stir in the minced garlic, the red wine vinegar, and the dried dill and cook 3 more minutes.
      8. Kill the heat, fold in the chopped cilantro, and taste for salt. Kharcho should be tangy, so add vinegar before you add salt.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight into each of 14 containers, stirring between scoops because the walnuts and rice sink. Each bowl lands at 562 calories with 38 grams of protein.
