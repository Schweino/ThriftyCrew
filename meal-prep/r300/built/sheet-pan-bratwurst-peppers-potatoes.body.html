      Shop Smart

      • The mushrooms and red peppers are the whole story. Together they are more than half the batch cost. Cut either one in half and this recipe drops into normal territory fast.
      • Green peppers instead of red saves real money. Red peppers are just ripened green ones and they carry a hefty premium for it. Roasting sweetens either color.
      • Watch for the sliced mushroom packs on markdown. Mushrooms hit the discount rack constantly because they bruise. They roast down to nothing here, so cosmetic flaws do not matter.
      • Brats on sale are the move. Buy the five packs when the price drops and freeze them. They thaw in the fridge overnight with no loss.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F and get two or three large sheet pans out. Crowding is the enemy of browning, so use more pans than you think you need.
      3. Cut the potatoes into small chunks, slice the onions and red peppers thick, quarter the mushrooms and mince the garlic.
      4. Toss the potatoes with olive oil, dried thyme, salt and black pepper, spread them out, and give them a head start in the oven since they take the longest.
      5. Add the onions, peppers, mushrooms and garlic to the pans and toss everything together with the potatoes.
      6. Lay the bratwurst on top of the vegetables so they roast in the open air and get color on the skin.
      7. Roast until the brats are browned and cooked through and the vegetables have caught at the edges, turning the pans once partway.
      8. Splash the chicken broth onto the hot pans to lift the browned bits, then combine everything with those juices in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, slicing the brats first so the meat spreads evenly. Every bowl lands near 780 calories and 39 grams of protein.
