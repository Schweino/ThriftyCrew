      Makes 14 servings · ~861 cal · 62g protein · 87g carbs · 31g fat · ~$5.62 per serving.

      Peru has its own green sauce and it is not Italian pesto, whatever it looks like. Tallarines verdes leans on spinach for body, basil for perfume, walnuts for richness, and a soft fresh cheese plus milk to make it creamy instead of oily. Crackers get blended in to thicken it, which is the trick Peruvian home cooks have used forever. Con carne means seared steak goes on top. Traditionally the sauce coats long noodles, and this build serves it over a rice base so it holds up through a week of reheats.

      Shop Smart

      • This is one of the priciest dishes in the batch and the beef is why. If you want it closer to the rest of the library, buy the steak deeply discounted or swap in a cheaper braising cut and cook it longer.
      • Fresh basil is the second big line. A basil plant on a windowsill costs about what two clamshells do and keeps producing for months, which is the real fix if you make this more than once.
      • Frozen spinach is the right call over fresh. The sauce gets blended, so the texture does not matter, and frozen costs a fraction per usable ounce because fresh spinach cooks down to almost nothing.
      • Crackers and ricotta are the cheap creaminess. They do the job that heavy cream would do at a lower price and with better body.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Thaw and squeeze the frozen chopped spinach as dry as you can get it. Water is the enemy of this sauce.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a pan and cook the garlic gently until fragrant but not browned.
      4. Blend the spinach, fresh basil, cooked garlic and its oil, walnuts, butter crackers, milk, ricotta cheese, and parmesan cheese until smooth and thick, then season with salt and black pepper.
      5. Cook the rice according to the package to serve underneath.
      6. Season the beef with salt and plenty of black pepper and sear it hard in a very hot pan, in batches, until browned outside and still pink within, then rest it and slice against the grain.
      7. Warm the green sauce gently over low heat, stirring, and thin it with a splash of milk if it has tightened up. Do not boil it or the cheese will go grainy.
      8. Taste and adjust the salt, since spinach and cheese sauces almost always need more than you expect.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice, the sauce, and the sliced beef separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1 where it applies, and divide each by 14. Rice down first, sauce over it, beef on top. Every serving comes in at 861 calories with 62 grams of protein, which is one of the highest protein counts in the batch.
