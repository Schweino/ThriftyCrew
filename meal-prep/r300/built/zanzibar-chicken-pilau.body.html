      Makes 14 servings · ~574 cal · 39g protein · 68g carbs · 14g fat · ~$1.26 per serving.

      Zanzibar built its whole economy on spices, and pilau is what that history tastes like at the dinner table. Red onions get cooked down dark, cumin and cinnamon and cloves go in whole-hearted, and the rice cooks in the same pot as the chicken so every grain ends up brown and perfumed instead of white and bored. One pot, one lid, and each bowl comes in at 574 calories and 39 grams of protein for about $1.26.

      Shop Smart

      • The spice blend is the single largest line in this recipe, which almost never happens. Buy cumin, garam masala, cloves and cinnamon in bags from an international grocer instead of branded supermarket jars and that line drops by more than half.
      • Red onions are the body of the dish and you need a lot of them. Buy the two pound bag rather than loose, and pick the firm ones with tight papery skins so none of them are soft in the middle.
      • Buy rice in the biggest bag you have room for. It is the cheapest calorie in this recipe and it stores indefinitely.
      • Tomato paste in the small can is cheaper per ounce than the tube, and this recipe uses about a whole can, so nothing dries out in the fridge afterward.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Rinse the rice in cold water until the water runs clear, then leave it to soak while you cook. Rinsed rice is what keeps pilau grains separate instead of gluey.
      3. Melt the butter in your largest heavy pot over medium heat and cook the sliced red onion for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring, until it goes past golden and into deep brown. This is where pilau gets its color, and there is no shortcut.
      4. Add the garlic, then the ground cumin, garam masala, ground cloves, ground cinnamon, black pepper, poultry seasoning and bay leaves. Stir for a minute in the hot fat.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it another minute until it turns brick red, then add the chicken thighs cut into chunks and turn them until coated. Cook 5 minutes.
      6. Drain the rice and add it to the pot, folding gently so it takes on the spiced onion. Pour in the chicken broth and beef broth, season with salt, and bring it to a boil.
      7. Drop the heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly, and cook 20 to 25 minutes without lifting the lid. Then pull it off the heat and let it sit covered another 10 minutes.
      8. Fish out the bay leaves and fluff the whole pot with a fork, lifting from the bottom so the browned rice at the base gets distributed.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop that number into each container while the rice is still warm and fluffy. Each bowl carries 574 calories, and a splash of water before reheating brings the rice right back.
