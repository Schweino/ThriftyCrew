      Shop Smart

      • Cook the rice the day before and refrigerate it uncovered. Cold, dried out rice fries into separate grains. Fresh warm rice turns into a sticky clump no matter how good your pan is, and no technique will save it.
      • Eggs are the cheapest protein in the store. Two or three scrambled into a batch of fried rice add real nutrition for pocket change.
      • Chicken breast is used sparingly here on purpose. Fried rice is a rice dish with chicken in it, not the other way around, and stretching a small amount of meat across a big pan is the entire tradition.
      • Soy sauce, sesame oil, and cornstarch are pantry staples that will outlast dozens of batches, so after the first shopping trip this recipe costs almost nothing but rice and eggs.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook the rice a day ahead, spread it on a sheet pan, and chill it uncovered in the fridge overnight. This is not optional if you want real fried rice.
      3. Dice the chicken breast small and toss it with a spoonful of cornstarch, a splash of soy sauce, and the chicken broth. Let it sit 15 minutes so it stays tender in a screaming hot pan.
      4. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt. Heat a little of the vegetable oil in your largest skillet, scramble them just until set, and slide them out onto a plate.
      5. Add more oil, get the pan very hot, and cook the chicken in a single layer until browned and cooked through. Remove it.
      6. Add the rest of the oil and the diced onion and cook 2 minutes, then add the cold rice and press it into the pan. Let it sit undisturbed for a minute at a time so it toasts instead of steaming. Constant stirring is the most common fried rice mistake.
      7. Return the chicken and eggs, add the remaining soy sauce, sesame oil, sugar, salt, and pepper, and toss everything together for two minutes.
      8. Kill the heat and fold in the sliced green onions.

      Portion It

      Set the full skillet on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Toss the pan thoroughly first so the chicken and egg are spread through the rice, otherwise the containers on the bottom of the stack end up as plain rice. Every portion lands at 586 calories.
