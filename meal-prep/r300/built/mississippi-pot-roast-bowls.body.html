      Shop Smart

      • Chuck roast is the correct cut and the cheap one. Do not substitute a lean roast, because there is no added liquid here and the fat in the chuck is what keeps it moist.
      • Ranch seasoning and gravy mix both come in multi packet boxes that cost less per packet than the singles. They keep in the pantry indefinitely.
      • A jar of pepperoncini lasts several batches and the brine is half the flavor, so do not drain it entirely.
      • Butter goes on sale hard around the holidays and freezes for a year. Buy several boxes then and stop paying peak price the rest of the year.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Pat the chuck roast dry and season it with salt and black pepper. Set it in the slow cooker whole.
      3. Sprinkle the ranch seasoning mix and the brown gravy mix evenly over the top of the roast. Do not stir and do not add water.
      4. Lay the butter on top of the roast in slices so it melts down through the seasonings as it cooks.
      5. Scatter the pepperoncini around and over the roast, along with a few spoonfuls of their brine.
      6. Cover and cook on low for 8 hours. Resist opening the lid, since the roast is making its own liquid the whole time.
      7. Shred the beef with two forks directly in the cooker and stir it through the gravy that has collected at the bottom.
      8. Cook the rice separately and keep it plain, because the gravy is doing plenty.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of shredded beef and gravy into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 629 calories with 40 grams of protein.
