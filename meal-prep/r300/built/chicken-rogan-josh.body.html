Makes 14 servings · ~659 cal · 54g protein · 16g carbs · 41g fat · ~$4.26 per serving (at everyday cost).

Rogan josh is a Kashmiri dish, and the authentic version does something almost no other Indian curry does: it uses no onions and no tomatoes at all. The deep red color comes from Kashmiri chiles, which are more about pigment than punishment. The body comes from yogurt, whisked in slowly so it does not split, and the perfume comes from dried ginger and fennel. It is a short list of ingredients doing very precise work. Each serving runs 659 calories and 54 grams of protein for about $4.26 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Milk (Fairlife): 7 tbsp (105 g)
  • Garam Masala (McCormick Gourmet): 2.75 tbsp (17 g)
  • Garlic: 9.25 tbsp (79 g)
  • Greek Yogurt (Great Value): 0.75 cups (214 g)
  • Chili Powder: 10.5 tbsp (84 g)
  • Ground Fennel (generic (USDA)): 2 oz (56 g)
  • Ground Ginger (Great Value): 2 oz (56 g)
  • Ground Coriander (Great Value): 3.5 tbsp (19 g)
  • Ground Cumin: 2.5 tbsp (15 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 2.25 tbsp (42 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 1 cups (245 g)
  • Bay Leaves (Great Value): 0.25 oz (4 g)
  • Dried Guajillo Chiles (generic (USDA)): 3.25 oz (94 g)
  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Thigh (Member's Mark): 7.75 lb (3500 g)
  • Butter (generic): 1 cups (199 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Dried chiles, ground ginger and ground fennel together make up more of this batch than the vegetables in most of our recipes. All three are dramatically cheaper in bags from an international grocer than in branded jars, and all three keep for a year in sealed containers.
    • Greek yogurt is the sauce. Buy the big tub of plain full fat, not the flavored cups, and let it come to room temperature before it goes in the pot so it is less likely to split.
    • Butter and oil are both here for a reason: the oil takes the heat and the butter carries the flavor. Buy butter in the one pound box rather than sticks sold individually, which run higher per ounce.
    • This curry has no rice line, so plan for a starch on the side if you want it. A pot of plain rice adds almost nothing to your cost and stretches the batch further.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Soak the dried chiles in hot water for 20 minutes, then blend them with a little of the soaking liquid into a smooth red paste. That paste is the color and the soul of the dish.
    3. Whisk the greek yogurt with the milk until it is completely smooth and pourable. Cold, thick yogurt is what splits, so take the minute.
    4. Heat the vegetable oil and butter together in your largest heavy pot over medium-high, add the bay leaves, and let them sizzle for 20 seconds.
    5. Add the chicken thighs and sear them, working in batches so the pot stays hot. Return them all to the pot with the minced garlic and cook 2 minutes.
    6. Stir in the chile paste, chili powder, ground fennel, ground ginger, ground coriander, ground cumin and salt and cook 3 to 4 minutes until the oil turns red and separates.
    7. Turn the heat to low and add the yogurt mixture a ladle at a time, stirring constantly and letting each addition come together before the next goes in. Rushing this is how you end up with curdled sauce.
    8. Cover and simmer gently 35 to 40 minutes until the chicken is tender and the sauce is glossy, then stir in the garam masala off the heat.

    Portion It

    Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen even portions, no arguing about it. Each one carries 659 calories, and reheat this one gently at half power, because a yogurt sauce that boils will break on you.
