      This is Tuscan peasant cooking, which is to say it was designed from the start to feed a lot of people for very little. Hot Italian sausage, cannellini beans, tomatoes, potatoes and kale in a broth that thickens itself as the beans break down.       Shop Smart

      • Canned white beans are the cheapest protein in the pot. A few cans deliver more protein per dollar than anything else on this list. Dried cannellini cost even less if you soak them the night before.
      • Chicken broth is the biggest line after the sausage. Buy the large cartons of store brand. In a soup this heavily seasoned, nobody can tell the difference.
      • Kale bunches on sale beat the bagged stuff. Strip the leaves off the stems yourself. It takes two minutes and cuts the price meaningfully.
      • Tomato paste is the cheapest depth you can buy. One small can gives the broth a backbone that diced tomatoes alone will not.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the onion, carrots, celery and potatoes, mince the garlic, and strip the kale leaves off their stems.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large pot and brown the hot Italian sausage, breaking it into crumbles, until it has real color on it.
      4. Add the onion, carrots and celery and cook until softened, then add the garlic, italian seasoning, dried thyme and oregano for a minute.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it for two minutes so it darkens, then add the diced tomatoes, the chicken broth and the bay leaves.
      6. Add the cubed potatoes and simmer until they are tender and the broth has picked up their starch.
      7. Stir in the drained cannellini beans and cook long enough for them to warm through and start softening the broth.
      8. Fold in the kale at the very end and cook just until it wilts, then pull the bay leaves and season with salt and black pepper.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount into each container, stirring the pot between scoops so the beans and sausage spread out. Every bowl lands near 569 calories and 29 grams of protein.
