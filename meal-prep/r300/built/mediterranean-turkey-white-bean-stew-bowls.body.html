      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 29g protein · 84g carbs · 11g fat · ~$1.95 per serving.

      This is the stew that quietly beats every protein bar on the shelf for cost per gram. Ground turkey, cannellini beans, kale, olives, and tomatoes simmer into something thick and savory, then it goes over rice with fresh basil on top. Beans are the reason it works: they carry protein and fiber for pennies. Each bowl comes to 561 calories and 29 grams of protein for about $1.95.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned cannellini beans are among the cheapest cans in the store and they add protein for a fraction of what the meat costs. Any time a recipe lets beans carry part of the protein load, your cost per gram drops through the floor.
      • Kale by the bunch is cheap and cooks down to nothing. Strip the leaves off the stems, and do not pay triple for the pre-washed bag of the same plant.
      • Fresh basil is the priciest herb in this batch. If it is out of season, cut the amount and lean on the italian seasoning instead, or grow a plant on the sill for the price of one clamshell.
      • Olives are the one splurge, and they earn it by making the whole pot taste briny and finished. Buy the canned sliced ones, not the deli bar, and you save half.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the olive oil in a heavy pot and cook the diced onion for 6 minutes, then add the garlic and italian seasoning for one minute.
      3. Add the ground turkey and brown it, breaking it apart as it goes, until it is cooked through to 165F.
      4. Stir in the diced tomatoes, the chicken broth, the bay leaves, salt, and black pepper, and simmer 15 minutes.
      5. Drain and rinse the cannellini beans and add them, then simmer another 10 minutes so they take on the flavor of the pot without falling apart.
      6. Stir in the chopped kale and the sliced olives and cook 5 minutes more, just until the kale wilts and goes deep green.
      7. Pull the bay leaves, add the lemon juice, and stir in most of the torn basil. Save some for the top.
      8. Cook the rice, move it into your weighed pot, and keep the stew in its own pot for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Ladle the stew evenly over all 14, stirring the pot between scoops so the beans and turkey do not all settle into the first few, then finish each with the reserved basil. Each bowl lands at 561 calories and 29 grams of protein.
