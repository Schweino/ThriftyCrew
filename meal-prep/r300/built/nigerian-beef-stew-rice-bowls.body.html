      Makes 14 servings · ~588 cal · 29g protein · 54g carbs · 27g fat · ~$3.14 per serving.

      This is the red stew that sits at the center of Nigerian home cooking, the one that gets spooned over rice at every table and every party. The beef gets boiled first so its stock becomes part of the sauce, then a blended pepper base is fried down until the oil separates and the color goes deep. Each bowl carries 29 grams of protein and 588 calories for about $3.14.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned diced tomatoes are the backbone and you need a stack of them. This is a case buy, so watch for the multi can promotions and stock the shelf when they hit.
      • Boiling the beef first is not a shortcut, it is the point. That liquid becomes the stock, so do not pour it down the drain and do not buy extra broth to replace it.
      • Bell peppers are the priciest produce on this list. Red ones look prettier in photos but green ones cost less and are what most home cooks actually use.
      • The stew freezes flat and thaws clean. Make the full batch even if you only need half this week, because the second half costs you nothing extra in effort.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the chuck roast into chunks and boil it with some of the onion, the bay leaves, thyme, curry powder, and salt until just tender. Strain and keep the stock.
      3. Blend the diced tomatoes with the bell peppers, jalapeno, remaining onion, garlic, and ginger until smooth.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil and olive oil in a wide pot and pour in the blended base. Fry it, stirring, until it thickens noticeably and the raw edge cooks off.
      5. Stir in the tomato paste and keep frying a few more minutes so the paste loses its metallic taste.
      6. Add the boiled beef, the reserved stock, and the beef broth. Simmer uncovered 30 to 40 minutes until the sauce is thick enough to sit on a spoon.
      7. Taste and correct the salt and pepper. The stew should taste sharper than you think, because the rice will mute it.
      8. Cook the rice separately and keep it plain.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight of stew over the same weight of rice in each of 14 containers. Each bowl lands at 588 calories with 29 grams of protein.
