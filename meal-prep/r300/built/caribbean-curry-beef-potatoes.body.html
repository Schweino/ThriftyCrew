      Makes 14 servings · ~586 cal · 30g protein · 60g carbs · 25g fat · ~$2.89 per serving.

      Trinidadian curry beef starts somewhere most curries do not: you burn the curry powder in hot oil first, on purpose, until it goes dark and nutty. Then the beef goes in, the potatoes go in later, and the whole thing simmers into its own thick gravy. Green seasoning does the rest. Each bowl carries 30 grams of protein and 586 calories for about $2.89.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy potatoes in the ten pound bag. The per pound price is usually less than half of what the small bags run, and this recipe uses a serious amount of them.
      • Curry powder is the soul of the dish and Caribbean blends are different from Indian ones. If you can find a Trinidadian style blend, buy the big bag, it will not sit unused.
      • Green seasoning is just cilantro, green onion, garlic, onion, and hot pepper blended together. Make a double batch and freeze half in an ice cube tray for the next cook.
      • Chuck roast holds up to a long curry simmer better than any expensive cut would. Cheaper and better is a rare combination, so take it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Blend most of the cilantro, the green onions, the jalapeno, half the garlic, and some of the onion into a rough green seasoning. Rub it into the cubed chuck roast and let it sit at least 30 minutes.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot, add the curry powder and cumin, and stir constantly for 1 to 2 minutes until it darkens and smells toasted. Do not walk away.
      4. Add the remaining chopped onion and garlic and cook until soft.
      5. Add the seasoned beef and stir until every piece is coated in curry, then let it sear and stick a little before you add liquid.
      6. Stir in the chopped tomato and the ketchup, add enough water to nearly cover, and simmer covered 60 to 75 minutes until the beef is tender.
      7. Add the peeled and chunked potatoes and simmer uncovered another 25 to 30 minutes, until they are soft and starting to break down and thicken the gravy.
      8. Taste for salt and pepper and finish with the last of the chopped cilantro.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight into each of 14 containers, stirring between scoops so the beef does not all end up in the first four. Each bowl lands at 586 calories with 30 grams of protein.
