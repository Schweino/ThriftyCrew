      Makes 14 servings · ~584 cal · 28g protein · 90g carbs · 10g fat · ~$1.80 per serving.

      Mexican albondigas are not Italian meatballs with different seasoning. They are softer, bound with bread soaked in milk, and they finish by simmering directly in the sauce rather than getting browned to a crust first. That means they drink up the chipotle sauce from the inside out. The sauce itself is blended tomatoes and chipotles in adobo, smoky and just hot enough to notice. Everything goes over rice, and the whole thing cooks in one pot after the meatballs are rolled.

      Shop Smart

      • Bread and milk stretch the beef, and that is on purpose. The soaked bread panade is traditional, it keeps the meatballs tender, and it means less beef per serving without anyone noticing.
      • One can of chipotles in adobo lasts a long time. Freeze the leftovers in a covered ice cube tray and pull out one cube at a time for future batches.
      • Canned diced tomatoes beat fresh here. They are cheaper year round, they are already peeled, and this sauce gets blended anyway so texture does not matter.
      • Cilantro goes in at the end. It loses everything if it simmers, so save the whole bunch for the finish where you actually taste it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Tear the sandwich bread into pieces, soak it in the milk until soft, then mash it into a paste.
      3. Mix the ground beef with the bread paste, eggs, some of the garlic, salt, and black pepper, and roll it into evenly sized meatballs.
      4. Blend the diced tomatoes with the chipotles in adobo, the yellow onion, and the rest of the garlic into a smooth sauce.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot and pour in the blended sauce carefully, since it will spit. Cook it down for several minutes until it darkens and thickens.
      6. Add the chicken broth, bring the sauce to a gentle simmer, and lower the raw meatballs in one by one.
      7. Cover and simmer gently, without stirring for the first fifteen minutes so the meatballs set, until they are cooked through.
      8. Cook the rice according to the package, then finish the pot with the chopped cilantro and adjust the salt.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the meatball pot separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Count the meatballs out evenly first, then even the containers up with sauce by weight. Every bowl lands near 584 calories with 28 grams of protein.
