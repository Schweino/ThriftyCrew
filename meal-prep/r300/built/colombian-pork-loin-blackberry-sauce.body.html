      Makes 14 servings · ~650 cal · 55g protein · 74g carbs · 13g fat · ~$2.56 per serving.

      Lomo de cerdo con salsa de moras is a Colombian classic, and it is the rare fruit sauce that is genuinely tart instead of just sweet. Roasted garlic rubbed pork loin, sliced thin, under a blackberry sauce cooked down with lime and a little sugar, all over rice. Pork loin is one of the leanest cuts we track, so this plate carries 55 grams of protein for about $2.56.

      Shop Smart

      • Watch the blackberries. They are more than half the cost of this entire batch. In peak season the price falls hard, and this is the recipe to make when the board shows berries cheap.
      • Frozen blackberries work perfectly here. The sauce gets cooked down and strained anyway, so nobody can tell. Frozen is usually the cheaper and steadier option out of season.
      • Pork loin sells cheapest as a whole roast. Buy the untrimmed loin rather than pre cut medallions and cut it yourself. The per pound gap is not small.
      • Buy garlic by the head. This recipe uses a lot of it, and a couple of heads is still one of the cheapest lines on the list. Jarred garlic costs more and tastes like the jar.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 375F. Pat the pork loin dry and rub it all over with minced garlic, salt and black pepper.
      3. Sear the loin in a hot pan on every side until it takes on color, then move it to a roasting pan.
      4. Roast until the center reaches 145F, then pull it out and let it rest at least ten minutes before you touch it. Cutting early dumps all the juice on the board.
      5. While the pork roasts, put the blackberries, sugar, lime juice and chicken broth in a saucepan and simmer until the berries collapse.
      6. Press the sauce through a strainer to catch the seeds, then return it to the pan and reduce until it coats a spoon.
      7. Cook the rice per the package so it is hot when everything else is ready.
      8. Slice the rested pork thin against the grain, then combine the rice, sliced pork and blackberry sauce in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, keeping the sliced pork evenly counted so the protein lands the same in every one.
