      Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 35g protein · 62g carbs · 17g fat · ~$2.04 per serving.

      Kefta mkaouara is the Moroccan dish that taught the rest of the world you can build a whole dinner out of small spiced meatballs and a pot of tomatoes. Turkey kefta go in with paprika, cumin, cinnamon, and a fistful of cilantro, the sauce cooks down thick, and an egg finishes each portion. It runs 561 calories and 35 grams of protein for about $2.04, which is a lot of flavor for pocket change.

      Shop Smart

      • Canned diced tomatoes are the entire sauce, so buy them deep when they hit sale. They do not spoil, they are cheap on a normal week, and they get cheaper on a good one.
      • Eggs land at roughly one per container here and still cost very little for the whole batch. When protein is that cheap, use it.
      • One bunch of cilantro covers this batch with leaves to spare. Stand the stems in a jar of water in the fridge and you get another week out of it instead of composting half a bunch like most people do.
      • Rice from the big bag is where the price stays low. It is the cheapest bulk calorie in the store and it is the reason this bowl beats a drive-thru value meal.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Grate about a third of the onions and mix them into the ground turkey with half the minced garlic, half the chopped cilantro, paprika, cumin, cinnamon, salt, and black pepper.
      3. Roll the mixture into small meatballs, roughly an inch across. Small is the whole point here, so resist the urge to make them big.
      4. Slice the remaining onions and soften them in the olive oil over medium heat for about 8 minutes, then add the rest of the garlic and cook one minute more.
      5. Add the diced tomatoes with more paprika and cumin, season with salt and pepper, and simmer 15 minutes until the sauce pulls together.
      6. Nestle the meatballs into the sauce, cover, and simmer 25 to 30 minutes until they are cooked through to 165F and the sauce is thick enough to hold a spoon track.
      7. Cook the rice while the tagine simmers, then crack the eggs into wells in the sauce, cover, and cook 6 to 8 minutes until the whites are set.
      8. Move the finished rice into your weighed pot and leave the meatballs, sauce, and eggs in the pan so you can divide them evenly on top.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then split the meatballs, sauce, and eggs evenly across all 14 so nobody ends up with the sad container. Each one lands at 561 calories and 35 grams of protein.
