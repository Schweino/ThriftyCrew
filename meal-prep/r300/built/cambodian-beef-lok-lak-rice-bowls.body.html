      Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 36g protein · 75g carbs · 12g fat · ~$3.21 per serving.

      Lok lak means to shake, which is what you do to the pan while the beef sears. Cambodia's version leans hard on black pepper, and Kampot pepper from the southern coast is what makes people write love letters about this dish. Beef gets marinated in oyster sauce and soy, seared fast and hard, then piled over rice with tomato and red onion. Lime juice goes over the top at the end. Fast, sharp, and completely unlike anything else in our library.

      Shop Smart

      • The beef is the entire cost of this dish. Buy steak when the case marks it down and freeze it. Slicing it thin means a smaller amount goes a long way per bowl.
      • Freeze the steak thirty minutes before slicing. Firm meat slices thin with a regular knife, and thin slices sear in seconds instead of stewing in their own juice.
      • Black pepper is the star, so grind it fresh. Pre-ground pepper has already lost most of what makes this dish worth making, and a peppermill pays for itself fast.
      • Oyster sauce and kecap manis are two bottles that keep for months. They are what give the marinade its depth, and they cost pennies per batch once they are in your pantry.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Firm the beef in the freezer for about thirty minutes, then slice it thin against the grain.
      3. Toss the beef with the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sweet soy sauce, garlic, sugar, a pinch of baking soda, the all-purpose flour, and a heavy hand of freshly ground black pepper, and let it sit at least twenty minutes.
      4. Cook the rice according to the package while the beef marinates.
      5. Slice the red onions and cut the drained tomatoes into wedges, then set them aside.
      6. Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet until it is smoking, and sear the beef in batches in a single layer, shaking the pan, until browned outside and barely done inside.
      7. Return all the beef to the pan with any marinade left in the bowl and let it bubble down into a glaze for about a minute.
      8. Kill the heat, add the lime juice, more black pepper, and salt to taste, and toss the red onion and tomato through so they warm without cooking.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice pot and the beef pan separately, subtracting the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide each by 14. Even rice base, even weight of beef and sauce, and make sure the onion and tomato get spread around. Every bowl comes in at 570 calories with 36 grams of protein.
