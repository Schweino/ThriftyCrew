      Makes 14 servings · ~644 cal · 53g protein · 48g carbs · 24g fat · ~$1.72 per serving.

      Senfbraten is a German Sunday roast: a pork loin slathered thick with mustard, seared, then braised low with onions and carrots until the pan liquid becomes a mustard gravy you will want to drink. It is the leanest, highest protein plate in this whole batch at 53 grams for about $1.72, which is a rare combination.

      Shop Smart

      • A whole pork loin is one of the best protein deals we track. Buying the whole roast rather than cut chops drops the price per pound, and loin is lean enough that you are paying for meat, not trim.
      • Mustard is the flavor and it is nearly free. This uses a real amount of it and it still barely registers on the cost list, because a jar of dijon goes a long way.
      • Broth carries the gravy, so buy the big carton. Beef broth does most of the work here and chicken broth rounds it out. Store brand is fine in a mustard gravy.
      • Skip the wine. The traditional version adds white wine, but the broth and mustard carry this on their own, and that is one whole bottle you do not have to buy.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Pat the pork loin dry, season it with salt and black pepper, and coat the whole surface in a thick layer of the dijon mustard. Hold a couple of spoonfuls back for the gravy.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot or roasting pan and sear the mustard coated loin on all sides until it browns.
      4. Take the roast out and cook the sliced onions, the garlic and the sliced carrots in the same pot until they soften and pick up the fond.
      5. Pour in the beef broth and chicken broth, add the bay leaves and dried thyme, and scrape the bottom of the pot clean.
      6. Set the roast back in, cover, and braise low until the center reaches 145F and the meat slices easy.
      7. Move the roast to a board to rest. Reduce the braising liquid on the stovetop, whisk in the butter and the reserved mustard, and taste it for salt.
      8. Slice the rested pork thin against the grain, cook the rice separately, and combine everything with the gravy in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, counting the pork slices so the protein lands even. Every bowl comes in near 644 calories with 53 grams of protein.
