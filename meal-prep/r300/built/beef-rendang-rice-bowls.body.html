      Makes 14 servings · ~836 cal · 38g protein · 71g carbs · 43g fat · ~$4.28 per serving.

      Rendang is Indonesian beef cooked in coconut milk until the liquid disappears entirely and what is left clings to the meat like a dark, fragrant paste. It is not a curry you eat with a spoon. It is beef that has absorbed everything in the pot. That dryness is also why it is one of the best meal prep dishes there is, because there is no sauce to separate or go watery by Thursday. Each serving carries 836 calories and 38 grams of protein for about $4.28 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • The spice paste is onion, garlic, ginger, and jalapeno blitzed together, which is the cheapest way in the world to build enormous flavor. Buy the onions and garlic in bulk bags and the whole paste costs less than a single jar of curry sauce.
      • Canned coconut milk is the second biggest line and the one thing you should not cut corners on. Full fat is what breaks and fries the beef at the end, and light coconut milk will simply never get there.
      • Lemongrass paste in a tube keeps for months in the fridge and saves you from buying fresh stalks you will use once. One tube covers several batches.
      • Chuck roast is the right cut and the cheap one. Rendang cooks for hours, so a tender expensive cut would just fall apart into nothing and cost you more for the privilege.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Blend the onion, garlic, ginger, and jalapeno into a rough paste. Add a splash of water if the blender needs help moving.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide heavy pot and fry the paste over medium heat until it darkens and smells sweet rather than raw, about 10 minutes. This is the single most important step, so do not shortcut it.
      4. Stir in the lemongrass paste, turmeric, cinnamon, bay leaves, and black pepper and cook another minute.
      5. Add the beef cut into large cubes and turn it in the paste until every piece is coated.
      6. Pour in the coconut milk with the brown sugar and salt, bring it to a simmer, and cook uncovered on low for 2 to 3 hours. Stir every 20 minutes or so, more often near the end.
      7. Keep going past the point where it looks done. The liquid will reduce, then break, then the beef starts frying in the coconut oil that separates out and everything turns deep brown. That is rendang, and it is a different dish from the milky stew it was an hour earlier.
      8. Taste for salt and pull it off the heat while there is still a little gloss on the meat.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop to that gram target and each container lands at 836 calories. Rendang is dense, so the portions look smaller than you expect and eat bigger than you expect. Trust the scale over your eyes.
