      Shop Smart

      • Sausage is two thirds of the cost. Everything else on the list combined is a small fraction of the total. Buy brats on sale, freeze them, and this recipe gets meaningfully cheaper.
      • Potatoes and carrots are the cheap bulk. Bagged potatoes and bagged carrots fill both pans for very little, and both keep for weeks.
      • Red onions cost more than yellow. They look prettier roasted, but if the board shows a gap, yellow onions do the identical job for less.
      • The gravy is butter, flour and broth. That is a small line on the cost list and it beats any jarred gravy you can buy.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Heat the oven to 425F. Cut the potatoes into chunks, cut the carrots into thick batons, and cut the red onions into wedges.
      3. Toss the vegetables with the olive oil, minced garlic, dried thyme, dried oregano, salt and black pepper, then spread them across two large sheet pans.
      4. Nestle the bratwurst among the vegetables and roast until the sausages are browned and the vegetables are tender and caught at the edges.
      5. Move the sausages and vegetables to a holding pan and keep them warm. Do not wash the roasting pan, because everything stuck to it is the gravy.
      6. Set that pan across two burners, melt the butter in it, and scrape hard while it bubbles to lift all the browned bits.
      7. Whisk in the flour and cook it for a minute, then pour in the beef broth in a steady stream while whisking until the gravy thickens.
      8. Slice the sausages, combine them with the vegetables in your big pot, and pour the gravy over the top.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, counting sausage slices so the protein lands even. Every bowl lands near 856 calories and 35 grams of protein.
