      Minestrone was never a recipe. It was Italian for whatever is in the garden plus beans plus pasta, and that is exactly why it belongs in a budget kitchen. Ours is built with turkey breast, cannellini beans, and small shells so it eats like dinner rather than a first course. Each bowl comes to 561 calories and 39 grams of protein for about $2.29.

      Shop Smart

      • Cannellini beans are the cheapest protein in this pot. Beans and pasta together are the reason a soup like this can carry 39 grams of protein without a big meat bill.
      • The vegetables are meant to flex. Carrot, celery, and zucchini are the classic base, but this is the one recipe in the library where you should buy whatever is marked down and use it.
      • Small shells are one of the cheapest boxes in the pasta aisle. Cook them separately from the soup, because pasta left sitting in broth turns to porridge by day two and you lose the whole batch.
      • A tub of parmesan lasts months and finishes every bowl. If your store sells parmesan rinds cheap, drop one into the simmering pot and it adds savory depth for almost nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the onion, carrot, and celery small and cook them in the olive oil in a big pot for 10 minutes until soft and sweet. This is the base of the soup and rushing it is the most common mistake.
      3. Add the minced garlic, the oregano, dried basil, red pepper flakes, salt, and black pepper, and cook one minute.
      4. Cut the turkey breast into small cubes, add them to the pot, and cook until they are no longer pink and reach 165F.
      5. Add the crushed tomatoes and the chicken broth and simmer 20 minutes so the flavors come together.
      6. Add the diced zucchini and the drained cannellini beans and simmer 10 minutes more, until the zucchini is tender but not falling apart.
      7. Stir in the chopped kale for the last 3 minutes and taste for salt one more time.
      8. Cook the pasta shells separately, drain them, and move them into your weighed pot.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of drained pasta on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the pasta in each container. Ladle the soup evenly across all 14, digging deep so the beans and turkey get shared out, then grate parmesan over each. Every bowl lands at 561 calories and 39 grams of protein.
