      Makes 14 servings · ~609 cal · 29g protein · 68g carbs · 23g fat · ~$2.58 per serving.

      Japchae is the Korean party dish, sweet potato glass noodles tossed with vegetables in sesame and soy until every strand goes glossy. Those noodles are called dangmyeon and they are the reason this holds up in a container: they stay chewy and springy for days instead of going mushy the way wheat noodles do. We add turkey to turn a side dish into dinner, at 609 calories and 29 grams of protein for about $2.58.

      Shop Smart

      • Korean glass noodles come in a big bag that covers this batch several times over for a small share of the batch. Find them at an Asian market where a large pack costs less than the small grocery store box.
      • Frozen chopped spinach is the second biggest line. Fresh spinach would cost several times more for the same cooked volume, since it wilts down to almost nothing.
      • Red bell peppers are one of the pricier produce lines. Buy them in the seasonal window or swap in whatever pepper is cheapest that week, because in a dish this heavily sauced the color matters more than the variety.
      • Sesame oil is the signature flavor and one bottle lasts many batches. Buy the toasted kind, keep it in the fridge so it does not go rancid, and use it as a finisher rather than a cooking oil.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Boil the Korean glass noodles (dangmyeon) for 6 to 7 minutes until springy, drain them, rinse them cold, and cut through the pile a few times with scissors so they are eatable with a fork.
      3. Beat the eggs, cook them thin in an oiled pan like a flat omelet, slide it out, and slice it into ribbons for the top.
      4. Whisk the sauce: soy sauce, brown sugar, sesame oil, and the minced garlic. Set it aside where you can reach it.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in a wide pan and brown the ground turkey, breaking it up, until it is cooked through to 165F, then move it to a bowl.
      6. Stir-fry the vegetables in stages so each one keeps its own texture: mushrooms first, then onion and carrot, then red bell pepper, then the thawed and squeezed spinach. Move each batch to the bowl as it finishes.
      7. Return everything to the pan with the noodles, pour the sauce over, and toss over medium heat for 3 minutes until the noodles turn dark and glossy and every strand is coated.
      8. Kill the heat, fold in the green onions, and move it into your weighed pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Use tongs and lift from the bottom so the vegetables and turkey ride along with the noodles, then lay the egg ribbons across the top of each. Every serving lands at 609 calories and 29 grams of protein.
