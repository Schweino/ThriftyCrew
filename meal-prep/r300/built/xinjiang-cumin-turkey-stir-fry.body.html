      Shop Smart

      • Turkey breast is two thirds of the batch. Frozen roasts are the cheapest form by a wide margin, and the post-holiday clearance shelf is where the smart money buys turkey for the whole year.
      • Dried arbol chilies cost very little for a bag that covers several batches. Whole dried chilies keep for a year in a jar and beat any bottled chili sauce on both price and punch.
      • Buy cumin in the large bag from an international market instead of the little grocery store jar. This recipe uses a serious amount of it, and the price difference between the two aisles is usually four to one.
      • Baking soda is the cheap restaurant trick in this recipe. A few cents worth tenderizes lean turkey so it stays soft through a hard sear, which is the difference between a stir-fry and a chew toy.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Slice the turkey breast into thin strips against the grain, then toss them with the baking soda and a splash of water and let them sit 20 minutes. This is velveting, and it is why restaurant stir-fry meat is tender and yours usually is not.
      3. Rinse the turkey well, pat it dry, then toss it with the soy sauce, chicken broth, and the flour so each strip has a light coating.
      4. Heat the vegetable oil in a wok or heavy skillet until it is nearly smoking. Sear the turkey in batches, giving each batch room, until browned and cooked through to 165F. Crowding the pan is how you steam meat by accident.
      5. Pour off most of the oil, then toast the dried arbol chilies and the cumin for 30 seconds until the kitchen makes you cough a little. That is the correct amount.
      6. Add the sliced onion, grated ginger, and minced garlic and stir-fry 3 minutes until the onion softens at the edges but still has structure.
      7. Return all the turkey, add a pinch of sugar, salt, and black pepper, and toss over high heat for 2 minutes so everything gets coated in spice. It should look dry, not saucy.
      8. Kill the heat, fold in the chopped cilantro and sesame seeds, then cook the rice and move it into your weighed pot.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then divide the stir-fry evenly across all 14, and pull the whole dried chilies out as you go unless you want a surprise on Wednesday. Each bowl lands at 561 calories and 37 grams of protein.
