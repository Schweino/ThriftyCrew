      Shop Smart

      • Flank and sirloin are both correct here and whichever is cheaper that week is the right answer. Slice against the grain and thin, and either one comes out tender.
      • Bell peppers are the second biggest line. Green are cheaper than red almost every week, so if the budget is tight, run all green and the dish loses nothing but a little color.
      • Cornstarch is the cheapest ingredient in the recipe and the most important one. It thickens the sauce into that glossy coat and it also velvets the beef if you toss the slices in a little before searing.
      • Soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, and honey are all pantry bottles that last through many batches. Once they are on the shelf, the per-bowl cost of this sauce is close to zero.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Freeze the steak for about 30 minutes so it firms up, then slice it thin against the grain. Toss the slices with a spoonful of the cornstarch and a splash of the soy sauce and let them sit while you prep everything else.
      3. Start the rice.
      4. Whisk the rest of the soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, sesame oil, and remaining cornstarch together with a little water. Having the sauce ready in a cup before you start is the whole secret to stir frying, because once the pan is hot there is no time to measure anything.
      5. Get a large skillet very hot with the vegetable oil and sear the beef in batches, about 90 seconds a side. Pull it out while it is still slightly underdone. It will finish in the sauce.
      6. Add the sliced peppers, onion, and jalapeno to the same pan and cook hard for 3 to 4 minutes. You want them blistered at the edges and still crisp in the middle, not soft.
      7. Add the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds, then pour in the sauce and let it bubble and thicken.
      8. Return the beef and any juices, toss everything together for a minute, and season with paprika, salt, and plenty of black pepper.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into the containers first, then set the skillet on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That gets an even share of steak into every bowl, which matters more here than in almost any other recipe because the peppers are easy to over-scoop. Each portion lands at 577 calories.
