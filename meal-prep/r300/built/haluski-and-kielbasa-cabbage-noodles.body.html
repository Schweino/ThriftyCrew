      Haluski is Polish home cooking in its purest form: cabbage cooked down in butter until it goes sweet and golden, tossed with wide egg noodles. Add sliced smoked sausage and it stops being a side dish and becomes dinner. Five ingredients, one skillet, 25 grams of protein, and about $1.42 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • Cabbage is the best value vegetable in the store, period. Two heads cover this whole batch and a head keeps in the fridge for weeks without wilting on you.
      • The smoked sausage is the budget. It is most of the cost line by itself, so buy the ropes when they go on sale and freeze them. They freeze and thaw without losing a thing.
      • Wide egg noodles beat fancy pasta here. Store brand twelve ounce bags are the cheapest way to buy them and the wide cut is what holds the buttery cabbage.
      • Do not skimp on the butter. It is one of the smallest lines on the cost list and it is the entire reason haluski tastes like anything. This is not the line to cut.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Core the cabbage and slice it into thin ribbons. Slice the onion thin as well.
      3. Boil the egg noodles in well salted water until just barely tender, then drain them and set them aside.
      4. Slice the smoked sausage into coins and brown them in a dry skillet until the edges char a little, then move them to a plate.
      5. Melt the butter in that same skillet and cook the onion until it turns soft and translucent.
      6. Add the cabbage in batches, letting each addition wilt down before you add more. Cook it slowly until it goes golden and sweet. Rushing this is the one mistake people make.
      7. Season with salt and black pepper, then return the sausage to the pan and stir it through.
      8. Fold the drained noodles into the cabbage and sausage in your big pot until everything is coated in butter.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, tossing the pot between scoops so the sausage does not sink to the bottom. Every bowl lands near 595 calories and 25 grams of protein.
