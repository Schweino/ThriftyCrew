      Makes 14 servings · ~670 cal · 54g protein · 66g carbs · 20g fat · ~$2.58 per serving.

      Sudado means sweated, and that is exactly what happens here. Pork chops go into a covered pot with tomato, onion, green onion and cilantro, and everything steams down into its own sauce with hardly any liquid added. Potatoes and carrots cook right in it. In Colombia this is Tuesday dinner, served over white rice, and it is one of the most efficient one-pot dishes on the planet.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the family pack of bone-in pork chops. Bone-in costs less per pound than boneless and the bones make the sauce taste like it simmered all day.
      • Potatoes in the big bag are the cheapest calories in the store. This pot uses a lot of them, and buying the ten pound bag instead of loose is one of the biggest single savings in the whole recipe.
      • Sazon seasoning is the shortcut Colombian cooks actually use. A box of packets costs very little and it delivers the color and savor that would otherwise take a long list of jars.
      • Green onions and cilantro are the finish, not the base. One bunch of each is plenty. Chop them in at the very end so they stay bright instead of stewing into nothing.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Season the pork chops with salt, black pepper, ground cumin and the sazon seasoning, then brown them in the vegetable oil in a heavy pot, in batches, until both sides have color.
      3. Cook the diced onion and minced garlic in the same pot until soft, scraping up whatever the pork left behind.
      4. Add the diced tomatoes with their juice and let them cook down for about five minutes into a thick base.
      5. Return the pork chops to the pot, tuck in the sliced carrots and the peeled and quartered potatoes, cover tightly, and cook on low heat for about an hour.
      6. Resist adding water. The vegetables and tomato give off enough liquid to make the sauce, and that is where the flavor concentration comes from.
      7. When the potatoes are fork tender, stir in the chopped green onions and cilantro and taste for salt. Cook the rice per the package to serve underneath.

      Portion It

      Spread the cooked rice across your 14 containers, then set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight of pork, potatoes and sauce over the rice. Every plate lands near 670 calories with 54 grams of protein, which is one of the highest protein numbers in this batch.
