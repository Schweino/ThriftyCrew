      Makes 14 servings · ~670 cal · 53g protein · 58g carbs · 22g fat · ~$1.42 per serving.

      Smothered chicken is Southern cooking at its most practical. You dredge the chicken in seasoned flour, brown it, build a roux gravy in the same pan with the drippings, then put the chicken back and let it finish in the gravy. Nothing gets wasted and nothing gets dry. It is also one of the best reheating dishes there is, because the gravy protects the meat all week. Each bowl carries 670 calories and 53 grams of protein for about $1.42 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Boneless thighs are the entire protein budget and they cost a fraction of breast. For a gravy braise there is no argument to be made for the more expensive cut.
      • Flour is the cheapest thickener in existence. A roux made from flour and the fat already in your pan replaces every gravy packet and jar in the store, and it tastes better.
      • Onions are the flavor of this dish, not a background note. Buy them in the bag and cook them long enough to go truly golden, because that sweetness is what makes Southern onion gravy taste like something.
      • Cajun seasoning, paprika, thyme, onion powder, and garlic powder are jars you likely already own. If you are buying, get whole containers rather than the small bottles, since the price per ounce is much better and these turn up constantly.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the flour with the cajun seasoning, paprika, cayenne, onion powder, garlic powder, thyme, salt, and pepper. Dredge the chicken thighs in it and shake off the excess. Keep the leftover seasoned flour, because that is your roux.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy skillet and brown the chicken on both sides until it has real color. Set it aside. It does not need to be cooked through.
      4. Add the butter to the same pan and cook the sliced onions over medium heat until they are deep golden, about 15 minutes. Patience here is the whole recipe.
      5. Add the garlic, then sprinkle in the reserved seasoned flour and stir constantly for 2 minutes to cook out the raw taste. This is a roux and it will scorch if you walk away.
      6. Whisk in the chicken broth a little at a time so no lumps form, then the milk, and bring it to a gentle simmer until it thickens.
      7. Return the chicken and any juices, cover, and simmer 20 to 25 minutes until the chicken is tender and the gravy coats a spoon.
      8. Cook the rice and taste the gravy for salt before portioning.

      Portion It

      Weigh the rice into each container first, then set the pan on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Be generous and even with the gravy, since it is doing double duty as flavor and as the thing that keeps the chicken moist through four days of reheating. Every portion lands at 670 calories.
