      Makes 14 servings · ~607 cal · 35g protein · 92g carbs · 8g fat · ~$2.85 per serving.

      Gyudon is the Japanese lunch counter bowl: paper-thin beef and onions simmered in a sweet soy broth and spooned over rice. It takes about twenty minutes of actual cooking, which makes it the fastest beef dish in this whole lineup. Slicing the meat thin is the budget trick too, because a little beef spread across a lot of surface area feels like a lot of beef. Each bowl runs 607 calories and 35 grams of protein for about $2.85 a serving.

      Shop Smart

      • Freeze the steak for about 45 minutes before you slice it. Firm meat cuts thin and clean, and thin slices are what make a small amount of beef stretch across fourteen bowls. This one habit is worth more than any coupon.
      • Mirin and soy sauce are the whole sauce. Both are one-bottle purchases that last for months of cooking, so the per-bowl cost of the flavor here is close to nothing.
      • Rice is the cheapest calorie in the store. Buy the big bag, not the box, and the price per serving drops to loose change.
      • Yellow onions are the other half of the dish, not a garnish. They cook down sweet in the broth and add real volume to the bowl for pennies.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Put the steak in the freezer for 30 to 45 minutes until it is firm but not frozen solid, then slice it against the grain as thin as your knife will allow.
      3. Start the rice so it finishes about when the beef does.
      4. Combine the chicken broth, mirin, soy sauce, and sugar in a wide skillet and bring it to a simmer. Taste it. That broth is what the whole bowl tastes like, so adjust the sugar and soy now.
      5. Add the sliced onions and simmer them for 5 to 7 minutes until they turn soft and translucent.
      6. Lay the beef into the simmering broth in batches, separating the slices as you go, and cook just until the pink disappears. Two minutes is plenty. Overcooked gyudon beef goes tough and stringy, and there is no coming back from it.
      7. Skim any foam off the top, season with a little salt and pepper, and scatter the sliced green onions over the pan.
      8. Let it sit off the heat for five minutes so the beef soaks up the broth before you portion.

      Portion It

      Weigh the cooked rice into the containers first so every bowl gets the same base, then set the beef pan on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon a little of the broth over each one, since that is where most of the flavor is. Every bowl lands at 607 calories.
