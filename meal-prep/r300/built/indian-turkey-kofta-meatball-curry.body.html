      Kofta curry solves a problem every meal prepper eventually runs into: ground meat crumble turns to sawdust by day four, but a meatball holds together and stays juicy. These turkey kofta bake on sheet pans, then finish in a coconut curry with zucchini, over rice. Each bowl runs 706 calories and 31 grams of protein for about $2.35.

      Shop Smart

      • Coconut milk is the second biggest line on this list. Store brand full-fat cans are usually cheaper than the name on the label and behave exactly the same in a simmer.
      • Red curry paste in a small jar covers this batch almost exactly. It is one of those ingredients that looks expensive per ounce and turns out to cost very little for a pot big enough to feed you all week.
      • Zucchini is one of the bigger produce lines here, so buy it in season. Mid-summer it practically falls off the shelf at half this price, and it freezes fine once cooked into the curry.
      • Fresh ginger costs almost nothing for what this recipe uses. Buy a knob, freeze it whole, and grate it frozen. It grates easier that way and never rots in your crisper drawer again.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Mix the ground turkey with the eggs, the flour, about a third of the minced garlic, the grated ginger, salt, and black pepper. Mix it just until it holds and stop there.
      3. Roll the mixture into meatballs, set them on oiled sheet pans, and bake at 400F for 18 to 20 minutes until they are browned and cooked through to 165F.
      4. While they bake, heat the olive oil in a wide pot and cook the diced onion for 8 minutes until soft and golden at the edges.
      5. Add the rest of the garlic with the red curry paste and fry it for 2 minutes, until the paste darkens and the kitchen tells you it is ready.
      6. Pour in the coconut milk, bring it to a low simmer, and add the sliced zucchini. Cook 8 minutes until the zucchini is just tender and still holding its shape.
      7. Slide the baked meatballs into the curry and simmer 5 minutes so they soak up the sauce. Taste for salt.
      8. Cook the rice, move it into your weighed pot, and keep the curry in its own pot for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then divide the meatballs evenly across all 14, count them out instead of eyeballing, and ladle the curry and zucchini over the top. Each bowl lands at 706 calories and 31 grams of protein.
