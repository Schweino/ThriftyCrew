      Makes 14 servings · ~565 cal · 35g protein · 47g carbs · 25g fat · ~$2.24 per serving.

      Spanish albondigas have a trick worth stealing: rice goes inside the meatball, not just under it. That rice holds moisture, which is exactly what lean ground turkey needs to survive a week in the fridge. They simmer in a smoked paprika tomato sauce with a little cream stirred in at the end, and each bowl runs 565 calories and 35 grams of protein for about $2.24.

      Shop Smart

      • The turkey is two thirds of this batch, so the 5 pound pack is the buy. Larger packs almost always beat the pound price of the small trays, and turkey freezes without complaint.
      • Tomato sauce in the 15 ounce can is the cheapest sauce base in the store. Seven cans still cost less than one jar of the fancy stuff with a chef on the label.
      • Smoked paprika is the one seasoning that makes this taste Spanish instead of generic. One jar costs a few dollars and lasts a year of cooking, which is the definition of money well spent.
      • A pint of heavy cream covers this batch and keeps for weeks unopened. Do not substitute milk here, since cream is what keeps the sauce from breaking when you reheat it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cook about a third of the rice and let it cool. This is the rice that goes inside the meatballs, so it has to be cooked before it gets mixed in.
      3. Mix the ground turkey with the cooled rice, the eggs, half the minced garlic, cumin, smoked paprika, oregano, salt, and black pepper. Mix with a light hand and stop as soon as it comes together.
      4. Roll it into small meatballs and set them on sheet pans. Bake at 400F for about 15 minutes to firm them up so they do not fall apart in the sauce.
      5. While they bake, heat the olive oil in a wide pot, cook the rest of the garlic for a minute, then add the tomato sauce, chicken broth, red wine vinegar, more smoked paprika, a pinch of cayenne, and black pepper.
      6. Slide the meatballs into the sauce, cover, and simmer 20 minutes until they reach 165F and the sauce has tightened up.
      7. Stir in the heavy cream off the heat and taste for salt. It should look like tomato soup that went to finishing school.
      8. Cook the remaining rice, move it into your weighed pot, and keep the meatballs in their sauce for portioning.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Then count the meatballs, divide them evenly across all 14, and ladle the sauce over the top. Each bowl lands at 565 calories and 35 grams of protein.
