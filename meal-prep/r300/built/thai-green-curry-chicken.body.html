      Shop Smart

      • Curry paste is the flavor engine and it is worth buying the real thing. If your store carries green paste, grab it. If it only stocks red, the jars we track work fine and nobody at your table will file a complaint.
      • Coconut milk is the biggest line. Buy full fat store brand cans, never light, and do not shake the can. You want the thick cream at the top for frying the paste, which is the whole technique.
      • Fresh basil is expensive by weight no matter where you buy it. A pot of living basil from the produce section costs about what two clamshells do and keeps producing on a windowsill for weeks.
      • Snow peas and eggplant go in at the very end. Buy them the day you cook, and if snow peas are priced like a luxury that week, frozen green beans slot right in without hurting anything.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Open the coconut milk cans without shaking them and spoon the thick cream off the top into your largest pot. Reserve the thin liquid underneath.
      3. Heat the pot over medium-high and cook that coconut cream with the vegetable oil for 3 to 5 minutes until it splits and you can see clear oil pooling.
      4. Add the curry paste, minced garlic, grated ginger and lemongrass paste and fry them in that oil for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens and smells sharp and fragrant. This is the step people skip and it is the one that matters.
      5. Add the chicken thighs cut into strips and turn them in the paste for 3 minutes, then pour in the reserved coconut liquid and the chicken broth.
      6. Add the cubed eggplant and simmer uncovered 15 to 20 minutes until the eggplant is soft and the sauce has reduced and thickened.
      7. Season with the fish sauce, sugar, salt and black pepper, then add the snow peas and cook 2 minutes so they stay bright and snappy.
      8. Kill the heat and stir in the lime juice, the fresh basil and the thin-sliced onion. Cook the rice separately and fold it in, or hold it in the containers underneath.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same scoop, fourteen times. Each one carries 711 calories, and reheat coconut curries at half power so the sauce stays emulsified instead of turning grainy.
