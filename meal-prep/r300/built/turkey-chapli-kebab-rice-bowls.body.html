      Makes 14 servings · ~571 cal · 34g protein · 71g carbs · 13g fat · ~$1.83 per serving.

      Chapli kebab is the flat, spiced patty of northwest Pakistan, cumin and coriander forward with tomato and green chili worked right into the meat. Traditionally they hit a hot pan of oil. We put them on sheet pans and finish under the broiler, which means you can make fourteen servings without standing over a skillet all afternoon. Over rice they come to 571 calories and 34 grams of protein for about $1.83.

      Shop Smart

      • Ground turkey is nearly two thirds of this batch. The 5 pound pack drops the price per pound noticeably over the small trays, and anything you do not use goes straight in the freezer.
      • Jalapenos cost almost nothing for what this batch uses. Fresh chili is the cheapest way to add real heat, and it beats any bottled sauce on both price and flavor.
      • Cumin, coriander, and garam masala are the spine of this recipe and they live in your cabinet already. Buy them in the larger bags at an international market and you will pay a third of the grocery store spice aisle price.
      • One bunch of cilantro covers the whole batch. Chop the tender stems too instead of throwing them out, because they carry more flavor than the leaves do.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Drain the diced tomatoes well and chop them fine. Wet tomato makes patties that fall apart, so squeeze the liquid out before it goes anywhere near the meat.
      3. Mix the ground turkey with the drained tomato, minced garlic, grated ginger, chopped jalapeno, chopped cilantro, eggs, flour, cumin, coriander, garam masala, salt, and black pepper.
      4. Chill the mixture 20 minutes in the fridge. Cold mix holds its shape and this is the difference between kebabs and a sheet pan of crumbles.
      5. Form flat patties, about half an inch thick and wider than you think, and lay them on sheet pans brushed with the melted butter.
      6. Bake at 425F for 15 minutes, then run them under the broiler 3 to 4 minutes until the tops char in spots and the meat reaches 165F.
      7. Squeeze the lime juice over the hot patties and scatter the thinly sliced onion on top so it softens in the residual heat.
      8. Cook the rice and move it into your weighed pot while the kebabs rest.

      Portion It

      Set the pot of rice on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the rice bed for each container. Lay the kebabs on top, divided evenly across all 14, with a share of the lime-dressed onions on each. Every bowl lands at 571 calories and 34 grams of protein.
