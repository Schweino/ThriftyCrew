      Makes 14 servings · ~605 cal · 43g protein · 37g carbs · 30g fat · ~$2.30 per serving.

      Five minutes of work in the morning, barbecue for a week. Country style ribs are cut from the shoulder, which means they are cheap, forgiving, and they turn to shreds after a long slow cook. Pile them over rice and you have 43 grams of protein sitting at about $2.30 a bowl.

      Shop Smart

      • Buy the whole pork shoulder, not the trimmed ribs. Country style ribs come off the shoulder anyway. Buying the bigger primal cut usually drops the per pound price, and you cut it down at home.
      • Barbecue sauce is the second biggest line. Buy the large bottle of store brand rather than a boutique one. This batch uses a lot of it, and the big bottle is often half the price per ounce.
      • The dry rub is entirely jars you own. Garlic powder, onion powder, chili powder, salt and pepper. That is the whole seasoning bill and it costs pennies per batch.
      • Rice is the cheapest filler on the board. Buy the big bag. It stretches this batch and about thirty more after it.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the pork shoulder into thick rib style strips and pat them dry so the rub sticks.
      3. Stir the garlic powder, onion powder, chili powder, salt and black pepper together and rub it over every surface of the pork.
      4. Slice the yellow onions and spread them across the bottom of the slow cooker to make a bed the meat can sit on.
      5. Lay the pork on the onions, sprinkle the brown sugar over the top, and pour about half the barbecue sauce across everything.
      6. Cover and cook on low until the meat pulls apart with light pressure from a fork.
      7. Cook the rice near the end so it is fresh and hot when the pork is done.
      8. Shred the pork right in the cooker, stir in the rest of the barbecue sauce and the juices, then combine with the rice in your big pot.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container so nobody gets shorted on meat. Every bowl lands near 605 calories and 43 grams of protein.
