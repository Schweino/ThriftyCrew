      Makes 14 servings · ~572 cal · 30g protein · 74g carbs · 15g fat · ~$2.36 per serving.

      Pad krapow is the dish Thai people actually eat on a Tuesday. Chicken and chile and garlic hit a screaming hot pan, a sauce of oyster and soy goes in, and then an absurd amount of basil gets folded through at the last second so it wilts but never cooks. Five minutes of cooking, a lifetime of craving it afterward. Over rice, each bowl runs 572 calories and 30 grams of protein for about $2.36. That price is higher than it looks like it should be, and I will show you exactly why.

      Shop Smart

      • Basil is the largest line in this batch, larger than the meat. Buy a living basil plant from the produce section instead of clamshells and you will pay a fraction of the price, or grow one on a windowsill and this recipe becomes one of the cheapest things you make all month.
      • Thai holy basil is the traditional leaf and Italian sweet basil is the one your store stocks. Either one works. Do not pay a premium for a specialty basil shipped in from three states away.
      • Oyster sauce and soy sauce are pantry buys that last months and cost pennies per batch. One bottle of each covers a dozen recipes on this site.
      • Boneless thighs beat breast here because the pan is hot and fast and thighs forgive a few extra seconds. The four pound club pack is the best price in the case.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Whisk the oyster sauce, soy sauce and sugar together in a small bowl with a splash of water. Have it standing by, because once this cooks there is no time to measure anything.
      3. Chop the chicken thighs into small pieces, roughly the size of a thumbnail, so they cook through in the time a stir fry allows.
      4. Cook the rice first and let it steam covered off the heat while you do everything else.
      5. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest skillet or wok over the highest heat you have. Add the minced garlic and sliced jalapeno and cook 20 seconds, just until the garlic turns pale gold.
      6. Add the chicken in batches so the pan never cools down and stir hard for 4 to 5 minutes until it is cooked through and browned in spots. Crowding the pan is the one mistake that ruins this dish.
      7. Return all the chicken to the pan, pour in the sauce, and let it bubble and reduce for a minute until it clings to the meat.
      8. Kill the heat, throw in the fresh basil and the sliced green onions all at once, and fold until the basil just wilts. Then combine with the rice in your mixing pot.

      Portion It

      Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Same number in every container. Each one carries 572 calories, and if you want it to taste freshly made on day four, stir a few torn basil leaves in after reheating.
