      Makes 14 servings · ~638 cal · 50g protein · 14g carbs · 40g fat · ~$4.33 per serving.

      Irish beef stew goes darker and maltier than the American version, and bacon is why. A pound of it renders into the pot before the beef ever hits, and everything that follows tastes like it. The stout is the traditional finish and the one thing we do not price on our grocery board, so treat it as optional. Each bowl carries 50 grams of protein and 638 calories for about $4.33.

      Shop Smart

      • Bacon ends and pieces, if your store carries them, cost far less than sliced bacon and are perfect for a stew where nobody is looking at the strips.
      • Chuck roast bought whole and cubed at home beats packaged stew meat on both price and quality. That holds for every stew in this section.
      • You will use a lot of broth. Bouillon base in a jar makes more broth per dollar than boxes and it keeps in the fridge for months.
      • One can of stout is the traditional addition and it is the only thing here that is not on our grocery board, so it is not in the numbers above. Beef broth in its place still makes a very good stew.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Chop the bacon and render it slowly in the pot until it is crisp, then lift it out and leave the fat behind.
      3. Cut the chuck into large cubes, pat them dry, season with salt and black pepper, and brown them hard in the bacon fat and olive oil in batches.
      4. Cook the chopped onion and garlic in the same pot until soft, scraping the bottom clean.
      5. Stir in the flour and tomato paste and cook 2 minutes.
      6. Pour in the beef broth and chicken broth, and add a can of stout here if you are using it. Add the bay leaves and thyme.
      7. Return the beef and bacon, cover, and simmer very low for 2.5 hours, until the beef surrenders completely.
      8. Add the carrots and celery for the last 40 minutes, then pull the bay leaves and correct the salt.

      Portion It

      Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that weight into each of 14 containers, stirring between scoops so the bacon and beef do not collect in the first few. Each bowl lands at 638 calories with 50 grams of protein.
