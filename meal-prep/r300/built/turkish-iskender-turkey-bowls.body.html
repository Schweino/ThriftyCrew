      Makes 14 servings · ~583 cal · 46g protein · 37g carbs · 31g fat · ~$2.80 per serving.

      Iskender kebab is named for Iskender Efendi, the man in Bursa who supposedly turned the lamb spit sideways in the 1860s and changed Turkish cooking. The dish is sliced meat over toasted bread cubes, a bright tomato sauce poured across it, melted butter drizzled over that, and cool yogurt alongside. Each serving carries 46 grams of protein and 583 calories for about $2.80. It is the most expensive plate in this group and the one most worth making when you want to eat something you cannot get locally.

      Shop Smart

      • Two frozen turkey breast roasts cover this batch. Roast them, chill them completely, and then slice thin against the grain, which is far easier cold than warm.
      • Canned diced tomatoes are the base of the sauce and this recipe uses a lot of them. Store brand blends down identically and goes on sale by the case.
      • Green peppers cost less than any other color and get charred here, which is the traditional garnish for Iskender. If your store carries Turkish or Anaheim long peppers at a similar price, those are closer to the original.
      • Greek yogurt in the 32 oz tub costs far less per ounce than the single-serve cups. The tub also keeps for weeks, so nothing goes to waste.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Cut the flatbread into bite-sized squares, spread them on a sheet pan, and toast at 375F for 8 to 10 minutes until dry and lightly golden. They need to be dry enough to hold up under sauce.
      3. Char the green peppers whole under the broiler for 8 to 10 minutes, turning once, until the skins blister, then slice them into strips.
      4. Heat most of the olive oil in a large skillet and cook the sliced onion for 6 minutes, then add the tomato paste, paprika, red pepper flakes, salt, and black pepper and cook two minutes until the paste darkens.
      5. Add the canned diced tomatoes and simmer 20 to 25 minutes until the sauce reduces and thickens enough to hold its shape on a spoon.
      6. Sear the sliced turkey breast in the remaining olive oil over high heat in batches, just long enough to get color on the edges without drying it out.
      7. Whisk the Greek yogurt smooth with a pinch of salt and a splash of water so it pours rather than sits in a lump.
      8. Build the bowls: toasted bread cubes on the bottom, seared turkey over them, tomato sauce spooned across, melted butter drizzled over the top, charred peppers on the side, and the yogurt in its own corner.

      Portion It

      Weigh the bread, turkey, and sauce together, subtract your empty pot weight, and divide by 14, then keep the yogurt in a separate small container for each bowl. Building the base to a weight is what keeps the bread-to-meat ratio honest. Each serving comes to 583 calories and 46 grams of protein.
