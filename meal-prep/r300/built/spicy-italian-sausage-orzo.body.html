      Shop Smart

      • Orzo is just a pasta shape, so buy whatever is cheapest. Store brand costs a fraction of the imported boxes and cooks identically in the sauce.
      • Fresh basil is the priciest line for how little you get. If the board shows it high, skip it or use dried oregano alone. The dish is still good.
      • Canned tomato sauce is the cheap backbone. Three cans cover this and store brand is indistinguishable once cream and parmesan go in.
      • Heavy cream is the second biggest cost. A little goes a long way in a one pot pasta because the starch from the orzo is already thickening things.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Dice the onion, mince the garlic, and chop the fresh basil, holding the basil back until the very end.
      3. Brown the hot Italian sausage in crumbles in a large deep pot until it has color on it. Do not drain the fat, because that fat is going to season the orzo.
      4. Add the diced onion and cook until soft, then the garlic and the dried oregano for a minute.
      5. Pour in the tomato sauce and the chicken broth, scrape the bottom of the pot clean, and bring it up to a simmer.
      6. Stir in the dry orzo and cook uncovered, stirring often. Orzo settles and scorches if you walk away from it, so stay close and keep the spoon moving.
      7. When the orzo is tender and the liquid has thickened around it, turn the heat low and stir in the heavy cream and the parmesan.
      8. Season with salt and black pepper, then fold in the chopped fresh basil off the heat so it stays bright.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount into each container, stirring between scoops. Every bowl lands near 600 calories and 26 grams of protein.
