      Makes 14 servings · ~633 cal · 28g protein · 94g carbs · 15g fat · ~$2.79 per serving.

      Pilau is the dish Kenyan families make when there is something to celebrate. It came up the Swahili coast with the spice trade, which is why it tastes like cumin, cardamom, cloves, and cinnamon rather than like the curry powder most Americans expect from East African food. The rice cooks in the same pot as the beef so every grain takes on color and spice. It is one of the few dishes where cooking a huge amount is actually easier than cooking a small one, which makes it a natural fit for fourteen containers.

      Shop Smart

      • The beef is the largest line here by a wide margin. Buy it when the case is marked down. A cheaper braising cut also works, since everything simmers long enough to tenderize.
      • Build your own pilau masala from jars you already own. Cumin, cloves, cinnamon, black pepper, and garam masala get you there for a fraction of the price of a specialty blend.
      • Brown the onions properly and you buy nothing else for color. Deeply browned onions are what make pilau dark. Skipping that step is why home versions come out pale and flat.
      • Potatoes stretch the pot for almost nothing. They soak up the same spiced broth as the rice and they cost less per pound than anything else in the cart.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear and let it drain, which keeps the grains separate.
      3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy pot and cook the sliced red onions over medium heat, patiently, until they go deep brown. This takes longer than you want it to and it is the most important step.
      4. Add the garlic, ginger, and minced jalapeno and cook until fragrant.
      5. Stir in the ground cumin, ground cloves, ground cinnamon, garam masala, black pepper, and bay leaves and let them toast in the oil for a minute.
      6. Add the cubed beef and brown it on all sides, then add the diced tomatoes and cook until the sauce darkens and thickens.
      7. Add the cubed potatoes and the beef broth, bring to a boil, then stir in the drained rice and season with salt.
      8. Cover tightly, drop the heat as low as it goes, and cook undisturbed until the liquid is absorbed and the rice is tender, then rest ten minutes off the heat and fluff with the chopped cilantro.

      Portion It

      Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight from Step 1, and divide by 14. Fluff first, then weigh, because rice packs down hard in the bottom of a pot and a scoop from the top is not the same as a scoop from the bottom.
