      Peri peri traveled from Mozambique to Portugal and back again, picking up garlic and lemon on the way, and it has been making cheap chicken taste expensive ever since. This version does the whole thing in one pot: chicken thighs, seasoned rice, bell pepper and a hot sauce base that stains everything a deep orange. Each bowl comes in at 565 calories and 34 grams of protein for about $1.83. One pot, one lid, fourteen dinners.

      Shop Smart

      • Bell peppers are the second biggest line after the chicken and the price swings hard by color and season. Green is almost always the cheapest of the three, and green is what this recipe uses, so do not upgrade to red out of habit.
      • The hot sauce is your peri peri base and a cup and a half sounds like a lot until you see the per-ounce price on the big bottle. Buy the family size, not the little one, because the big bottle is usually half the cost per ounce.
      • Buy rice in the biggest bag your cabinet will hold. It is the cheapest ingredient in this recipe by a wide margin and it never goes bad, so the twenty pound bag pays you back over a dozen batches.
      • Green onions and cilantro are the finishing herbs and they are both under a dollar fifty a bunch. Buy them the same day you cook, since neither one survives a week in the crisper drawer.

      Make It

      1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
      2. Build the peri peri base: stir the hot sauce together with the cajun seasoning, ground coriander, ground turmeric, dried oregano, minced garlic, salt and black pepper. Toss the chicken thighs in about two thirds of it and let them sit while the oven heats to 375F.
      3. Heat the olive oil in a large oven-safe pot or deep roasting pan over medium-high and sear the marinated thighs in batches until they are browned on both sides. They will not be cooked through yet. Set them aside.
      4. Cook the diced onion and green bell pepper in the same pot for 5 minutes, scraping up the fond as they release their water.
      5. Stir in the dry rice and the remaining peri peri base and toast for two minutes so every grain picks up color.
      6. Pour in the chicken broth, bring it to a boil, then lay the chicken back on top with any juices from the plate.
      7. Cover tightly and bake 35 to 40 minutes, until the rice is tender and the chicken reads 165F. Let it sit covered off the heat for 10 minutes before you touch it.
      8. Chop the chicken, fold everything together in your mixing pot, and finish with the sliced green onions and chopped cilantro.

      Portion It

      Set the loaded pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. That number goes in every container. Each bowl lands at 565 calories, and because the rice cooked in the chicken drippings it stays moist through a week of reheats.
