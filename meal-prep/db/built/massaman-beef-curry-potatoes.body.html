Makes 14 servings · ~886 cal · 46g protein · 71g carbs · 45g fat · ~$7.62 per serving (at everyday cost).

Massaman is the gentlest of the Thai curries and the only one built for a tough cut of meat. It gets its character from dry warm spices, cloves, cinnamon, cumin, and nutmeg, rather than from raw chile heat, and the potatoes and peanuts turn it into a full dinner rather than a sauce. Each bowl comes in at 886 calories and 46 grams of protein for about $7.62 a serving (at everyday cost).

    Shop Smart

    • You are making the curry paste from scratch out of onion, garlic, ginger, dried chiles, and lemongrass paste. Jarred massaman paste costs several dollars a batch and tastes flatter, so this is one of the rare cases where the from-scratch version is both cheaper and better.
    • Dried arbol chiles are sold in bags that last through many, many batches. Soak them in hot water before blending and they blitz smooth instead of leaving grit in the sauce.
    • Full fat coconut milk is not optional. This build already runs a lighter pour than the original recipe, so cutting it further with the light stuff will leave you with thin, sad curry.
    • Peanuts add crunch and fat for very little money. Buy a jar of dry roasted, use a handful here, and eat the rest. Same with the fish sauce, which is a one-bottle purchase that will outlive several months of cooking.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Soak the dried arbol chiles in hot water for 15 minutes, then blend them with the onion, garlic, ginger, cilantro stems, and lemongrass paste into a smooth curry paste.
    3. Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot and fry the paste over medium heat until it darkens and the raw smell burns off, about 8 minutes.
    4. Add the ground cloves, cumin, nutmeg, garam masala, and cinnamon and toast for 30 seconds, then stir in the thick top layer of the coconut milk and let it bubble until the oil starts to separate.
    5. Add the beef cut into large cubes and turn it in the paste, then pour in the beef broth and remaining coconut milk with the bay leaves. Bring it to a bare simmer.
    6. Cover and cook on low for about 2 hours, until the beef is tender enough to cut with the side of a spoon.
    7. Add the potatoes in chunks and simmer uncovered for 30 minutes more, then season with the fish sauce, sugar, and lime juice. Massaman should taste salty, sweet, and sour all at once, so adjust until all three are there.
    8. Cook the rice, then stir the chopped peanuts into the curry off the heat.

    Portion It

    Put the rice in the containers first, then set the curry pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Ladle to that gram target and every container hits 886 calories with the same number of beef chunks in it. This is the biggest meal in the lineup, so it is the one to pack on a long workday.
