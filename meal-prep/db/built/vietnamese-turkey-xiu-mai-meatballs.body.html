Makes 14 servings · ~586 cal · 41g protein · 72g carbs · 13g fat · ~$4.79 per serving (at everyday cost).

Xiu mai are the Vietnamese meatballs that get braised in a garlicky tomato sauce and traditionally scooped up with a torn piece of baguette. We serve them over rice noodles instead so they portion clean. Because they braise rather than fry, they get better as the week goes on, which is exactly what you want out of a container that has to survive until Friday. Each one runs 586 calories and 41 grams of protein for about $4.79 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • 93/7 Ground Turkey (Jennie-O): 5.25 lb (2380 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 4.5 (490 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (21 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 0.12 cups (28 g)
  • Sugar (Great Value): 8.25 tbsp (102 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
  • Garlic: 1.25 cups (175 g)
  • Tomato Paste (Great Value): 3.5 cans (595 g)
  • Fish Sauce (Thai Kitchen): 0.75 cups (189 g)
  • Diced Tomatoes (Great Value): 7 cans (2877 g)
  • Green Onions: 10 tbsp (60 g)
  • Fresh Cilantro: 35 tbsp (35 g)
  • Rice Noodles (Thai Kitchen): 24.75 oz dry (700 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Fish sauce is the ingredient that makes this taste Vietnamese rather than Italian. One bottle costs a couple dollars, lasts a year, and turns up in a dozen Southeast Asian recipes after this one.
    • The turkey is the biggest line by far. The 6 pound buy is the cheapest per pound, and this is the recipe where you use the whole thing.
    • Rice noodles are the second biggest line. Buy them at an Asian market where the same product usually runs a third less than the boxed brand in the grocery store international aisle.
    • Tomato paste in the small cans is what builds the sauce color and body. Buy four, use them all, and skip the ritual of a half-used can turning gray in your fridge.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Finely dice about a third of the onion and mix it into the ground turkey with a good portion of the minced garlic, some of the fish sauce, a spoon of the sugar, salt, and black pepper.
    3. Roll the mixture into large meatballs, about golf ball size, and chill them 15 minutes so they hold their shape when they hit the pan.
    4. Cook the rest of the diced onion and garlic in a wide pot until soft and fragrant, about 6 minutes. There is a lot of garlic here and that is the point of the dish.
    5. Stir in the tomato paste and fry it for 2 minutes until it darkens, then add the diced tomatoes, the rest of the fish sauce, the sugar, and the chicken broth.
    6. Lower the meatballs into the sauce, cover, and simmer gently for 30 minutes, turning them once, until they are cooked through to 165F and the sauce has thickened around them.
    7. Taste the sauce and adjust with a little more sugar or fish sauce. It should be savory, faintly sweet, and unmistakably garlicky.
    8. Soak and boil the rice noodles just to tender, rinse them cold, and move them into your weighed pot with the chopped cilantro and green onions folded through.

    Portion It

    Set the pot of noodles on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is the noodle bed for each container. Then divide the meatballs by count across all 14 and spoon the sauce over the top. Each container lands at 586 calories and 41 grams of protein.
