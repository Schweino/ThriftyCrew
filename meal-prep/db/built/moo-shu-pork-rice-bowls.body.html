Makes 14 servings · ~593 cal · 25g protein · 66g carbs · 24g fat · ~$3.09 per serving (at everyday cost).

Strip away the pancakes and moo shu pork is just an excellent cabbage and pork stir fry, which is exactly why it works as meal prep. Sliced pork, scrambled egg, cabbage, carrots and mushrooms in a hoisin and oyster sauce glaze, served over rice instead of wrapped. Cabbage and carrots are two of the cheapest things we track, so this lands at 25 grams of protein for about $3.09 a bowl (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Pork Shoulder (generic (USDA)): 2.75 lb (1276 g)
  • Oyster Sauce (Lee Kum Kee): 4.75 tbsp (84 g)
  • Cornstarch (generic): 1.25 tbsp (10 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 0.75 cups (141 g)
  • Hoisin Sauce (generic): 0.5 cups (149 g)
  • Soy Sauce (generic): 5.75 tbsp (93 g)
  • Sesame Oil (generic): 1 tbsp (13 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.25 tbsp (1 g)
  • Eggs (Great Value): 5 large eggs (233 g)
  • Garlic: 2.75 tbsp (23 g)
  • Green Cabbage (generic): 1 lb (490 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 6.3 (385 g)
  • White Mushrooms: 6.75 oz (188 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 0.25 cups (37 g)
  • Green Onions: 11.75 tbsp (70 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 5 cups dry (936 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Cabbage is the reason this is cheap. One head fills the pan and costs less than almost anything else in produce. It also keeps for weeks, unlike the bagged slaw mixes.
    • Hoisin and oyster sauce are one time buys. Both bottles last for many batches and both live in the fridge for months. They are what make this taste like the restaurant version.
    • Buy a whole pork shoulder and slice it yourself. Pre sliced stir fry pork carries a real markup for knife work you can do in ten minutes. Freeze it for twenty minutes first and it slices thin easily.
    • Sesame oil goes in off the heat. It burns fast and it is one of the pricier bottles in the pantry. A small amount at the end flavors the whole batch.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Slice the pork shoulder thin against the grain and toss it with some of the soy sauce, the cornstarch and the black pepper. Let it sit while you prep everything else.
    3. Shred the cabbage, cut the carrots into matchsticks, slice the mushrooms, mince the garlic, and separate the green onion whites from the tops.
    4. Beat the eggs and scramble them in a little of the vegetable oil until just set, then take them out and chop them roughly.
    5. Get your largest pan very hot, add more oil, and stir fry the marinated pork in batches until it is browned and cooked through. Crowding the pan will steam the pork instead of searing it.
    6. Add the garlic and the green onion whites, then the mushrooms, carrots and cabbage, and stir fry until the cabbage wilts but still has some bite left in it.
    7. Return the pork, add the hoisin sauce, oyster sauce, the rest of the soy sauce and the chicken broth, and toss until everything is glazed.
    8. Kill the heat, fold in the scrambled egg and the green onion tops, finish with the sesame oil, and serve over rice with warm tortilla strips standing in for the pancakes.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and scoop the exact amount over rice in each container, tossing between scoops so the pork does not settle. Every bowl lands near 593 calories and 25 grams of protein.
