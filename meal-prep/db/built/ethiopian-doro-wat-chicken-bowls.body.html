Makes 14 servings · ~574 cal · 54g protein · 13g carbs · 32g fat · ~$3.09 per serving (at everyday cost).

Ethiopia's national dish: chicken thighs braised in a deep red berbere and slow-cooked onion sauce, finished with hard boiled eggs. Each bowl brings a serious 54 grams of protein at just 574 calories for about $3.09 (at everyday cost). The onions cook down into the sauce itself, so there is no flour, no cream, and no filler.

Ingredients

  • Red Onion: 7 (770 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 0.75 cups (147 g)

  • Ginger (generic): 3.5 tbsp (21 g)

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chicken thighs into large chunks and toss with the lemon juice and a big pinch of salt. Let them sit while you cook the onions.
    3. Finely chop the red onions and cook them dry in your big pot over medium-low heat, stirring often, 15 to 20 minutes until soft and deeply golden. This slow onion melt is the backbone of doro wat, so do not rush it.
    4. Add the vegetable oil, garlic, and ginger and cook 2 minutes. Stir in the berbere and paprika and toast 1 to 2 minutes until brick red and fragrant.
    5. Stir in the tomato paste, then the chicken broth. Simmer 10 minutes to marry the sauce.
    6. Add the chicken and simmer gently, partially covered, 25 to 30 minutes until it reaches 165 F and the sauce has thickened. Stir in the butter at the end for gloss.
    7. Meanwhile, hard boil the eggs: 11 minutes in boiling water, then an ice bath, then peel. Prick or slash each egg lightly and let them warm in the sauce for the last 5 minutes.
    8. Portion into 14 containers with a hard boiled egg or so in each, covered in sauce.

    Portion It

    Weigh the full pot before adding the peeled eggs, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14 for the stew, then add the eggs evenly across containers. Each bowl comes out to about 574 calories with 54 grams of protein.
