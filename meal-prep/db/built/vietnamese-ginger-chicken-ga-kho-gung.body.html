Makes 14 servings · ~562 cal · 39g protein · 65g carbs · 14g fat · ~$2.56 per serving (at everyday cost).

Ga kho gung is a kho, which is the Vietnamese family of caramel braises where sugar gets cooked dark and then chicken simmers down in it until the sauce is thick and glossy and clings to everything. What sets this one apart is the sheer volume of fresh ginger, sliced thin and cooked until it turns from sharp to sweet. It is traditionally a clay pot dish and it scales into a Dutch oven without complaint. Each bowl runs 562 calories and 39 grams of protein for about $2.56 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Thigh (Member's Mark): 5.25 lb (2333 g)
  • Honey (Great Value): 4.75 tbsp (98 g)
  • Garlic: 2.75 tbsp (23 g)
  • Ginger (generic): 19.5 tbsp (117 g)
  • Oyster Sauce (Lee Kum Kee): 4.75 tbsp (84 g)
  • Fish Sauce (Thai Kitchen): 3.5 tbsp (63 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 0.05 cups (12 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 7 tbsp (98 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 5.25 cups dry (985 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Fresh ginger is the whole personality of this dish and you need a real amount of it. Buy a full hand rather than a knob, use what you need, and freeze the rest, since frozen ginger grates easily and lasts for months.
    • Honey is the second largest line. Store brand honey performs identically in a hot braise, so save the good jar for tea and use the cheap one here.
    • Fish sauce and oyster sauce are the salt and depth. One bottle of each runs a few dollars and covers a dozen batches across this whole site.
    • Boneless skinless thighs are the only right cut for a kho. Breast will dry out in the reduction and thighs get better the longer they sit in the caramel.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chicken thighs into large bite-size pieces and toss them with the minced garlic, a spoonful of the fish sauce, black pepper and salt. Let them sit 20 minutes.
    3. Slice the fresh ginger into thin matchsticks. This takes a few minutes and it is the difference between a ginger dish and a dish with ginger in it, so do not shortcut it with a grater.
    4. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest heavy pot over medium-high and cook about a third of the ginger for 2 minutes until it smells sharp and the edges curl.
    5. Add the honey and let it bubble and darken for a minute or two into a loose caramel. Watch it, because honey burns faster than sugar does.
    6. Add the chicken and turn it hard in the caramel until every piece is coated and starting to brown, about 6 to 8 minutes.
    7. Stir in the oyster sauce, the rest of the fish sauce, the chicken broth and the remaining ginger. Bring it to a simmer, then cook uncovered 25 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce reduces down to a thick glaze that coats the chicken.
    8. Cook the rice separately, let it steam covered for 10 minutes, and fold it into the pot so the caramel sauce reaches every grain.

    Portion It

    Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, same number in each. Each one carries 562 calories, and caramel braises like this one reheat beautifully because the sauce protects the meat.
