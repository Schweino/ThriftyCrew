Makes 14 servings · ~582 cal · 40g protein · 54g carbs · 22g fat · ~$3.73 per serving (at everyday cost).

Cape Malay curry came out of Cape Town, built by cooks who brought Indonesian and Malay spicing into a South African kitchen. What makes it different from every other curry on this site is the balance: warm spices like cinnamon and garam masala, a tomato base with no coconut milk anywhere, and a spoonful of sugar and lemon at the end that turns the whole thing sweet-savory. Each bowl runs 582 calories and 40 grams of protein for about $3.73 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

    Shop Smart

    • Crushed tomatoes carry this sauce, so buy the 28 ounce cans. They run well under half the per-ounce price of the small cans and this recipe uses close to two of them.
    • Fresh ginger keeps for months in the freezer. Buy a whole hand, use what you need, and grate the rest straight from frozen next time instead of watching a shriveled knob die in the crisper.
    • Whole spices are the budget trap in curries. Cinnamon, cumin, coriander, turmeric and garam masala together are one of the bigger lines in this batch, and buying them in bags from an international grocer instead of branded jars cuts that cost by half or better.
    • Boneless skinless thighs are the right cut for a tomato braise and the six pound club pack beats every tray pack in the case on price per pound.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Heat the vegetable oil in your largest pot over medium and cook the sliced onion slowly for 10 to 12 minutes, until it is deeply soft and golden. Cape Malay curry starts with patient onions, not fast ones.
    3. Add the garlic and grated ginger and cook one minute, then add the red pepper flakes, ground coriander, italian seasoning, ground cumin, ground turmeric, garam masala, ground cinnamon and black pepper. Stir them in the hot oil for about 45 seconds until the kitchen smells like the spice aisle.
    4. Pour in the crushed tomatoes, scrape the bottom, and cook 10 minutes until the sauce darkens and thickens.
    5. Add the chicken thighs, cut into large chunks, and turn them until every piece is coated. Cover and simmer 30 to 35 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is tender and reads 165F.
    6. Stir in the brown sugar and lemon juice. This is the step that makes it Cape Malay instead of generic, so taste as you go and adjust until it tastes bright and slightly sweet at the same time.
    7. While it finishes, cook the rice and let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes.
    8. Fold the cooked rice through the curry so every serving gets sauce and grain in the same scoop.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen equal containers, no guessing. Each one lands at 582 calories, and a tomato-based curry like this one reheats better than a cream or coconut sauce ever will.
