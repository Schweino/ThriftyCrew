Makes 14 servings · ~630 cal · 26g protein · 90g carbs · 20g fat · ~$3.54 per serving (at everyday cost).

Leczo is the pepper and sausage stew that shows up on Polish and Hungarian tables all summer long, when peppers are stacked cheap at every market. Smoked sausage, a mountain of bell peppers, onions and a paprika tomato base, ladled over rice. It carries 26 grams of protein for about $3.54 (at everyday cost), and it is one of those pots that tastes better the second day.

Ingredients

  • Yellow Onion: 5.6 (616 g)
  • Smoked Turkey Sausage (Butterball): 4 lb (1772 g)
  • Green Bell Peppers: 11 (1344 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 5.5 tbsp (78 g)
  • Garlic: 3.25 tbsp (28 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 0.5 tbsp (8 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 1.5 tbsp (11 g)
  • Smoked Paprika (Great Value): 2 tbsp (13 g)
  • Paprika: 0.5 tbsp (3 g)
  • Tomato Sauce (generic): 6 cans (2520 g)
  • Tomato Paste (Great Value): 5.5 tbsp (90 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 5.25 cups dry (985 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Peppers are the price of this dish. They are the biggest single line after the sausage. Late summer is when they bottom out, and that is the week to cook a double batch.
    • Green bell peppers are almost always cheaper than red. Leczo is traditionally made with whatever is ripe, so green is authentic and easier on the wallet at the same time.
    • Canned tomato sauce beats fresh tomatoes here. It gives you a consistent base year round for very little, and the stew simmers long enough that fresh would break down anyway.
    • Smoked and sweet paprika together are the flavor. Both come from jars you keep on hand, so the seasoning line on this recipe is almost nothing.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Slice the onions, cut the bell peppers into wide strips, slice the smoked sausage into coins and mince the garlic.
    3. Heat the vegetable oil in a large pot and cook the onions until they turn soft and translucent.
    4. Add the sausage coins and brown them so the edges caramelize and leave color in the pot.
    5. Add the pepper strips and cook until they slump and start to sweeten, which takes longer than you think it will.
    6. Stir in the garlic, then pull the pot off the heat and add the sweet and smoked paprika so it does not scorch.
    7. Back on the burner, stir in the tomato sauce and tomato paste, season with salt and black pepper, and simmer uncovered until the sauce thickens and turns glossy.
    8. Cook the rice separately and keep it apart from the stew until you portion, so it does not turn to mush in the fridge.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on your scale and subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1. Divide that total by 14 and ladle the exact amount over a scoop of rice in each container. Every bowl lands near 630 calories and 26 grams of protein.
