Makes 14 servings · ~561 cal · 40g protein · 89g carbs · 4g fat · ~$4.09 per serving (at everyday cost).

Nikujaga is Japanese comfort food at its plainest and best: meat and potatoes simmered in soy, mirin, and a little sugar until the potatoes go soft enough to break with chopsticks. Every Japanese household makes it a slightly different way and every one of them is convinced theirs is correct. Ours leans turkey-forward so it works as a full container, at 561 calories and 40 grams of protein for about $4.09 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Turkey Breast (generic (USDA)): 4 lb (1811 g)
  • Potato (generic): 24 (5060 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 2 (123 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 2.6 (282 g)
  • Green Beans (generic): 7.75 oz (221 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 1.3 cartons (1199 g)
  • Mirin (Mizkan): 1 cups (217 g)
  • Sugar (Great Value): 0.75 cups (151 g)
  • Soy Sauce (generic): 1 cups (258 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 2 tbsp (28 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (23 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Potatoes by the 12 pound bag are the cheapest starch in the store. This recipe uses a serious pile of them, so the bag price is the difference between cheap and very cheap.
    • Turkey breast is the big line here. Frozen roasts run far below fresh cuts, and the deepest discounts land in the weeks right after the holidays.
    • Mirin and soy sauce together cost very little for the batch and both bottles keep for a year. They are the entire seasoning, so there is no long spice list to buy into.
    • This is a braise that gets better on day three, so it is one of the best uses of a Sunday afternoon in this whole library. Cheap ingredients plus time is the oldest trick in budget cooking.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the turkey breast into thin strips. Thin is important, because strips soak up the broth while chunks just sit in it.
    3. Peel the potatoes and cut them into large chunks, slice the carrots on a diagonal, and cut the onion into thick wedges.
    4. Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy pot and sear the turkey strips until they lose their pink, then add the onion wedges and cook 3 minutes.
    5. Add the potatoes and carrots, then pour in the chicken broth, soy sauce, mirin, and sugar. The liquid should come most of the way up the vegetables but not drown them.
    6. Bring it to a simmer, then lay a round of parchment right on the surface and cover the pot. That paper lid is the Japanese trick that keeps everything basted without boiling the potatoes apart.
    7. Simmer on low for 25 to 30 minutes until the potatoes are fork tender and the turkey has reached 165F, shaking the pot instead of stirring so nothing breaks up.
    8. Add the green beans in the last 5 minutes, then let the pot sit off the heat for 15 minutes, which is when the potatoes actually drink up the broth.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Scoop gently from the bottom so each container gets potato, carrot, and turkey along with a share of the broth. Every serving lands at 561 calories and 40 grams of protein.
