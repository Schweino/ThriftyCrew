Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 26g protein · 66g carbs · 21g fat · ~$3.31 per serving (at everyday cost).

Normandy is apple country, and their cooking shows it. Chicken braises in apple cider with onions, mustard, and cream until the sauce goes silky and the apple slices soften into it. It tastes like a restaurant dish and costs less than most weeknight chicken. Each bowl carries 567 calories and 26 grams of protein for about $3.31 a serving (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Thigh (Member's Mark): 3.25 lb (1470 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (21 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
  • Olive Oil: 3.5 tbsp (47 g)
  • Butter (generic): 3.5 tbsp (49 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 2 (245 g)
  • Garlic: 4 tbsp (35 g)
  • Apple (generic): 7 (1260 g)
  • Apple Juice (generic (USDA)): 2.5 cups (651 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 1.75 cups (420 g)
  • Heavy Cream (Great Value): 1.75 cups (416 g)
  • Dijon Mustard (generic): 2.25 tbsp (35 g)
  • Dried Thyme (Great Value): 1.25 tbsp (4 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 4.25 cups dry (772 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Apples are the flavor of this dish and they are cheapest in fall. A firm variety holds its shape in the pan while a soft one dissolves into applesauce, so pick something crisp rather than something mealy.
    • Apple juice stands in for hard cider here and it works. Buy the big bottle, use what the recipe needs, and drink the rest, which is a much better outcome than a half used bottle of cooking cider going flat in the fridge.
    • Heavy cream is the priciest thing on the list and you need less of it than you think. Buy a pint, use what the recipe calls for, and it will hold in the fridge for weeks.
    • Boneless thighs are the cheap cut and the right one. Cream sauces are unforgiving to dry meat, and breast dries out in exactly this kind of braise.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Season the chicken thighs with salt, pepper, and thyme and brown them in the olive oil and butter until they have real color on both sides. Set them aside.
    3. Cook the sliced onions in the same pan until soft and golden, then add the garlic for a minute.
    4. Add the apples cut into thick wedges and cook 3 or 4 minutes just until they start to color at the edges. Do not cook them soft yet, because they still have the braise to get through.
    5. Pour in the apple juice and chicken broth and scrape the bottom of the pan clean, then return the chicken.
    6. Cover and simmer 25 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and the liquid has reduced by about a third.
    7. Stir the dijon mustard into the heavy cream in a bowl first, then add that mixture to the pan off the boil. Cream added to a hard boiling pan can split, and the mustard tempers it.
    8. Simmer gently 5 more minutes until the sauce coats a spoon. Cook the rice and taste the sauce for salt before portioning.

    Portion It

    Weigh the rice into each container, then set the pan on the scale, subtract the empty weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon the sauce evenly, since that is where the calories and most of the flavor live. Every portion lands at 567 calories.
