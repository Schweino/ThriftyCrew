Makes 14 servings · ~566 cal · 42g protein · 32g carbs · 28g fat · ~$3.67 per serving (at everyday cost).

Youvarlakia are Greek meatballs with rice mixed into them, poached in broth and finished with avgolemono, the egg and lemon sauce that turns thin liquid into something silky without a drop of cream. It is one of the great tricks in Mediterranean cooking and it costs about a dozen eggs and some lemon juice. Ours is built thick, as a stew rather than a soup, at 566 calories and 42 grams of protein for about $3.67 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • 93/7 Ground Turkey (Jennie-O): 5.5 lb (2450 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 2.25 cups dry (404 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 5 (539 g)
  • Eggs (Great Value): 15 large eggs (735 g)
  • Olive Oil: 0.75 cups (137 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 2.4 (149 g)
  • Dried Dill (Great Value): 9.75 tbsp (29 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.5 tbsp (29 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 3 tbsp (20 g)
  • Lemon Juice (ReaLemon): 10 (461 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Ground turkey is two thirds of the cost here, and the 6 pound buy is the cheapest per pound. Split it, freeze half, and the next batch of anything gets easier.
    • Bottled lemon juice is the right call for this one. This batch uses the equivalent of ten lemons, and squeezing ten lemons by hand for a dish that also asks you to temper eggs is more adventure than a Sunday needs.
    • Eggs are the sauce, not a garnish. Fifteen of them cost very little here and they replace every drop of cream this dish would otherwise want.
    • Dried dill is the whole seasoning bill and one jar runs a couple of dollars. Fresh dill costs more per batch and wilts in three days, so the jar wins for meal prep.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Mix the ground turkey with the uncooked rice, grated onion, the dried dill, salt, and black pepper. The rice goes in raw and cooks inside the meatball, which is the whole idea.
    3. Roll the mixture into meatballs and set them aside. Do not pack them tight, since the rice needs room to swell.
    4. Heat the olive oil in a wide pot, soften the remaining diced onion and the grated carrot for 6 minutes, then add water to make a light broth and bring it to a simmer.
    5. Lower the meatballs in gently, cover, and simmer 40 minutes until the rice inside is tender and the meat is cooked through to 165F. Do not stir hard, just shake the pot.
    6. Make the avgolemono: whisk the eggs until frothy, whisk in the lemon juice, then ladle in hot broth one spoon at a time while whisking constantly. This tempering step is the only part that can go wrong, so go slow.
    7. Pour the sauce back into the pot off the heat and swirl the pot to combine. Never let it boil again, because boiling scrambles the eggs and there is no undo.
    8. Scatter the chopped fresh herb over the top, then move everything into your weighed pot.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. That is your per-container gram target. Divide the meatballs by count first, then pour the lemon sauce over them so every container gets the same amount of both. Each one lands at 566 calories and 42 grams of protein.
