Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 39g protein · 78g carbs · 9g fat · ~$3.66 per serving (at everyday cost).

Biryani is the crown jewel of rice dishes: yogurt-marinated chicken steamed with spiced rice, buttery golden onions, mint, and a little jalapeno heat. This simplified batch version lands at 39 grams of protein and 567 calories per bowl for about $3.66 (at everyday cost). Restaurant biryani wishes it had these unit economics.

    Shop Smart

    • Chicken breast at the 4 lb pack price is what keeps this batch affordable; grab an extra pack when it dips to a real sale. The garam masala, turmeric, and whole spices look like a long list, but they are all pantry jars that season a dozen batches. Buy them once, ideally from the bulk or international aisle where they cost half as much. A tub of store-brand Greek yogurt does double duty: marinade for this recipe, breakfast for the rest of the week. Skip the tiny single cups; the 32 oz tub wins per ounce.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chicken breast into large bite-size chunks and marinate in the Greek yogurt, garlic, lemon juice, turmeric, chili powder, garam masala, and salt for at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the fridge.
    3. Rinse the rice until the water runs mostly clear; this keeps the biryani fluffy instead of gummy.
    4. Melt the butter in your largest heavy pot over medium heat and cook the sliced onions 10 to 12 minutes until deep golden. Set a third of them aside for the top.
    5. Add the marinated chicken with all its marinade, plus the cumin, cinnamon, cloves, and bay leaves. Cook 5 to 6 minutes until the chicken firms up.
    6. Add the rinsed rice, sliced jalapeno, most of the mint, salt, and the right amount of water for your rice (about 2 cups water per cup of dry rice, minus the marinade moisture). Bring to a boil.
    7. Cover tightly and simmer on the lowest heat 20 minutes, then rest off the heat, still covered, 10 minutes. No peeking; the steam is doing the work.
    8. Check that the chicken reads 165F, pull the bay leaves, and fluff gently with a fork. Top with the reserved golden onions and remaining mint.

    Portion It

    Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Scoop each portion gently into its container so the rice grains stay whole. Every bowl delivers 567 calories and 39 grams of protein.
