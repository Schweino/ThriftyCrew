Makes 14 servings · ~885 cal · 55g protein · 33g carbs · 57g fat · ~$7.98 per serving (at everyday cost).

Soto Betawi is Jakarta in a bowl: beef simmered until tender in a coconut and milk broth, built on a fried spice paste of shallot, garlic, cashew, and ginger, then brightened at the table with lime and green onion. It is a soup that eats like a dinner. Each bowl carries 55 grams of protein and 885 calories for about $7.98 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Beef Chuck Roast: 7.5 lb (3360 g)
  • Lemongrass Paste (Gourmet Garden (McCormick)): 7 tbsp (105 g)
  • Bay Leaves (Great Value): 0.25 oz (4 g)
  • Lime Juice: 6.5 tbsp (98 g)
  • Coconut Milk (Thai Kitchen): 4.4 cans (1750 g)
  • Milk (Fairlife): 2.75 cups (700 g)
  • Potato (generic): 3.6 (762 g)
  • Diced Tomatoes (Great Value): 3.8 cans (1575 g)
  • Green Onions: 17.5 tbsp (105 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 3.25 tbsp (22 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 6 tbsp (84 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 9 (980 g)
  • Garlic: 8.25 tbsp (70 g)
  • Cashews (generic): 1.25 tbsp (10 g)
  • Ginger (generic): 17.5 tbsp (105 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Canned coconut milk is the flavor and the body of this soup. The store brand cans work fine, but shake one before you buy it. If it sloshes thin, it has been watered down.
    • Lemongrass paste in a tube lasts months in the fridge and costs less over time than fresh stalks you throw half of away. It is the one convenience item worth it here.
    • Cashews are the traditional thickener in the spice paste. You only need a small handful, so buy the smallest container rather than the club size.
    • Chuck roast is the correct cut for a long simmer. Brisket is what the source uses, and chuck gets to the same place for less money.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chuck into bite sized pieces and simmer them in water with the lemongrass paste, bay leaves, and salt for about 90 minutes, until tender. Keep every bit of that broth.
    3. While the beef cooks, blend most of the onion with the garlic, cashews, and ginger into a smooth paste.
    4. Fry the paste in the vegetable oil over medium heat until it darkens and smells nutty rather than raw, about 8 to 10 minutes.
    5. Stir the fried paste into the beef pot, then add the coconut milk and the milk. Keep it at a bare simmer so the coconut milk does not break.
    6. Add the diced potato and simmer 15 to 20 minutes until it is soft but still holding its shape.
    7. Add the tomatoes near the end so they stay in pieces, then finish with the lime juice and a heavy hand of black pepper.
    8. Slice the green onions and the remaining onion thin for scattering over each bowl at serving.

    Portion It

    Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Ladle that exact weight into each of 14 containers, making sure every one gets its share of beef and not just broth. Each bowl lands at 885 calories with 55 grams of protein.
