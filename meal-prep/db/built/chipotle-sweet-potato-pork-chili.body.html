Makes 14 servings · ~571 cal · 41g protein · 46g carbs · 28g fat · ~$4.92 per serving (at everyday cost).

Most chili is ground beef and a packet. This one is cubed pork shoulder braised until it shreds, sweet potato that goes soft enough to thicken the pot, poblano peppers for a mild green heat, and chipotles in adobo for the smoke. Sweet, smoky, a little bit hot, and a great deal more interesting than what comes out of a can. Two kinds of beans keep the cost down and the protein up.

    Shop Smart

    • Chipotles in adobo are the best flavor per dollar in the aisle. One small can brings smoke and heat to the entire pot. Freeze the leftovers in a small bag and chip off what you need next time.
    • Sweet potatoes are cheapest by the pound loose in fall. They also thicken this chili naturally as they break down, so you are buying body and flavor in the same item.
    • Poblanos are milder and cheaper than bell peppers. They bring a deeper roasted flavor for less money. If your store keeps them near the jalapenos, that is where the value hides.
    • Two cans of beans, two different kinds. Black and kidney beans cost about the same and using both gives the chili more texture than a double dose of either.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cube the pork shoulder, pat it dry, season with salt, black pepper, chili powder, smoked paprika, ground cumin and a pinch of ground allspice, and brown it hard in the olive oil in batches.
    3. Cook the diced onion and chopped poblano peppers in the same pot until soft, then add the minced garlic and the chopped chipotles in adobo and stir for a minute.
    4. Stir in the Dijon mustard, the red wine vinegar and the splash of fish sauce, which sounds strange and is the reason this chili tastes like it cooked twice as long.
    5. Add the diced tomatoes with their juice, return the pork, and simmer covered for about 2 hours until the pork shreds.
    6. Add the cubed sweet potatoes and both drained beans and simmer another 30 minutes, until the sweet potato is tender and starting to break apart and thicken the pot.
    7. Warm the tortillas and keep the sour cream and chopped cilantro in separate containers for topping at reheat.

    Portion It

    Set the pot on your scale, subtract the empty pot weight from Step 1, divide by 14, and spoon that exact weight into each container with the tortillas packed alongside. Every serving lands near 571 calories with 41 grams of protein once the sour cream and cilantro go on top.
