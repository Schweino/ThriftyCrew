Makes 14 servings · ~660 cal · 57g protein · 74g carbs · 16g fat · ~$4.31 per serving (at everyday cost).

Takeout lo mein without the takeout markup: chewy noodles, tender chicken, and a glossy hoisin-soy sauce that clings to everything. Each bowl packs 57 grams of protein at 660 calories for about $4.31 (at everyday cost). The delivery apps want triple that before the fees even start.

Ingredients

  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Breast (Member's Mark): 6.25 lb (2800 g)
  • Red Bell Pepper (generic): 2 (210 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 3.5 tbsp (49 g)
  • Garlic: 8.25 tbsp (70 g)
  • Ginger (generic): 3.5 tbsp (21 g)
  • Green Onions: 4.25 cups (210 g)
  • Egg Noodles (Great Value wide): 31.5 oz dry (892 g)
  • Hoisin Sauce (generic): 2.25 cups (560 g)
  • Chicken Broth (Swanson): 1 lb (420 g)
  • Soy Sauce (generic): 1 cups (224 g)
  • Oyster Sauce (Lee Kum Kee): 4.5 oz (126 g)
  • Sesame Oil (generic): 2.25 tbsp (32 g)
  • Cornstarch (generic): 4.75 tbsp (38 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Chicken breast in the big club pack drives the per-pound price way down. The 6.25 pounds here is the priciest line in the batch, and it is still cheaper than a couple pounds of restaurant chicken.
    • Regular wide egg noodles do the job of 'lo mein noodles' for a third of the price of anything labeled Asian noodles. Same wheat, same egg, different label.
    • Hoisin and oyster sauce look expensive per bottle, but they last across several batches. Counted at what this recipe actually uses, they add just pennies per bowl of restaurant-level flavor.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Whisk the sauce: hoisin, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sesame oil, chicken broth, and cornstarch in a bowl until smooth. Set it by the stove.
    3. Boil the egg noodles 1 minute short of the package time, drain, and toss with a little oil so they do not clump.
    4. Slice the chicken breast into thin strips. Working in batches, sear in vegetable oil over high heat until cooked through to 165 F. Set aside.
    5. In the same pot, stir fry the sliced red bell pepper 2 minutes, then add the garlic, ginger, and most of the green onions for 1 minute more.
    6. Return the chicken, pour in the sauce, and simmer 1 to 2 minutes until it thickens and turns glossy.
    7. Add the noodles and toss until every strand is coated. Finish with the remaining green onions.
    8. Portion into 14 containers while still warm so the sauce distributes evenly.

    Portion It

    Set the full pot on the scale, subtract the empty pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Weigh that amount into each container. Every bowl comes out to about 660 calories with 57 grams of protein.
