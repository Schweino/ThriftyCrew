Makes 14 servings · ~567 cal · 38g protein · 69g carbs · 15g fat · ~$4.77 per serving (at everyday cost).

Bibimbap is the original meal prep bowl: rice on the bottom, savory-sweet gochujang beef, crisp cucumber and carrot, and an egg on top. Ours delivers 38 grams of protein per bowl for about $4.77 (at everyday cost), which is roughly what the restaurant version charges just for the egg and the smile.

Ingredients

  • Gochujang (generic): 0.5 cups (140 g)
  • Soy Sauce (generic): 3.5 tbsp (56 g)
  • Rice Vinegar (Nakano): 3.5 tbsp (52 g)
  • Honey (Great Value): 3.5 tbsp (74 g)
  • Sesame Oil (generic): 1.25 tbsp (16 g)
  • Garlic Powder: 0.25 tbsp (3 g)
  • Toasted Sesame Seeds (generic): 1 tbsp (10 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 5.25 cups dry (970 g)
  • 93/7 Ground Beef (Member's Mark): 3.5 lb (1588 g)
  • Sugar-Free Maple Syrup (generic): 7 tbsp (119 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 5 (320 g)
  • Cucumber: 3.5 (1050 g)
  • Green Onions: 13.25 tbsp (79 g)
  • Eggs (Great Value): 14 large eggs (700 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (20 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Ground beef is over 60 percent of this batch cost. The 4 pound value pack drops the per-pound price enough to matter, and the extra half pound freezes fine.
    • One tub of gochujang covers several batches of this recipe. It looks pricey on the shelf, but spread across all those batches it works out to cheap heat.
    • Cucumbers are the sneaky cost here. If your store sells them loose, weigh a few and pick the heavy ones. Same price, more food.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cook the rice: 5.25 cups dry rice with water per your rice cooker or pot method.
    3. Whisk the sauce: gochujang, soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, and sesame oil.
    4. Brown the ground beef in your big pot over medium-high until no pink remains, then drain. Stir in the sugar-free maple syrup, garlic powder, and half the sauce, and simmer 3 minutes.
    5. Shred the carrots and thin-slice the cucumbers. Toss the cucumbers with a pinch of salt so they stay crisp in the fridge.
    6. Fry or soft-scramble the 14 eggs. For meal prep that keeps, cook the yolks through.
    7. Build each bowl: rice, beef, carrots, cucumber, one egg, a drizzle of the remaining sauce, and a sprinkle of green onion and sesame seeds.

    Portion It

    Weigh the pot of finished beef, subtract the empty pot number from step 1, and divide by 14 so every bowl gets the same share. Do the same quick math on the rice. Each assembled bowl lands at 567 calories and 38 grams of protein.
