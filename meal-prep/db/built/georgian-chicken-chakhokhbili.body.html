Makes 14 servings · ~735 cal · 56g protein · 57g carbs · 28g fat · ~$3.16 per serving (at everyday cost).

Chakhokhbili is a Georgian braise, from the country in the Caucasus, and it is built on a ratio most American recipes would call a typo: nearly as much onion as chicken. No water goes in the pot. The onions and tomatoes release everything the dish needs, and it cooks down into something concentrated and herby with a fistful of fresh cilantro stirred in at the very end. Each bowl runs 735 calories and 56 grams of protein for about $3.16 (at everyday cost), and that is a genuinely high protein figure for a braise.

Ingredients

  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Thigh (Member's Mark): 7.75 lb (3500 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 14.3 (1575 g)
  • Diced Tomatoes (Great Value): 4.6 cans (1911 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 4 tbsp (70 g)
  • Fresh Cilantro: 70 tbsp (70 g)
  • Jalapeno: 5.5 tbsp (49 g)
  • White Vinegar (generic): 0.75 cups (175 g)
  • Vegetable Oil (Great Value): 1.25 cups (252 g)
  • Bay Leaves (Great Value): 0.25 oz (9 g)
  • Garlic: 8.25 tbsp (70 g)
  • Red Bell Pepper (generic): 0.5 cups (56 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 3.75 cups dry (700 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Chicken thighs are the biggest line here, and that is by design. Thighs are the budget and flavor pick for an hour in a covered pot, so the money is already going to the right cut. If you spot breast on a deep markdown it works, but it dries out in a long braise.
    • Onions are the second largest line and you need a lot of them, so buy the four or five pound bag rather than loose. They keep for a month somewhere cool and dry.
    • Canned diced tomatoes provide all the liquid in this braise, which is why the recipe adds no water at all. Buy the cans on sale and stack them, since they keep for a year.
    • Cilantro goes in by the bunch at the end, not by the sprig. Buy two bunches the day you cook and keep the stems in water in the fridge, because wilted cilantro adds nothing.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chicken into large pieces and season it with salt and black pepper. Heat a dry heavy pot over medium-high and brown the chicken in the vegetable oil in batches until it takes color, then set it aside.
    3. Slice all the onions thin and add them to the pot. Cook them over medium heat for 15 to 20 minutes until they are completely soft and starting to turn gold. This is the largest ingredient in the dish, so it deserves the time.
    4. Add the garlic, minced jalapeno and diced red bell pepper and cook 3 more minutes.
    5. Return the chicken with its juices, add the diced tomatoes and the bay leaves, and stir. Add no water at all; the tomatoes and onions provide everything this braise needs, and that concentration is the whole point of chakhokhbili.
    6. Cover and simmer over low heat for 40 to 50 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is tender and the sauce has thickened around it.
    7. Stir in the white vinegar and taste. It should read savory first with a sharp edge behind it. Adjust the salt now, while everything is hot.
    8. Kill the heat, pull the bay leaves, and fold in the whole pile of chopped cilantro so it wilts but stays green. Cook the rice separately and fold it in or serve the braise over it.

    Portion It

    Set the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, one number, no favorites. Each one carries 735 calories, and a handful of fresh cilantro stirred in after reheating brings it right back to day one.
