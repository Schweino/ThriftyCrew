Makes 14 servings · ~571 cal · 36g protein · 66g carbs · 18g fat · ~$3.63 per serving (at everyday cost).

Madras is the hot one. It came out of South India by way of British curry houses, and what defines it is the combination of real heat with real acid, chili powder up front and lemon and lime at the back. This build marinates the chicken first, then simmers it with tomatoes, carrots and bell pepper and rounds the edge off with a single can of coconut milk. Each bowl runs 571 calories and 36 grams of protein for about $3.63 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Boneless Skinless Chicken Breast (Member's Mark): 3.75 lb (1750 g)
  • Garlic: 6.5 tbsp (56 g)
  • Fresh Cilantro: 7 tbsp (7 g)
  • Lemon Juice (ReaLemon): 1.7 (79 g)
  • Chili Powder: 1.75 tbsp (14 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1 tbsp (16 g)
  • Curry Powder (generic): 7 tbsp (44 g)
  • Garam Masala (McCormick Gourmet): 1.25 tbsp (7 g)
  • Butter (generic): 0.75 cups (147 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 3 (350 g)
  • Ginger (generic): 4.25 tbsp (26 g)
  • Diced Tomatoes (Great Value): 1.7 cans (700 g)
  • Carrots (generic): 2.4 (149 g)
  • Red Bell Pepper (generic): 1 (149 g)
  • Lime Juice: 3.5 tbsp (52 g)
  • Sugar (Great Value): 2.75 tbsp (33 g)
  • Coconut Milk (Thai Kitchen): 1 can (396 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.5 tbsp (4 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 5 cups dry (938 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Chicken breast is the biggest line by a mile. Boneless thighs would cost noticeably less per pound and hold up better to a simmer, so make that swap if the budget is the priority.
    • One can of coconut milk is all this recipe needs, which is unusual for a curry. Buy the store brand full fat can and skip the light version, which is mostly water at nearly the same price.
    • Curry powder and garam masala are the two blends doing the heavy lifting. Buy both in bags from an international grocer instead of branded jars and you will pay a fraction of the price for a fresher product.
    • Carrots and bell pepper are the volume vegetables. Buy carrots in the bag, and buy the bell pepper in whatever color is cheapest that week, since it is going into a red sauce anyway.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Cut the chicken into chunks and marinate it with the lemon juice, chili powder, salt and half the minced garlic for at least 30 minutes while you prep everything else.
    3. Melt the butter in your largest pot over medium and cook the diced onion for 8 minutes until soft and browning at the edges, then add the rest of the garlic and the grated ginger and cook one minute.
    4. Add the curry powder and garam masala and stir them in the hot butter for 45 seconds until they bloom and stop smelling raw.
    5. Add the diced tomatoes and cook down for 8 to 10 minutes, until the sauce darkens and thickens and the fat starts to come to the surface.
    6. Stir in the marinated chicken along with the sliced carrots and diced red bell pepper. Cover and simmer 20 to 25 minutes until the chicken reads 165F and the carrots are tender.
    7. Stir in the coconut milk, the sugar, the lime juice and the black pepper, and let it come back to a bare simmer for 5 minutes. Taste it: madras should be hot first and sour second.
    8. Cook the rice, let it steam covered off the heat for 10 minutes, then fold it through the curry with the chopped cilantro.

    Portion It

    Put the pot on the scale, subtract the empty-pot weight you wrote down, and divide by 14. Fourteen containers, one number. Each one lands at 571 calories, and this curry gets hotter as it sits, so day five bites harder than day one.
