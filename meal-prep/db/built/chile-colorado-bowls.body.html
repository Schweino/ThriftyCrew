Makes 14 servings · ~570 cal · 36g protein · 51g carbs · 23g fat · ~$6.08 per serving (at everyday cost).

Chile colorado is beef stewed in pure red chile sauce. No beans, no tomato base, no shortcuts through a can of enchilada sauce. Dried guajillos and anchos get soaked and blended, the chuck browns in flour first so the sauce clings, and then it just simmers until the beef gives up. Each bowl carries 36 grams of protein and 570 calories for about $6.08 (at everyday cost).

Ingredients

  • Dried Guajillo Chiles (generic (USDA)): 4 oz (112 g)
  • Dried Ancho Chiles (generic (USDA)): 2.75 oz (79 g)
  • Dried Arbol Chiles (generic (USDA)): 0.08 cups (2 g)
  • Yellow Onion: 1 (128 g)
  • Dried Oregano (Great Value): 0.75 tbsp (2 g)
  • Salt (Morton): 1.25 tbsp (21 g)
  • Garlic: 1.5 tbsp (12 g)
  • Ground Cumin: 0.25 tbsp (2 g)
  • Beef Chuck Roast: 4.75 lb (2117 g)
  • All-Purpose Flour (Great Value): 4.5 tbsp (37 g)
  • Black Pepper (Great Value): 0.25 tbsp (1 g)
  • Olive Oil: 4.75 tbsp (63 g)
  • Beef Broth (Great Value): 5.75 cups (1400 g)
  • Bay Leaves (Great Value): 0.04 oz (1 g)
  • Rice (Member's Mark): 3.75 cups dry (700 g)

    Shop Smart

    • Buy dried chiles by the bag in the Latin foods aisle rather than in spice jars. The price difference per ounce is not small, it is enormous.
    • Chuck roast is the correct cut for a two hour braise. Anything more tender would cost more and fall apart into strings.
    • The flour dredge is a free thickener. It gives you a browned crust and a sauce that coats instead of running, from an ingredient you already own.
    • Rice cooked plain is the right base. The chile sauce is doing enough, and seasoning the rice would just muddy it.

    Make It

    1. Weigh your empty mixing pot and write the number down for portioning later.
    2. Stem and seed the guajillo and ancho chiles along with the arbol chiles from the pantry list, and toast them briefly in a dry pan until fragrant.
    3. Soak the toasted chiles in hot water for 20 minutes, then blend them with the onion, garlic, oregano, cumin, and a cup of the soaking liquid until smooth. Strain the sauce.
    4. Cut the chuck roast into large cubes, season with salt and black pepper, and toss them in the flour.
    5. Brown the floured beef in the olive oil in batches in a heavy pot, working in batches so the flour crisps rather than steams.
    6. Return all the beef, pour in the strained chile sauce and the beef broth, and add the bay leaves.
    7. Cover and simmer on the lowest heat for 2 to 2.5 hours, stirring now and then, until the beef is fork tender and the sauce has darkened and thickened.
    8. Pull the bay leaves, taste for salt, and cook the rice separately.

    Portion It

    Weigh the full pot, subtract the empty-pot number you wrote down, and divide by 14. Spoon that weight of beef and red chile sauce into each container, then add the same weight of rice to every one. Each bowl lands at 570 calories with 36 grams of protein.
