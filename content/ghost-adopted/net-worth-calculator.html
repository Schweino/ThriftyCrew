Your net worth is the single truest scoreboard in personal finance. It is everything you own minus everything you owe, in one honest number. Income tells you what flows in. Net worth tells you what you are actually keeping. Fill in what you can and watch it add up.

A negative number is a starting point, not a verdict. Plenty of people with big incomes have a net worth in the red, and plenty of quiet, steady savers are millionaires. What matters is the direction. Come back once a year, same date, and compare. As long as the trend is up, you are winning.

Why net worth beats income

It is easy to look rich and be broke. A big paycheck spent on big payments leaves you with nothing to show for it. Net worth cuts through the noise. It does not care what you make or what you drive. It only asks one question: after everything you owe, what is actually yours? That is the number the wealthy quietly watch, and it is the one to start watching too.

How to make it grow

There are only two levers. Grow the assets side by saving and investing consistently, and shrink the debt side by paying down what you owe. Do both at once and your net worth climbs from both directions. You do not need a windfall. You need a small, steady gap between what you earn and what you spend, pointed at these two jobs, month after month.
