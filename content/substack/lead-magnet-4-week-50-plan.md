## Here's the whole idea

Most food money doesn't leak at the grocery store. It leaks at 6pm — tired, hungry, nothing thawed, "let's just grab something." Do that four or five nights a week and it's a few hundred dollars gone a month, one harmless choice at a time.

This plan removes the decision. You cook **one batch each Sunday** — about 20 minutes of hands-on work — and dinner already exists all week. No willpower required at the worst possible moment.

**How to use it:**
- Each week is **one recipe, one shop, one cook.** A batch makes **14 servings** — a week of dinners for two, or two weeks for one.
- Groceries land around **$35–$50 a week**, roughly **$3 a plate.**
- Cook Sunday, portion into containers, grab-and-go all week. Most of these freeze and reheat great.
- Nothing fancy in the cart — store brands, family-pack proteins on sale, compare price per ounce. That's the whole game.

*(Cost and nutrition numbers are honest estimates — they shift a little by store and season.)*

## Week 2 — Chicken Marinara Pasta
**14 servings · about $40 · ~$2.85 a plate**

Pizza night energy, meal-prep price — chicken, penne, marinara, melty mozzarella, and turkey pepperoni stirred through.

**Shopping list:**
- Chicken breast — ~5 lb
- Marinara sauce — four 24-oz jars (store brand)
- Penne pasta — 2 boxes
- Turkey pepperoni — ~15 oz
- Reduced-fat shredded mozzarella — ~16 oz
- Cottage cheese — ~8 oz (blended into the sauce)

*The gist: boil the penne, cook and cube the chicken, blend the cottage cheese into the marinara, then stir it all together with the mozzarella and pepperoni until melty. Full step-by-step, macros, and portioning are in the membership.*

## Week 4 — Shredded BBQ Chicken Sammies
**14 servings · about $45 · ~$3.20 a plate**

The comfort-food payoff — slow-cooker BBQ chicken with crinkle fries. Set it and forget it.

**Shopping list:**
- Chicken breast — ~5 lb
- BBQ sauce — about three 18-oz bottles (sugar-free keeps calories down; regular store brand is cheaper — your call)
- Crinkle-cut fries — the big bag (~5 lb is the better deal per pound; freezes great)
- Buns — whatever's cost-effective

*The gist: season the chicken, slow-cook on HIGH ~4 hours, shred, mix in the BBQ sauce; bake the fries fresh. Full step-by-step, macros, and portioning are in the membership.*

## The one-page grocery list (print this)

**Week 1:** chicken (5 lb) · cottage cheese (24 oz) · alfredo sauce (3 jars) · rotini (2 boxes) · broccoli (big bag)
**Week 2:** chicken (5 lb) · marinara (4 jars) · penne (2 boxes) · turkey pepperoni (15 oz) · mozzarella (16 oz) · cottage cheese (8 oz)
**Week 3:** ground beef 93/7 (6 lb) · pasta sauce (3 jars) · penne (2 boxes) · cottage cheese (24 oz)
**Week 4:** chicken (5 lb) · BBQ sauce (3 bottles) · crinkle fries (big bag) · buns

**Two shopping rules that save the most:**
1. **Buy your protein on sale and freeze it.** Chicken and beef swing in price constantly — grab family packs when they're marked down. One good chicken buy (say 50 lb at $1.99 instead of $2.99) can save $50 in a single trip.
2. **Compare price per ounce, every time.** It's the one number that actually tells you the deal. Store brands usually win; Costco/Sam's usually wins on cottage cheese and mozzarella.

## The money page (this is the point)

Add it up: four weeks, four batches, **56 dinners for about $169.**

Now picture those same 56 dinners as "let's just grab something." Even at a modest **$12 a pop, that's about $670.** At real takeout-and-delivery prices it's well over a thousand.

So this plan quietly hands you **roughly $500 back in a single month** — not by earning a dollar more, not by feeling deprived. Just by cooking on Sunday.

Here's the part that matters: **that $500 is only a win if you catch it.** Money you "saved" but left in checking has a way of evaporating. So do this — the moment you finish a no-takeout week, move that difference somewhere on purpose:
- Send it at your smallest debt (that's how a $1,000 balance disappears in months instead of years), or
- Pay yourself first — move it to savings automatically, before it can drift off.
