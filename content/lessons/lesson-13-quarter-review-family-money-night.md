# Week 13 — Quarter Review + Family Money Night

*Twelve weeks in. Let's stop, look back, and make what you've built visible.*

**This week's big idea:** Celebrating progress isn't a soft bonus — it's the fuel that keeps the habits alive. Counting your wins, out loud and on purpose, is how you make the next quarter easier than the first.

## You Made It Through the First Quarter

This isn't just a check-in. It's a moment worth naming.

Over the past twelve weeks, you and your kid have covered a lot of ground — Future You, compounding, morning routines, the gap, needs vs. wants, budgets, tracking, first jobs, paying yourself first, bank accounts, allowance structures, and money conversations that aren't fights. That's not a small list.

Here's the thing about twelve weeks of building something: it rarely looks dramatic while it's happening. A little more awareness here. A slightly different conversation there. Maybe your kid started a spending log and kept it for three weeks before life interrupted. Maybe you finally opened that youth savings account. Maybe you just talk about money differently now — less tense, more matter-of-fact.

All of it counts. None of it is wasted.

## Why Celebrating Small Wins Actually Matters

There's a trap that catches a lot of well-meaning families here. They look at a quarter of money lessons and immediately think about what they didn't do — the budget that lapsed, the savings habit that slipped, the conversations that turned into lectures. And then they quietly quit.

Here's what's more useful: **count what happened, not what didn't.**

Small wins compound just like money does. The kid who tracked spending for ten days straight — even if they stopped — built a ten-day habit they didn't have before. That matters. The parent who had one genuinely calm money conversation matters. The kid who said "I know, but wait — that's a want, not a need" even once? That's the whole lesson landing.

Don't grade this quarter against a perfect version. Grade it against where you started.

## Making Progress Visible

One of the best things you can do tonight — yes, tonight — is make progress visible in some small way.

That might look like:
- Writing down three money things your kid knows now that they didn't twelve weeks ago.
- Pulling up their bank account and looking at the balance together, even if it's small.
- Revisiting the "Future You" paragraph your kid wrote in Week 1 and reading it out loud.

Teens especially need progress to be named for them. They won't usually volunteer that they've grown. But if you say, "Hey, three months ago you didn't know what a budget was. Now you've built one," — that lands. It stacks another small brick of identity: I'm someone who knows about money.

## What to Carry Forward

Before you step into Quarter 2, it's worth making a quick list of what's still in motion and what needs a nudge.

Ask yourself:
- Is the **pay-yourself-first** habit actually running? Even a small percentage, moved on payday, matters more than the amount.
- Is there a **bank account** open? If not, this is a good week to finally do it.
- Are we **tracking** at all — even loosely? A simple notes-app list of purchases is enough.
- Are we talking about money **without it turning into a fight**? That one's the long game, and every calm conversation is a win.

You don't need all four humming perfectly. But notice which one's wobbling most, and give it a little extra attention this week before you turn the page to Quarter 2.

## Try this together

Hold your first real **Family Money Night** this week. It doesn't need to be long — 30 to 40 minutes is plenty. Get snacks. Sit down somewhere comfortable. Keep the phones face-down (yours too).

Here's a simple structure you can use:

**1. Name three wins each.** You go first. Be specific. ("I moved money to savings the same day I got paid twice this quarter" is better than "I saved more.") Then your kid. No minimizing — if they mention something that seems small, take it seriously.

**2. Look at the Week 1 "Future You" paragraphs together.** Read them out loud. Does anything feel different now? Does any piece of the plan feel more real?

**3. Ask one question:** "What's one money habit from this quarter you actually want to keep?" Listen without adding to it. Whatever they say is the right answer.

**4. Celebrate.** A small treat, a movie, a dessert — something that says "this mattered."

Family Money Night works best when it's low-pressure and a little fun. The goal isn't a lecture. It's a tradition.

## Questions to sit with

**For you (the parent):**
- What's one win from this quarter you didn't expect — something your kid did or said that surprised you?
- Where did you feel the most resistance — in yourself or in your kid — and what does that tell you?
- What would it look like to make Family Money Night a monthly habit going forward?

**For your kid (ask them, or do it together):**
- What's one money habit from the last twelve weeks that's actually stuck — even a little?
- When you think about the "Future You" paragraph you wrote back in Week 1, does anything feel different now?
- What's one thing about money that you understand now that used to feel confusing or off-limits?
